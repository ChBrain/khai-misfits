# Order: Settle the Two Cautions

## Direction

The slate carried two lines at P3 that were slated with cautions rather than
with confidence, which was honest and is not a state to leave them in. **An
undecided line is worse than no line**, because it will be read as work waiting
to be done and will cost its full authoring price before anybody discovers it
should not have been started. Both were settled by reading the incumbents rather
than by arguing from memory, and both strike.

**Gibbard-Satterthwaite is Whoever Asks Last's own governing law, seen from the
strategic side.** That misfit does not merely stage cyclical majorities: its
Line of Work states Arrow's theorem as the law of the play, that no method of
aggregating individual orderings can, for three or more alternatives, be at once
defined for all preferences, faithful to unanimity, independent across pairs and
free of a dictator. Gibbard-Satterthwaite is that impossibility restated as a
claim about misreporting, and the two are interderivable. **The caution recorded
on the slate was the real objection**: one domain restriction, single-peaked
preferences, removes both harms at once, and Whoever Asks Last already names
that escape in its own prose.

And the half that is not the theorem is held too. The practical shape, that
every real system rewards tactical voting, is **The Wasted Vote**. So both ends
are occupied, the structural and the lived, and there is nothing between them
for a third play to stand on.

**The chain store paradox goes quiet when You Have to Mean It's engine is
switched off, and that misfit survives the chain store's, which is the asymmetry
the contract says decides it.** You Have to Mean It's engine is the
time-inconsistency of a threat: every reason to carry it out is a reason to have
intended it, and none survives into the moment when acting is possible.
Stipulate that away, so that fighting an entrant is worth doing on the day for
its own sake, and the monopolist fights in the last town as readily as the
first, the induction never starts, and the chain store harm vanishes entirely.
Run it the other way and stipulate an unknown or unbounded horizon, which is the
chain store's own distinctive element: reputation becomes sustainable and that
harm vanishes, while a one-shot deterrent threat is still irrational to keep.
**The incumbent survives the candidate's engine being switched off and the
candidate does not survive the incumbent's**, which is an Origin row and not a
play.

## Orders

Strike both lines, and record which move each strike is, because they are not
the same move. Gibbard-Satterthwaite is **struck outright**: nothing would bring
it back, since it is one incumbent's law and another's practice with no residue
between them. The chain store is **struck as a misfit and slated as an Origin
row**, since a mechanism demonstrated in a second domain is what an Origin table
is for and this house's warrants are the better for running deeper.

Record that the strike came from reading the incumbent's own Line of Work rather
than from reasoning about the concept. Whoever Asks Last carries Arrow in its
prose and the slate line did not know it: **a caution about a candidate is
usually a question about an incumbent, and the incumbent is written down.**

Record the general rule this pass establishes about the slate itself. A line
slated with a caution is a **debt**, not an item, and it should be settled in
the next pass that has room rather than carried. The cost of settling both here
was two files read; the cost of not settling them was two authoring passes that
would each have died at the dedup.

## Implementation

The two strikes are appended in place on the Playwright's standing slate,
[Fill the Season](../plan_fill_the_season.md), beneath the lines they close, with
the switch-offs written out in both directions and the distinction between the
two kinds of strike stated. A new line is opened for the chain store's Origin
row, on the misfit's own lane, since it ships package content and rebuilds the
index.
