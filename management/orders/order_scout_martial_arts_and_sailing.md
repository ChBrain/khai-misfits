# Order: Scout Martial Arts and Sailing

## Direction

Two domains were pointed in one message, judo and sailing, the second carrying its own
hook: that a boat sails best against the wind. The pointing rule as the thirty-seventh
pass left it says a domain named by somebody who knows a practice from the inside yields
roughly one candidate against roughly one per ten from an instrument sweep, and it had
only ever been run one domain at a time. **Two at once is the first real test of it**,
because the obvious failure of a doubled pointing is that the author works whichever
domain is easier and returns one candidate wearing two labels.

It held. One P1 came out of each, they share no literature, no adjacent incumbent and no
shape, and neither borrowed from the other. The martial-arts candidate is the technique
too severe to rehearse; the sailing candidate is the lee shore, which is the hook taken
at its word rather than around it, since the rig that makes a boat sail to windward is
what gives it a sector it cannot sail into, and the sector points at the escape.

The pass tested two candidates hard rather than eight lightly, and both survived the
switch-off test against the incumbent that looked most likely to annex them. One
candidate was tested and **struck**: rapid weight loss in combat sports goes quiet the
moment The Arms Race's engine is switched off, since a competitor with no rivals has no
weight to make, and the categorical boundary that looked like a separate mechanism does
not save it. That strike is recorded as a strike because the test was actually run.

The scan owed at the end of a pass was run at the start of this one, and it earned its
place: `Ericsson` is already held by Neil Ericsson and the natural source for the
feedback requirement is K. Anders Ericsson. **The cost of that was then mis-read**, and
the mis-reading is the finding. `--surname` reports the index key, and an undeclared
surname is bare by construction, so the output invited the cells-first reading and the
probe returned zero cells owed.
