# Order: Scout SDLC, DevOps and CMMI

## Direction

The thirty-fifth pass established that a domain named by somebody who works in it
outyields an instrument sweep, and this pass takes three more from the same author:
the software development lifecycle, DevOps, and CMMI for Development.

The framework vocabulary is unstaged throughout, which by the standing rule means
nothing until each mechanism is tested. Three domains produced **one** P1, against the
thirty-fifth's one from one and the thirty-fourth's one from ten domains and seventy
terms. **The rule to carry is the ratio and not either number: a pointed probe yields
about one candidate per domain, an instrument sweep about one per ten.**

Most of the ground was already staged and the kills were quick. One candidate is held
with its cut owed rather than dropped, because switching off two incumbents' engines
takes most of it with them and the surviving version has to be established first.

The pass also found the Source-column defect a third time, in the form no wall can
catch, and that reading is the part of this pass most worth keeping.

## Orders

Slate the one candidate that survived as P1, carrying both halves of its warrant, the
organisational asymmetry and the measured cognitive one, and the four cuts that clear
it.

Record the kills with the incumbent each collided with. Record the DevOps team that
becomes a third silo as an **Origin row in The Mirror** rather than a play: Conway's
law applied to the remedy is still Conway's law.

Hold the long-lived branch with its cut owed. Switching off Good Money After Bad's sunk
cost and Seed Corn's short horizon takes most of the postponement with them, so the
candidate stands only if it can be cut to the symmetric case in which nobody is
postponing anything, and whether that version has a harm is to be settled before it is
slated.

Record that `Baron` is one scholar on two works and **must not be declared**, since
declaring it would split Jonathan Baron across two keys.

Record the Source-column reading in full: the index is a record of who the house cites
**in one column**, the two earlier instances were caught only because a namesake forced
a resolution, this one has no namesake and never will, and the difference is invisible
to every wall because the walls check the index against itself rather than against the
warrants. Leave the remedy to the kit and record what a clear `--surname` result does
and does not mean.
