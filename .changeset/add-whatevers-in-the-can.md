Add the misfit Whatever's in the Can: a staging of the garbage can model of organizational choice, where an organized anarchy with ambiguous goals, unclear means, and a shifting cast produces decisions not by a march from problem to solution but by the temporal coincidence of four independent streams, problems looking for a forum, solutions looking for a use, participants coming and going, and choice opportunities opening. A choice opportunity is a can into which the participants present tip whatever problems and solutions they carry, so the decision turns on who was in the room and what they brought, and a solution that existed before its question attaches to whatever choice happened to open. The choice is often reached by oversight, closed before problems attach, or by flight, the problems drifting off to a more inviting can, so the decision and the problem it named come apart, the meeting productive and the concern unsolved, with no one scheming and no one in error. Structure, an access rule, a decision rule, a deadline that times the streams, can tighten the coupling but is costly, narrows the useful openness, and cannot be fully imposed where goals stay ambiguous. Cut to the decisions-couple-by-timing vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from The Law of the Instrument (a coincidence of streams, not one tool reshaping every problem), Everybody's Business (decisions are made, briskly, they simply do not track problems, not dropped for want of an owner), The Colour of the Bike Shed (sorting by temporal coincidence, not by triviality and comprehensibility), and No One in Charge (a plain chair and plain authority, the decoupling from the streams and not from hidden power), and held to its bound, since it needs organized anarchy and loose structure and lifts under clear goals, understood means, stable participation, or an imposed access and timing (Cohen, March, and Olsen; March and Olsen; Kingdon).
