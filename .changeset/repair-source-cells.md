Repair eleven Origin source cells that the science index was reading wrongly, in two directions. Five real authors were being dropped from the index entirely and two people who never existed were being listed in it. Both faults come from the same habit: a source cell written "Gresham / Macleod" or "Negroponte / Media Lab", where the slash is doing the work of a comma. The index splits an author list on commas, semicolons, ampersands and the word "and", and not on a slash, so it read each of those cells as one author and keyed it on the last word. That silently discarded Gresham, Khazzoom, Shirky, Eisenhower and Negroponte, and it manufactured a scholar called **Island** out of "EEMUA; ISA / Three Mile Island" and one called **Lab** out of "Negroponte / Media Lab". A twelfth cell, "Bretton Woods", was keying the 1944 monetary settlement as a person under **Woods**, where it merged with the resilience-engineering researcher David Woods, so one entry claimed a scholar had written both about the sharp end of human error and about the dollar-gold exchange standard. The slash is not fixed in the parser and deliberately so: this house uses it in two incompatible senses, as an author separator in "Campbell / Goodhart" and as a concept pair in "Attribution / reward" or "Take-up / practice", and a parser taught to split on it would recover eight authors while inventing nine new phantoms out of the concept pairs, which is a worse index than the one we have. So the cells are corrected instead: a comma where the slash meant "and", and the house's existing drop pattern where a source is a record or an institution rather than a person, ending the cell in an ordinary lowercase noun so the parser passes over it, exactly as The Coinage does in Loudest When Hushed. Verified against the rebuilt index: **Island** and **Lab** are gone, Eisenhower, Gresham, Khazzoom, Negroponte and Shirky are present, the Goodhart, Campbell and Daniels rows now attach to keys that already existed, and Woods holds two rows and both are David Woods. No staging changes and no citation changes: the same work is credited to the same people, and only the punctuation between their names has moved.
