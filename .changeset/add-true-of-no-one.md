Add the misfit True of No One: a staging of heterogeneity of treatment effects, where a randomised trial can validly estimate only the average effect across its participants while those participants differ substantially in what they stood to gain, so a guideline written from the average and applied to an individual treats many patients for a benefit that accrued, in the trial itself, mostly to a minority at high risk. The trial is run properly and its number is correct; the trap is downstream of a sound result. The trialist reports the average because it is the honest ceiling of the design, and declines the finer answer because one-variable-at-a-time subgroup analysis is documented to produce claims that do not replicate. Outcome risk is typically skewed, so a high-risk minority collect most of the benefit while harms and costs fall far more evenly, since a side effect does not check baseline risk before arriving. A panel then converts the average into a recommendation, because guidance must be statable, followable, and auditable to function at all, and a guideline hedged into fifty branches is not one, so usability requires uniformity and the last representation of the distribution leaves the chain there. And a clinician applies it, because departing from validated evidence on a feeling about this patient is precisely the failure evidence-based practice was built to end, and the only instrument for individualising is the clinical impression the discipline is right to distrust. The result is a low-risk patient carrying years of side effects and cost for a gain near zero, inside a chain where every step was correct, the decision fully documented and beyond audit, and nothing in the record marking the mismatch. Risk-stratified analysis recovers who benefits, and costs individual participant data pooled across trials never designed to be pooled, a risk model that must be externally validated or the overfitting merely relocates, and an output that returns as a probability rather than an answer. Cut to the valid-average-wrong-shape vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from The Proxy (statistical discrimination runs a self-confirming loop that changes the group's real average; here nothing is confirmed and nothing changes, the average being simply correct), Better in Both (there the average is an artefact of reclassification; here it is accurate and undistorted, and the failure is downstream of a correct number), The Bigger Picture (there two levels of one table disagree and the question is which to trust; here they are consistent, and the difficulty is that the level the evidence supports is not the level the decision requires), and Overfitting (there the fault is too much fitting; here it is deliberate under-fitting, one parameter for a whole population, adopted because fitting further is known not to hold). Held to its bound: it needs a genuinely heterogeneous population and a trial unable to resolve who is who, and the play is not offered as medical advice, as a reason to stop any treatment, or as a claim that guidelines are wrong or trials mislead, the opposite being nearer the point, since the difficulty survives all of that soundness (Kravitz, Duan and Braslow; Kent, Hayward and colleagues; Rothwell).
