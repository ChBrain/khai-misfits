Add the misfit Never Aimed at You: a staging of bystander selection for antibiotic resistance. A systemic antibiotic does not know what it was prescribed for. Somebody arrives with a urinary infection and is given a drug that is right for the organism causing it, which is the whole of the transaction as anybody in the room understands it. And the drug goes everywhere: the pneumococcus in the throat gets the dose, the staphylococcus on the skin gets the dose, the enterobacteria in the gut get the dose, none of them causing anything and none of them why anybody is here, and the community does not return to what it was when the course ends. Do that across a population and count where the exposure falls: for most species and most drugs, the large majority of the pressure on a species is bystander pressure, generated by prescriptions written for something else entirely. And the part that will not go away is that those prescriptions are appropriate, not most of them but the appropriate ones, because the drug was right and the dose was right and the patient needed it and the prescriber would write exactly the same thing again with the entire mechanism laid out. At the moment of decision the alternative is not a narrower drug but no drug: identification takes days, the patient is present now, and narrowing empirically before the culture returns kills people, so the remedy that would work is blocked by a clinical fact rather than by ignorance or haste. Then the harm surfaces somewhere else entirely, years later, in a different person, as an isolate that answers to nothing, so the decision and the damage share no patient, no clinician and no decade, and nothing travels back to the seat that generated the pressure. And the correction that has actually been built and funded and staffed is appropriateness-based stewardship, which is genuine and necessary work aimed at the prescriptions that should not have been written, while the mass generating the pressure is appropriate and passes the audit, so no amount of doing that job better reaches the bulk. Cut to the harm-outside-the-decision vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from The Commons, where each taker draws from a stock they benefit from and know they draw on, in that here the prescriber has no interest in the organism that pays and no view of it, so restrain-your-take does not reach the taking that matters; from First Do Harm, whose iatrogenic harm lands on the treated patient, in that here that patient is helped exactly as intended and the harm lands on a population years later by way of their commensals; from The Tyranny of Small Decisions, where each chooser is a party to the outcome and would have chosen otherwise had it been put as one question, in that here the prescriber would write the same prescription with the whole mechanism in front of them, because withholding it harms the patient in the room; and from The Horizon Problem, in that nothing here is bounded out and no redrawing helps, the organism being invisible because it is the subject of no decision at all and no test at the point of care names it. Held to its bound: the estimates are model-based and vary by species and by drug, the trap needs a systemic agent reaching a commensal flora, and withdrawing the drug does not reliably restore susceptibility because compensatory evolution reduces the cost of carrying resistance, so the finding redirects the target and argues against neither stewardship nor treating the patient (Christine Tedijanto, Scott Olesen, Yonatan Grad and Marc Lipsitch; Marc Lipsitch and Matthew Samore; Les Dethlefsen and David Relman; Dan Andersson and Diarmaid Hughes).
