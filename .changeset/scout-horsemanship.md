Scout horsemanship: the thirty-seventh pass, and a refinement to the pointing rule.

The two preceding passes showed that a domain named by somebody who works in it
outyields an instrument sweep by about ten to one. This one is a domain the author
knows from outside their profession, and it behaved identically: one domain, one P1, on
the same ratio. So the rule is not about employment but about knowing a practice from
the inside, and an avocation qualifies on the same terms.

The domain is wholly unstaged in both its own vocabulary and its mechanism vocabulary,
and the nearby terms that do appear belong to misfits about adaptive preferences, the
experience-sampling asymmetry, the hedonic treadmill and the expertise reversal effect,
none of which holds operant conditioning.

Slated at P1: The Release Is the Reward. In pressure-release training the reinforcing
event is the removal of the pressure, not its application, so a rider who asks more
firmly holds the pressure through the horse's attempt and punishes the response they
were asking for. The literature makes it worse than the pass first framed it: there is
a described continuum from poorly timed negative reinforcement into punishment, with no
boundary the rider can feel. Effort runs against instruction.

The lesson horse is slated P2 with a cut against Alarm Fatigue that may not be
makeable, and four further candidates are recorded untested rather than struck, because
a strike asserted without the switch-off test is a claim. Register only.
