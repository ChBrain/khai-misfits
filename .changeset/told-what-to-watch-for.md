Add the misfit **Told What to Watch For**: before a drug is prescribed the patient has to be told what it may do to them, and the telling is one of the things that does it. The duty is not a formality, because a risk not disclosed is a risk the patient never got to decline, so a clinician who left it out would be deciding on somebody else's behalf. The list is true in every line, since each named effect is on the label because it happened to somebody. The patient then watches for what they were told to watch for, which is the only sensible response to being warned, and the body they are watching is one that aches sometimes anyway. **The mechanism needs no new sensation, only a new reason to notice an old one.**

**The symptom is real and the attribution is the only false part**, and it is the part neither the patient nor the prescriber can check, because a body does not label which of its sensations came from where. The evidence that separates them is within-patient rather than between-group: the same patients took the drug, an identical placebo and no tablet in randomised months, and most of the burden recorded on the drug was recorded on the placebo too, with placebo months clearly worse than months with nothing. And the cost is not the ache. The drug is stopped, humanely and on everything anybody knows, for a harm it did not do, and the risk it was preventing comes back, which nobody will experience as an event either.

Joins the axis `disclosure-detail` at its existing negative sign as its second member, with **The Fix Tells Them Where**, and the two now name each other. In both, the harm scales with how exactly a disclosure specifies its subject, and in both the disclosure is owed: a fleet that is not told cannot patch and a patient who is not told cannot refuse. **What differs is who it is turned against, a third party who reads it there and the person it was owed to here.** Distinct from You Would Have to Tell, where the harm is that something never gets said; from Treated and Counted, where the patient holds a false belief and better explanation is the remedy, whereas here understanding it better is what makes it worse; and from A Lower Bar Set by a Higher One, which is about what a trial may compare against and would leave this play untouched.
