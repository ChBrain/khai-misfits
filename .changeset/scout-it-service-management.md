Scout IT service management: the thirty-fifth pass, pointed by the author rather than
by an instrument, at a domain the house had never entered.

That pointing is the pass's first finding. The last two passes ran the house's own
instruments and both came back qualified, the isolate list naming an exhausted
neighbourhood and the canon families turning out to be pairs rather than
conversations; the thirty-fourth then swept ten domains for one candidate. This pass
was one domain and produced a P1 in a single probe. An instrument reports where the
house is thin and a practitioner reports where the world is lumpy, and at 313 misfits
the second is the better yield.

Most of the domain's mechanisms died against incumbents: the known error and its
workaround is Shifting the Burden, priority inflation is Alarm Fatigue, and the
incident and problem split is The Firefighter's Medal. The watermelon service level is
held rather than slated, because the name collides with Watermelon Reporting and the
mechanism does not: that misfit stages the MUM effect, a sender softening bad news,
and here nothing is distorted and the numbers are true.

Slated: The Change Advisory Board at P1, on four years of survey data showing external
approval has no correlation with change failure rate while correlating negatively with
lead time, deployment frequency and time to restore service. The Unowned Seam at P2,
with its obvious spine closed to it by the shared-work wall. Register only; ships no
package content.
