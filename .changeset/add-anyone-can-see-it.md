Add the misfit Anyone Can See It: a staging of naive realism, where two parties look at the same situation and read it oppositely, each through an interpretive lens it does not notice using, so each experiences its own reading not as a construal but as the plain facts. From that felt plainness follow the three tenets: I see it as it objectively is, a reasonable person would agree, so the one who disagrees is uninformed, irrational, or biased. Each in good faith files the other's honest vantage as a defect, and because a construal cannot be caught from inside, each detects the bias in the other and never in itself, so the charge runs one way from each seat. More contact and more facts widen the gap rather than close it, since each construes the new facts too and reads the other's persistence as fresh proof of bias, and a neutral account is called slanted by both sides, so two reasonable people end further apart for having engaged, with no one lying and no one the fool the other takes them for. Cut to the construal-mistaken-for-reality vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Closing Ranks (inter-party entrenchment, not intra-group conformity), The Trip to Abilene (false discord by misreading vantage as bias, the mirror of false accord by misreading assent), Nobody Was Looking at Your Shirt (one's own construal projected as reality, not one's salience), and The Filter Bubble (two parties diverge on the very same account, the lens in the eye and not the feed), and held to its bound, since it needs a construal disagreement and low self-insight and lifts under a checkable fact, a genuine act of perspective-taking, or a disagreement that truly is the other side's error (Ross and Ward; Robinson, Keltner, Ward, and Ross; Vallone, Ross, and Lepper; Pronin, Lin, and Ross).
