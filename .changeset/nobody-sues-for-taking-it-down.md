Add the misfit **Nobody Sues for Taking It Down**: a host holding other people's material can lose the statutory protection its whole activity depends on by leaving infringing material up, and is immunised in terms for removing lawful material in good faith, so the two errors available to it are not comparable and the rational answer to any notice arriving in the right form is to act on it. The form is the whole of the test and nothing in the process asks whether the claim behind it is good. The party who pays for the cheap error was never in the exchange, was not told before the removal, and has a remedy that works and asks for a consent to be sued in a federal court. Distinct from And Then Who Makes Them, since stipulating a fully willing enforcer removes that harm and produces this one; from Nobody to Ask, where the missing party is the owner and the harm is a use that never happens; and from You Would Have to Tell, where the party who changes behaviour is the one the rule speaks to.

Joins the axis `removal-asymmetry` at a positive sign, which is the second member and the first opposition on it. **Who Took It Out** holds the other end and now names this play, as this play names it: there a removal is the attributed act and procedures accrete, here a removal is the protected act and everything complained of comes down. What sets the sign is where the cost of the cheap error lands, on the decider in one case and outside the transaction in the other. Also gives `removal-asymmetry` its sign comment, which it was missing.
