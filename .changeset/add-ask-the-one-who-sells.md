Add the misfit **Ask the One Who Sells** (supplier-induced demand): where the provider of a service is also the buyer's trusted advisor and is paid for each service rendered, and the buyer cannot judge what they need, the provider can recommend more than an informed buyer would choose and profits by doing so, often sincerely believing more is better, so demand is set in part by the supplier who meets it and the service is over-supplied at rising cost without a matching benefit, no fraud required. After Evans (1974), Roemer's law, and McGuire's physician-agency, with Arrow (1963) credited narrowly to the information asymmetry. Held to its bound: the effect is real but its magnitude is debated, not all care is induced, and the salary remedy trades the risk of too much care for the risk of too little.
