Add the misfit **The Same Molecule**: an old injectable comes off patent, a regulator certifies two versions interchangeable and they are, buying runs on price because that is what interchangeable means, and the medicine that got cheapest stops being made. What the manufacturer economises on is not hidden inside the vial but absent from the transaction, since the age of the fill line is not a property of the goods being bought. The failure arrives as an absence rather than a defect, which is what separates it from adverse selection: every unit delivered was exactly what was certified, so there is no bad instance for a market to select against and no claim for anybody to make.
