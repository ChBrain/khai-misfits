Add the misfit Worth More Nearly Gone: a staging of the anthropogenic Allee effect, where demand values a species for its rarity as such, so the price rises as the population falls and exploitation becomes more rewarding exactly as the population becomes least able to bear it. For most exploited populations scarcity protects: a thinned stock is expensive to work, effort moves elsewhere, and the species is shielded by its own unprofitability, which is why most decline without vanishing. For a class of goods the relation inverts, because rarity is not an obstacle to the value but its source, so the ordinary brake becomes an accelerator and each animal taken raises the worth of those remaining and pays for the next expedition. The loop contains no biology at all: no mate-finding fails and no cooperative defence collapses, the positive density dependence being supplied wholly by a price responding to a headcount. Nobody in it is a monster. Valuing a thing because it is rare is close to the definition of collecting, answering a price is what a supplier does, often from a place with few other ways to earn, and no party wants the species gone, a species gone being a market ended. Both remedies turn in the hand. Listing the species is right, necessary, and the precondition of every protective measure that follows, and the same publication announces to every buyer that the thing is officially scarce, so the premium can rise in the season the protection lands and there is no version of the act that carries the protection without the announcement. And flooding the market with bred or farmed stock, the only intervention that attacks the premium directly, can collapse the price and can also give illegal stock a channel to be laundered through and normalise a larger market that recruits demand faster than it satisfies it, so unit price and total volume must be read together and neither alone decides the species. Cut to the scarcity-prices-its-own-extinction vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Safety in Numbers, which stages the classical Allee effect where positive density dependence is intrinsic and biological and arises whether or not any human is present, whereas here the feedback is supplied wholly from outside and the two share a spiral shape and nothing of its engine; from The Commons, where the trouble is a resource too cheap to whoever takes it and the remedy is to price it, whereas here the resource is extremely expensive and its price is the thing killing it; and from Chasing the Price, where a production lag makes price and quantity cycle around an equilibrium, whereas here there is no lag and no oscillation but a monotonic spiral, since the stock does not grow back into the next round. Held to its bound: it needs demand keyed to rarity as such and a supply still reachable, and the play is not offered as an argument that conservation listing is counterproductive, that protection should be withheld, or that captive breeding is the wrong policy, the source literature raising these as hazards to be managed inside conservation practice rather than as arguments against it (Courchamp, Angulo, Rivalan and colleagues; Hall, Milner-Gulland and Courchamp; Allee).
