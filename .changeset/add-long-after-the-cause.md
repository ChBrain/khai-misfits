Add the misfit Long After the Cause: a staging of metastable failure in distributed systems, where a service running near its capacity holds two stable operating points and not one, a healthy point and a jammed point, so a transient trigger, a collection pause, a deploy that empties a cache, a dependency slow for ninety seconds, can push it across a threshold into the second and leave it there. What holds it there is the correct behaviour of everything inside it: a client retries a request that timed out, which every guide prescribes and every shipped default already does; a cache emptied by a restart fetches what it lacks, which is the whole of a cache's job; and a backend accepts the work in front of it rather than refusing capacity it has. Together these multiply the work, so timeouts make retries, retries make load, load makes timeouts, and a cold cache misses into a backend too saturated to let it warm, while much of that backend's effort goes to requests whose callers stopped waiting long ago. When the trigger ends nothing improves, because the loop no longer needs it, and the load at which the system could climb back out sits far below the load that broke it, so returning to an ordinary afternoon's traffic is nowhere near low enough. Every component passes its own tests at the moment the service is down. The only lever that reaches the loop is shedding load hard and holding it, which means refusing users the machines could have served, and the standing alternatives, budgeted retries, capped queues, and idle headroom, are bills paid on every quiet day against a rare one. Cut to the loop-outlives-its-trigger vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Faster Than It Can Cool (thermal runaway leaves no equilibrium at all and destroys the system; here it falls into a second one and sits in it, fully powered), Fixes that Fail (there a remedy applied to a symptom feeds it back through a delay; here nothing is applied as a remedy and there is no cycle of return), The Brittle Optimum (there slack is trimmed until the system shatters; here margin was present and nothing breaks), and Both Take the Crown (there an actual partition forks the state; here the network is fine and no invariant is violated), and held to its bound: it needs work amplification and a system already in its vulnerable band, and a system that recovers when its trigger ends was by definition never in one (Bronson, Aghayev, Charapko and Zhu; Huang, Magnusson and colleagues; Beyer, Jones, Petoff and Murphy; Nagle; Jacobson and Karels).
