Add the misfit The Weather We Make: a staging of endogenous risk, where every institution measures risk the same way and must act on what the measure says, so the premise that prices are given from outside is falsified by the fact that everyone holds it. A risk model is built the way a barometer is built: it reads what the world has done, reports what the world is likely to do, and does not suppose that reading it changes anything. For one institution acting alone that is exactly true, since one bank's selling does not move a market, and the finding is that this last clause is the whole of the matter and fails precisely when it is needed. Two conditions do the work and neither is sufficient alone. The first is harmonisation: a supervisor asking for a measure that is quantitative, auditable and sensitive to conditions is asking for a particular kind of model on a particular kind of history, and the good answers converge, so the models do not merely resemble one another, they agree and they update together. The second is the binding constraint: a measure wired to a capital rule is an instruction rather than an opinion, so agreement between models becomes simultaneity of trade. When the shock arrives nothing that follows is a mistake. Volatility rises, every model correctly reports that risk has risen, every limit binds, every holder reduces the same exposure, and the only counterparties available are the others doing the same, so the price falls because they are selling and volatility rises because it fell. The extreme at the end was priced as a once-in-many-lifetimes event and was not missed but manufactured. Nobody is a villain: the risk officer reports what a validated model produces and would be removed for reporting anything else, the supervisor requires an auditable number because a discretion it cannot inspect is the thing it may not accept, the desk cuts because the limit binds and holding is a breach, and there is nothing to enforce against because no rule was broken. Every remedy is a purchase rather than a repair: model diversity means ordering named institutions to run models measurably worse at describing their own risk, countercyclical buffers mean idle capital released on the judgement hardest to make at the moment it is needed, and a crude leverage cap punishes the safe balance sheet alongside the dangerous one. Cut to the identical-measurement-as-contagion vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Metric Fixation, where effort flows toward the measured dimension and the corruption is in the behaviour, whereas here nobody redirects any effort and an honest number is falsified after the fact by the aggregate of honest responses to it; from The Long Calm, whose driver is belief revised over years as a calm validates itself, whereas here a mandated mechanical rule needs no revision of anyone's beliefs and the loop can run in an afternoon, and Minsky has no counterpart to the everyone-holds-the-same-model vector; from The Bank Run, which needs a queue on a finite fund and is cured by a guarantor, whereas here nothing is depleted, there is no first-come advantage, and the selling is compelled by a limit rather than by belief about others; and from The Beauty Contest, which runs on a regress of beliefs about others' beliefs, whereas here no participant forms any belief about any other and the loop would run in full under common knowledge of every position. Held to its bound: it needs a common measure wired to a binding constraint and lifts where either is missing, and the play is not offered as a claim that risk measurement is useless or capital regulation a mistake, the source literature arguing for how such systems should be designed rather than against measuring risk (Daníelsson and Shin; Daníelsson, with Embrechts, Goodhart, Keating, Muennich, Renault and Shin on the Academic Response to Basel II).
