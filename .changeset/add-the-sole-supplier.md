Add the misfit The Sole Supplier: a staging of Niskanen's budget-maximizing bureau, where a government bureau is the only supplier of its service to a sponsor who cannot see what it truly costs, so it offers output and funding as one take-it-or-leave-it package and extracts a budget above the efficient level, and government over-supplies at no one's fault. There is no rival to compare the bureau against and no market price to read, and only the bureau knows its own cost function, so the sponsor cannot tell an efficient budget from an inflated one. The chief is rewarded not by profit but by the size of the budget, which brings staff, salary, standing, and discretion, so it maximizes the budget and pads the request invisibly, then bundles output and funding whole, since as the sole supplier it can offer no smaller portion. The sponsor, wanting the service and unable to price it or shop it, funds the package, and the bureau captures the surplus as budget, delivering more than the cost warrants or the right amount at an inflated cost, the public bearing the bill with no thief behind it. Making the cost visible by audit, yardstick comparison, or contracting out restores the sponsor's leverage, but the bureau knows its own costs best, comparison is imperfect, and some public goods have no market. Cut to the monopoly-supplier-extracts-under-hidden-cost vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Rent-Seeking (a bilateral-monopoly information gap, not wasteful competition for a transfer), The Iron Triangle (a two-party bargain, not a three-way ring), The High Water Mark (a standing feature, not a crisis ratchet), and Ask the One Who Sells (a whole budget bargained under hidden cost, not advice on a course of treatment), and held to its bound, its magnitude contested, since it needs a monopoly bureau, an under-informed sponsor, and budget-maximizing preferences and lifts under cost visibility, a rival to compare, or a chief rewarded for efficiency (Niskanen; Tullock; Migue and Belanger; Downs; Blais and Dion).
