Add the misfit Saved to Spread: a staging of leaky-vaccine-driven virulence evolution (the imperfect-vaccine hypothesis), carried with unusual care as a case for how to design a vaccine and never whether to take one. A pathogen's virulence is naturally capped, since a strain too deadly kills its host before it can spread, so the deadliest variants self-limit. A leaky vaccine, one that blunts disease and death but does not block infection or transmission, is life-saving and standard, and it changes the arithmetic: a vaccinated host survives an infection that would have killed an unvaccinated one, and while it survives it sheds, so a hyper-virulent strain that could never have spread from a dead host now spreads from a living, protected one. The cost of virulence is paid off by the vaccine, the cap lifts, selection stops punishing lethality, and the pathogen evolves deadlier, the disease staying mild in the protected and growing worse in itself. The burden of the evolved hypervirulence falls on the unprotected, the unvaccinated and those whose immunity has waned, who meet a strain more lethal than would ever have existed, with no one acting wrongly, the vaccinated shedding without choosing to and the pathogen only following selection. A sterilizing vaccine that blocks transmission removes the pressure and does not drive virulence up, but is harder to make and not available for every pathogen, so where only a leaky vaccine exists the right choice is still to use it. Cut to the protection-lifts-the-cap-on-virulence vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Sick Enough to Spread (the trade-off relaxed from outside, not the trade-off itself), Borrowed Immunity (no free-riding, arising from the vaccinated even under high coverage), and The Spent Margin (through selection on the pathogen, not through any change in human behaviour), and held to its bound, since it needs a leaky vaccine and a transmission-limited virulence and lifts under a sterilizing vaccine, a virulence not tied to transmission, or coverage that removes susceptibles (Read, Baigent, Powers, and colleagues; Gandon, Mackinnon, Nee, and Read; Anderson and May; Ewald).
