Add the misfit Must Be a Catch: a staging of reactive devaluation, where an offer's evaluated worth falls for no reason but that an adversary proposed it, since a party assumed to act in its own interest is assumed to have offered only what serves that interest. The discount lands hardest on the most generous concessions, a gift from a rival read as the largest gift from the least trusted source, exactly the ones most likely to close a deal, and it is reinforced by a political cost: a negotiator who looks to take a rival's offer at face value risks looking duped by its own side, so even one who privately judges the offer sound has reason to discount it publicly. The trap runs both ways, whichever side proposes the good deal, the other discounts it the same way, so terms both sides would accept from a neutral hand go unmade, with no side at fault. A trusted mediator relaying the identical terms stripped of their source can restore much of what the discount took, but only where the channel is already trusted and its neutrality is not itself contested. Cut to the source-devalues-the-offer vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Over a Barrel (a sunk, relationship-specific investment exposed to opportunism, not an offer devalued by its source before any investment is made) and The Third Option (a dominated decoy shifting choice between two other options, not a direct offer discounted by attribution), and held to its bound: it needs an adversarial relationship and a traceable source, and it lifts where the offer is relayed blind, a trusted mediator carries it, or the relationship stops being adversarial (Ross; Stillinger, Epelbaum, Keltner and Ross; Ross and Stillinger).
