Add the misfit Nobody Was Looking at Your Shirt, a staging of the spotlight effect: people overestimate how much others notice and judge their appearance and behavior, since one's own actions and appearance are so salient to oneself that this salience is projected onto the perspective of observers who are in fact paying far less attention, no villain required since the felt spotlight is a real anchoring effect and not vanity, and the room's ordinary inattention is not coldness but the same self-absorption the anchored person is themselves feeling, simply not returned. Held against its bound and hedged: the bias shrinks, though it does not vanish, with feedback and with familiarity between observer and observed, not a claim that others never notice anything.
