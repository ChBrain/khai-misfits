Add the misfit Too Dear to Fake: a staging of costly signaling and the handicap principle, where one party holds a quality another cannot see, so a bare claim proves nothing since anyone can make it, and the only credible proof is a signal too expensive to fake, a degree that need not teach, a tail that only handicaps, a spend that only shows. The holder burns real resource on a display whose whole force is its cost, and the receiver believes it precisely because a pretender could not have afforded it, so the cost is not the worth but the toll that makes the worth believable, a pure deadweight paid to no one. And because the signal works only by being dear, everyone who holds the quality must burn too or be pooled with those who do not, so the waste is field-wide and no one can stop it alone, and a reformer who would cut it finds a cheaper test that can be faked collapses to cheap talk, a direct check spends its own resource, and a banned signal breeds another. Cut to the waste-guarantees-honesty vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from The Arms Race (the spend conveys real information and persists one-to-one, it does not cancel for rank), The Done Thing (the cost itself carries the information, not the conferred legitimacy of a copied form), The Market for Lemons (two-sided reveal by a costly signal, not one-sided hidden quality unraveling the market), and The Leadership Script (an honest proof bought with real waste, not a cheap proxy mistaken for the thing), and held to its bound, since the honesty needs a differential cost and a hidden quality and lifts where the signal is cheap to fake, the quality is plain, or the cost is productive (Spence; Zahavi; Grafen; Veblen).
