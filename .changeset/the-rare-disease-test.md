Add the misfit The Rare Disease Test, a staging of the base rate fallacy: in judging the probability of a hypothesis given specific evidence, people fix on how well the evidence fits the hypothesis and neglect the prior probability, so a positive test for a rare condition is read as near-certain when it is in fact more likely a false positive, a sound test and an ordinary act of reasoning together yielding a confident wrong conclusion, no villain required since the test performs exactly as advertised and the reader who weighs a specific positive result heavily is doing what representativeness makes natural. Held against its bound and hedged: the neglect is strongest when the base rate is presented as a pallid statistic and weakens sharply when the same information is cast as natural frequencies or given a causal story, a documented tendency under ordinary framing and not a fixed incapacity to use base rates at all.
