Read **Good Enough to Leave** against `asset-specificity`, the question the last pass slated, and the answer is **no**. The deciding test is to redeploy the asset by fiat and watch its value: put the nurse in a foreign ward with no paperwork question and she is worth every penny; put the specialised plant beside a different buyer and the value collapses. **Asset specificity is a property of the asset and recognisability is a property of the market's ability to observe it**, so a credential nobody will read is an outside option that exists and cannot be exercised, while a specific asset has no outside option to exercise at all. Specificity is not the dial here: it is pinned near zero throughout, since clinical competence is clinical competence anywhere.

The reading found a different axis instead. **Good Enough to Leave moves from `credential-recognisability` to `merit-legibility`**, keeping its negative sign, joining The One Everybody Used at the legible end and opposing The Proxy at the other. The existing discriminator covers it without a word changed, "what sets the sign is what a decision falls back on when it cannot read the individual, and what it converges on when it can", and the pair with the popular sire is close enough to be one sentence: **a legible individual is drawn out of the population that produced them, and the population thins**, a studbook in one play and a labour market in the other. `credential-recognisability` is retired, having named the instrument rather than the quantity: a certificate is one way merit becomes cheap for an outsider to read, and a progeny index is another. Ships nothing.
