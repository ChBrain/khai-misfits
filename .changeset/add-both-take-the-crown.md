Add the misfit Both Take the Crown: a staging of split-brain and the CAP theorem, where a network partition cuts a cluster of replicas in two, and because no node can tell a crashed peer from an unreachable one, each side that stays available correctly promotes itself, so two primaries accept conflicting writes and the shared state forks into two irreconcilable histories. A replicated system holds one shared state, one primary taking writes and the rest following; the network is not perfectly reliable, and when it partitions a node can no longer hear the primary and cannot tell why, since silence on the wire means the same whether the peer crashed or is merely cut off, and it cannot wait, because freezing is itself a failure. So each side, reasoning correctly and blind to the other, makes the available choice and crowns itself, and the client that writes to whichever primary answers splits its writes across two heads, the one state becoming two with no channel to join them. And it cannot be designed away for free, because the CAP theorem proves that under a partition a system can be consistent or available but not both, so preventing two heads means a quorum that stands the minority down, buying consistency by surrendering availability on the minority side, with no node in error. Cut to the both-sides-correctly-crown-themselves vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from The Bank Run (a partition and partial information, not a self-fulfilling belief cascade), Accidental Adversaries (a single-shot forced impossibility, not an escalating feedback loop over rounds), and The Price Neither Will Name (a distributed-systems impossibility over a partition, not a bilateral-trading impossibility over private information), and held to its bound, since it needs an actual partition and a state that must agree and lifts where the network holds, a majority quorum stands the minority down, or the workload tolerates reconciling later (Gilbert and Lynch; Brewer; Fischer, Lynch, and Paterson; Lamport).
