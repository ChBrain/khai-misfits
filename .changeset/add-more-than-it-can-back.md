Add the misfit **More Than It Can Back** (the Triffin dilemma): when a single national currency is also the world's reserve asset, the issuer must run persistent deficits to supply a growing world's demand for reserves, and the very outflow that supplies it piles up claims against the issuer faster than the backing behind them, so the more the asset is supplied the less credible its value becomes, and the issuer cannot both furnish the world's liquidity and preserve confidence in what it furnishes. After Triffin (1960), with Bretton Woods and its 1971 collapse as the prediction borne out, Keynes's Bancor and the IMF's SDR as the remedy anticipated, and the "new Triffin dilemma" (Farhi, Gourinchas & Rey; Obstfeld) as the modern reading. Held to its bound: sharpest under a fixed convertibility promise, softening under fiat, floating money into a slower question of sustainability.
