Add the misfit Not All Hers to Give: a staging of parent-offspring conflict and its genomic imprinting extension, where a fetus is related to itself completely but a mother is related by half to it and by half to every offspring she will ever have, so the fetus is selected to demand more investment than the mother is selected to give. Within the fetus's own genome, a gene copy inherited from the father, having no stake in a possible future half sibling by a different man, silences its more restrained maternal counterpart at growth loci and presses the mother's blood sugar and blood pressure higher than her own genes are selected to give. The mother's system usually covers the demand, insulin and vasculature adjusting with room to spare, ordinary gestation and no sign of conflict, but where the press outruns the cover, real pathology follows, gestational diabetes or preeclampsia, with no toxin, no infection, and no one to blame. Screening catches the acute overshoot but never the imprint that produced it, since the silencing at each locus is fixed in the genome before the pregnancy begins. Cut to the relatedness-asymmetry-tugs-the-transfer vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from The Arms Race (unrelated rivals in a zero-sum race, not close kin sharing most of their genetic interest) and Saved to Spread (host-pathogen coevolution under a trade-off, not Hamiltonian relatedness asymmetry between kin), and held to its bound: it needs a viviparous kin system with loci under imprinting control, it lifts toward convergence wherever a mother's every offspring shares the one father, and its scope is held to the structural contribution the theory describes, not stretched into a claim that every case of either condition traces to this mechanism alone (Trivers; Hamilton; Haig; Haig and Moore; DeChiara, Robertson and Efstratiadis).
