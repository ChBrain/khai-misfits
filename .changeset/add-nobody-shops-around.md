Add the misfit Nobody Shops Around: a staging of the Diamond paradox, where many sellers offer the same good and a competitive market on paper settles at the full monopoly price, held there by nothing but a small cost of comparing that keeps anyone from comparing. To check the next seller a shopper must pay a little time, effort, or a walk. From a competitive price, a seller raises by a hair, by less than that cost of looking, and the shopper before it weighs the walk against the saving, finds the walk costs more, and stays. No rival can take the custom back by undercutting, since a shopper already in a shop will not leave over a hair and one outside will not come for one, so a cut only forfeits margin for customers it cannot win. Each small raise sticks and each is followed, and the only price at which no seller wants to raise further is the monopoly price, so the many competitors settle exactly where a single owner would, a discontinuous jump from a vanishing friction, with the seller who raised, the rival who would not cut, and the shopper who would not walk each acting soundly. Making search free, by posting and advertising prices, restores the discipline, but transparency is costly to build and keep and sellers obscure what they charge. Cut to the tiny-search-cost-collapses-competition vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from Priced In (the price level driven to monopoly by search cost, not the price ceasing to aggregate information), The Price Neither Will Name (a monopoly price paid on a deal that is made, not a deal foreclosed by private information), and The Market for Lemons (identical known goods and no quality sorted, only the price lifted), and held to its bound, since it needs a positive search cost, a homogeneous good, and unposted prices and lifts under free search, a fraction of zero-cost searchers, or advertised prices (Diamond; Stigler; Salop and Stiglitz; Varian).
