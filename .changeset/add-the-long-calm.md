Add the misfit The Long Calm: a staging of Minsky's financial instability hypothesis, where a stretch of stability, low defaults and repaid loans and rising prices, is a genuine record that borrowers and lenders read rationally, so caution once earned now looks overpriced and the financing structure migrates rung by rung from hedge, covering interest and principal, to speculative, covering interest and rolling the principal, to Ponzi, covering neither and riding the price, and the system grows more fragile the longer it stays calm, until a small shock keeps refinancing-dependent units from rolling their debt and the accumulated fragility tips into collapse, stability itself destabilizing, the fragility built by sound responses to a real calm and not by recklessness.
