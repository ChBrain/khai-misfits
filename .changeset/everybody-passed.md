Everybody Passed: criterion collapse under successful regulation.

Ships are inspected in port, detentions are counted over three rolling years, and flags are ranked
and published. It works: a place in the top band means fewer inspections and easier trade, so the
registries carrying the world's tonnage answer it. Then an owner choosing a flag reads down the
safety column and finds every serious candidate in the same band.

The column is accurate and the improvement behind it is real, and it cannot separate two options.
Information for choosing lives in difference rather than in quality, so the choice falls to what
still varies: registration cost, processing speed, crewing nationality rules and ownership
disclosure, none of them ranked and none of them aboard a ship.

The instrument built to detect a flight from standards reports there is not one. Panama, Liberia
and the Marshall Islands are all on the white list, the leading flag of all belongs to an offshore
financial centre, and the black list holds small registries carrying very little.

The twenty-second sweep's output, found by a fourth instrument reading the house as a graph and
pointing at a founding misfit nothing had ever named.
