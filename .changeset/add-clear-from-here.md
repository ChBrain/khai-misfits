Add the misfit Clear from Here: a staging of hindsight bias, or creeping determinism, where knowing how something turned out reorganises how foreseeable it appears, so a decision taken under genuine uncertainty is evaluated against a reconstruction in which the actual outcome was always the likely one. Fischhoff gave people accounts of historical episodes and asked how probable each possible ending had been; told which ending had actually occurred, they rated it as far more probable than people judging the same account without that knowledge, and did not notice. Fischhoff and Beyth then had people forecast the outcomes of Nixon's trips to China and the Soviet Union and afterward recall their own predictions, and the recalled forecasts drifted toward what had happened, diverging from what those same people had written down. They were not lying; they could not get back to what they had thought before they knew. This is what makes it a trap rather than a lapse: it is not produced by carelessness and is not cured by care, since people warned of the bias and instructed to disregard the outcome show it regardless, the outcome having already reorganised what the record looks like. The three warning signs that were three signals among two hundred are now the three that pointed at it all along, and the reviewer is seeing that pattern rather than inventing it, because it is genuinely there once the ending is known. The consequence runs both ways: a sound call with a bad result reads as a warning ignored, and a reckless call that came off reads as judgement, so luck is scored as skill and skill as luck in the same motion. Nobody behaves badly. The harmed are entitled to an account and a serious failure met with no inquiry would be worse, the reviewer reads honestly and reaches what the record now supports, and the decider is not being framed, so every part of the process works and arrives at a wrong answer about a right decision. The deeper cost is that the review was convened to teach something and hindsight replaces the lesson with a culprit, since a finding that someone missed an obvious sign yields the instruction to notice obvious signs, which is no lesson at all. A contemporaneous sealed decision log hands the inquiry the fog rather than a reconstruction, and costs upkeep across decisions that will never be reviewed, is composed by someone who knows it may be read at an inquiry, and can be furnished afterward. Cut to the vantage-does-the-work vector no staged misfit holds, and held closest against The Paper Trail, with which it is genuinely related: there a bad outcome met with blame for departing from procedure makes each worker rational to be defensible rather than effective, a behavioural trap running forward through incentives, whereas here the trap is perceptual and runs backward, the distortion living entirely in the evaluator and operating on a decision already taken; the two compound in the world and are staged separately because The Paper Trail lifts by changing what is judged and this one does not lift by changing what is judged at all, any standard being applied to a reconstructed record. Also distinct from The Planning Fallacy (ex ante and first-person, the planner underestimating its own future task, whereas this is ex post and third-person) and Known Too Late (a genuine timing mismatch between knowledge and power, with nobody misperceiving anything). Held to its bound: it needs a genuinely uncertain decision judged after its result is known, and nothing here claims that inquiries should not be held, that decision-makers should not be accountable, or that a decision criticised after a failure was therefore sound (Fischhoff; Fischhoff and Beyth; Baron and Hershey).
