Add the misfit Better in Both: a staging of the Will Rogers phenomenon, or stage migration, where outcomes are reported against a fixed ladder of categories and the instrument that assigns cases to those categories improves. Cancer is staged by extent of spread and survival is reported stage by stage, which is the correct method, since a stage I case and a stage IV case are not the same case and a pooled figure would track the mix of stages rather than the medicine. Then imaging improves and finds deposits the older machine could not resolve, so a patient who a decade ago would have been staged I is now staged II, correctly. No one errs: the radiologist reports disease genuinely present and would be negligent to omit it, the registrar applies the current rule to the evidence before it, and the classification is more truthful than it was. But the migrating cases are not a random sample. Having hidden spread makes them the sickest of the stage they leave, and having spread slight enough to have needed a new machine makes them the healthiest of the stage they join, so they exit one distribution at the bottom and enter the other at the top, and both averages rise together. Every rung of the ladder can be lifted at once by the single movement, with no patient living a day longer and no treatment working better, and the better the new scanner the larger the apparent gain. The oncologist comparing this decade's table with last decade's is using the field's most severity-controlled yardstick, which is the more rigorous choice and exactly what carries the error, since the stage labels were assigned by different instruments and no column records which. The cost is not confined to the page: a protocol can be credited with a gain it did not produce, a scanner justified by its own artefact, and a patient treated for a stage rather than for a disease. The only figure the migration cannot move is whole-cohort mortality, since a patient moved between rungs is still in the total, and it is blunter, slower and less useful than what it replaces; restaging the earlier cohort needs decades-old imaging that often cannot answer. Cut to the reclassification-lifts-every-group vector no staged misfit holds, distinct from The Bigger Picture (Simpson's paradox reverses a trend by pooling one fixed population against an uneven confounder; here nothing is pooled, nothing reverses, and every group moves up), It Saved My Life (a feedback loop of overdiagnosed advocates growing a programme; here no loop, no advocacy, and no overdiagnosis, the found disease being real), Prevalence-Induced Concept Change (there the definition drifts and the cases stand still; here the boundary is fixed and the cases cross it, the exact inverse), and The Survivors (there units leave the data; here nobody leaves and every case stays counted), and held to its bound: it needs stage-specific comparison across a change in detection, the staging itself is genuinely more accurate, and the artefact arises only in the comparison across eras (Feinstein, Sosin and Wells; Will Rogers; Welch, Schwartz and Woloshin).
