Declares `Meade`, the surname the scouted play needs, **before** authoring it.

**The scan found one collision in seven names.** Weldon, Bellinger, Roediger, Bergman, Basden and Rajaram are clear. **Meade is taken and bare**: the index carries Ellen Meade on the Federal Reserve's publicity of debate, in The Vote Takes Four Minutes, and the scouted play needs Michelle L Meade on the social contagion of memory. Two people, one key, no declaration.

**The governance half went first, and it costs nothing.** The existing cell already names its person, so a declaration made ahead of the new cell fires on nothing: no index key changes, and the only thing that moves in any artefact is the label.

**And the count did not move, exactly as the rule says it should not.** 1702 scholar rows before and after; `Meade (bare)` became `Meade (Ellen)` and nothing else in `docs/SCIENCE.md` changed. A declaration resolves a key's label always, and moves the count only when the key was holding more than one person — stated in the register long ago and never before checked with a diff.

**The authoring cannot ship here and that is the point.** Declaring a surname is `khai-guard.config.json`, which is governance; staging a play is `misfits/**`. One namesake obligation cannot be discharged in one pull request, and the house learned that by authoring into a live collision and paying for it across two passes. This time the scan ran first and the wall was never armed against anything.
