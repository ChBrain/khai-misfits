# Plot: The Wrong Question

## Taxonomy

[The Rare Disease Test](play_the_rare_disease_test.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The result has been weighed, and a rate that was never hidden goes on sitting unweighed beside it.

## Action

At [the Clinic](place_the_clinic.md), [the Reader](persona_the_reader.md) from [the reader's seat](position_reader.md) through [Reading](process_reading.md) meets [the Crowded-Out Prior](piece_the_crowded_out_prior.md), the base rate set aside not by refusal but by the greater vividness of the result in hand, the right question left unasked.

## Stage

The population's rate was never withheld: it was published, knowable, sitting in the record the whole time the test was performed and read. What happens to it here is not concealment but eclipse, the specific, present, vivid fact of a positive result crowding out an abstract number that describes a distant population rather than the case at hand (Bar-Hillel). The right question, how likely is the disease given this result, would require both figures at once, the rarity of the condition and the accuracy of the test, held together and weighed against each other. Instead the reader's attention, already spent answering how likely the result is given the disease, simply does not return to fetch the other number, because nothing about the scene marks it as still owed. The prior does not compete well against a specific case: it is pallid, statistical, non-causal, exactly the kind of fact representativeness treats as secondary to a concrete instance in front of the reasoner (Bar-Hillel; Kahneman and Tversky). No villain: nothing is hidden and no one refuses the prior, it is simply outweighed, honestly, by evidence that feels more relevant because it is closer to the case. The scene closes with the right question never posed, not forgotten so much as never reached.

## Tension

An abstract truth loses to a vivid one, and the loss is not a failure of honesty on anyone's part, only of reach.
