# Plot: Sure of It

## Taxonomy

[The Rare Disease Test](play_the_rare_disease_test.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The wrong question has been answered clearly, and a confidence now follows it as though it were the right one.

## Action

At [the Clinic](place_the_clinic.md), [the Reader](persona_the_reader.md) from [the reader's seat](position_reader.md) through [Reading](process_reading.md) meets [the Inflated Confidence](piece_the_inflated_confidence.md), reading the positive result as near certain when the true probability may sit under one in ten.

## Stage

The reader now holds a conclusion, and it feels earned: the test is accurate, the result is positive, and the question that was answered, how likely a result like this given the disease, was answered with a clear, high figure. Physicians and students given exactly this information, a disease's known prevalence and a test's false positive rate, mostly failed to compute the true probability of disease from a positive result, many estimating near certainty in cases where the correct figure sits under one in ten (Casscells, Schoenberger and Grayboys). The confidence is not a wild leap, it is the natural resting point of reasoning that has, so far, been careful and correct at every step it actually took, a sound test read by an ordinarily attentive mind. Nothing about the moment announces that a number is still missing, the felt weight of the specific result stands in for the calculation that was never performed, and the gap between the two is invisible from inside the reading. No villain: the confidence follows exactly from correctly weighing vivid evidence the way representativeness makes natural, and the error belongs to no one's design, not the instrument's, not the reader's. The scene closes on a conclusion held with real certainty, sound test and sound-seeming reasoning together producing it, and the arithmetic that would correct it still undone.

## Tension

The confidence feels exactly as earned as a correct one would, and nothing in the feeling itself tells the two apart.
