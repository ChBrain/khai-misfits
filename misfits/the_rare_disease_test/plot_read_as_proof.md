# Plot: Read as Proof

## Taxonomy

[The Rare Disease Test](play_the_rare_disease_test.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The test returns positive, and a specific result now sits in front of a reader who must make something of it.

## Action

At [the Clinic](place_the_clinic.md), [the Reader](persona_the_reader.md) from [the reader's seat](position_reader.md) on [Weigh the Evidence](plan_weigh_the_evidence.md) through [Reading](process_reading.md) takes up [the Positive Result](piece_the_positive_result.md), asking how likely a result like this is given the disease, and reading the clear answer as near proof of the disease itself.

## Stage

The reader does what a mind does with a specific piece of evidence: it asks how well the evidence fits the hypothesis, how likely a positive result is if the condition is actually present, and the test answers that question clearly, very likely (Kahneman and Tversky). This is the natural move, the one representativeness prompts by default, weighing a case by how well it resembles the category it is being sorted into rather than by how common that category was to begin with. Asked in isolation, the question is answered correctly: yes, a positive result of this kind is indeed very likely if the condition is present, the instrument's own numbers say so plainly, and the reader has not misread a single figure the test produced. The reasoning is sound on its own terms, careful even, and it arrives at a confident reading of the evidence that no one examining only the test's rates could fault. No villain: the question the reader asks and answers is a sensible one to ask of a specific result, and answering it correctly is exactly what careful attention to the evidence in hand looks like. The scene closes with a sound answer, correctly reasoned, to a question that was never the one that mattered.

## Tension

The answer is right, and it is an answer to the wrong question, and nothing in the reading itself signals which one was asked.
