# The Rare Disease Test: Reference

The research warrant for **The Rare Disease Test**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon named
the **base rate fallacy**: in judging the probability of a hypothesis given specific evidence,
people fix on how well the evidence fits the hypothesis and neglect the prior probability, the base
rate, so a positive test for a rare condition is read as near-certain when it is in fact more likely
a false positive, a sound test and an ordinary act of reasoning together yielding a confident wrong
conclusion, all cited below, held with the finding that the neglect is strongest when the base rate
is presented as a pallid statistic and weakens sharply when the same information is given as natural
frequencies or made causally vivid.

## Line of Work

Staging the base rate fallacy as a **neglected-prior structure**: a rare condition exists in a population at some low
rate, one in a thousand, one in ten thousand. A test for the condition is good, wrong only rarely, a low false positive
rate and a low false negative rate, and by any ordinary standard a fine instrument. A person takes the test, in good
faith, because it is a fine instrument and a positive result is exactly the kind of specific evidence a person should
attend to. The result comes back positive. And here the mind does what minds do with specific evidence: it asks how well
the evidence fits the hypothesis, how likely a positive result is if the person has the condition, and the test answers
that clearly, very likely, so the mind reads the positive as near proof (Kahneman and Tversky). But this is the wrong
question answered instead of the right one. The right question is not how likely the evidence given the hypothesis, but
how likely the hypothesis given the evidence, and that depends on how rare the hypothesis was to begin with. Because the
condition is rare, even a small false positive rate applied to the vast healthy majority produces more false positives
in absolute numbers than true positives drawn from the small affected minority, so among all the positive results, most
are false, and a physician told the false positive rate of a test for a disease with a known prevalence typically
overestimates the chance of true disease enormously, some estimating near certainty where the true probability is under
one in ten (Casscells, Schoenberger and Grayboys). The mechanism is representativeness: people judge probability by how
well a case represents a category and set aside how common the category is, so the base rate is not ignored out of
laziness but crowded out by a more vivid and more available kind of evidence, the specific result in hand (Kahneman and
Tversky; Bar-Hillel). It is authored by no villain: the test was built and used in good faith, the physician who orders
it and reads it follows sound clinical reasoning about the evidence in front of them, and the patient who trusts a
positive result is trusting an instrument that really is accurate in the sense advertised, mistaken only about what that
accuracy implies once the rarity of the condition is brought back into the sum. The governing law is not that anyone is
careless, but that **specific evidence about a hypothesis crowds out the prior probability of that hypothesis in
ordinary probabilistic reasoning, so a positive result for a rare condition is read as far more conclusive than the
arithmetic supports, a sound test and sound-seeming reasoning together yielding a confidently wrong conclusion, no
villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the rare condition and its low prior, the accurate test
offered in good faith, the positive result read as specific evidence, the base rate crowded out by that evidence, the
inflated confidence in the diagnosis, and the arithmetic that restores the missing prior and corrects it (Bar-Hillel;
Kahneman and Tversky; Casscells, Schoenberger and Grayboys), as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale
of an incompetent reader misreading a plain result.

It is a neglected-prior structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest neighbours. It is
distinct from The False Trade-Off, where selecting on the sum of two unrelated traits makes them look opposed among the
selected, a trade-off the population never had: there the distortion is a spurious correlation manufactured by a
selection rule applied across a population, a collider bias with no single judgment being made, whereas here no
selection filters a population at all, one person's positive test is read by one reasoner who has all the relevant
numbers available and simply weighs the specific evidence over the prior, an error of inference and not of sampling. And
it is distinct from Larger in the Looking, where the very act of attending to a factor inflates its felt importance in a
judgment of well-being: there the mechanism is attention magnifying whatever it lands on and the domain is a
forward-looking prediction about a life, whereas here the mechanism is that a specific, vivid piece of evidence crowds
out an abstract, pallid prior in a probability judgment, the domain a backward-looking inference about a hypothesis
given data, attention playing no defining role in either the finding or its name.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the test as fraudulent or
  the physician or patient as careless, because the source finding it stages is that the fallacy needs no incompetence:
  the test is built and used in good faith and really does perform at the rate advertised, and the one who reads a
  positive result and weighs it heavily is doing what representativeness makes natural, attending to the evidence most
  directly in front of them, a specific result, over an abstract number, the base rate, that sits further from the case
  at hand. No one hides the prevalence and no one refuses to use it; it is simply crowded out by more vivid evidence. The
  error is authored by no one: it is the ordinary weighing of representative evidence over an abstract prior, and the
  misfit is that a sound test read by a sound mind can still yield a confidently wrong diagnosis.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The arithmetic that restores the prior,
  Bayes's rule applied to the prevalence and the false positive rate, gives the true probability and is not hard to state,
  but it demands holding two numbers in mind at once, the rarity of the condition and the accuracy of the test, and
  weighing them against a single vivid result that alone feels far more persuasive than either. The documented remedy,
  recasting the numbers as natural frequencies rather than probabilities, works because it changes what is vivid, not
  because it teaches the sum better, so the correction depends on how the problem happens to be posed and is not always
  available to a reasoner working from a single alarming result.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the mechanism of representativeness and its naming are Kahneman and Tversky's, the
  clinical demonstration among physicians is Casscells, Schoenberger and Grayboys's, the systematic account of when base
  rates are neglected is Bar-Hillel's, and the natural-frequency remedy and its bound are Gigerenzer and Hoffrage's. The
  mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: the neglect is strongest when the base rate is presented as a pallid,
  non-causal statistic and weakens sharply under natural-frequency framing or a causal story for the base rate, so the
  house stages the neglect as a real and well-documented tendency under ordinary framing, and not a claim that people are
  incapable of using base rates at all.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Rare Condition**: carries the setting (Casscells, Schoenberger and Grayboys), the low prior, the condition present
  in only a small fraction of the population the test is applied to.
- **The Accurate Test**: carries the good-faith instrument (Casscells, Schoenberger and Grayboys), the low false positive
  and false negative rates, a fine test by any ordinary standard, offered and taken in good faith.
- **The Positive Result**: carries the specific evidence (Kahneman and Tversky), the vivid, present, case-specific finding
  that the mind naturally weighs, the thing directly in front of the reasoner.
- **The Crowded-Out Prior**: carries the mechanism (Kahneman and Tversky; Bar-Hillel), the base rate set aside not by
  refusal but by the more vivid pull of the specific evidence, representativeness answering the wrong question clearly.
- **The Inflated Confidence**: carries the harm (Casscells, Schoenberger and Grayboys), the diagnosis read as far more
  certain than the arithmetic supports, drawn distinct from The False Trade-Off and Larger in the Looking.
- **The Arithmetic**: carries the remedy and the bound (Bar-Hillel; Gigerenzer and Hoffrage), the prior restored by
  Bayes's rule, easiest when recast as natural frequencies, and the marker of the hedge, since the neglect is a
  documented tendency under ordinary framing and weakens sharply under the right one.
