# After You: Reference

The research warrant for **After You** (play and company not yet staged;
this warrant is drafted first, to prove the science and the distinction before authoring).
The title is provisional, a reading of the phenomenon the literature calls the **two-sided
market's chicken-and-egg problem**: Rochet and Tirole's account of a platform that must bring
two distinct sides together, each valuable to the other, and Caillaud and Jullien's chicken-and-egg,
where each side rationally waits for the other and the platform fails to form, all cited below,
held with the finding that the failure is one of two equilibria and not an impossibility, the good
one reachable by coordinating the two sides' expectations at a cost.

## Line of Work

Staging the two-sided cold start as a **cross-side coordination structure**: a platform, an
intermediary, a marketplace, creates value only by bringing two distinct groups together, buyers and
sellers, riders and drivers, cardholders and merchants, readers and advertisers, and each group's
benefit from joining rises with how much of the other group is already there. This is a cross-side
network externality: the value to one side is not in its own numbers but in the other side's (Rochet
and Tirole). So each side, deciding whether to join a platform that is still empty, rationally waits:
a seller will not come to a marketplace with no buyers, and a buyer will not come to one with no
sellers, and neither is wrong, because for each the platform is genuinely worth little until the
other side is present. With both waiting, the cross-side value never forms, and a platform that would
have served everyone, once both sides were on it, never launches, not because the demand was absent
but because no side would go first. The governing law is not that anyone miscalculates, but that
**a platform creates value only by joining two distinct sides whose benefit from participating
depends on the other side's participation, so each side rationally waits for the other to commit
first, and with both waiting the cross-side value never forms and a platform that would have served
everyone fails to launch, no side wrong to wait and nothing scarce but the coordination itself**. The
play models the documented mechanics: the two-sided market with cross-side externalities and a price
structure, not just a price level, that must get both sides on board (Rochet and Tirole), and the
chicken-and-egg standoff resolved, where it is resolved, by divide-and-conquer pricing and the
management of each side's expectations (Caillaud and Jullien), as the load-bearing vectors of the
structure, not as a tale of a market that simply lacked demand.

It is a coordination interaction no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its
nearest neighbours. It is distinct from The Stag Hunt, the single-population assurance game where all
prefer the stag that needs everyone but each, fearing another takes a hare, takes a hare too: there
the players are one group choosing a common action, and the fear is that a fellow hunter defects,
whereas here the two sides are distinct populations, and each waits not for its own kind but for the
other side entirely, joined by a cross-side externality with no counterpart in the stag hunt, and the
lever that resolves it, an asymmetric price that subsidises one side to draw the other, has no analog
in a game where everyone faces the same choice. It is distinct from Path Dependence, where a lead won
by accident compounds as each adopter picks what others picked until a worse standard locks in: there
something has already formed and hardened, and the trap is that the wrong thing persists, whereas here
nothing has formed at all, and the trap is that the right thing never starts, a failure to launch and
not a lock-in, the two running opposite in time, one stuck because too much has settled and the other
stuck because nothing has.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the
  side that waits as stubborn or short-sighted, because the source finding it stages is precisely that
  waiting is correct: a platform empty on the other side is genuinely worth little to the side
  deciding, so declining to join it first is the right reading of its present value, not a failure of
  vision or nerve (Rochet and Tirole; Caillaud and Jullien). Each side's wait is rational, and the two
  rational waits compose into a standstill no one intends, so the loss is authored by no one: it is the
  cross-side value that would have formed had both sides been present at once, never formed because
  neither would be present first, and the misfit is that a platform good for everyone is the one that
  cannot get anyone to begin.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The remedies exist,
  divide-and-conquer pricing that subsidises one side heavily, even below cost, to build it and then
  recoups from the other side that now has a reason to come, seeding a side directly, courting a
  marquee participant whose presence draws the rest, and above all shaping the two sides' expectations
  so each believes the other will be there (Caillaud and Jullien; Rochet and Tirole), but none is free
  and none is sure: the subsidy is a real cost carried against an uncertain launch, it can be spent on
  the wrong side or captured by users who take the inducement and do not stay, and the coordination it
  buys is a belief that can fail to hold. The reckoning is that the standstill is a genuine
  equilibrium, and moving the platform out of it means paying to coordinate two sides that each,
  rationally, would rather wait.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here,
  never claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the two-sided market with its cross-side
  externalities and its price structure is Rochet and Tirole's, the chicken-and-egg standoff, the
  divide-and-conquer remedy, and the role of expectations are Caillaud and Jullien's, and the
  underlying network externality with self-fulfilling expectations is Katz and Shapiro's, credited as
  the antecedent the two-sided case extends. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: that
  the trap requires two distinct sides bound by a cross-side externality and not one population
  choosing a common action, that the harm the play centers is the launch that never happens and not a
  standard that locks in, and above all that the failure to launch is one of two equilibria and not an
  impossibility, the good one, where both sides join because each expects the other to, reachable by
  coordinating expectations at a cost (Caillaud and Jullien). The scope is held to the two-sided
  launch, and not stretched into a claim that platforms cannot form or that network effects doom every
  market.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the
proposed load-bearing pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Two Sides**: carries the two distinct groups the platform must join (Rochet and Tirole), each
  useless to the platform without the other, buyers and sellers, riders and drivers, the two
  populations whose meeting is the whole value and whose separateness is the whole difficulty.
- **The Cross-Side Pull**: carries the cross-side network externality (Rochet and Tirole), the fact
  that each side's benefit from joining rises with the other side's participation and not with its own,
  the bond that makes the platform valuable when full and worthless when empty.
- **The Wait**: carries the rational withholding (Caillaud and Jullien), each side declining to join
  first because a platform empty on the other side is genuinely worth little, the correct reading of
  present value that composes with the other side's identical reading into a standstill.
- **The Empty Platform**: carries the bad equilibrium (Caillaud and Jullien; Rochet and Tirole), the
  cross-side value never forming and the platform that would have served everyone failing to launch,
  the loss that is authored by no one because neither side was wrong to wait.
- **The Expectations**: carries the selector between the two outcomes (Caillaud and Jullien; Katz and
  Shapiro), the belief each side holds about whether the other will come, self-fulfilling either way,
  so that the same platform reaches the full outcome or the empty one according to what the sides
  expect, the hedge that the failure is one equilibrium and not a fate.
- **The Subsidy**: carries the remedy and its price (Caillaud and Jullien; Rochet and Tirole), the
  divide-and-conquer pricing that subsidises one side to build it and recoups from the other, the
  seeding and the marquee participant and the shaping of expectations, each a real cost against an
  uncertain launch and each able to fail, spent on the wrong side or captured without a stay.
