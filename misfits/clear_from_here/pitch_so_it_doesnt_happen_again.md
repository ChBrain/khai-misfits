# Pitch: So It Doesn't Happen Again

## Taxonomy

[Clear from Here](play_clear_from_here.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Serious and accountable.
A bad thing has happened, a review is convened, the record is read carefully, and someone asks the reasonable question of whether it could have been foreseen.
Nothing false in it: the harm is real, the account is owed, and a failure met with no inquiry at all would be the worse arrangement by a wide margin.

## Undertow

Under the careful reading, a record already rearranged.
Knowing how it ended has made the three signals that pointed that way the salient three and let the rest recede, so the episode reads as a line that could only have run one way, and the reviewer is seeing that pattern rather than inventing it (Fischhoff).
No liar and no witch-hunt: warned of the bias and told to disregard the outcome, people show it still, and even the decider's own memory of its reasoning drifts toward what happened.

## Nerve

The purpose of an inquiry, and the catch that the vantage does the work:
that the outcome which makes the review necessary is the same outcome that makes the decision unreadable, so the harm and the distortion are the one ending seen twice,
and so it doesn't happen again is the sentence under which the lesson is replaced by a culprit.

## Echo

After the run: a finding that someone ignored an obvious sign, an action item to notice obvious signs, and a sign that was not obvious then and will not be next time (Baron and Hershey).
And the remedy, real and priced: write it down before you know, sealed at the time, since recollection provably will not serve, though the log costs upkeep across decisions that will never be reviewed, is composed by someone who knows it may be read at an inquiry, and can be furnished afterward (Fischhoff and Beyth).
And the bound held in view: it needs a genuinely uncertain decision judged after its result is known, so the whole of it is that the road only has one lane when you are looking back down it.
