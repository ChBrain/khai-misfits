# Just Keeping Their Feet: Reference

The research warrant for Just Keeping Their Feet.
The term is the house's; the play runs on pedestrian-induced lateral vibration of footbridges, anchored on
the London Millennium Bridge of June 2000 and the work that followed it, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging pedestrian-induced lateral instability as **a load that is a function of the response**: a slender
footbridge is analysed against every load that can be written down in advance, including the vertical
pedestrian resonance the profession has known about for a century, and passes. The lateral case is set
aside on stated grounds, that the sideways force from a walker is roughly an order of magnitude smaller
than the downward one and that in the absence of coherence between pedestrians the response is negligible.
The play models the documented mechanics, the mode that is cheap to excite and matched by accident to the
rhythm of walking, the balance correction that is involuntary and below awareness, the deck that couples
the crowd to itself through their own sense of balance, the threshold that hides the effect from every
test, the operator deciding under genuine ignorance, and the retrofit that concedes the mechanism and takes
the energy instead, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a bridge designed badly.

The governing law is that the excitation depends on the motion it produces. Every other load can be
tabulated before the structure exists; this one is a conversation between the deck and the people on it,
and cannot be held until both are present. So it is not a quantity that was overlooked in the analysis, it
is a quantity the analysis had no way to contain.

What removes any possibility of blame from the crowd is that the correction is not an action. A person on a
laterally moving surface widens their stance and places the next foot differently, faster than deliberation
and beneath notice, and the timing is set by the deck rather than by the walker, so the force arrives in
phase with the motion that prompted it. Nobody is pushing, nobody knows anything about anybody else, and
the correction cannot be declined, since declining it means falling over.

What removes it from the engineers is that guidance is memory. Codes are assembled out of failures that
have already happened, so vertical resonance is taught, codified and checked, and a mode nobody had met
cannot be in the book. The premise that pedestrians do not act coherently had held on every bridge ever
built, and setting the lateral case aside was an inference from the entire available record.

And the threshold hides it from testing. Below a critical crowd size the structure's damping absorbs
everything the walkers supply, so the deck is perfectly still for every walk-through and trial, which are
conducted with the people available and never with a crowd. The effect does not exist at the scales at
which structures are checked, and the first occasion on which it exists is the first on which it matters.

It is distinct from the house's **All at Once**, where a cached value spares a backend until it expires and
every request misses together: there the agents are synchronised by a shared clock, the coupling is a
common external schedule, and no feedback runs from the outcome back to the timing. Here there is no
schedule and no shared signal external to the system: the coupling medium is the structure being loaded,
and the motion it makes is what sets the timing of the next push, so the loop closes through the thing
being damaged.

It is distinct from the house's **The Weather We Make**, where everybody measures risk the same way and
must obey it, so the storm the models called impossible is the one they make: there the coupling is a
shared model, held knowingly, and the participants are following an instrument. Here nobody is following
anything, the participants have no model, no instrument and no knowledge of each other, and the
coordination is done by their own reflexes through a floor.

It is distinct from the house's **The Tyranny of Small Decisions**, where each small choice is rational and
free yet the sum is an outcome nobody chose: there the aggregation is of decisions, each of which could
have gone otherwise. Here nothing is decided at any point, the contributions are reflexes, and the sum is
not an aggregate of choices but a resonance.

It is distinct from the house's **The Load It Always Carried**, where every cycle passes every check and
the increments sum until the part fractures: there the accumulation is over time in a component, and the
checks genuinely cannot see the damage. Here nothing accumulates and nothing is hidden inside a material:
the structure is exactly as sound after the event as before, and what appeared was a behaviour of the
combined system rather than a change in the bridge.

## Origin

The phenomenon in practice, and the research spine beneath it.

| Source                         | Key Work / Event                                                                                                                                      | Scope                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The event**                  | the London Millennium Bridge, opened 10 June 2000 and closed two days later after large lateral movement, remaining shut for about two years          | **The anchor**: a completed, checked and celebrated structure exhibiting an unanticipated lateral motion on its first day of public use.                                                                                          |
| **The mechanism**              | synchronous lateral excitation, in which pedestrians adjust their gait in response to lateral movement and in doing so reinforce it                   | **The loop**: a force whose timing is set by the deck's own motion, so that it arrives in phase with the motion and increases it.                                                                                                 |
| **Why the case was set aside** | the pre-2000 treatment of lateral vibration in design practice                                                                                        | **The reasoning**: the lateral component of the ground reaction force being roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the vertical, and the lateral response assumed negligible in the absence of coherence between pedestrians. |
| **The remedy**                 | the retrofit of 37 viscous dampers, comprising chevron dampers beneath the walkway, pier dampers and vertical units, together with tuned mass dampers | **The correction, and its price**: a conclusion that the phenomenon could not be stopped, and a decision to absorb the energy instead, applied to a structure that was already complete.                                          |
| **The continuing argument**    | later work proposing that the instability can emerge from pedestrians' balance response without synchronisation between them                          | **The live dispute**: whether the walkers fall into step at all, or whether each person's individual balance control is sufficient to produce negative damping.                                                                   |

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the designers
  as careless or the crowd as behaving oddly, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong:
  the analysis covered every load that can be specified in advance and passed the resonance the profession
  teaches; the premise that pedestrians do not act coherently had held on every bridge ever built; the
  walkers are only avoiding a stumble, faster than deliberation and beneath awareness, and cannot decline
  the correction without falling; the operator closed the bridge in two days on evidence nobody had
  modelled, which was the only defensible course. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that the load is a response, and a response cannot be tabulated before the
  thing it responds to exists.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Damping works, and
  concedes that the mechanism cannot be prevented, altering a complete and correct structure after the fact
  at substantial cost and over two years. Limiting the number of people on the bridge works and is the
  opposite of having a bridge. Stiffening the deck works and produces a different bridge from the one that
  was designed and built. Instructing the crowd reaches nothing, since the correction is a reflex rather
  than a choice.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the incident, the investigation, the remedial design
  and the subsequent dynamics literature are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the mechanism is
  still argued over, with later work proposing that the instability arises from individual balance control
  without pedestrians synchronising with each other at all, so the play deliberately rests on the balance
  response rather than on synchrony; the critical crowd numbers and modal frequencies are specific to a
  given structure and do not transfer; lateral pedestrian excitation had been observed on other bridges
  before 2000 and was documented in the literature, so it was not wholly unknown but was not reflected in
  general design practice, which is a weaker and more accurate statement than saying nobody knew; and the
  design codes have since been revised to address the lateral case directly. **The play makes no claim that
  the bridge was unsafe**, since the motion was a serviceability problem rather than a threat to the
  structure, only that a load which is a reply to the structure's own movement is not a load anybody could
  have written down.
