# Play: Just Keeping Their Feet

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Just Keeping Their Feet.
A footbridge is checked against resonance, and everybody knows which resonance: the vertical one, the reason soldiers break step. So the deck is designed and analysed against people bouncing it up and down, and it passes. Nobody checks the sideways mode with any seriousness, because the sideways push a person makes while walking is roughly a tenth of the downward one, and because a crowd of strangers has no reason to push sideways together. That second assumption is the one that fails, and it does not fail because anybody coordinates.

## Arc

The bend is this: the deck is not only what the crowd stands on, it is what connects them to each other.
It runs on a correction nobody can feel themselves making.
The Sideways Mode is small, real, and present in any slender deck. It needs very little energy to grow, and it moves at a frequency close to the rate at which people transfer weight from one foot to the other, which is not a coincidence anybody arranged but a consequence of how long legs are.
The Ones Staying Upright do nothing collective at all. A person on a surface that moves sideways under them widens their stance and puts the next foot down slightly differently, which is an involuntary balance correction that happens below the level of noticing. Every one of them is only trying not to stumble.
And The Deck Answers Back, which is the whole mechanism. Those corrections push against the deck, in a direction and at a moment set by the deck's own motion, so the crowd is not a load on the structure but part of it: the structure supplies the signal and the walkers supply the energy, and neither of them is doing anything but their own job.
Then there is a number, and below it nothing happens at all. With few enough people the damping wins and the deck is still, so the bridge can be walked, tested and opened without anybody seeing anything. Past the number the feedback outruns the damping, the motion grows, and the growth recruits more of the crowd into correcting harder.
The Ones Who Checked It applied the code that existed. Lateral vibration had been given little attention precisely because the lateral force is an order of magnitude smaller than the vertical, and because in the absence of coherence between pedestrians the response was assumed to be negligible: which is sound reasoning from a premise that was true of every bridge anybody had built and false of this one.
So it was opened, and closed two days later. And the corrections all cost something: thirty-seven viscous dampers were fitted along with tuned masses, at a price and over two years, because the phenomenon could not be prevented and could only be absorbed. Limiting the crowd works and is the opposite of a bridge. Stiffening it works and is a different bridge.

## Stakes

Whether a structure can be checked against the loads it will carry, when some of the load is a response to the structure's own motion.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a code applied correctly, a mode analysed properly, walkers doing nothing but staying upright, and an operator closing a bridge the moment it moves, and away from any check that could have caught it, because the quantity in question does not exist until the crowd and the deck are put together. The production cannot ask a person not to keep their balance, and it cannot analyse a load that is a function of the response. What is left to fight over is not whether the bridge was designed properly, it was, but who owns a failure mode that is not a property of the structure or of the people but of the two of them at once.
