## Stage

The bias shrinks, though it does not vanish, once a person is given feedback about how little was actually noticed, and it shrinks somewhat further with familiarity, so the pattern documented here is a variable tendency, not a claim that other people never notice anything at all (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000; the wider egocentric-bias and self-focus literature that followed). No one in this story is the villain of it. The Self formed an honest estimate from the only evidence it had, its own vivid attention in the moment; the Crowd gave exactly the ordinary notice any moment like this earns, no more and no less; the Familiar Eye noticed a little more out of real closeness and no more than that; and the Follow-Up delivered the true count honestly and then let the next guess correct itself, without pushing it further than the evidence warranted. This is also the place to hold the full shape of the distinction from a sibling misfit, Larger in the Looking, which stages a different bias entirely: the focusing illusion, where the very act of weighing how much a factor bears on one's own well-being, an income, a climate, a house, inflates that factor's felt importance because attending to it magnifies it, a self-directed, forward-looking judgment about how much something will matter to a life. Here, in Nobody Was Looking at Your Shirt, the inflated judgment is not about one's own well-being at all but about a third party's perspective, how much someone else notices and evaluates the self, a social projection outward and not a self-directed importance weighing. The next estimate comes in a little smaller than the last one, shaped now by an actual count instead of a felt intensity, though it will never fully close, because the next moment will feel just as large from the inside as this one did. What the play is really staging, across all four of its seats, is this: the spotlight a person feels on themselves is the truest thing in the room to them, and, to nearly everyone else standing in it, very nearly nothing at all.

## Tension

A guess a little smaller now, and just as vivid the next time it matters.
