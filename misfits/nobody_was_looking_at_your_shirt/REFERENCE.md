# Nobody Was Looking at Your Shirt: Reference

The research warrant for **Nobody Was Looking at Your Shirt** (play and company not yet staged; this warrant is drafted
first, to prove the science and the distinction before authoring). The title is provisional, a reading of the
phenomenon named the **spotlight effect**: people overestimate how much others notice and judge their appearance and
behavior, since one's own actions and appearance are so salient to oneself that this salience is projected onto the
perspective of observers who are in fact paying far less attention, all cited below, held with the finding that the
bias shrinks, though it does not vanish, with feedback and with familiarity between observer and observed.

## Line of Work

Staging the spotlight effect as a **self-salience-projected-outward structure**: a person does something mildly
conspicuous, wears a shirt they would rather not be seen in, stumbles, gives a wrong answer aloud, and to that person
the moment is vivid, the whole of their own attention for a stretch of time. Asked, or simply left to guess, how many
of the people around them noticed or will remember it, the person's estimate comes out far too high: because the thing
is so salient to them, they anchor on that salience and adjust insufficiently for the fact that everyone else in the
room is the center of their own attention and not of anyone else's, an egocentric anchor carried outward onto a
perspective it does not fit (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, "The Spotlight Effect in Social Judgment: An Egocentric
Bias in Estimates of the Salience of One's Own Actions and Appearance", 2000). When the actual count is taken, in the
classic demonstration a student wearing a conspicuous shirt into a room of peers and then guessing how many noticed it,
the true number who noticed or later recalled it comes in well under half of what the wearer guessed, the gap between
the felt spotlight and the room's actual, ordinary attention laid bare (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000). The
mechanism is not a failure of the people who fail to notice, nor a delusion on the part of the one who feels watched:
each person in the room is, in fact, absorbed in their own concerns, their own appearance, their own conversation, and
has only a fraction of their attention left over for anyone else, so the felt spotlight is real to the one who feels it
and simply does not exist from where anyone else is standing. And the bias is bounded in a way that keeps it honest: it
shrinks, though it does not vanish, once a person is given feedback about how little was actually noticed, and it
shrinks somewhat further with familiarity, a close friend or a familiar face noticing more than a stranger would, but
even a familiar observer notices and recalls far less than the one at the center of the moment fears (Gilovich, Medvec
and Savitsky, 2000; the wider egocentric-bias and self-focus literature that followed). The governing law is not that
people are vain or that the people around them are unobservant, but that **the salience of one's own action or
appearance to oneself is anchored on and insufficiently adjusted when estimating how salient it is to anyone else, so
a person is judged, by their own account, to be far more watched and remembered than they in fact are, no vanity or
inattention required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the conspicuous moment vivid to the one living it,
the anchored estimate of how much others noticed, the room's real and ordinary attention mostly elsewhere, the count
that reveals the gap, the somewhat closer but still far short notice of a familiar observer, and the estimate's
correction, partial and never complete, once feedback is given, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a
tale of a self-absorbed person corrected by an indifferent crowd.

It is a self-salience-projected-outward structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its
nearest neighbour. It is distinct from Larger in the Looking, where the very act of weighing how much a factor bears
on one's own well-being, an income, a climate, a house, inflates that factor's felt importance because attending to it
magnifies it, a self-directed forward-looking judgment about how much something will matter to a life: there the
inflated judgment concerns the importance of a factor to the one doing the judging, and no one else's perspective
enters the estimate at all. Here the inflated judgment is about a third party's perspective, how much someone else
notices and evaluates the self, a social projection and not a self-directed importance weighing: a person does not
overestimate how much their shirt matters to their own life, they overestimate how much it registers in a stranger's
notice and memory. The two can compound, a conspicuous thing may be both felt as unduly important to oneself and
believed to loom unduly large in others' eyes, but the mechanisms are separable, one an attention-driven inflation of a
factor's weight in a self-directed judgment and the other an egocentric anchor carried, unadjusted, onto someone else's
perspective, and the house stages the projection outward and not the weighing inward.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the self-conscious person
  as vain or the people around them as cold or unobservant, because the source finding it stages is that both are
  ordinary: the felt spotlight is a real anchoring effect and not a character flaw, and the room's inattention is not
  indifference but the same ordinary self-absorption the anchored person is themselves feeling, projected outward and
  simply not returned. No one is preening and no one is failing to care; the gap between the felt spotlight and the
  room's real attention is authored by no one, the predictable cost of an anchor that fits only the person who set it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a fix that closes the gap for good. The remedy, being told
  the true count or growing familiar to one's observers, narrows the bias but does not erase it, since even a familiar
  face notices and recalls far less than the one at the center of the moment fears, and there is no single act of
  feedback that permanently recalibrates the anchor for every future conspicuous moment.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the case, the mechanism, and the documented bound belong to Gilovich, Medvec
  and Savitsky, and the boundary and familiarity extensions belong to the wider egocentric-bias and self-focus
  literature that followed. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: this is a bias demonstrated in
  classroom and social-embarrassment paradigms that shrinks, though it does not vanish, with feedback and with
  familiarity, and the house holds that not a claim that others never notice anything, only that they notice far less
  than the one at the center fears.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the proposed load-bearing
pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Blemish**: carries the setup (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000), the conspicuous moment, real and vivid to
  the one living it, the whole of their attention for a stretch of time.
- **The Estimate**: carries the mechanism and the turn (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000), the anchored guess of
  how many others noticed, egocentric salience projected outward and insufficiently adjusted.
- **The Glance**: carries the room's real state (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000), the ordinary, mostly elsewhere
  attention of the people actually present, each absorbed in their own concerns.
- **The Count**: carries the harm (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000), the true, much lower tally revealed, the gap
  between the felt spotlight and the room's actual attention laid bare, drawn distinct from Larger in the Looking's
  self-directed importance weighing.
- **The Nearer Look**: carries the domain grounding (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000; the wider egocentric-bias
  and self-focus literature that followed), a familiar observer noticing somewhat more than a stranger, though still
  far short of what is feared.
- **The Adjustment**: carries the hedge (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000; the wider egocentric-bias and
  self-focus literature that followed), the bias shrinking, though not vanishing, once feedback is given, a real but
  bounded and partial correction.
