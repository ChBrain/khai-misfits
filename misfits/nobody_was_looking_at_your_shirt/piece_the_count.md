# Piece: The Count

## Taxonomy

[Nobody Was Looking at Your Shirt](play_nobody_was_looking_at_your_shirt.md). The harm: the true, much lower tally revealed, the gap between the felt spotlight and the room's actual attention laid bare.

## Owner

## Place

At [the Debrief](place_the_debrief.md), carried by [Noticing](process_noticing.md): the guess and the truth meet here, set side by side once the room can finally be asked.

## Load Bearing

In the classic demonstration, the true number of people who noticed or later recalled a conspicuous shirt comes in well under half of what the wearer guessed, the estimate and the reality laid side by side (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000). This is worth telling apart from a sibling distortion, Larger in the Looking, the focusing illusion: there, the inflated judgment concerns how much a factor matters to one's own well-being, a self-directed importance weighing; here, the inflated judgment concerns a third party's perspective, how much someone else notices and evaluates the self, a social projection outward. The gap the Count reveals is not a failure of memory or honesty on anyone's part, just two different tallies that were never going to match.

## Apparent

Looks like proof the Self was foolish to worry; is actually proof only that the worry was miscalibrated, not that it was baseless.
