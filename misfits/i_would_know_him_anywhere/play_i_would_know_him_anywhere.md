# Play: I Would Know Him Anywhere

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

I Would Know Him Anywhere.
Recognising a face is not one skill evenly held. It is a skill built by looking, over a whole life, at the faces that happened to be around, and it tunes itself to the differences that mattered in that crowd. Put in front of a face from outside that crowd and the same person is measurably worse: less likely to pick the right one, and more likely to pick a wrong one. Not because they are careless, and not because of anything they believe. It is a property of what their eyes have had practice at. And the sentence that names this play is the one witnesses actually say, in good faith, about a stranger they saw for nine seconds.

## Arc

The bend is this: the accuracy of an identification depends on a relationship between the witness and the face, and nothing in the witness can detect which relationship they are in.
It runs on a skill that was built rather than issued.
The Years of Looking did the tuning, quietly, and it is not an attitude. The effect turns up in infants long before anybody has an opinion about anything, it tracks how much contact a person has had rather than what they think, and it sits identically in a witness with no measurable prejudice of any kind. So the one explanation everybody reaches for first is the wrong one, and the misfit survives the removal of every bad motive in the room.
The One Who Saw is honest. A brief look at a stranger, under stress, is a thin encoding for anyone. Across the line it is thinner in both directions at once: fewer correct identifications and more false ones, which is the shape the research calls a mirror effect. Both halves matter, because the second one is what puts a particular innocent person in a cell.
The One Who Runs It does everything right. Fillers matched, no suggestion, the administrator blind to which one is the suspect, the whole modern protocol observed. And every one of those safeguards is aimed at contamination arriving from outside the witness, which is a real danger and is not this one. A perfectly clean procedure preserves the identification exactly as it was made, including the part that was wrong.
Then The One Who Saw says how sure they are, and they are very sure, and they are telling the truth. Confidence at a first clean identification does carry real information, which is precisely why the room is right to listen to it. What it does not carry is this. The certainty is produced by the same machinery that produced the pick, so it cannot report on that machinery's blind spot, and the witness has no private sensation of having been in the harder condition.
And The Twelve have nothing else. Faced with an honest witness, a clean procedure and a confident identification, a reasonable juror weights it heavily, because weighting it heavily is what the evidence deserves on every visible dimension. There is no instrument in the room for the invisible one. Among DNA exonerations where an eyewitness misidentified someone, a large share were across this line.
And every correction charges somebody. Sequential presentation lowers false identifications and lowers correct ones too, so fewer innocent people are picked and more guilty ones walk. A jury instruction about cross-racial identification works, and can generalise into distrust of eyewitnesses in the cases where they are right. Requiring corroboration protects the wrongly identified and abandons the correctly identified. And more contact helps, at a relationship the research puts at about minus fifteen hundredths, strongest in childhood, which is not a thing available to a witness on the day.

## Stakes

Whether evidence can be trusted when the thing that determines its reliability is a relationship between two people, and is visible to neither of them.
Every plot moves the same way: toward honest witnesses, clean procedures and confident testimony, and away from any test that could separate the accurate identification from the mistaken one, because on every observable dimension they are identical. The production cannot give a witness practice they did not have, and it cannot make certainty report on its own foundations. What is left to fight over is not whether to believe witnesses, mostly they are all there is, but what a system owes to the person in the row when the strongest evidence against him is a true statement about how sure somebody honestly is.
