# I Would Know Him Anywhere: Reference

The research warrant for I Would Know Him Anywhere.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the own-race bias in face recognition, its role in eyewitness
misidentification, and the reforms built against it, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the other-race effect as **an error rate that belongs to a pair rather than to a person**: face
recognition is a skill built by a lifetime of ordinary exposure, tuned to the distinctions that mattered in
whichever crowd a person grew up looking at, so the same honest witness is measurably worse at faces from
outside that crowd. The play models the documented mechanics, the tuning that is not an attitude, the thin
encoding degraded in both directions at once, the flawless procedure aimed at a different danger, the
confidence that is real and cannot report on its own foundations, the jury weighing the only signal it has,
and the corrections that each discount the accurate identification along with the mistaken one, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a prejudiced witness.

The governing law is that the accuracy of an identification depends on a relationship between the witness
and the face, and nothing inside the witness can detect which relationship they are in. The effect is a
mirror: own-race faces produce a higher proportion of correct identifications and a lower proportion of
false ones than other-race faces. Both halves matter, and the second is the one that puts a particular
innocent person in a cell.

What removes the easy reading is that this is not prejudice. It appears in infants long before anyone holds
an opinion, it varies with contact rather than with belief, and it is present at full strength in people
with no measurable bias of any kind. Strip every bad motive out of the room and the mechanism is exactly
where it was.

What makes it structural rather than a procedural failure is where the safeguards point. The modern
protocol, matched fillers, a may-not-be-present instruction, blind administration and a confidence
statement taken at the moment of the pick, is aimed at influence entering the witness from outside. That is
a real and frequent danger and it is not this one, which was inside the witness before the array existed.
Worse, fair composition requires the fillers to resemble each other, so a properly built array asks the
witness to discriminate among similar faces, which is precisely the operation the effect degrades: the
better the procedure, the more weight it places on the failing faculty.

And the room's one reliability instrument cannot reach it. Confidence expressed at a first clean
identification does carry real information about accuracy, which is why recording it early is an advance
and why a jury is right to attend to it. What it cannot register is this effect, because the certainty is
produced by the same machinery that produced the pick. From the inside, a mistaken cross-race
identification and a correct own-race one are the same experience.

It is distinct from the house's **The Skill to See It**, where the competence needed to judge a task is the
competence needed to do it, so those who lack it cannot see that they lack it: there the blindness is about
a domain, and it yields to learning, since a person who acquires the skill acquires the judgement with it.
Here knowing changes nothing at all. An expert on this very effect who becomes a witness has it identically
and gains no advantage from their expertise, because the missing signal is not knowledge about a domain but
a sensation that does not exist.

It is distinct from the house's **The Proxy**, where a market unable to see an individual prices them by
their group's average and the discount makes the average come true: there a statistic is knowingly applied
to a person, and the loop is self-fulfilling. Here no statistic is in anybody's head, no group is being
priced, an individual is looked at directly and honestly misremembered, and nothing about the outcome makes
the next case more likely.

It is distinct from the house's **Anyone Can See It**, where two reasonable people read the same scene
oppositely and each is certain the other is biased: there the disagreement is the phenomenon. Here there is
no disagreement at all. Everybody in the room reads the evidence the same way, all of them sincerely, and
the reading is wrong.

It is distinct from the house's **The Worst and the Last**, where an episode is remembered by its peak and
its end rather than its duration: there memory misweights an experience the person genuinely had. Here the
memory is not misweighted but under-resolved, and the failure is in telling similar faces apart rather than
in summarising an event.

## Origin

The phenomenon in practice, and the research spine beneath it.

| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The foundational finding**     | Malpass and Kravitz, Recognition for faces of own and other race, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 1969                                                         | **The effect**: recognition accuracy for faces differing by the race of the viewer and the viewed, established experimentally and replicated for decades afterwards.                                                                                |
| **The meta-analysis**            | Meissner and Brigham, Thirty years of investigating the own-race bias in memory for faces: a meta-analytic review, Psychology, Public Policy, and Law, 2001                  | **The shape**: 39 articles, 91 independent samples and nearly 5,000 participants, showing a mirror effect in which own-race faces yield a higher proportion of hits and a lower proportion of false alarms.                                         |
| **Contact rather than attitude** | Singh, Mellinger, Earls, Tran, Bardsley and Correll, Does cross-race contact improve cross-race face perception? A meta-analysis of the cross-race deficit and contact, 2021 | **The cause, and its limit**: a real relationship between contact and the size of the deficit, around minus fifteen hundredths and stronger for contact in childhood, which locates the mechanism in exposure and also declines to explain it away. |
| **The consequence**              | the Innocence Project's analysis of DNA exonerations and the role of eyewitness misidentification                                                                            | **The cost**: misidentification appearing as a leading contributing factor in DNA exonerations, with a large share of those misidentifications crossing racial lines.                                                                               |
| **The corrections**              | cross-racial identification jury instructions, sequential presentation, blind administration and confidence statements taken at the time                                     | **The remedies and their price**: reforms that reduce mistaken identifications, reduce accurate ones alongside them, and cannot distinguish the cases they are needed in from the cases they are not.                                               |

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the witness as
  prejudiced or the officer as careless, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  witness saw a stranger briefly under stress, wants to help, has nothing to gain and reports their
  certainty truthfully; the administrator closes every documented route for contamination and does so
  blind; the jury weights a confident identification heavily, which is what the evidence deserves on every
  dimension available to them, and confidence at a first clean identification really does carry
  information. The tuning that causes it is not an attitude and is present in people with no bias at all.
  The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the safeguards
  are aimed at the outside of a witness while the variable that governs the outcome arrived years earlier,
  inside.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Presenting the
  photographs one at a time lowers mistaken identifications and lowers correct ones with them, trading
  innocent people convicted for guilty people released. A cross-racial jury instruction is true and useful
  and cannot pick out the identifications in its class that were right, so it discounts the accurate
  witness by exactly as much as the mistaken one. Requiring corroboration protects the wrongly identified
  by abandoning the correctly identified. And building contact works at a modest size and works best in
  childhood, which is not a remedy available to anybody on the day.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the experiments, the meta-analyses, the exoneration
  data and the procedural reforms are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: effect sizes vary
  considerably with method, stimulus set, retention interval and the populations studied, and the
  literature continues to argue about the mechanism, with perceptual expertise and social categorisation
  accounts both in play; the exoneration figures describe cases where DNA happened to exist and be
  preserved, so they are a floor and not a rate; the relationship between contact and the deficit is real
  but modest, which cuts against any simple exposure remedy; and the confidence and accuracy literature has
  moved substantially, with a pristine initial identification now understood to be considerably more
  informative than was once thought. **The play makes no claim that eyewitness identification should not be
  used**, only that its reliability turns on a relationship neither party can see.
