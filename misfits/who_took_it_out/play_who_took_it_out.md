# Play: Who Took It Out

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Who Took It Out.
A defined process, ninety pages of it, every step traceable to something that once went wrong. Ask about any single line and you get a good answer: it was added after the incident in March, and it would have caught that incident.
Ask who decided the whole thing should be ninety pages and there is no answer, because nobody decided that.
And ask why nothing is ever removed, and the answer is a question: if we take it out and something happens, who signed for that?

## Arc

The bend is this: the process only grows, and not because anybody is attached to it, but because adding and removing are recorded in different ways and only one of them has a name on it.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: a control is added one line at a time in response to something real, and a control is removed all at once by somebody in particular.
Take the additions first, because the play has to establish that each of them was **right**. Something went wrong. A review was held, competently and in good faith, and it found a step that would have caught it. The step was added. Repeat that across fifteen years and ninety pages of entirely justified procedure is what you get, with no bad decision anywhere in the sequence.
Then the asymmetry, which is the whole play. **An addition is a drift and a removal is a decision.** Nobody signs for the total, because the total was never proposed: it accrued in increments each of which was defensible on its own day. A removal cannot accrue. It is a single act, it has an owner, and it enters the record with a date and a name against it.
And that changes what the two moves cost the person making them. Leaving a control in place is an omission, and if harm follows an omission it is judged more gently than the same harm following an act. This is not a folk belief about committees: it is measured, in people asked to judge identical outcomes reached by doing and by not doing, and they do not judge them alike. So the person contemplating a removal is correctly reading a real asymmetry in how they will be treated, which is why knowing about the asymmetry does not dissolve it.
Then the control whose reason has gone. The system it protected was decommissioned four years ago and the step remains, because the step costs an afternoon a month and removing it costs a signature that somebody would have to be willing to put on a form. **The cheapest thing anybody can do with an obsolete control is comply with it.**
Reach for a settlement and each one moves the signature rather than removing it. Sunset every clause, and somebody must decline to renew, which is a signed act wearing different clothes. Appoint a body to prune, and it is a body whose members will be asked the same question. Require every addition to name a removal, which works and is a real answer, and buys it by making the addition harder at the moment somebody has just been hurt and everybody wants the control. **There is no move that makes taking a control out cost the same as leaving it in**, because the difference is not in the process but in who can be named afterwards.

## Triggers

**[It Would Have Caught That One](plot_it_would_have_caught_that_one.md)**
Something went wrong, a review was held, and a step was found that would have caught it. Closes when the addition is established as correct: competent people, acting in good faith, adding a control that genuinely addresses a thing that genuinely happened.

**[Nobody Signed for Ninety Pages](plot_nobody_signed_for_ninety_pages.md)**
Cue: the procedure, at its full length. Closes when the asymmetry is in the open: the total was never proposed and so was never approved, having accrued one defensible line at a time, and a removal cannot accrue because it is a single act with an owner.

**[Judged More Gently for Leaving It](plot_judged_more_gently_for_leaving_it.md)**
Cue: identical harms, one reached by acting and one by not acting. Closes when the asymmetry is shown to be measured rather than assumed, so the person contemplating a removal is reading their situation correctly and knowing about the effect does not dissolve it.

**[The System It Protected Is Gone](plot_the_system_it_protected_is_gone.md)**
Cue: a control whose cause was decommissioned four years ago. Closes when the cheapest available action is shown to be compliance: the step costs an afternoon a month and removal costs a signature somebody must be willing to put on a form.

**[Name the One You Are Removing](plot_name_the_one_you_are_removing.md)**
Cue: the settlements, each of which works. Closes when each is shown to relocate the signature rather than abolish it, and when the best of them is shown to buy its effect by making addition harder at the exact moment somebody has just been hurt.

## Stakes

Whether a body can put down anything it has picked up.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the controls are silly, because each was added by competent people after something real and would have caught it, and not whether anybody is attached to them, because nobody is, but whether a process can shed a step when shedding is an act and keeping is not. The production cannot make it shed one. It can only show that every addition was justified on its own day, that the total was therefore never proposed and never approved, that a removal is a single signed act where an addition is a drift, that the asymmetry in how the two are judged afterwards is measured rather than imagined, that a control outlives the thing it protected because compliance is cheaper than a signature, and that every remedy available moves the signature somewhere else.
