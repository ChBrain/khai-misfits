# Play: Kept for the Worst

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Kept for the Worst.
A medicine is supposed to be the easy case. It helps people, so people take it, so it earns, so the firm makes more like it and a competitor tries to beat it. Usefulness and revenue point the same way, which is the whole reason a pharmaceutical market is worth having, and for most classes of drug the alignment simply holds.
And for a last-resort antibiotic it inverts, exactly, and by design.
Because the right thing to do with a newly licensed agent against a resistant organism is to not use it. Treat with the old generic first. Reserve the new one for the cases nothing else will touch. Keep its useful life as long as it will go, because every dose given is selection pressure spent, and what the drug is worth is measured in the years before resistance catches up with it. That is not caution or rationing. It is the correct clinical practice, it is in the guidance, and a hospital that used the new agent freely would be doing real harm to people who are not in the room yet.
And meanwhile the money runs on doses. Revenue is units dispensed, as it is for every medicine, and as any payer with a budget would sensibly arrange, because paying for what is used is how a health service avoids paying for what is not. Nobody chose that arrangement to punish anybody. It is what buying things looks like.
And put those two together and the arithmetic is not subtle. The compound that would save the most lives in the worst future is a compound with almost no sales. A short acute course, not a chronic therapy. A flat market that does not grow because nobody wants it to grow. And a board setting an antibacterial programme beside an oncology programme is not facing a hard decision, it is facing an obvious one (Projan).
And so the firms leave. They leave first from the compounds with genuinely novel mechanisms, because those are the expensive ones, which means the exit is worst exactly where the science is most needed.
And nobody in it is a villain. The steward is following the guidance that saves lives. The payer is refusing to pay for what is not used, which is what a payer is for. The board is discharging a duty it would be criticised for neglecting. And nobody wants the shelf empty, the firms least of all, since a company with no antibacterial franchise sells no antibacterials at all.
And now the bedside, years later, where the list of options for a resistant isolate has one line on it and the line is blank. The missing drug is not one that failed in trials or was withdrawn for harm. It is one that was never made, because there was no way to be paid for making it, and the reason there was no way to be paid is that everyone agreed it must be saved.
And the remedy is known and it is awkward in a specific way. Delink the reward from the volume: a market-entry prize, a pull incentive, or a subscription that pays for access to the drug whether or not a single course is dispensed. It works. It also means a payer committing public money up front for a medicine it hopes will never be needed, which is the hardest line in any budget to defend and the first to be cut; it means guessing a compound's worth decades before the resistance it is being kept for arrives; it means policing claims from compounds that qualify on the letter and add no new mechanism; and it means sustaining the scheme, because a reward withdrawn returns the market to precisely the arithmetic it was built to escape (Outterson and colleagues).
And so the misfit is not that industry is rapacious but that we have built a market which underprovides exactly what everybody agrees is most valuable, because they agree it must be saved. The statement of the conservation-reward conflict as a design fault, and delinkage as the instrument, is Outterson, Powers, Daniel, and McClellan's; the account of why an antibacterial programme loses a portfolio comparison, and why the novel mechanisms go first, is Projan's. The house stages the reserve, the tariff, the inversion, the exit, the empty shelf, and the subscription that has to be bought, and holds the bound in view: that it needs a drug whose correct use is minimal and a reward tied to volume, and that it lifts only where those two are separated, at a price paid in advance for something nobody takes, so the whole of it is that the medicine we most want to exist is the one we have arranged to make worthless.

## Arc

The bend is this: where a drug's worth to the public rises with how carefully it is withheld and its revenue rises only with how much is sold, correct stewardship drives the return on the most valuable compounds toward zero, so the medicines most worth having are the ones a volume market cannot fund, with every party doing its job properly.
It runs on a restraint that is correct.
The new agent is restricted on the day it is licensed, the old generic is tried first, and every dose withheld is useful life preserved, which is the guidance and is right.
And a payment that is sensible. Revenue follows units dispensed, as it does for every medicine, since paying for what is used is how a payer avoids paying for what is not.
And an inversion the two produce between them. The better the stewardship the smaller the volume, and the more valuable the drug the more carefully it is withheld, so worth and earnings point in opposite directions (Outterson and colleagues).
And an ordinary portfolio comparison that goes one way. A short acute course against a flat market, set beside an oncology programme, is not a hard decision for a board, and the novel mechanisms are abandoned first because they are the dearest (Projan).
And an exit nobody wanted, the firms least of all. A company with no antibacterial franchise sells no antibacterials, so the departure is a loss on both sides of the table.
And a bedside years later with one blank line on it. The missing drug did not fail in trials and was not withdrawn for harm: it was never made, because there was no way to be paid for making it.
And a remedy that is a standing purchase of the unused. Delinked payment works and means public money committed for a medicine nobody hopes to need, hardest to defend in the years when it is working.
And the whole is held against its own limit. It needs a drug whose correct use is minimal and a reward tied to volume, and it lifts only where the two are separated and the scheme is sustained.
