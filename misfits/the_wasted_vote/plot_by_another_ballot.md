# Plot: By Another Ballot

## Taxonomy

[The Wasted Vote](play_the_wasted_vote.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The field has locked to two, and the third view, genuinely held by some, goes unheard in the Return.

## Action

At [the Return](place_the_return.md), [the Reformer](persona_the_reformer.md) from [the Reformer's seat](position_reformer.md) on [Count It Different](plan_count_it_different.md) through [Reforming](process_reforming.md) holds [the Other Ballot](piece_the_other_ballot.md), the proportional or ranked and runoff rule under which a third vote is no longer wasted.

## Stage

Here is the remedy, and here is the bound. The desertion traced through this misfit was forced by one specific rule: that a vote for any but the first is thrown away. Change that rule, to proportional representation or to ranked and runoff ballots, and a vote for the third is no longer simply wasted; the pressure to desert eases, and more than two parties can stay viable (Duverger's corollary). But the remedy is not free. It is itself a political act, resisted by the very two parties the current rule favours and who must legislate any change to it; and the systems it opens carry their own costs, of fragmented coalitions, slower government, and votes that take longer to resolve into a decision. And the law this whole misfit stages is a tendency, not a determinism, so the bound must be held alongside the remedy: it holds most strongly at the level of the single-member district, and at the national level a regionally concentrated third party can persist even under plurality, as in Canada and India (Riker); its psychological effect needs voters to share a common read on who is viable, and can misfire without it (Cox); and the number of parties that survive is shaped by enduring social cleavages as much as by the counting rule itself (Lipset and Rokkan). No villain: the incumbents who resist reform are not conspiring against the third view, they are protecting the rule that elected them, as any officeholder would.

## Tension

The way out is real, but it is dearly bought, and the law it answers is a tendency, never a determinism.
