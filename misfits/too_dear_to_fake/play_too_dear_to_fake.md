# Play: Too Dear to Fake

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Too Dear to Fake.
Someone has a quality another would pay to know: real ability, sound health, true wealth, genuine commitment. The trouble is that it cannot be seen from outside, and a claim about it proves nothing, because the one who lacks it can make the same claim for free. So a bare word is worthless here: everyone says they are able, and saying so costs nothing, so saying so tells nothing.
The only word that carries is one too expensive to say falsely. A signal believed is a signal the pretender could not have afforded: four years of a degree that need not teach the job, a peacock's tail that only weighs the bird down and feeds the fox, a launch thrown on borrowed runway to look already funded. The holder burns real resource on a display that does nothing but cost, and the receiver believes it precisely because faking it would have cost the faker more than the prize was worth (Spence; Zahavi; Grafen). The proof is the price. Not the content of the degree, the price of it; not the beauty of the tail, the handicap of it.
And here is the catch beneath the credential. The cost is not the worth. It is the toll that makes the worth believable, paid to no one and producing nothing, a pure burn whose only product is that it could not have been faked. And because the signal works only by being dear, everyone who truly holds the quality is forced to pay it: decline to signal and you are pooled with those who cannot, and lose the thing your quality would have won, so the able buy the degree they do not need and the fit grow the tail that endangers them, each because the others do (Spence; Zahavi).
And so the waste is field-wide and no one can stop it alone. The resource burned across all the signalers is deadweight, spent not to do the work but to prove one could, and it stays spent because any one who quits is read not as thrifty but as lacking. No one is a poseur for burning and no one is a snob for trusting only the dear signal: the burning is the sound move for a real quality that cannot be shown cheaper, and the trusting is right, because the cheap claim really is unreliable. The loss is authored by no one; it is the price honesty costs when the truth can be proved only by waste (Veblen).
And here is what a reformer tries, and why the waste resists. Replace the costly signal with a cheaper test, and if the cheaper test can be faked it collapses into the cheap talk it replaced; verify the quality directly, and the verification spends its own resource and is often the very thing the signal existed to spare; ban one costly proof, and the pressure to prove a hidden quality does not vanish but finds a new and equally dear one to burn (Spence; Grafen). The reformer is not wrong that the burn is waste. They are only unable to make the belief cheaper than the truth it stands for.
And so the misfit is not that credentials are empty but that a hidden worth has a price. The signaling equilibrium is Spence's, the handicap that is honest by being costly is Zahavi's, the condition that keeps it stable is Grafen's, and the conspicuous burning read as proof of standing is Veblen's. The house stages the waste and holds the bound in view: that the signal is honest only where its cost falls harder on the pretender, and lifts where the signal is cheap to fake, the quality is plain to see, or the cost is productive, so the trap is real and common and bounded, and the whole of it is that to be believed you must burn, and the burning proves you exactly because it buys nothing else.

## Arc

The bend is this: where a hidden quality must be believed and can be signaled only at a cost that falls harder on those who lack it, the signal is honest exactly because it is wasteful, so real resource is burned to prove what cannot otherwise be shown, and each holder must burn or be taken for a pretender, the waste the price of the belief.
It runs on a quality that cannot be seen.
One party holds a worth another would pay to know, and it is invisible from outside, so a claim about it is free to make and therefore proves nothing (Spence; Zahavi).
And only a costly signal can carry it. A proof is believed when the pretender could not have afforded it, so the holder burns real resource on a display that does nothing but cost, and the receiver trusts it because faking it would have cost more than the prize (Spence; Zahavi; Grafen).
And the cost falls harder on the pretender. This is the whole of the honesty: the signal separates the quality from its absence only because the one who lacks it cannot profitably match the burn, so the differential, not the display, is what carries the truth (Spence; Grafen).
And the burn produces nothing but the proof. The resource spent conveys no benefit except that it could not have been faked, so the cost is not the worth but the toll on the worth, deadweight paid to no one (Zahavi; Veblen).
And each holder must burn or be pooled. Decline to signal and you are read as lacking, so the able buy the credential they do not need and the fit grow the handicap that endangers them, each because the others do (Spence).
And the waste is field-wide and unstoppable alone. The resource burned across all signalers is pure deadweight, and it stays spent because any one who quits is taken for a pretender, so no single hand can end it (Veblen).
And a reformer can bound it, at a cost. A cheaper test that cannot be faked, a direct verification, or a productive signal each reduces the waste, but a fakeable cheap test collapses to cheap talk, verification spends its own resource, and a banned signal is replaced by another (Spence; Grafen), so no reform is free.
And the whole is held against its own limit. The honesty is conditional: it needs a differential cost and a hidden quality, and lifts where the signal is cheap to fake, the quality is plain, or the cost is productive, so the trap is real and common and bounded (Spence; Grafen).

## Stakes

Whether a hidden quality can be believed without burning real resource to prove it, when the only credible proof is one too dear to fake.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward belief, which both the holder and the receiver want, and toward waste, because the only signal that earns the belief is one that costs enough to exclude the pretender, so the resource spent buys the truth and produces nothing else. The production cannot make the proof cheap, because the cost is not a friction on the signal but the very thing that makes it honest: remove the burn and the pretender sends the same signal, so the belief sought and the waste that secures it are the one cost seen twice. The remedies, a cheaper unfakeable test, a direct verification, a productive signal, each spend a real and separate cost and none is certain, since a fakeable cheap test collapses to cheap talk, verification spends what the signal was meant to spare, and a banned proof is replaced by another. It can only show that no one poses and no one snobs, that the holder who burns and the receiver who trusts only the dear signal are both acting soundly, that the waste is a deadweight and not a fraud, and that each holder burns because to decline is to be pooled with the pretenders. And it holds the bound in view, because the honesty is conditional and not a law: it needs a cost that falls harder on the pretender and a quality that cannot be seen, and lifts where the signal is cheap to fake, the quality is plain, or the cost is productive (Spence; Grafen), so the whole of the misfit is that to be believed you must burn, and the burning proves you exactly because it buys nothing else.
