# The One Everybody Used: Reference

The research warrant for The One Everybody Used.
The term is the house's; the play runs on pedigree analysis of closed breed registers, and on what happens to a closed
population when the best individual in it can be identified precisely, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the breed register as **a closed pool narrowed by the quality of its own evidence**: a breed is defined by
descent from a recorded set of animals, so nothing arrives from outside; a health programme measures, tests and
publishes, so that a breeder chooses on evidence rather than on reputation; and the better that evidence gets, the more
precisely it identifies one animal for everybody at once. The play models the documented mechanics, the closure that is
a definition rather than a policy, the machinery that works and sharpens its answer each year, the convergence that
needs no copying, the effective number that bears no relation to how many animals there are, the variant carried
invisibly because no test exists for what nobody has found, and the remedies that each cost the register its function,
as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a careless breeder or a vain fancier.

The governing law is that a closed population loses variation in proportion to how few individuals contribute, and how
few contribute is set by how well the best one can be identified.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The register
records accurately. The scheme tests and publishes honestly. The sire genuinely is the best-documented animal. The
breeder reads the evidence and uses him, which is the diligent thing and what the guidance asks. There is exactly one
decision in the play and it is made correctly every time.

**And the evidence is sound, which the play establishes before anything else.** No proxy has decayed and nothing is
gamed: the scores are real, the panels detect what they are built to detect, the progeny records confirm them, and
screened disorders do fall in prevalence where the schemes are followed. Every later difficulty rests on how well this
works, which is what puts the misfit outside anybody's competence as well as outside their conduct.

**Then the closure is a definition, not a policy.** Closing the stud book is what makes a breed a breed. A register
admitting outside animals is not a laxer register but a different kind of document, certifying nothing, so there is no
relaxed version of the rule that keeps its function. The population is therefore closed by the same act that
constitutes it, and variation inside it can be redistributed and never replenished.

**And the improvement is the mechanism, which is the hardest thing in the play.** A scheme that could not discriminate
between sires would be useless, so its whole value is its power to identify one animal as the best bet. **The better
the evidence, the more precisely it points at one animal**, and the more precisely it points, the more of the breed's
next generation descends from him. The improvement and the concentration are the same event, and no design of the
scheme separates them.

**Then the convergence needs no copying, which is the sharp part.** Give every breeder complete independence, no
knowledge of anybody else's choice, no fashion, no reputation and no watching each other, and they still arrive at the
same sire, because the evidence is public and correct and there is one best answer in it. So this is not herding, and
telling anybody to think for themselves changes nothing: thinking for themselves is what produces it. And nobody
overuses him, since each breeder uses him once, which is the only quantity any of them controls.

**And the measurement says the reassuring number is the wrong one.** A pedigree analysis of ten breeds from a national
register, eight generations deep, found extremely inbred animals in every breed but the greyhound and an inbreeding
effective population size of **between forty and eighty** for all but two, with popular sires the major contributor to
the rate. Across all **two hundred and fifteen** breeds of that register from 1980 to 2014, the rate of inbreeding was
**not correlated with census population size**. A breed of tens of thousands of living dogs can be behaving genetically
like a population of fifty, and how many dogs there are does not tell you which breeds those are.

**Then what was not tested for arrives with what was.** A test exists only for a variant somebody has already
characterised, so an animal clear on every existing panel is clear on the known list and that is the whole of the
claim. Whatever else the best-documented animal carries travels at the same rate as his merit, is invisible to every
instrument in the room including the ones that just certified him, and appears two or three generations later, in a
litter belonging to somebody who did everything correctly.

**And every remedy costs.** Capping a sire's litters instructs breeders to use an animal they can see is worse.
Bringing in an outside animal restores the variation and removes the descent the register exists to certify.
Characterising the new variant adds a test to the panel, which improves the evidence, which sharpens the answer.
**Rates of inbreeding did fall after 2000, which is real and is worth saying**, and they fell by breeders deliberately
spreading matings rather than by any of the mechanism weakening.

It is distinct from the house's **Nothing Left to Pool**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages the Hirshleifer
effect, where better public information about individual risk destroys the ability to pool it, because a risk known is
a risk nobody will share. Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating no pool, nothing being insured and no ex-ante
sharing arrangement of any kind to dissolve, and this misfit stands whole: **nothing here is being pooled.** Breeders
are not sharing a risk and no arrangement is unravelling. The two are the same family, better information destroying
something that depended on not knowing, and they act on different quantities: there it is how precisely an individual's
risk can be measured, here it is how precisely the best individual can be identified, and the loss is variation rather
than insurability.

It is distinct from the house's **Information Cascade**, which stages rational herding, where each actor correctly
infers from what others chose and the private signal stops being expressed. Remove that engine, by stipulating that no
breeder can observe any other's choice, that reputation carries nothing and that every judgement is made from the
published evidence alone, and this stands entire, because **the convergence here is independent rather than
inferential.** There the actors are reading each other; here they are reading the same correct document, so the
outcome arrives faster without the copying rather than slower.

It is distinct from the house's **Bred for the Ark**, which stages genetic adaptation to captivity, where a managed
population is resampled every generation under conditions that select, so the lineage handed back is not the one taken
in. Remove that engine, by stipulating an environment that selects for nothing whatever, and this stands, because
**nothing here is being selected by an environment.** The narrowing is done by choice at the point of mating rather
than by differential survival, and it would happen identically in a population where every animal survived and bred
equally well.

It is distinct from the house's **The Head Start**, which stages the Matthew effect, where an early advantage compounds
so that a lead widens by accumulation. Remove that engine, by stipulating that nothing compounds and every generation
begins fresh, and this stands, because **one round is already the finding**: a single generation in which most matings
go to one sire collapses that generation's contribution to a handful of ancestors, and the effective number is a
property of how evenly one generation's matings were spread rather than of any accumulation across them.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the breeder as vain, the
  registry as obstinate or the health scheme as inadequate, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong:
  the register records accurately and its closure is the definition of a breed rather than a policy anybody is
  defending; the health scheme tests honestly, publishes openly and extends its panel whenever a variant is
  characterised, which is what reducing inherited disease consists of; the sire genuinely is the best-documented animal
  of his generation and does nothing at all, being a dog; the breeder reads the published evidence rather than relying
  on reputation, selects the animal it identifies and books one mating, which is the diligent choice and the one the
  guidance recommends, and no single booking moves the effective number perceptibly; and the variant that is not tested
  for is not concealed but undiscovered. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the
  misfit is that better evidence about which individual is best concentrates the choice on that individual.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Capping the number of litters a sire
  may have is an instruction to use an animal the evidence shows is worse. Outcrossing restores the variation and
  removes the certified descent the register exists to produce, which is not a relaxation of the rule but the deletion
  of its output. Extending the panel improves the evidence, which is the thing that sharpens the answer and narrows the
  choice. Optimal contribution selection genuinely spreads the matings and requires a coordinating body with standing
  over decisions that belong to individual breeders. And knowing the whole structure changes nothing at the point of
  choosing, since the animal the evidence identifies is still the animal the evidence identifies.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the pedigree analysis and the effective population sizes are Calboli, Sampson,
  Fretwell and Balding's, and the whole-register trend is Lewis, Abhayaratne and Blott's. The bounds are carried with
  them: **how far pedigree-derived inbreeding tracks genomic measures, and which remedy is right, are genuinely
  contested**, and the play carries that argument rather than settling it; the figures are one national register in one
  period and are offered as the documented shape of the concentration rather than as constants, and registers elsewhere
  differ in rules and in practice; rates of inbreeding fell after 2000 and the play says so, since a mechanism that
  operates is not a mechanism that cannot be moderated; the account treats breeds as comparable where they differ
  greatly in size, history and use, which is its own simplification; and the mechanism is stated for a closed
  population with an asymmetry in how many offspring each sex can have, which is not every population. **The play makes
  no claim about any breed, any register, any breeder or any animal, and gives no veterinary, breeding or animal
  husbandry advice of any kind**, only that a closed pool narrows in proportion to how few individuals contribute, and
  that how few contribute is decided by how well the best one can be seen.
