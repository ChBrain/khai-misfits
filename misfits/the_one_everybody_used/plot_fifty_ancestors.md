# Plot: Fifty Ancestors

## Taxonomy

[The One Everybody Used](play_the_one_everybody_used.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The pedigree analysis, eight generations deep, on a national register's records.
[The Effective Number](piece_the_effective_number.md), set beside [The Census Count](piece_the_census_count.md).

## Action

[Concentrating](process_concentrating.md), measured after the fact: the distribution of matings across sires read out as a genetic population size.

## Stage

The analysis of ten breeds finds extremely inbred animals in every one but the greyhound, and an inbreeding effective population size **between forty and eighty** for all but two, with popular sires identified as the major contributor to the rate.
Across all two hundred and fifteen breeds of that register from 1980 to 2014, the rate of inbreeding is **not correlated with census population size**.

## Tension

The number everybody reassures themselves with turns out to predict nothing.
A breed with tens of thousands of living dogs is behaving genetically like a population of fifty, and the count of living dogs does not tell you which breeds those are. That is why the finding had to be measured: effective size depends on how **evenly** contributions are spread rather than on how many animals exist, so a large breed used unevenly behaves like a small one and looks like a large one from every direction anybody normally looks. The rate did fall after 2000, which is real and worth saying, and it fell by breeders deliberately spreading matings rather than by any of the mechanism weakening.
