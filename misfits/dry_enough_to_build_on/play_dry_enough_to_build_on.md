# Play: Dry Enough to Build On

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Dry Enough to Build On.
A river takes the low ground every few years, as it has always done, and the town on the rise above it keeps its distance out of long habit. Then a wall goes up, well designed, built to hold the flood that comes once in a century. It works. The small floods stop coming. Within a few years the low ground is dry every spring, and dry ground beside a river is good ground: level, serviced, close to everything. So it is built on, by people who checked, by lenders who lent against a plain that is now demonstrably drier than it was, under a plan approved by a planner reading the same true fact. Eighty years later there is a town down there, and nobody in it has ever seen the river come up.

## Arc

The bend is this: the wall does not cause the flood, it causes the town that the flood finds.
It runs on a memory that only floods can refresh.
The One Who Raised It builds against a real hazard and builds well, and everything the wall promised it delivers: the small floods stop, the spring is dry, the ground is safer than it was. Nothing here is overstated. The plain genuinely changed.
The One Who Builds There reads that change correctly. Building on ground that has become safer is what a person should do, what a lender should lend against, what a planner should approve. Nobody is gambling. They are responding to a verified improvement, and the improvement is real.
And The Last Flood recedes, not from any record but from every living memory, because a memory of flooding is refreshed by flooding and by nothing else. Where the river keeps arriving, a place stays adapted: the habits hold, the low ground stays cheap, and nobody has to be told why. Where a wall removes the small floods, the caution has nothing to feed on and is gone inside a generation. The people who build there are not ignoring a warning. There is no warning left, and the reason there is none is that the wall worked.
So the exposure accumulates in the open, and nothing marks it. In the contiguous United States, building a levee is associated with floodplain urban expansion accelerating by around sixty-two per cent, which is not a secret and is not a scandal: it is a great many sensible decisions, each one correct, adding up behind a wall to a quantity nobody totalled.
Then The Bigger Water arrives, larger than the rating, as the rating always said one day would. It takes a town that would not have been there. On an unprotected plain it would have taken a field.
And the correction is the one thing nobody can sell: let some water in. Give the river room, move the buildings back, accept small floods so the plain stays wet enough to be remembered and cheap enough to leave alone. All of that is paid now, in visible damage and moved households, to prevent a loss that arrives after the payers are gone and that, if the correction works, nobody ever sees. Raise the wall instead and it works too, and buys a drier plain, a longer quiet, a fainter memory, and more standing behind it than before.

## Stakes

Whether a work that genuinely reduces a hazard can be counted honestly, when what it changes most is not the water but what comes to stand in its way.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a real protection, correctly built and correctly trusted, and away from any ledger in which the town it invites appears as one of its effects. The production cannot make the wall stop working, because it works, and it cannot make the memory survive, because only floods refresh a memory of floods and the wall was built to stop those. What is left to fight over is not whether to build the defence, everyone agrees the hazard is real, but who counts what accumulates behind it, and who is willing to be the one that lets water in while the sun is out.
