# Dry Enough to Build On: Reference

The research warrant for Dry Enough to Build On.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the levee effect, observed by Gilbert White in 1945, named by
Tobin in 1995 and modelled since by socio-hydrology as a coevolution of water and settlement, all cited
below.

## Line of Work

Staging the levee effect as a **protection that recruits its own exposure**: a work built to keep water off
a floodplain succeeds, and its success makes the ground behind it worth building on, so people and value
accumulate exactly where the water will go when a flood larger than the work's rating arrives. The play
models the documented mechanics, the protection that is real and stops the small floods it was built for,
the ground that becomes genuinely safer and is therefore genuinely worth settling, the memory that decays
because nothing happens for a generation, the exposure that accumulates unseen behind the wall, the flood
that exceeds the rating and takes more than it could have taken from an unprotected plain, and the
correction that works by admitting water on purpose, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a
tale of hubris.

The governing law is White's, stated in 1945 and not improved on since: floods are acts of God, but flood
losses are largely acts of man. The wall does not cause the flood. It causes the town that the flood
finds. And it does so through no one's error, because every party is responding correctly to a real
change: the engineer to a real hazard, the settler to real protection, the lender and the planner to a
plain that is now, verifiably, drier than it was.

What makes it structural rather than a failure of foresight is the memory. Socio-hydrology's models put
social memory of flooding at the centre: where floods keep arriving, a society stays adapted, keeps its
habits, and prices the plain accordingly; where a wall removes the small floods, the memory has nothing to
refresh it and fades within a generation, so the caution disappears from living knowledge at exactly the
rate the exposure behind the wall is rising. The people who build there are not ignoring a warning. There
is no warning left to ignore, because the wall worked.

The scale is measurable. Levee construction across the contiguous United States is associated with a
sixty-two per cent acceleration in floodplain urban expansion, which is the exposure being built, in the
open, by everyone acting sensibly.

It is distinct from the house's **The Spent Margin**, which is the nearest neighbour and the cut is in who
spends and whether they know: there one agent is handed a felt margin by a safety measure, holds a roughly
constant target level of accepted risk, and re-spends the margin as speed or inattention within their own
experience. Here nobody feels a margin at all. The people who build behind the wall never saw a flood, are
not trading safety off against anything, and are not defending any risk target: the spending is done across
generations by parties who do not know there was ever a margin, and what they build cannot be un-built the
way a driver can slow down.

It is distinct from the house's **The Long Calm**, where years without a crash make leverage look safe and
the calm breeds the crash: there the calm is produced by the market's own behaviour and no structure stands
between anyone and the hazard, and the exposure is positions that can be closed. Here a physical work
genuinely removes the small events, which is what erases the signal, and the exposure is houses, roads and
lives on particular ground, which cannot be sold out of before the water arrives.

It is distinct from the house's **The Unmarked Edge**, where cost and effort push the working point toward
a boundary nobody can see: there the operating point drifts and the boundary is invisible. Here nothing
drifts and the boundary is published, since the wall's rating is a known number, and what changes is only
what has come to stand behind it.

It is distinct from the house's **The Brittle Optimum**, where slack is trimmed away as idle waste until
the system shatters: there the resilience is removed. Here nothing is removed, something is added, and the
floodplain that was the slack is not trimmed but occupied.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the engineer
  as arrogant or the settler as reckless, because the source finding is precisely that both are right: the
  wall stops the floods it was built to stop, the ground behind it really is drier, and building on
  genuinely safer land is what a person, a lender and a planner should each do. Nor is the forgetting a
  fault. Memory of a flood is refreshed by floods, so a wall that works removes the only thing that could
  have sustained the caution, and the generation that builds there is not ignoring a warning but living
  where no warning exists. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, a work that
  lowers frequency and, by lowering it, raises what stands in the way of the event it cannot stop.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The correction that
  works is to let water in: give the river room, move buildings back, accept small floods so that the plain
  stays wet enough to be remembered and cheap enough to leave empty. Every part of that is paid now, in
  visible damage and lost land and moved households, to prevent a larger loss that will arrive after the
  people paying are gone and that will never be observed if the correction succeeds. The alternative is to
  raise the wall, which works, and which produces a drier plain, a longer quiet, a fainter memory and more
  behind it, so the instrument that most obviously answers the problem is the one that compounds it.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the observation, the naming, the coevolution models
  and the measured association are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the effect is an association
  measured across settings and not a law that holds at every wall, its strength is contested in the
  literature and depends on local planning, insurance and land supply, and a levee that protects an
  existing town from a hazard it already faces is not the case being staged. **The play makes no claim that
  flood defences should not be built**, only that a defence changes what stands behind it, and that the
  change is not counted anywhere in the decision to build.
