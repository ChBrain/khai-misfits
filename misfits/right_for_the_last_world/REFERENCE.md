# Right for the Last World: Reference

The research warrant for Right for the Last World.
The term is the organisation theorists'; the play runs on structural contingency theory, from Burns and
Stalker's mechanistic and organic forms through Lawrence and Lorsch's differentiation and integration, all
cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging structural contingency as a **fitted system overtaken**: an organisation adopts the form its
environment actually calls for, is rewarded for it, and holds it after the environment has turned. The play
models the documented mechanics, the environment that sets a real requirement rather than a preference, the
form genuinely matched to it and correct when chosen, the performance that follows and is read as proof of
the form rather than of the match, the hardening by which a validated form becomes the way things are done
and the ground of every promotion and process built on it, the turn in the environment that no one inside
is positioned to see because nothing inside has changed, and the resulting misfit in which a form that is
still executed well is now the wrong form, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of
complacency or stupidity. The governing finding of the source literature is the play's governing law: there
is no one best way to organise, and no way of organising is equally effective in all conditions, so a
structure is never right in itself but only right for a world, and a world does not hold still.

It is distinct from the house's **The Brittle Optimum**, where slack is trimmed for efficiency and lost
resilience is the by-product nobody sought: there something is removed from the system; here nothing is
removed and nothing degrades, the form is intact and the world moved. It is distinct from the house's
**The Success Trap**, where an incumbent climbs the margin upward and is undercut from below: there the
mechanism is a market's shape and a rational refusal of low-margin work; here there is no entrant at all
and no margin logic, only a requirement that changed. It is distinct from the house's **Path Dependence**,
where a standard is held by switching costs and coordination: there the better option is known and cannot
be reached alone; here nobody has yet established what the better form is, and the fit was real rather
than merely locked in.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the executive
  as complacent or the structure as sclerotic, because the source finding is precisely that the form was
  right: it was chosen against a real reading of the environment, it delivered the performance it promised,
  and holding a form that is working is not inertia but competence. The misfit is authored by no error and
  emerges from the structure, a fit that can only ever be a fit with something, so the misfit is that the
  better the form matched the last world, the more of the organisation is built on the match, and the more
  faithfully it will keep executing a form the world no longer asks for.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Regaining fit means
  dismantling the arrangements that the previous fit earned, and it must be done on a reading of the new
  environment that is not yet confirmed by any performance, against people who can point to results the
  current form is still producing. The correction is a bet made against present evidence, so the cure for
  a form that is right for the last world is to abandon it before the numbers say to.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; structural contingency, mechanistic and organic
  forms, and differentiation and integration are theirs. The theory's bound is carried with it: contingency
  claims fit predicts performance, a claim contested in strength and measurement, and the play stages the
  relation rather than a coefficient.
