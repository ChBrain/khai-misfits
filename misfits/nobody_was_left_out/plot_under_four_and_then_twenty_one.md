## Tension

The gradient was not revealed by the reform. It was made by it, and made out of equal treatment.
Before the programme, fewer than four per cent of teachers in these grades lacked a full credential in even the state's most disadvantaged schools. By the third year, more than twenty-one per cent lacked one in the lowest-income schools, against four point three per cent in the highest, which is roughly where everybody had been standing at the start. So the shortfall was not distributed: it was delivered whole, to one end, and to the end the reform was written for. The careful econometric work says the same thing from the other side, finding that smaller classes did raise attainment and that the rise in teachers with neither prior experience nor full certification dampened it, most where the children were poorest. And none of this is in the state average, because **a redistribution nets to nothing in a mean**, so the finding existed only for as long as somebody was willing to split the data by the income of the school.
