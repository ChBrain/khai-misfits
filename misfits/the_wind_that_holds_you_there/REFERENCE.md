# The Wind That Holds You There: Reference

The research warrant for The Wind That Holds You There.
The setting is a sailing vessel on a coast; the play runs on the fact that a sail generates force from wind
flowing across it and therefore cannot be aimed at the wind, and on the hydrodynamic relation that makes leeway
rise as a vessel's way falls, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the sailing rig as **a capability that fixes the one direction it cannot be pointed**: the sail is an
aerofoil, which is why a vessel can reach places the wind is coming from at all; a wing makes nothing aimed at
the wind, so there is an arc of roughly forty-five degrees either side of the wind into which no vessel can be
steered; a shore to leeward is by definition a shore the wind is blowing toward, so the arc covers the exit; and
as the wind rises the vessel is set down harder while her power to work off falls, by two mechanisms with one
cause. The play models the wing that is the achievement, the sector that is its price rather than its flaw, the
shore that is charted and doing nothing, the single rising variable, the sail that must come off, the side force
varying with the square of the speed, the two readings that are both correct, and the offing spent days earlier,
as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a reckless master or a badly found ship.

The governing law is that the same wind that sets a vessel toward a shore is the wind she must work against to
leave it, and her ability to do that falls as that wind rises, which is one movement and not two.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The rig is
sound and is the achievement of the whole art. The chart is correct and the position is known. Reducing sail in
rising wind is good seamanship and would be praised by anybody watching. The master reads the situation
accurately and has been in it before. The wind has no information about the vessel and no interest in the shore.

**And the rig is right, which the play establishes before anything else.** Working to windward is what a sailing
rig is for: without it a vessel goes where the wind sends her and nowhere else. The destination is reached by
never once pointing at it, at a cost of perhaps half again the distance sailed, and that price is paid daily
without remark. **A play that opens by faulting the wing has nothing left to say about the sector.**

**Then the sector, which is the same fact as the lift.** A sail makes force from flow across it, so a sail aimed
at the wind produces nothing, and the arc is not a defect that better design removes: **a rig that could face the
wind would be a rig generating no force at all.** The capability and the exclusion are one object seen from two
sides, and in open water the arc is ninety degrees nobody has any use for.

**Then the geometry, which turns a property into a danger without anybody acting.** A shore to leeward is a shore
the wind is blowing onto; leaving it means going to windward; going to windward means entering the arc.
**Nothing has gone wrong and the trap is already complete.** And no ignorance is available to be removed: the
shore is surveyed, marked and known, so the master can name precisely which direction the vessel cannot be
steered in, which is knowledge and is not capability.

**Then the coupling, which is what makes this a structure rather than a hazard.** One variable moves both
quantities. As the wind rises the vessel is set toward the land faster **and** her power to leave it falls, and
these are not two misfortunes arriving together. Sail must be reduced, which is correct, and the drive it removes
is the drive that was working her off. And the seaway takes her way: the keel resists the wind's sideways push by
generating side force, side force varies with the **square** of the speed through the water, so halving her way
quarters the resistance and the leeway rises to make up the shortfall. **She is set toward the shore by her own
loss of speed**, with the helm where it should be and every hand doing the right thing.

**And it is invisible from on board, which is why competence does not catch it.** The heading has not changed and
the helm feels the same; what has changed is the gap between where she is pointed and where she is going. The
compass reports the first and nothing aboard reports the second, so **the only instrument that reports it is the
land**, and the news arrives as breakers on a bearing that has not moved.

**Then the choice, which contains no right answer.** Carry sail and there is drive to claw off, and the rig may
come down, after which nothing else matters. Reduce sail and the rig stands and the ground is given away. **Each
branch is correct about the danger it is looking at**, and there is no vantage point from which either is the
mistake, so this is not a test of nerve or of prudence and being better at it changes the wording of the order
rather than the position of the vessel. HMS St George went ashore on the Jutland coast in December 1811 under a
jury rig, which is the same variable acting twice in its plainest form: the weather that was setting her down had
already taken the rig she would have left with.

**And the one settlement was spent before the play opened.** Sea room works completely: with enough of it every
mechanism here runs and nothing happens, because the leeway is paid out over water. It is the answer and it is
the only answer, and **it is not a manoeuvre.** It is an offing kept days earlier in fair weather, for no visible
reason, by somebody who could not have known which day would need it, so it is not available to anybody standing
on the deck in the gale. It has either already been kept or it has not.

It is distinct from the house's **Widest When You Need It**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages adverse
selection in market making, where the quoter's spread is their only defence against a better-informed
counterparty and must therefore be widest exactly when the greatest number of people need to trade. Remove that
engine entirely, by stipulating no informed traders anywhere and nothing to be adversely selected against, and
this misfit is unchanged, because **the wind holds no information and is not selecting against the vessel.**
There the withdrawal of capability is a deliberate defensive response by a party who would supply it if it were
safe to; here nothing withdraws anything, and the incapacity is a fixed property of how lift is made that would
be identical with the shore a hundred miles away. **One is a defence and the other is a shape.**

It is distinct from the house's **The Irony of Automation**, which stages capability degraded at the worst moment
by the operator having been relieved of the easy work and deskilled. Remove that engine, by stipulating no
automation of any kind, a square-rigger and a full crew hand-steering in a gale, and this stands whole, because
**nothing here has been taken away from anybody's practice.** That play needs a skill that has decayed from
disuse; this one needs a vessel whose skill is intact and whose geometry is not.

It is distinct from the house's **The Spent Margin**, which stages risk compensation, where a safety improvement
is consumed as extra performance and the margin returns to where it was. Remove that engine, by stipulating a
crew who take no additional risk whatever on account of any equipment they carry, and this is untouched, because
**nobody here has spent anything.** The margin is not being consumed by behaviour: it is being consumed by the
weather, and a crew standing further off than they need to is exactly the settlement this play names and cannot
supply.

It is distinct from the house's **The Brittle Optimum**, which stages tight coupling and the fragility of the
lean, where slack removed for efficiency leaves nothing to absorb a disturbance. Remove that engine, by
stipulating that nothing anywhere has been optimised and every margin is as generous as anybody could wish, and
the mechanism stands, because **the sector does not depend on how much slack exists.** Sea room is indeed slack,
and its absence here is not the product of an efficiency drive: it is the product of a decision taken in
conditions that gave no reason to take it, by somebody with no information that would have distinguished the day.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the master as
  reckless, the vessel as badly found, or the weather as an antagonist, because nothing in the sequence has a
  decision to get wrong: the rig is the achievement of the art, the chart is correct and the position known,
  reducing sail in rising wind is good practice and would be praised, the master reads the situation accurately
  and has been in it before, and the wind has no information and no intent. The failure is authored by nobody and
  emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the capability fixes where it cannot be pointed.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Better information changes
  nothing, since the shore is already charted and the position already known. Better seamanship changes nothing,
  since both readings on deck are correct and neither is the mistake. Carrying sail buys drive and risks the rig,
  after which nothing else is available. Reducing sail keeps the rig and gives away the ground. And sea room,
  which genuinely and completely works, is not a manoeuvre: it is an offing kept days earlier in fair weather for
  no visible reason, so it is unavailable to everybody in the play by the time the play begins.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the treatment of the sail as an aerofoil and of the hull's side force
  is Marchaj's, the record of how such vessels were actually handled is Harland's, and the examination of windward
  capability as an evidential question is Willis's. The bounds are carried with them, and one is owed plainly:
  **the Willis paper was not read in full**, so the play cites it for the standing of the question and puts **no
  numerical figure** on how close such vessels could lie, and any figure a reader carries away from this
  production is not from here. The others: the square relation between speed and side force is stated as the
  standard hydrodynamic account and not as a measurement of any particular vessel; the account is written for
  sailing vessels under sail and is not a claim about ships generally; **whether an auxiliary engine answers this
  or relocates it, and how much offing is prudent, are genuinely contested** and the play adjudicates neither;
  and the 1811 losses are given as an event with its circumstances and not as evidence of a rate. **The play makes
  no claim about any vessel, any master, any navy or any coast, and gives no navigational, seamanship or safety
  advice of any kind**, only that a rig which earns its living going against the wind cannot be aimed at it.
