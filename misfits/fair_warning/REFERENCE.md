# Fair Warning: Reference

The research warrant for **Fair Warning**, a staging of the phenomenon the literature calls the **green
paradox** (Sinn): that a credibly announced future tightening of demand for fossil carbon lowers the
expected future net price of the resource, so the owners of an exhaustible stock re-optimise their
extraction path forward and bring supply into the present, and the announcement of the remedy
accelerates the harm before the remedy arrives. It is held with the finding that the announcement must
be **credible** for the policy to work at all and that its credibility is exactly what triggers the
acceleration, that no participant is behaving badly, and that the corrections which reach the
mechanism are supply-side and expensive, since they require paying an owner for wealth the policy is
extinguishing.

## Line of Work

Staging the green paradox as a **the-warning-brings-the-harm-forward structure**: the ordinary
expectation of a phase-out is that announcing it early is both fair and effective. Fair, because
industry, workers and investors are given time to adapt rather than being stranded overnight;
effective, because a policy nobody believes will happen changes nobody's plans, so credibility is the
thing a legislator must establish. Both halves of that expectation are correct, and together they set
the trap. The owner of an exhaustible stock faces a question that is not whether to sell but **when**,
because the resource does not spoil and the only return to leaving it in the ground is the expected
rise in its future net price (Hotelling). A credible announcement that demand will be cut in fifteen
years is therefore an announcement that the future net price will be lower, which lowers the return to
waiting, which moves the optimal extraction path forward. The owner is not defying the policy or
racing to beat it out of spite. It is doing arithmetic that any holder of a depleting asset does,
and doing it correctly, and the answer has changed because the policy changed it. So carbon that would
have been burned slowly over forty years is burned quickly over fifteen, the near-term emissions rise,
and if the stock is eventually extracted anyway then the only thing the announcement altered was the
timing, in the wrong direction. The play models the notice that has to be credible, the pressure to
make it more credible still, the revaluation in the owner's ledger, the extraction that follows, the
carbon arriving sooner, and the supply-side correction that reaches the mechanism and costs, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, and not as a tale of a greedy extractor. The ordinary
expectation is that a warning gives people time to change course; the finding is that where the thing
being warned is an asset holder, the warning changes the course before the deadline does. The
governing law is that **where an owner's only real choice about an exhaustible stock is when to sell
it, a credible announcement that future demand will be cut lowers the value of holding it, so the
announcement brings the extraction forward and the harm arrives sooner than if nothing had been said**.

It is an announcement-effect structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its
nearest neighbours. It is distinct from Time Inconsistency, where a policymaker's announcement is
**not** credible and agents rationally discount it, so the trouble is that promises cannot bind: this
is the exact inverse, since here the announcement is fully believed, and being believed is what does
the damage, so the remedy that fixes time inconsistency, making the commitment more credible, is the
thing that makes this worse. It is distinct from Sandbag, the ratchet effect, where agents restrain
current output because tomorrow's standard will be indexed to today's performance: there the response
to a future rule is to do **less** now, and the indexing runs from present output to future demands,
whereas here the response is to do **more** now, and the indexing runs from a future price to a
present decision, so the two are opposite in sign and different in what is being indexed to what. It
is distinct from Seed Corn, managerial short-termism, where a manager knowingly trades tomorrow's
capacity for today's reported number: there someone sacrifices a future they will still be standing
in, whereas here the owner is not consuming its own future at all but correctly maximising over a
stock that a legislature has announced it will render worthless, so there is nothing being neglected
and no myopia to correct. And it is distinct from Jevons Paradox, where efficiency lowers the cost of
using a resource and total consumption rises: there the channel is **demand** responding to a lower
effective price and the total burned goes up, whereas here demand is being deliberately cut, the
channel is **supply** responding to an expected future price, and the total burned may be unchanged
while its arrival is merely brought forward, which is a different quantity going wrong for a different
reason.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast a
  cynical extractor racing to beat the deadline out of spite, or a legislator in the pocket of anyone,
  because the finding it stages needs neither. The legislator announces years ahead precisely so that
  workers, investors and regions are not stranded overnight, which is the humane and orthodox design
  and the one any decent government would choose. The campaigner presses for a firmer date and an
  earlier one, which is exactly the right thing to press for, since a phase-out nobody believes in
  changes nobody's plans. And the owner is doing the arithmetic any holder of a depleting asset does:
  the resource does not spoil, the only return to waiting is the expected rise in its price, and the
  expected rise has just been legislated away. Nobody is defying the policy. The policy is being
  believed, which is what it asked to be.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The instruments that
  reach the mechanism act on supply rather than demand: buy the reserves and retire them, tax the
  stock rather than the flow, or assemble a demand cartel comprehensive enough that no unregulated
  buyer remains. Each is expensive and politically brutal in the same specific way, which is that they
  require **paying the owner** for wealth the policy is extinguishing, a transfer to exactly the party
  the public believes should bear the loss, and the cartel must be very nearly universal or the
  leakage restores the arbitrage it was built to close. Meanwhile the cheap and available remedy,
  announcing sooner and more credibly, is the thing that causes the harm, so the trap punishes the
  instrument a government can actually reach for and rewards only the ones it can least afford.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here,
  never claimed as the house's. The green paradox, the supply-side account of why announced future
  demand cuts can raise present emissions, is Sinn's; the rule the owner is following, that the return
  to leaving an exhaustible resource in the ground is the expected rise in its net price, is
  Hotelling's, drawn to mark the mechanism and not to claim the modern result. The scope is held to
  owners of exhaustible stocks who are forward-looking and able to accelerate extraction, and the play
  is not offered as an argument that climate policy is counterproductive, that phase-outs should be
  announced late or vaguely, or that the effect has been shown to dominate in practice: the magnitude
  is contested in the literature, it depends on extraction costs, on how much of the stock is
  ultimately economic, and on whether supply-side instruments accompany the demand-side ones, and the
  source presents it as a design problem to be solved inside climate policy rather than as a case
  against it. The play stages the difficulty exactly as a difficulty.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Notice**: carries the announcement (Sinn), a future cut in demand stated far enough ahead to
  be fair and clearly enough to be believed, both of which it must be, since a phase-out nobody credits
  changes nobody's plans and a phase-out with no notice strands the people it lands on.
- **The Certainty**: carries the pressure that makes it worse by making it better, the entirely correct
  campaign for a firmer date and an earlier one, since credibility is the property the policy needs and
  is also the property that sets the owner's arithmetic moving.
- **The Devaluation**: carries the revaluation (Hotelling), the expected future net price falling, so
  the return to leaving the stock in the ground falls with it and waiting stops being the better trade.
- **The Acceleration**: carries the response, extraction moved forward, not in defiance of the policy
  but in correct answer to it, by an owner doing the arithmetic every holder of a depleting asset does.
- **The Bringing Forward**: carries the outcome, near-term emissions rising and, where the stock is
  ultimately extracted anyway, a policy that has altered the timing and not the total, in the wrong
  direction.
- **The Boundary**: carries the condition and the hedge, that the trap needs an exhaustible stock, a
  forward-looking owner able to accelerate, and a credible announcement, that its magnitude is
  contested and turns on extraction costs and on how much of the stock is ultimately economic, and that
  it lifts where supply-side instruments accompany the demand-side ones, at a price that must be paid
  to the owner rather than argued away.
