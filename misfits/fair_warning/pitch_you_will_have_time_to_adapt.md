# Pitch: You Will Have Time to Adapt

## Taxonomy

[Fair Warning](play_fair_warning.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Decent and steady, the tone of a government doing the difficult thing properly.
Fifteen years' notice, a date in statute, no ambiguity about whether it will happen, and a transition package for the towns.
Nothing false in it: five years would strand the people who built their lives around the industry, thirty would mean nothing, and a deadline nobody believes changes nobody's plans, so both the length and the firmness are required rather than chosen.

## Undertow

Under the notice, a price forecast.
The owner of a seam is not deciding whether to sell but when, since the stuff does not spoil and the only return to leaving it in the ground is the expected rise in its net price, so an announcement that demand will be cut is an announcement that the return to waiting has fallen (Hotelling; Sinn).
No cynical extractor and no captured legislator: nobody is defying the policy, and the field that opens this year opens because the policy was believed exactly as it asked to be.

## Nerve

The decency of the long notice, and the catch that the decency is the mechanism:
that credibility is the property the policy needs and the property that moves the extraction forward, so the standard cure for a rule nobody heeds is the accelerant here,
and you will have time to adapt is the sentence that tells an owner precisely when to sell.

## Echo

After the run: a field into production in the year the date was set, near-term emissions rising, and, where the stock is lifted anyway, a schedule moved and a total unchanged, on a problem where the schedule is most of what matters (Sinn).
And the remedy, real and priced: buy the reserves and retire them, tax the stock rather than the flow, or build a demand cartel with no unregulated buyer left, each of them a payment to the owner for wealth the policy is extinguishing, and the last of them useless unless it is very nearly universal.
And the bound held in view: it needs an exhaustible stock and an owner able to accelerate, its magnitude is contested and turns on extraction costs and on how much of the stock is ultimately economic, so the whole of it is that telling someone honestly when you will stop buying is telling them exactly when to sell.
