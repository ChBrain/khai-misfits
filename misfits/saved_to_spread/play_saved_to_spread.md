# Play: Saved to Spread

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Saved to Spread.
A pathogen cannot be as deadly as it likes. A strain that kills its host too fast kills its own way of spreading, since a dead host meets no one and infects no one, so the very deadliest variants burn out with the bodies they fell, and virulence settles at the level that trades harm against transmission and pays. This is the natural cap on how bad a disease can get, and it holds on its own, with nothing to enforce it but the death of the strains that overreach (Anderson and May).
And then a vaccine arrives that saves lives, and it is right to give it. But it is a leaky vaccine: it blunts the disease and prevents death in the vaccinated, and it does not stop them from being infected or from passing the pathogen on. This is the ordinary kind for many diseases, and it does exactly what it should, sparing the protected the illness and its worst. There is nothing wrong in the shot and nothing foolish in taking it.
And here is the shape beneath the protection. A vaccinated host survives an infection that would have killed an unvaccinated one, and while it survives, it sheds. So a hyper-virulent strain that could never have spread from an unvaccinated host, dead before it could pass anything on, now spreads freely from a vaccinated host the shot kept alive. The cost of virulence, the one thing that capped how deadly the pathogen could be, has been paid off by the vaccine, and paid on the strain's behalf (Read, Baigent, Powers, and colleagues).
And so the cap lifts and the pathogen climbs. With the death of the host no longer the death of the strain, selection stops punishing lethality, and variants deadlier than any the unvaccinated world would have sustained now survive, spread, and come to dominate, so the pathogen population evolves more virulent than it ever was before the vaccine (Gandon, Mackinnon, Nee, and Read). The disease in the vaccinated stays mild, because they are protected. The disease itself has grown worse.
And the burden falls on the unprotected. The vaccinated are shielded from the deadlier strain their own protection helped select, but the unvaccinated and those whose immunity has waned are not, and they meet a pathogen more lethal than would ever have evolved in a world without the leaky vaccine. No one spread it on purpose and no one was reckless: the vaccinated took a life-saving shot and shed without choosing to, the makers gave the standard and correct protection, and the pathogen only did what selection allowed. The harm is authored by no one, and it lands on those the shield does not cover.
And here is what avoids it, and why it costs. A sterilizing vaccine, one that blocks not only the disease but the transmission, stops the vaccinated from passing the pathogen on, so it removes the selection pressure and does not drive virulence up. Where such a vaccine can be made, it is the answer. But it is harder to make, it does not exist for every pathogen, and where only a leaky vaccine is at hand the right choice is still to use it, since it saves lives, while designing toward transmission blockade and watching the virulence over time (Read and colleagues; Gandon and colleagues). The remedy is real and dear: the harder vaccine, and the longer view.
And so the misfit is not that vaccines harm but that a protection which saves without stopping the spread reshapes the pathogen. The experimental demonstration in Marek's disease is Read, Baigent, Powers, and colleagues', the theory that imperfect vaccines select for higher virulence is Gandon, Mackinnon, Nee, and Read's, and the trade-off that caps virulence is Anderson and May's. The house stages the lifted cap and holds the bound in unusual view: that the effect needs a leaky vaccine and a transmission-limited virulence, runs through selection and not through anyone's behaviour, and is a case for how to design a vaccine and never whether to take one, since a sterilizing vaccine does not drive it and the shot still saves the lives it shields, so the whole of it is that a host saved to spread can carry a deadlier strain to those the saving never reached.

## Arc

The bend is this: a vaccine that blunts disease without blocking transmission keeps its host alive to shed strains the cost of virulence would otherwise have capped, so it selects the pathogen deadlier, and the evolved hypervirulence falls on the unprotected, the protection of some raising the lethality met by others, with no one acting wrongly and the vaccine still saving the lives it shields.
It runs on a natural cap on virulence.
A strain too deadly kills its host before it can spread, so the deadliest variants self-limit and virulence settles where harm and transmission trade off best, bounded with nothing to enforce it but the death of the strains that overreach (Anderson and May).
And a leaky vaccine lifts onto it. One that blunts the disease and prevents death in the vaccinated but does not block infection or transmission, life-saving and standard, changing nothing in the host's own choices (Read and colleagues).
And the saved host sheds what should have died. A vaccinated host survives an infection that would have killed an unvaccinated one, and while it lives it sheds, so a hyper-virulent strain that could not have spread from a dead host now spreads from a living, protected one (Read and colleagues).
And the cap comes off. With the cost of killing the host paid by the vaccine, selection stops punishing lethality, so variants deadlier than the unvaccinated world would have sustained survive and spread (Gandon and colleagues).
And the pathogen climbs. The deadlier strains come to dominate and the pathogen population evolves more virulent than before the vaccine, the disease itself grown worse though it stays mild in the protected (Gandon and colleagues).
And the burden falls on the unprotected. The vaccinated are shielded from the deadlier strain their protection helped select, but the unvaccinated and the waning meet it, a lethality raised for them by the shielding of others.
And no one authored the harm. The vaccinated shed without choosing to, the makers gave the correct protection, and the pathogen only followed selection, so the evolved hypervirulence is a loss with no wrongdoer.
And the whole is held against its own limit, with care. The effect needs a leaky vaccine and a transmission-limited virulence and lifts under a sterilizing vaccine, a virulence not tied to transmission, or coverage that removes susceptibles, and it is a case for how to design a vaccine and never whether to take one (Read and colleagues).
