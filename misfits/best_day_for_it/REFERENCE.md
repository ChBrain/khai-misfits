# Best Day for It: Reference

The research warrant for **Best Day for It**, a staging of the phenomenon the literature calls the
**market-value decline of variable renewables** (Lion Hirth), also met as cannibalisation or the falling
value factor: that every unit of a zero-marginal-cost weather-driven technology produces in the same
hours as every other unit of it, so as the fleet grows it suppresses the price precisely in the hours it
earns, and revenue per unit falls with its own deployment, independently of any fall in its cost. It is
held with the finding that every actor is behaving correctly, that the measures policy watches cannot
see the effect, and that the two available remedies fail in opposite directions.

## Line of Work

Staging the value decline as a **success-destroys-its-own-revenue structure**: the whole of it follows
from a correlation that nobody chose and nobody can undo. A developer builds where the resource is best,
which is what a developer is for, and every other developer of the same technology is looking at the
same maps and reaching the same conclusion, so the fleet's output is not merely similar but very nearly
simultaneous. And a plant with no fuel bids zero, which is not aggressive pricing but the correct bid,
since its marginal cost genuinely is nothing. And the system operator dispatches in merit order,
cheapest first, which is what merit order is for. Put those three correct behaviours together and the
market clears at the bottom of the stack in exactly the hours the fleet is producing, which are exactly
the hours it has to earn in (Sensfuss, Ragwitz and Genoese). The revenue per megawatt-hour therefore
falls as a function of how much of the technology has been built, and it would fall even if the
technology got cheaper every year, because the two quantities are independent (Hirth). Meanwhile the
numbers that policy watches, installed capacity and levelised cost, both look excellent and neither can
see the effect, because levelised cost averages over output without asking when the output arrives, and
a comparison of that average across an intermittent and a dispatchable technology is not a comparison of
like with like (Joskow; Ueckerdt, Hirth, Luderer and Edenhofer). Then the remedies. Storage,
transmission across weather regimes and demand shifting all break the correlation and all work, and
every one of them is paid for by the system while its benefit accrues to every generator in the class,
so no developer will fund what would raise its rivals' revenue as much as its own. And the remedy that
does pay the developer, a fixed contract at a guaranteed price, works precisely by removing the price
signal from the party best placed to act on it, so the instrument that makes the build bankable is the
instrument that suppresses the incentive to build the assets that would end the problem. The play models
the correlated fleet, the zero bid, the value factor, the wrong metric, the unfunded fix, the contract
that hides it and the bound as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, and not as a tale of a
subsidy-chasing developer or a naive minister. The ordinary expectation is that a technology getting
cheaper and more widely built is a technology getting more valuable; the finding is that its cost and
its value move independently, and that the second falls because of the first's success. The governing
law is that **where every unit of a generating technology has zero marginal cost and produces in the
same hours as every other unit of it, the fleet suppresses the price precisely in the hours it earns, so
revenue per unit falls with its own deployment independently of any fall in its cost, while the remedies
that break the correlation are paid for by the system and benefit every generator alike**.

It is an inference structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Chasing the Price, the cobweb theorem, whose whole mechanism is a **lag**
between the price signal and the supply response, so that producers act on a price that no longer
obtains: here there is no lag at all but simultaneity, the output of the class perfectly correlated
within it, and the price is destroyed at the moment of production rather than by a decision taken a
season earlier. It is distinct from The Commons, where a shared stock is depleted by each user taking
their share: nothing here is depleted and no stock exists, what is destroyed is a price, and each
generator harms itself exactly as much as it harms the others, so there is no free rider and no
appropriation. It is distinct from Jevons Paradox, where efficiency gains raise total consumption of the
resource: there the technology's success increases the quantity used, whereas here the technology's
success reduces the unit price it can obtain, and the two run in opposite directions on different
variables. And it is distinct from The Driest Wells, where a common resource degrades as extraction
concentrates on the best of it: there the physical asset deteriorates, whereas here the best sites stay
as good as they ever were and only what the output fetches has changed.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast a
  subsidy-chasing developer or a naive minister, because the finding it stages needs neither. Building
  where the resource is best is what a developer is for, and one that deliberately built on poorer
  ground to decorrelate itself from its rivals would be destroying its own returns to raise theirs.
  Bidding zero is not aggression but arithmetic, since a plant with no fuel has no marginal cost to
  recover. Dispatching cheapest first is the whole point of a merit order, and an operator that did
  otherwise would be raising costs on purpose. And a policymaker tracking installed capacity and
  levelised cost is tracking the two measures the field itself standardised on, neither of which is
  capable of showing the effect. Nobody is gaming anything and no behaviour would change if everybody
  understood the mechanism perfectly.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction, and here there are two
  remedies that fail in opposite directions. Storage, transmission across weather regimes and demand
  shifting genuinely break the correlation and genuinely work, and each is paid for by the system while
  its benefit accrues to every generator of the class alike, so no developer will fund an asset that
  raises its rivals' revenue as much as its own. The remedy that does pay the developer, a long fixed
  contract at a guaranteed price, works by taking the price signal away from the party best placed to
  respond to it, so the instrument that makes the build possible is the instrument that removes the
  reason to build the thing that would fix it. There is no arrangement on offer that both funds the
  project and preserves the signal.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The market-value decline, the value factor and its measurement are Lion
  Hirth's; the merit-order effect measured on a live market is Frank Sensfuss, Mario Ragwitz and Massimo
  Genoese's; the argument that levelised cost cannot compare an intermittent technology with a
  dispatchable one is Paul Joskow's; and the system-cost framing that puts integration costs where a
  per-plant measure cannot carry them is Falko Ueckerdt, Lion Hirth, Gunnar Luderer and Ottmar
  Edenhofer's. The scope is held to a technology whose units share both zero marginal cost and a common
  weather driver, and is not stretched into a claim that renewables are uneconomic, that deployment
  should slow, or that the decline cannot be mitigated: the source literature says the opposite on the
  last point, and the play carries the mitigation as part of the finding rather than as an objection to
  it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Correlated Fleet**: carries the root, that every developer of a technology reads the same
  resource maps and builds on the same kind of ground, so the class's output is very nearly simultaneous
  and no individual builder chose that or can undo it.
- **The Zero Bid**: carries the clearing mechanism (Sensfuss, Ragwitz and Genoese), a plant with no fuel
  correctly bidding nothing into a merit order correctly dispatching cheapest first, so the price falls
  to the bottom of the stack through two entirely correct behaviours.
- **The Value Factor**: carries the finding (Hirth), revenue per unit falling as a function of the
  technology's own deployment, independently of its cost, so cheapness and worth move apart and the
  fleet's success is the cause of the decline.
- **The Wrong Metric**: carries the invisibility (Joskow; Ueckerdt, Hirth, Luderer and Edenhofer),
  installed capacity and levelised cost both improving while the captured price falls, because an
  average over output cannot see when the output arrives and a per-plant measure cannot carry a cost
  that lands on the system.
- **The Unfunded Fix**: carries the remedy nobody will pay for, that storage, transmission across
  weather regimes and demand shifting all break the correlation and all work, and that each raises every
  rival's revenue as much as the funder's, so no developer will build one.
- **The Contract That Hides It**: carries the remedy that is paid for and makes it worse, that a long
  guaranteed price makes the project bankable precisely by removing the price signal from the party best
  placed to respond to it, so financing the build and suppressing the fix are one instrument.
- **The Bound**: carries the condition and the honest note (Hirth), that the trap needs units sharing
  both zero marginal cost and a common weather driver, that the decline is real, measured and a function
  of penetration rather than a fixed property, and that it is mitigable by flexibility in the rest of
  the system, so it is a reason to count value and not a reason to stop building.
