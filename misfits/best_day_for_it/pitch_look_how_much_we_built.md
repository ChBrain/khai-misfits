# Pitch: Look How Much We Built

## Taxonomy

[Best Day for It](play_best_day_for_it.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Proud and evidence-led, the tone of a chart that goes up and to the right and has every right to.
Installed capacity has multiplied, the levelised cost has fallen year after year, the auctions clear, and on a bright windy afternoon the fleet is carrying most of the load.
Nothing false in it. Every figure is correct, the build is real, and the two measures being quoted are the two the field itself standardised on.

## Undertow

Under the capacity chart, a price that goes to the bottom of the stack in exactly the hours the fleet runs.
Every unit of the technology reads the same resource maps and produces in the same hours, and a plant with no fuel correctly bids zero into an operator correctly dispatching cheapest first, so the revenue per unit falls as a function of how much has been built (Sensfuss, Ragwitz and Genoese; Hirth).
And neither chart can show it, because a levelised cost averages over output without ever asking when the output arrives (Joskow).

## Nerve

The honesty of the numbers, and the catch that the numbers are the wrong ones:
that cost and value are independent quantities, that only one of them is being driven by the deployment, and that a technology getting cheaper every year would still be getting less valuable,
and look how much we built is the sentence in which the cause of the decline is presented as the proof that there is none.

## Echo

After the run: a fix that works and that no developer will fund, since storage, transmission across weather regimes and demand shifting each raise every rival's revenue as much as the funder's.
And a fix that is funded and makes it worse, a long guaranteed price that makes the build bankable by removing the signal from the party best placed to respond to it.
And the bound held in view: the decline is a function of penetration rather than a fixed property, it is mitigable by flexibility elsewhere in the system, and it is a reason to count value and not a reason to stop building (Hirth).
