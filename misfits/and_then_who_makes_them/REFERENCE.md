# And Then Who Makes Them: Reference

The research warrant for And Then Who Makes Them.
The term is the house's; the play runs on second-order free-riding and on what happens when the repair for a
public good has the shape of a public good, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging enforcement as **a repair with the shape of the thing it repairs**: four flats share a stair and a rota
everybody agreed to, and the rota slips. The repair is that somebody mentions it. Mentioning it costs one
person a difficult conversation, a soured landing and possibly a year of small hostilities, and produces a
clean stair shared equally by everybody in the building including the three who said nothing. Cost
concentrated, benefit spread: that is a public good exactly, one level below the first. So nobody mentions it,
and nobody reproaches the one who said nothing, and there is no level at which that stops being true. The play
models the documented mechanics, the agreement that states an obligation and cannot impose one, the repair
priced privately and paid collectively, the second level identical to the first, the party that is both the one
declining and the one harmed, the punishing impulse that is real and bounded, and the institution that sits on
top of the structure rather than outside it, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of
apathy or of anybody taking advantage.

The governing law is that enforcement costs the enforcer and benefits everybody, so a sanctioning system is
itself a public good and under-supplies for the same reason the first one did, and the sanction on the
non-enforcer is a third with the same property.

**Declining to punish is exactly as rational as declining to contribute, and that is what puts this outside
anybody's conduct.** There is no free-rider here enjoying a benefit at somebody else's expense, or rather
everybody is one and each is also the injured party: the person who said nothing walks up the same dirty stair
as the rest. Nobody is exploiting anybody, nobody has been taken in, and every party wants the outcome while
every party's own move away from it is correct.

What removes any possibility of blame from the contributor is that keeping a bargain and the bargain working
are two different things. One corner held up on a thing that needs four is worth almost nothing, and the seat
has no instrument by which to obtain anybody else's compliance.

What removes it from the person who says nothing is that the calculation they are making is the calculation the
missed turns were made on, applied to the cure. Speaking is cheap to produce and expensive to have said, and
the thing it buys is shared with everybody who did not speak, so the reasoning is not timidity or indifference:
it is the same sound reasoning one level down, and it is available unchanged at every level beneath that.

And the recursion closes the case against a rule, because there is no level at which the calculation comes out
differently, so there is no level at which the arrangement identifiably breaks and none at which a rule could
usefully be placed. A fix applied anywhere needs the same missing thing at the level beneath it.

It is distinct from the house's **Borrowed Immunity**, which holds the vaccination free-rider and stages the
**first order alone**: there a person declines a small private cost because others have made the risk remote,
and the analysis stops at the contribution. This misfit takes the first order as given, since it is the setup
rather than the mechanism, and starts where that one ends, at the question of what would make anybody
contribute and what that costs whoever supplies it.

It is distinct from the house's **Everybody's Business**, which holds the bystander effect: there a duty is
**diffused** across a crowd and each person's sense of responsibility is thinned by the presence of others, so
the mechanism runs through attention and social inference and the repair is to name somebody. Here nothing is
diffused and nobody is confused about whose turn it is: the rota is on the board, every party knows exactly
what is owed and by whom, and each declines on a calculation that is sound and would survive being named
directly. Naming a bystander works; naming an enforcer transfers the private cost onto the named person and
gives them the same reason to decline.

It is distinct from the house's **The Commons**, which stages a shared resource depleted by uncoordinated use:
there the difficulty is the extraction and the design principles that answer it, monitoring and graduated
sanctions among them, are the answer. Here those principles are precisely what is being asked about. The
monitoring has to be done by somebody, the graduated sanction has to be applied by somebody, and this misfit
stages the cost of being that somebody, which the design principles assume is met and do not themselves supply.

It is distinct from the house's **Not Worth My While**, which holds Rose's prevention paradox, where a measure
worth a great deal to a population is worth almost nothing to any individual in it: there the asymmetry is
between the population and the individual on the **same** act, and both figures are correct. Here the cost and
the benefit of one act fall on different parties, and the difficulty is that the party bearing the cost is a
single identifiable person while the party receiving it is everybody.

## Origin

The result, the finding stated as a public good, the recursion named, the evidence that people punish anyway,
the bound on that evidence, and where the recursion actually terminates.

| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pamela Oliver**                              | "Rewards and Punishments as Selective Incentives for Collective Action" (American Journal of Sociology, 1980)                                                       | **The result**: selective incentives are themselves collective goods, so providing the rewards and punishments that solve a collective action problem raises a collective action problem of the same kind.                      |
| **Toshio Yamagishi**                           | "The Provision of a Sanctioning System as a Public Good" (Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 1986)                                                       | **The spine**: the sanctioning system analysed and tested as a public good in its own right, which is the statement the play is built on and the one that names the structure rather than describing it.                        |
| **Douglas Heckathorn**                         | the work on collective sanctions and compliance norms (1988 onward)                                                                                                 | **The recursion named**: the regress from first-order to second-order and beyond, and the conditions under which group-directed sanctions substitute for individual ones, which is where the structure is examined as a series. |
| **Ernst Fehr & Simon Gächter**                 | "Altruistic Punishment in Humans" (Nature, 2002) and the public-goods experiments with a punishment stage                                                           | **The evidence that people punish anyway**: subjects fining free-riders at their own expense far more than the calculation allows, with contributions sustained where punishment is available, which the play carries as true.  |
| **The decay of cooperation without sanctions** | the experimental finding that contributions fall over repeated rounds where punishment is withdrawn, and that second-order punishment is far rarer than first-order | **The bound on that evidence**: the impulse is real, is weaker one level down, and does not by itself close the regress, which is what keeps the finding a bound rather than a refutation.                                      |

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast anybody as
  apathetic or as taking advantage, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the rota states
  an obligation and contains no way of imposing one, so no better document would help; the contributor keeps its
  side and holds up one corner of a thing needing four, with no instrument for the rest; speaking costs the
  speaker privately and pays the building collectively, which is a public good one level down; the same
  calculation is available unchanged at every level beneath that; the party that declines walks up the same
  dirty stair, so it is the injured party too; people do punish at real cost and it is bounded; and the
  institution that would end it has to be convened, argued and agreed by the same group. The failure is
  authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the repair for an under-supplied
  public good is itself an under-supplied public good.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Paying a warden removes the
  recursion entirely and is the real answer, and somebody uncompensated has to obtain the quote, put the notice
  up, chair the room and be argued with, which is a public good of the same shape sitting on top of the one it
  repairs. Writing a stronger rule changes nothing, since the difficulty begins where the paper stops. Naming
  an enforcer transfers the private cost onto the named person and gives them the same reason to decline.
  Making the group smaller genuinely helps and is not always available. The punishing impulse is real and does
  not by itself reach the second order. And knowing the whole structure, which every party may, still leaves
  whoever speaks first paying for it alone.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the result about selective incentives, the analysis of the
  sanctioning system, the account of the regress, the punishment experiments and the self-governance findings
  are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **how far the regress binds in practice is genuinely
  contested**, since people punish at real cost more than the theory allows and many groups do sustain
  cooperation, which the play carries as a live finding rather than an obstacle to be argued away; the
  laboratory results are from particular designs, group sizes and populations and the magnitudes do not
  transfer unchanged; the self-governance findings show what communication and institutional design achieve and
  are not offered as evidence that the difficulty is illusory, since what they demonstrate is the cost of
  meeting it; the stair is a domestic instance chosen for clarity, and the structure is claimed at the level of
  the mechanism rather than of any particular setting; and treating the levels as discrete steps is the play's
  own simplification of pressures that in real groups are continuous and overlapping. **The play makes no claim
  that any group failed, or that people are less cooperative than they are**, only that enforcing a rule costs
  the enforcer and pays everybody, and that this is true of enforcing the enforcement as well.
