# Plot: Two Fewer Trains an Hour

## Taxonomy

[As Fast as the Train](play_as_fast_as_the_train.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

[Frequency Is Bought by Riders](piece_frequency_is_bought_by_riders.md): a farebox that has fallen, and a timetable change due.

## Action

[Thinning](process_thinning.md): [The Timetable](persona_the_timetable.md), in [the service seat](position_service.md), works through [Run What the Riders Pay For](plan_run_what_the_riders_pay_for.md).

## Stage

[The Platform](place_the_platform.md): a departure board with two fewer entries an hour, and a notice explaining why.

## Tension

This is the step that carries the whole harm and it is the step nobody would question.
Demand has genuinely fallen. The operator is required to cover its costs. Running the old frequency into
emptier carriages would be criticised by everyone who saw the accounts, and with waiting time counted in
the cost of a journey the cut is not merely defensible, it is **efficient**: fewer riders do justify
fewer departures.
And this is where the play turns, because of what frequency is. **The wait is not a delay before the trip,
it is a share of the trip.** So the service does not become smaller when departures come out, it becomes
**slower**, and what has been lowered is the door-to-door speed of the alternative.
[Nobody Cut the Service](piece_nobody_cut_the_service.md). The service followed the riders, the riders
followed the road, and the road was built by an authority with no standing over the trains and no way to
have foreseen a timetable that is not its to see.
