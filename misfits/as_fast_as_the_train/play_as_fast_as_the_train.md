# Play: As Fast as the Train

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

As Fast as the Train.
A lane is added to a congested road, and on the first morning the journey is quicker by exactly the number of minutes the appraisal promised.
Commuters who had been taking the train move to the road, because it is now the quicker way, which is true.
The train loses the fares that paid for its frequency, two departures an hour come out of the timetable, and the wait on the platform gets longer.
The road refills until driving is no better than the alternative. The alternative is now worse than it was before anybody touched anything.

## Arc

The bend is this: the road's speed is not set by the road, it is set by whatever else the commuter could have done, and the scheme spends its saving on making that worse.

It runs on one relation from equilibrium and one from operations. **Any advantage the road holds over the train recruits drivers until it is gone**, so the road settles at the alternative's door-to-door speed and cannot settle anywhere else; and **the alternative's speed depends on how many people are using it**, because waiting is part of a journey and frequency is bought with fares.

Take the appraisal first, because the play has to establish that nothing was miscalculated. The saving is real and it is delivered. The arithmetic is correct at the mode shares it assumed, the scheme opens, and the journey time falls. What the appraisal does not contain is a column for what happens to the trains, because the trains are somebody else's asset and the mode shares were an input rather than an outcome.

Then the fact that inverts it. **Ridership is not a payment for the service, it is part of the service.** More riders justify more departures, more departures shorten everybody's wait, and the whole thing runs in reverse just as readily: fewer riders, fewer departures, a longer wait, a slower door-to-door journey. So the drivers the new lane attracted did not simply leave the train behind. They lowered the speed that the road's own equilibrium is keyed to.

Then the sharper half, which is why the usual answers do not reach it. **Every step is somebody's correct decision and the outcome is nobody's.** The authority built what it was asked to build. The commuter took the quicker way on the day it was quicker. The operator matched the timetable to the demand that remained, which with waiting time in the cost is not a retreat but the efficient response. And the seat where the two journey times are equalised, which is the seat that decides the answer, has nobody sitting in it: no party owns the equilibrium, appraises it, or answers for it.

And nobody has done anything wrong. The engineer designed to standard. The committee approved a scheme with a positive appraisal. The commuter is not obliged to keep taking the slower option out of solidarity. The operator is not obliged to run empty trains.

Reach for a settlement and each one gives back the thing that was wanted. Subsidise the frequency, which is what the scale economies imply, and you have a permanent claim on a budget for a benefit that shows up as an absence. Give the new lane to the buses and you have built a different scheme and forfeited the saving the committee approved. Price the road, which reaches the equilibrium directly and is the oldest answer in the subject, and you have proposed the one thing that is hardest to get agreed. Appraise it with mode shares free instead of fixed, which is correct, and you have spent more to return a smaller number to a committee that still has to choose.

## Triggers

**[The Lane Opens](plot_the_lane_opens.md)**
The saving established as real: the scheme is appraised honestly, built to standard, and on the first morning delivers the minutes it promised. Closes when it is clear that nothing has been miscalculated and that the appraisal's mode shares were an input rather than a finding.

**[The Quicker Way](plot_the_quicker_way.md)**
Cue: a commuter with two options and one of them newly faster. Closes when the switch is seen to be individually correct, repeated by enough people to matter, and owed to nobody.

**[Two Fewer Trains an Hour](plot_two_fewer_trains_an_hour.md)**
Cue: the farebox, down. Closes when the timetable has been matched to the remaining demand, when that is understood to be the efficient response rather than a retreat, and when the wait on the platform has become part of a longer journey.

**[Worse Than Before](plot_worse_than_before.md)**
Cue: the road, refilling. Closes when the equilibrium has re-established itself against an alternative that is now slower, so the road settles below the speed it had before the lane was added, with no step in the sequence that anybody would undo.

**[And the Committee Wants the Time Saving](plot_and_the_committee_wants_the_time_saving.md)**
Cue: the settlements. Closes when each is found to give back what it was for: a subsidy that is permanent and invisible, a bus lane that is a different scheme, a road price that is the right answer and the hardest to agree, and an honest appraisal that costs more and returns less.

## Stakes

Whether capacity added to one mode is capacity added at all.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the scheme was wrong, because it delivered what it promised, and not whether anyone behaved badly, because nobody did, but whether a city with two modes can be improved one mode at a time. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the saving was real, that the switch was correct, that the timetable followed the riders, that the equilibrium is keyed to an alternative nobody was minding, and that every escape either costs a budget for ever or hands the committee a smaller number.
