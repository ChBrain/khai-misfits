## Load Bearing

The rise is not automatic. It requires overcompensating density-dependence, a population whose net production curve bends back on itself so that a thinned stock yields more recruits than an unthinned one, and it requires the mortality to fall on the crowded, self-limiting stage where that overcompensation operates (Abrams). Where either condition is absent, where the density-dependence is merely compensating rather than overcompensating, or where the cull removes animals from a stage the crowding does not regulate, the cull reduces the population as intended, which is the ordinary and far more common outcome. The condition is load bearing because it is the hedge written into the science itself: the hydra effect is a real and named result (Abrams), not a law that culling always backfires, and most culls work.

## Apparent

It reads as proof that culling is futile everywhere, that any removal will only breed back worse, a lesson overgeneralized past the narrow case it actually describes.
