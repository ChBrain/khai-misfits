# Right Apart, Wrong Together: Reference

The research warrant for Right Apart, Wrong Together.
The term is the house's; the play runs on galvanic corrosion between dissimilar metals, the mechanism set
out by the galvanic series in corrosion science and demonstrated at length by the Statue of Liberty's iron
armature against its copper skin, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging galvanic corrosion as **a harm that belongs to the joint and to neither part**: two metals are
chosen well, each for the property its own job needs, and where they touch in the presence of moisture they
form a cell, so the less noble of the two is consumed to preserve the other. The play models the documented
mechanics, the two selections that are each correct in isolation, the junction that neither material
possesses and that only the assembly creates, the electrolyte that the setting supplies at no cost to
anyone, the barrier specified against exactly this and outlived by the structure it protects, the
asymmetry by which one member dissolves and the preserved member is deformed by the product of the
dissolving, and the corrections that mean either taking the whole work apart or choosing something to
destroy on purpose forever, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a careless
designer.

The governing law is that the couple is a property of the pair and not of either metal. Copper does not
corrode iron and iron does not corrode copper: a difference in electrical potential, an electrical path
between them, and an electrolyte across them together make a cell, and remove any one of the three and
nothing happens at all. So there is no bad material anywhere in the assembly, and no test of either part on
its own that could have found the problem, because the problem does not exist in either part.

What makes it structural rather than an oversight is the Liberty case, where the designers **knew**.
Shellac-impregnated asbestos insulators were specified between the copper saddles and the iron armature,
for precisely this reason, by people who understood the mechanism. Over the following century the shellac
disintegrated and the asbestos went dry and porous, and salt-laden harbour air reached both metals. The
guard was correct, deliberate, and shorter-lived than the monument. Iron bars were found reduced to as
little as a third of their thickness, and the corrosion product, occupying more volume than the metal it
replaced, swelled some bars to twice their original size and jacked the copper skin outward from inside,
threatening the silhouette. The 1980s restoration replaced the puddled iron with stainless steel and put
back an insulating layer, now of a different material.

The inversion is deliberate elsewhere and is the same mechanism used on purpose: a sacrificial anode is a
less noble metal attached specifically so that it will be eaten instead of the structure, and it is
replaced on a schedule forever.

It is distinct from the house's **Belt and Braces**, where each added backup introduces coupling, shared
cause and shirking so that more redundancy lowers reliability: there the parts are alike and duplicated for
safety. Here nothing is duplicated and nothing is redundant, the two parts are different by design and both
are load-bearing, and their coupling is electrical rather than organisational.

It is distinct from the house's **The Load It Always Carried**, where every cycle passes every check and
the increments sum until the part fractures under a load that never changed: there the mechanism lives
inside one component and its history. Here the mechanism lives between two components and in neither, so
inspecting either part to any standard reveals nothing.

It is distinct from the house's **The Brittle Optimum**, where slack is trimmed as idle waste until the
system shatters on a shock: there something protective is removed. Here nothing is removed and nothing is
optimised away, two good things are simply put in contact.

It is distinct from the house's **Accidental Adversaries**, where two allies each make a locally safe move
that quietly harms the other until partners become rivals: there both parties act and act repeatedly. Here
neither metal does anything, and the process runs at a junction that requires no behaviour from either.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the designer
  as careless, and the Liberty record makes that refusal a matter of fact rather than of charity: the
  insulators were specified against exactly this mechanism, by people who understood it, and what failed
  was that the guard had a shorter life than the work. Nor is either metal at fault. Copper is right for a
  skin and iron is right for a frame, each selection is correct on its own terms, and no inspection of
  either part to any standard could find a problem that exists in neither. The failure is authored by
  nobody and emerges from the structure, a couple being a property of a pair, so the misfit is that
  choosing the best material for each job is the act that creates the cell.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Insulating works and is
  a consumable: any parting layer ages, and it ages inside the joint where nothing can be seen, so the
  remedy's failure is silent and its service life is the real design life of the assembly. Replacing the
  base member works and means taking the whole work apart, which for the Liberty case meant a national
  restoration project. And the sacrificial anode works by accepting the mechanism entirely, choosing
  something to be destroyed deliberately and replacing it for as long as the structure stands, which is not
  a cure but a subscription.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the electrochemistry, the survey, the metallurgical
  findings and the restoration are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: galvanic attack depends on the
  potential difference between the specific metals, on the area ratio between them, and on the conductivity
  of the particular electrolyte, so it is a designable and largely solved problem in modern practice rather
  than an inevitability. **The play makes no claim that dissimilar metals cannot be joined**, only that the
  harm belongs to the junction, which is the one place neither component's own specification describes.
