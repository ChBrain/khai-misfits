# Play: Right Apart, Wrong Together

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Right Apart, Wrong Together.
A large work is to be made of two materials. The outer sheet must be thin, formable, and able to stand a century of weather, so it is copper. The frame inside must be strong, stiff and affordable in quantity, so it is iron. Both choices are correct, and would be made the same way today by anyone specifying either part on its own. The sheet is fixed to the frame where they meet. And in the presence of moisture, at every point of contact, the two of them together are something neither of them is separately: a cell, with a current in it, quietly dissolving the iron to preserve the copper.

## Arc

The bend is this: a couple is a property of the pair, so the harm exists at the junction and in neither part.
It runs on both selections being right.
The One Who Chose Them specifies copper for the skin because copper is right for a skin, and iron for the ribs because iron is right for ribs. Test either to any standard, in any laboratory, for any duration, and each will pass, because the problem is not in either of them. There is no defective material anywhere in this assembly and no test of a component that could have found anything.
The Damp arrives free. Salt in the air off a harbour is not supplied by anyone, costs nothing, and is present every day. It is the third thing the cell needs, after a difference in potential and a path between the metals, and the setting hands it over without being asked.
So The Inner Rib gives way, at the touching places, and The Outer Sheet is preserved by the giving. That is what the cell does: the less noble member is consumed and the nobler one is protected by the consuming. Bars are found later reduced to as little as a third of their thickness.
And the designers knew. This is the part that decides the misfit. Insulators were specified between the saddles and the frame, of shellac-impregnated asbestos, for precisely this reason, by people who understood the mechanism exactly. What failed was not the knowledge. Over a century the shellac disintegrated and the asbestos went dry and porous, and the salt air came through. The guard was correct, deliberate, and shorter-lived than the thing it guarded, and it aged inside the joint where nothing could be seen.
Then the damage appears, and it appears on the wrong member. Rust occupies more room than the iron it replaces, so the wasting bars swell, some to twice their original size, and jack the copper outward from inside. The protected part is the part that goes out of shape. The Pushed Line on the surface is the first thing anyone can see, and it is the preserved member being deformed by the product of its own preservation.
And every correction is real and none is free. Insulate, and you have installed a consumable inside a joint, and its life is now the true design life of the assembly whether anyone says so or not. Replace the frame, and you are taking the entire work apart, which is what was eventually done. Or accept the mechanism outright and hang a sacrificial anode on it, choosing a member to be destroyed on purpose and replacing it on a schedule for as long as the structure stands, which is not a cure but a subscription.

## Stakes

Whether an assembly can be judged sound when every part in it is sound and the fault is in the fitting.
Every plot moves the same way: toward two selections that are each right, and away from any inspection that could catch what they make together, because inspection examines parts and this is not in a part. The production cannot make copper wrong for a skin or iron wrong for ribs, and it cannot keep the damp out of a harbour. What is left to fight over is not which metal was the mistake, neither was, but who owns the junction, and who is answerable for a guard whose failure is silent, internal, and slower than anyone's attention.
