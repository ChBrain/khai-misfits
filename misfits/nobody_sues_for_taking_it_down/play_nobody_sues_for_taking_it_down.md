# Play: Nobody Sues for Taking It Down

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Nobody Sues for Taking It Down.
A notice arrives in the right form, from an agent with authority, about material its sender sincerely believes infringes.
The host removes it inside the hour, which is what the statute asks of it and what its own protection depends on.
The material was a lawful quotation, and its author learns it is gone from an automated message after the fact.

## Arc

The bend is this: the host has two ways to be wrong, and only one of them can be used against it.

Leave infringing material up against a compliant notice and the safe harbour is at risk, and the safe harbour is the thing that makes holding other people's material possible at all. Take lawful material down in good faith and the statute says, in terms, that no claim lies for it. **The two errors are priced separately in the text**, so the answer to any notice that arrives in the right form is to act on it, and a competent operator reading its exposure correctly reaches that answer every time.

Take the host first, because the play has to establish that nothing failed there. The operator is solvent, well advised and entirely able. It is not frightened and it is not over-reading its position: it is reading it exactly. **Removing is the dominant move and would be the dominant move for anybody in that seat**, so nothing about the decision improves if you replace the person making it.

Then the sender, because the second obvious answer has to close as well. **The test is the form and the form was met.** A notice is compliant if it contains what the statute asks for, and nothing in the process asks whether the claim behind it is good. The studies of notices in practice report a substantial share raising questions while remaining formally correct, from automated senders and from small human ones in different ways, and neither population is being asked by anybody to be right.

Then the fact that makes it a structure and not a complaint. **The party who pays was not in the transaction.** The speaker was not notified before the material went, was not asked, and had no standing in the exchange between the two parties who decided. The cheap error is cheap to the decider and expensive to somebody who is not there, which is what lets it be made at volume without anybody behaving badly.

And the remedy the statute provides works, and prices itself in the wrong currency. A counter-notice restores the material after the statutory wait, and it asks the speaker for a name, an address, and a consent to be sued in a federal district court. **The party with the least at stake is asked to accept the most**, so the remedy that exists is the one least used, and its non-use looks from the outside like an admission.

Reach for a settlement and each one gives back the thing that was wanted. Read the merits, and the host takes on precisely the liability the safe harbour exists to remove. Penalise bad notices, and the standard reaches the sender who knew and leaves the sender who was merely wrong, which is the population producing the volume. Add human review, and it scales with the notices rather than with the errors. Restore first and ask later, and the exposure moves back onto the host and the safe harbour is in play again.

## Stakes

Whether lawful material can survive a complaint about it.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether anybody acted badly, because nobody did, and not whether the protection is worth having, because it is, but whether a decision whose two mistakes are borne by different people can come out any other way. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the notice was compliant, that the removal was correct, that the asymmetry is in the text, that the remedy asks the wrong party to pay for it, and that every settlement moves the price rather than removing it.
