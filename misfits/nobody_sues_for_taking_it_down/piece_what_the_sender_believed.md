# Piece: What the Sender Believed

## Taxonomy

[Nobody Sues for Taking It Down](play_nobody_sues_for_taking_it_down.md). The provision that punishes bad notices asks what the sender knew, so being wrong and being dishonest are priced differently.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

At [the claim seat](position_claim.md), where the cost of an error would have to land.
In the standard a court applies when a speaker sues over a notice.

## Load Bearing

Without it the sender is unaccountable and the play overstates its case.
It is load bearing because **the accountability that exists is real and reaches a different population
from the one producing the volume**: a knowing material misrepresentation is actionable, courts have read
the standard as subjective, and the sender who swept too wide in good faith is not reached by it, which is
most of the sending.

## Apparent

That there is no penalty for a bad notice.
There is one, it has been used successfully, and it answers dishonesty rather than error, so it prices the
rare case and leaves the common one.

## Yearbook

The misrepresentation provision and its judicial reading, including the holding that a sender must form a
good faith belief that considers whether the use is permitted, assessed by what the sender actually
believed.
