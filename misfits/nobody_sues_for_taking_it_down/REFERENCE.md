# Nobody Sues for Taking It Down: Reference

The research warrant for Nobody Sues for Taking It Down.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the statutory structure of the notice-and-takedown safe harbour
and on the empirical studies of how notices are actually sent and answered, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging over-removal as **a decision whose two mistakes are priced by different parties**: a host that
leaves infringing material up can lose the protection its whole business depends on, and a host that takes
lawful material down is expressly protected for doing so in good faith, so the two errors are not
comparable and the rational answer to any notice that arrives in the right form is to act on it. The play
models the documented mechanics, the immunity that attaches to removal, the safe harbour that attaches
only to acting expeditiously, the compliance test that reads the form of a notice rather than the merits of
the claim, the counter-notice that works and asks the speaker for an address and a consent to a federal
court, and the misrepresentation remedy whose standard is what the sender believed, as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a censorious platform or a bullying rightsholder.

The governing law is the shape of the statute rather than anybody's conduct: **the host is liable for one
kind of error and immunised for the other**, and the party who bears the cost of the immunised one was
never in the transaction. A notice arrives from a rights agent, in the right form, about material the
sender sincerely believes is infringing. The host removes it inside the hour. The material was a lawful
quotation. Nothing in that sequence is a failure by anybody in it.

What makes it structural is that the decision is **correct at every step for the party making it**. The
host is not being cowardly and is not over-reading its exposure: it is reading it exactly. Leaving material
up against a compliant notice puts the safe harbour at risk and the safe harbour is what makes hosting
other people's material possible at all. Removing lawful material carries no liability, by design and in
terms. So the queue is answered the only way a solvent, competent, well-advised operator can answer it, and
the volume ensures the answer is a rule and not a judgement.

What removes any possibility of blame from the sender is that **the form is the test and the form was met**.
A notice is judged by whether it contains what the statute asks for, not by whether the claim behind it is
good, and the studies of notices in practice report a substantial share raising questions about their
validity while remaining formally compliant. The sender is often sincere, frequently automated, and in
either case is not being asked by anybody to be right.

And the party who pays is **not in the room**. The speaker whose lawful work came down was not notified
before it went, was not a party to the exchange that removed it, and has a remedy that works and costs
something the other two parties are not asked to spend: a counter-notice restores the material after a
statutory wait, and it asks the speaker to give a name, an address, and a consent to be sued in a federal
court. The misrepresentation provision is real, and courts have read its standard as what the sender
actually believed rather than whether the claim was sound, so the remedy that would price the free error
reaches only the sender who knew.

It runs against the house's **Who Took It Out**, on the same axis and at the opposite end, and the two are
the same dial read from its two harmful sides. There a removal is the attributed act and a non-removal
leaves no record, so nobody signs for taking a control out and procedures accrete; here a removal is the
protected act and a non-removal is the exposed one, so everything complained of comes down. **What sets
the sign is where the cost of the cheap error lands.** In Who Took It Out the decider bears both errors and
only one of them can be pinned on them, so the attributable direction is the one avoided and what
accumulates is their own unnecessary procedure. Here the cheap error's cost falls **entirely outside the
transaction**, on a speaker who is neither the decider nor the complainant, so the cheap direction is taken
freely and what accumulates is other people's lawful material. Same asymmetry, opposite occupant of the
loss, opposite behaviour.

It runs against the house's **The High-Water Mark** in the same way and for the same reason. There a
crisis lifts a spending floor and the floor does not come back down, because the benefit is concentrated
in people who will appear and defend it while the cost is spread over people who will not notice, so
granting is cheap and cutting is dear. **The discriminator is unchanged**: there the party who would bear
the cheap error is also the party who decides, and the expensive direction is the one that touches
somebody who can object, so what accumulates is a commitment nobody will end. Here the cheap error is
cheap to the decider and expensive to a party with no standing in the decision at all, so nothing objects
and what accumulates is other people's removed material. The cost of the cheap error lands inside the
transaction in that play and outside it in this one, which is the whole of the sign.

It is distinct from the house's **And Then Who Makes Them**, which stages second-order free-riding, where
enforcing a rule costs the enforcer and nobody wants the job: there the difficulty is that the enforcement
is **undersupplied**, and stipulating an enforcer who is fully willing removes the harm. Stipulate that
here, a host entirely willing to act, and the harm is not removed but produced, because acting is exactly
what takes the lawful material down.

It is distinct from the house's **Nobody to Ask**, the orphan works structure, where the rightsholder
cannot be found and the use therefore cannot be cleared: there the missing party is the **owner** and the
harm is a use that never happens. Here the owner is present, identified and answered within the hour, and
the missing party is the **speaker**, whose material existed and was taken down.

It is distinct from the house's **You Would Have to Tell**, where a duty to report deters the disclosure
the duty was meant to capture: there the party who changes behaviour is the one the rule speaks to, and the
harm is a thing not said. Here nobody is deterred from speaking in the first instance; the material is
written, published and then removed by a third party acting correctly, and the speaker's silence afterwards
is a consequence rather than the mechanism.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the host as
  censorious or the sender as a bully, because neither is required and neither is typical. The host is
  reading its exposure exactly and would be negligent to read it any other way; the sender is often sincere
  and often automated, and is not asked by the statute to be right, only to be compliant in form; the
  speaker did nothing and was not consulted. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that **one of the two mistakes available to the decider is free to it and
  costly to somebody who is not there**.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into better behaviour. A more careful host reads the
  merits and takes on exactly the liability the safe harbour exists to remove, which is a business decision
  and not a legal one. A penalty on bad notices reaches the sender who knew and leaves untouched the sender
  who was wrong, which is the population that produces the volume. Human review works and scales with the
  notices rather than with the errors. Restoring first and asking later inverts the exposure onto the host
  and puts the safe harbour back in play. The counter-notice is the remedy the statute actually provides,
  it works, and it asks the speaker for a consent to a federal court, so the party with the least at stake
  in the transaction is asked to accept the most.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the statutory analysis and the empirical studies are
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them. **This is one jurisdiction's statute**, and other regimes price
  the same two errors differently, so the play is about the shape rather than about the drafting. **The
  studies measure notices and not outcomes**, since a notice that raises a question is not the same as a
  removal that was wrong, and the literature is careful about that distinction where popular accounts are
  not. **The magnitudes those sources report are deliberately not carried here**, because the works
  themselves could not be read in the pass that cited them: publisher hosts and the article archives are
  blocked to it, so the bibliography, the findings and their directions were verified by search and
  corroborated across independent reports, which is the treatment a citation survives and a reported figure
  is not.
