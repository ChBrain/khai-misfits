## Place

## Load Bearing

It is what a quote is actually made of, and it is used up by the very trades the quote invites.
Every purchase the quoter makes to accommodate a seller sits on it until somebody buys it back, and on a day of one-way flow nobody does. So capacity falls as demand for it rises, which is the second half of the mechanism and is independent of the first: even a quoter with no fear of being picked off runs out of room. It was measured in the deepest market there is, where dealer capacity declined exactly as the demand for it soared, and where liquidity was worse than volatility alone predicted once utilisation was high enough.

## Apparent

An internal constraint, of interest only to the firm that has it.

## Yearbook

Is the reason the quote thins in size as well as widening in price, and it empties fastest on the day it is most wanted.
