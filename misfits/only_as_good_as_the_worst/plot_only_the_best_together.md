# Plot: Only the Best Together

## Taxonomy

[Only as Good as the Worst](play_only_as_good_as_the_worst.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

[The Gate](place_the_gate.md), where a firm decides whom to admit to its high-value chain.

## Action

[The Firm](persona_the_firm.md), from [the firm's seat](position_firm.md), runs [No Weak Links](plan_no_weak_links.md) through [Matching](process_matching.md), taking [The Match](piece_the_match.md): admitting only high-quality inputs and pairing skill with skill, because a skilled worker adds far more value beside other skilled workers than beside a weak link who would multiply the whole chain down. Beside it, [The Skilled](persona_the_skilled.md), from [the skilled's seat](position_skilled.md), runs [Keep Good Company](plan_keep_good_company.md), seeking out the peers where its own quality is worth the most. The scene closes with high matched to high and low matched to low, each side sorting toward the pairing that maximises value.

## Stage

This is positive assortative matching: high pairs with high, low pairs with low, because pairing them this way maximises total output where the tasks are complements (Kremer; Becker). Becker's marriage market supplies the antecedent, complements sorting to complements; Kremer carries it into production, where a firm gains more from matching two skilled inputs than from spreading skill thin across two mixed teams. There is no snobbery in [The Firm](persona_the_firm.md)'s choice, only the value-maximising response to a product function.

## Tension

The sorting that makes the best worth the most together is the very same sorting that shuts everyone else out of the room where that value is made.
