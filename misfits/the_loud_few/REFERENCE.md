# The Loud Few: Reference

The research warrant for **The Loud Few**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon the literature
calls **the logic of collective action**, and in its policy form **concentrated benefits and diffuse
costs**: Olson's account of why a small group whose few members each hold a concentrated stake
organises and prevails while a large group whose many members each hold a diffuse stake stays latent
and unrepresented, with the free-rider logic of non-excludable goods beneath it (Samuelson) and the
selective incentive as the one lever that moves a large group, all cited below, held with the
finding that the failure is a tendency conditioned on group size, non-excludability, and the absence
of selective incentives, and not a law that no large group ever acts.

## Line of Work

Staging the logic of collective action as a **concentrated-few-beat-diffuse-many structure**: a benefit shared by
a group is a collective good, and no member can be shut out of it once it is provided, so each would rather let
the others bear the cost of providing it and enjoy it for free. Whether that free-riding sinks the group turns on
its size. In a large group each member's share of the benefit is thin and their own contribution imperceptible,
so the cost of acting outweighs the sliver it buys them and each rationally sits out, and the large group stays
latent, its shared interest real and unadvanced. In a small group at least one member's stake in the good is large
enough to be worth the whole cost of providing it alone, so the small group is privileged and acts. The
consequence is an asymmetry: where a policy concentrates its benefit on a few and spreads its cost over the many,
the few find it worth their while to organise and press for it while each of the many finds the small cost not
worth the trouble of resisting, so the organised few prevail over the unorganised many and outcomes tilt to
concentrated interests, though the diffuse majority would gain more in sum. The governing law is not that anyone
is corrupt or duped, but that **where a benefit is spread thin across a large group and the cost of providing or
defending it falls on each who would act, every member of the diffuse many has too small a share and too slight an
influence to justify the cost, so each rationally free-rides and the large group stays latent, while a small group
whose members each hold a concentrated stake finds it worth acting, so the organised few prevail over the
unorganised many and outcomes tilt to concentrated interests, unless the large group is bound by coercion or moved
by a selective incentive available only to those who contribute**. The play models the documented mechanics: the
non-excludable collective good each would free-ride on (Samuelson), the group-size logic by which a member's share
and influence shrink until acting no longer pays and the large group stays latent while the small one is
privileged (Olson), the selective incentive or coercion that alone mobilises a large group (Olson), and the
systematic tilt of policy toward concentrated interests over diffuse ones (Wilson), as the load-bearing vectors of
the structure, not as a tale of a greedy lobby buying a pliant official.

It is a collective-action asymmetry no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from The Commons, where each rationally takes from a shared rivalrous stock and the
stock is depleted: there the good is rival and the harm is over-use of a thing that exists, whereas here the good
is non-rival and non-excludable and the harm is the under-provision of a thing that is never made, and the
load-bearing vector is the size asymmetry that lets the few organise and leaves the many latent, not the
depletion of a common pool. It is distinct from Everybody's Business, the bystander effect, where a single visible
task is dropped because each onlooker assumes another will act: there the engine is the psychological diffusion of
responsibility over one discrete act in a moment, whereas here it is the economic calculus of stakes across group
size, a standing structure in which the many never form and the few reliably do, and the harm is a durable tilt in
outcomes, not one unowned task. It is distinct from Not Worth My While, Rose's prevention paradox, where a measure
that saves a population offers each individual almost nothing so each declines: there the benefit is symmetric and
genuinely negligible to everyone, whereas here the asymmetry is the whole point, the same benefit thin for the
many and concentrated for the few, so that the few act and the many do not, and the distinct load is the tilt that
asymmetry produces, not a benefit small to all alike. It is distinct from Borrowed Immunity, the vaccination
free-rider, where each rationally abstains from a costly act as the herd's coverage rises: there the population is
symmetric and the free-rider payoff climbs with others' contributions toward a threshold, whereas here the
load-bearing vector is the asymmetry between a concentrated few and a diffuse many and the selective incentive
that alone moves the many, not a rising payoff to abstain in a uniform crowd. It is distinct from Rent-Seeking,
where real resources are burned competing to capture a transfer: that is what the organised few do once mobilised,
whereas this is the upstream question of who mobilises at all, the asymmetry that decides the few show up and the
many stay home, so Rent-Seeking is the spending and this is the sorting that precedes it. And it is distinct from
The Iron Triangle's closed triad of mutual exchange and Bootleggers and Baptists's two-coalition alliance around
one rule, in that here no ring is closed and no second coalition is needed, only the free-riding of the many
against the concentrated stake of the few.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the concentrated few
  as corrupt or the diffuse many as dupes, because the source finding it stages is that the tilt follows from the
  asymmetry of stakes and not from malice: a member of the few with much at stake rationally organises, and a
  member of the many with little at stake rationally sits out, each responding soundly to the size of their share
  and the reach of their influence (Olson). The loss is authored by no one: it is the collective good the many
  never provide themselves and the tilt of outcomes toward the few, and the misfit is that the same free-rider
  logic that is individually rational for every member of the large group is what leaves the group latent and the
  field to the few.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The ways out exist, a selective
  incentive that gives contributors a private benefit the good itself does not, coercion or compulsory membership
  that removes the choice to free-ride, an entrepreneur who bundles the collective cause with private inducements,
  or an encompassing organisation broad enough to internalise the whole interest, but none is free or general:
  selective incentives are costly to supply and can capture the organisation for their own sake, coercion trades
  the free-riding for a loss of liberty, and the encompassing organisation is exactly what the asymmetry makes
  hardest to build. The reckoning is that the free-rider incentive is inherent in a non-excludable good shared by a
  large group, so wherever benefits are diffuse and costs to act concentrated, the latency and the tilt return.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the theory of groups, the group-size logic, the latent and privileged
  groups, the selective incentive, and the tilt to concentrated interests are Olson's, the non-excludable
  public-good free-rider is Samuelson's, credited as the antecedent Olson extends, and the concentrated-benefits,
  diffuse-costs typology is Wilson's, credited as the political reading. The mechanism is carried as carefully as
  the finding: that the failure is a tendency conditioned on group size, non-excludability, and the absence of
  selective incentives, that large groups do sometimes mobilise through moral entrepreneurs, mass movements, or
  falling coordination costs, and that the harm the play centres is the latency of the many and the tilt to the
  few, not a claim that no diffuse interest is ever organised. The scope is held to that tendency and not stretched
  into a law that collective action always fails.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Thin Share**: carries the group-size logic (Olson), that a member of a large group receives too small a
  share of the collective good and their own contribution is too imperceptible to change the outcome, so acting
  costs more than the sliver it buys and each rationally sits out.
- **The Free Ride**: carries the non-excludability (Samuelson), that no member can be shut out of the good once it
  is provided, so each would rather let the others pay and enjoy it for free, the incentive that leaves the good
  unprovided when everyone reasons the same way.
- **The Latent Group**: carries the large diffuse group (Olson), its shared interest real and unadvanced because no
  member's stake justifies the cost of organising it, the many who would gain in sum and never assemble to claim
  it.
- **The Privileged Few**: carries the small concentrated group (Olson), whose members each hold a stake large
  enough that at least one will bear the whole cost of acting, so the few organise where the many cannot.
- **The Selective Incentive**: carries the lever and the costly remedy (Olson), the private benefit or coercion
  separate from the collective good that alone moves a large group to act, dear to supply and prone to capture the
  organisation for its own sake.
- **The Tilt**: carries the harm (Wilson; Olson), the systematic bias of outcomes toward concentrated interests
  and against the diffuse majority, each narrow win rational for its winners and the accreted sum a drag no one
  chose.
