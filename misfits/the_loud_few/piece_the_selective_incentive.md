# Piece: The Selective Incentive

## Taxonomy

[The Loud Few](play_the_loud_few.md). The private inducement or coercion handed only to contributors, separate from the collective good itself, the one lever proven to move a large group.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

At [the Turnstile](place_the_turnstile.md), carried by [Sweetening](process_sweetening.md): a gate the collective good itself does not have, added on top of it so that paying and not paying finally lead somewhere different.
Membership perks, fines, dues that buy a private service, threats that fall only on holdouts: whatever the form, it is handed out by contribution, not by mere presence in the group.

## Load Bearing

Olson's selective incentive (Olson, _The Logic of Collective Action_, 1965): since the collective good alone cannot motivate a large latent group, a large group only organises when a further, excludable benefit or cost is attached to the act of joining, one that rewards the contributor and not the free rider.
Guard against reading it as a bribe or as free: it is a real, costly private benefit paid for by the organisation offering it, not a gift and not a wink to look past the rules. And because it is what actually holds the group together, it can outgrow its purpose: the organisation starts serving the sweetener, and the cause becomes a vehicle for it rather than the other way round.

## Apparent

It looks like an add-on, a nice perk stapled to a good cause, incidental to why people really joined.
That reading has cause and lever backwards: strip the sweetener out and, per the same logic that explains the many's absence elsewhere, the group would not be there to enjoy the cause at all.
