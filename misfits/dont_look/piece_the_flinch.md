# Piece: The Flinch

## Taxonomy

[Don't Look](play_dont_look.md). The anticipated pain of learning, felt before the news is even seen: the disutility that turns a free look into a costly one.

## Load Bearing

Learning bad news carries a real psychological cost: the bad feeling itself, the pressure it puts on the reader to act, and the plain fact that once a thing is known it cannot be unknown again. So information that costs nothing to obtain is not free to receive; the price is paid in the reading, not the getting (Golman, Hagmann and Loewenstein). This is why the flinch tends to strike hardest just where the stakes are highest, and just when the unwelcome feeling would be sharpest, the demand to change most pressing, and the knowing least undoable (Sweeny and colleagues). The Flinch is not weakness. It is the ordinary cost of knowing, felt in advance.

## Apparent

It looks like sensible calm, the composure of someone who refuses to fret over every dip and every wobble. Nothing about it reads as fear; it reads as good sense.
