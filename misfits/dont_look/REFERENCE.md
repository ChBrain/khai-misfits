# Don't Look: Reference

The research warrant for **Don't Look**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon named the
**ostrich effect**: where an agent can look at its own situation for little or nothing but the news
is likely to be bad, it avoids looking, to spare itself the pain of learning it, so it watches least
exactly when the news matters most, all cited below, held with the finding that the tendency is real
but of varying and debated magnitude, not universal, since some watch more under stress and not
less, and not all not-looking is an ostrich flinch.

## Line of Work

Staging the ostrich effect as an **information-avoidance structure**: an agent has, to hand and at little or no cost,
information about its own state, the portfolio balance, the test result, the meter, the account, and could simply look. But
the news is likely to be bad, and learning bad news hurts, so the agent looks away: it does not open the statement, does not
log in, leaves the envelope sealed, and the not-looking is not laziness but a flinch from an expected blow. The choice is
locally rational in its own coin: it buys a real and immediate comfort, the pain of the bad news deferred, and it costs
nothing that is felt now. But what it defers is not the bad news itself, only the knowing of it, and while the eye is
averted the problem it could have acted on goes unwatched and often grows, so that attention is lowest exactly when it would
matter most, and the news arrives in the end regardless, larger for the waiting. The name is Galai and Sade's, coined for
investors who prefer not to receive information about their losses; the sharpest evidence is Karlsson, Loewenstein and
Seppi's, that people check their portfolios far less often when markets are falling than when they are rising, monitoring
that tracks not how much the news matters but how much it is expected to hurt. It is authored by no villain: the avoider is
neither lazy nor foolish, only human in flinching from a likely blow, and everyone does it. The governing law is not that
anyone is careless, but that **where an agent can obtain information about its own situation at little or no cost but the
news is likely to be bad, it avoids the information to spare itself the anticipated pain of learning it, so it monitors least
exactly when the news matters most, and a problem it could still act on goes unwatched while it remains addressable, the
avoidance locally comforting and globally costly, no villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the free
information whose news is likely bad (Galai and Sade), the anticipated pain that makes free information not free to receive
(Golman, Hagmann and Loewenstein), the averted eye that withdraws attention just as the news turns bad (Karlsson,
Loewenstein and Seppi), the unwatched stretch where the problem grows still addressable, the reckoning that comes larger for
the delay, and the forced look that alone breaks it, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a
sluggard who would not do their sums.

It is an information-avoidance-about-oneself structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Cheaper Not to Know, where the cost of acquiring information outweighs what it could change,
so declining to learn is a sound cost-benefit call: there the information is expensive to get and would not sway the
decision, whereas here the information is free and to hand and would help, and it is avoided not because learning costs but
because the news itself hurts, the pain of knowing and not the price of finding out. It is distinct from Plausible
Deniability, where an agent arranges not to know so that blame cannot be pinned on them, the ignorance a shield held up to
others: there the not-knowing is strategic and faces outward, guarding the agent against a judge, whereas here there is no
audience and no blame to dodge, the avoidance spares only the avoider's own feelings and harms the avoider themselves,
ignorance kept from oneself and not from a court. And it is distinct from Once Bitten, where a party shuns an option that
once burned it, the wary hand kept from the stove: there what is avoided is a choice or an action, whereas here what is
avoided is information about one's own state, the account not opened and the result not read, a fact declined and not an
option refused.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the one who looks away as
  lazy or foolish or in denial, because the source finding it stages is that avoiding painful news is a real and
  understandable comfort, a flinch from an expected blow, and one that nearly everyone reaches for. The avoider gains
  something genuine by not looking, the present pain of the bad news spared, and pays nothing that is felt in the moment.
  The loss is authored by no one: it is the problem that grew unwatched because looking hurt, the trouble still addressable
  while the eye was turned away, and the misfit is that the very moment the news matters most is the moment it is hardest to
  face.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, to commit in advance
  to look, to make the monitoring automatic so the news arrives whether or not it is sought, to make not-looking harder than
  looking, but it is not free and not always right: forcing attention onto bad news one can do nothing about is distress for
  no gain, some news is genuinely better not known, and the pull to look away returns each time the news turns bad. The
  reckoning is that the comfort of not-looking is real and immediate while its cost is deferred and diffuse, so the flinch
  will keep winning wherever the looking is left to the moment.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the effect and its name are Galai and Sade's, the selective-attention evidence is
  Karlsson, Loewenstein and Seppi's, the information-avoidance frame is Golman, Hagmann and Loewenstein's, and the
  psychology of when the eye turns away is Sweeny and colleagues'. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: the
  tendency is real but of varying and debated magnitude, and not universal, since some investors watch their portfolios more
  under stress and not less, the "meerkat" to the ostrich, and not all information avoidance is an ostrich flinch, some
  being the sound rational ignorance the house stages separately where the news is not worth its cost or cannot be acted on.
  The scope is held to freely available information about one's own likely-bad situation, and not stretched into a claim that
  all not-looking is avoidance or that ignorance never pays.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Ledger**: carries the setup (Galai and Sade), the information free and to hand whose news is likely bad, the account
  you could open, the result you could read, no cost to look but a blow expected.
- **The Flinch**: carries the engine (Golman, Hagmann and Loewenstein), the anticipated pain the bad news carries before it
  is even seen, the disutility that makes free information not free to receive and turns the eye away.
- **The Averted Eye**: carries the behaviour (Karlsson, Loewenstein and Seppi), the act of not-looking, the login not made
  and the statement left sealed, monitoring withdrawn just as the news turns bad.
- **The Blind Spell**: carries the harm in the making, the stretch the problem goes unwatched while it is still addressable,
  attention lowest exactly when it would matter most, the unmonitored trouble free to grow.
- **The Reckoning**: carries the cost realised (Sweeny and colleagues), the bad news arriving in the end regardless and
  larger for the delay, the present pain spared bought at the price of a worse one later.
- **The Forced Look**: carries the remedy and the bound, the commitment to look or the monitoring made automatic so the news
  comes whether sought or not, costly and not always right, and the marker of the hedge, since the tendency is real but not
  universal, some watch more under stress, and where the news is unactionable or truly better unknown the flinch is no error.
