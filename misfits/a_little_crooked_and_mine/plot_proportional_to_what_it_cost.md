# Plot: Proportional to What It Cost

## Taxonomy

[A Little Crooked, and Mine](play_a_little_crooked_and_mine.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The pattern plain across many builds, and the question of how far it truly reaches.

## Action

At [The Shelf](place_the_shelf.md), [The Witness](persona_the_witness.md) from [the witness's seat](position_witness.md), on [Hold the True Proportion](plan_hold_the_true_proportion.md) through [Comparing](process_comparing.md), holds [The Difficulty](piece_the_difficulty.md): the pattern held to its true scope, no wider than the evidence allows.

## Stage

The effect varies with task difficulty and is roughly proportional to the labor felt as meaningfully one's own, stronger where the building demanded more of the builder and weaker where it demanded little, so the pattern is a documented and variable tendency and not a claim that all effort breeds attachment regardless of outcome (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012; the psychological-ownership and effort-justification literature that followed). No one across this whole account has acted badly. The Builder felt an honest pride earned by real labor, the Appraiser offered an honest price for exactly what could be seen, the Abandoner set the task aside for any ordinary reason and felt nothing extra for it, and the Witness holds the pattern to no more than the evidence shows. This is distinct in full from a sibling misfit called Loath to Part, which stages the endowment effect, where mere possession, holding a thing simply because it is now one's own, lifts its value above what any buyer will pay, no labor of any kind required, an owner of a mug or a ticket valuing it more than a non-owner from the moment of acquisition alone. Here, in A Little Crooked, and Mine, the inflation comes specifically from the effort of construction, and possession by itself is not enough to produce it: a builder who abandons the task partway possesses the unfinished object exactly as long and exactly as fully as a builder who finishes it, yet shows none of the extra inflation the finisher shows, so the value added is the labor completed and not the holding. Put the cases side by side on the Shelf and the shape of the pattern is visible in full: proportional to the effort felt as one's own, absent where the effort never closed, and never a matter of simple possession or time. A thing can be worth everything to the one who made it, and still be worth, to everyone else, only what it looks like it is worth.

## Tension

A pattern real in its cases, and proportional to what it actually cost to make.
