# A Little Crooked, and Mine: Reference

The research warrant for **A Little Crooked, and Mine**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon
named the **IKEA effect**: people assign disproportionately higher value to things they partially
built or assembled themselves, even when the finished result is objectively no better than a
comparable pre-made version, all cited below, held with the finding that the effect requires a
completed or successfully assembled build and varies with task difficulty, not a claim that effort
always breeds attachment regardless of outcome.

## Line of Work

Staging the IKEA effect as a **labor-inflates-value structure**: a person undertakes to build or assemble something
themselves, a piece of furniture, a folded paper crane, a set of building blocks, and invests real effort in the task,
honestly engaged and not performing the labor for show. The building itself is the mechanism: the labor generates a
felt sense that the finished object is meaningfully one's own, produced by one's own hands and not merely acquired, and
this felt ownership inflates the object's value in the builder's own eyes independent of the object's objective quality,
non-experts regarding their own imperfect, even crooked, creations as comparable in worth to an expert's work (Norton,
Mochon and Ariely, "The IKEA Effect: When Labor Leads to Love", 2012). The inflation is not a claim about taste or a
failure of judgment; the builder is not wrong that the object means more to them, only mistaken in expecting that
meaning to be shared by anyone who did not build it. When the object meets an outside eye, a buyer, a stranger, an
appraiser with no stake in the labor that made it, the plain judgment lands far below the builder's own, and the trade
both sides would gain from, the builder wanting to sell and a buyer willing to pay a fair market price, does not happen,
since the builder's honestly felt price sits above what any buyer, feeling none of the labor, will pay (Norton, Mochon
and Ariely, 2012). The mechanism is bounded in a way that sharpens what it is: an abandoned build, begun but never
finished, shows none of the inflation, so the effect is not the mere passage of time spent on a task or simple exposure
to it, but requires a completed, successfully assembled object the builder can call finished and their own (Norton,
Mochon and Ariely, 2012). And the effect is not uniform: it varies with task difficulty and is roughly proportional to
the labor felt as meaningfully one's own, stronger where the building demanded more of the builder and weaker where it
demanded little (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012; the psychological-ownership and effort-justification literature that
followed). The governing law is not that people are irrational about what they make, but that **labor invested in
building something generates a felt ownership that inflates the builder's own valuation of the finished result,
independent of its objective quality, so a trade both sides would gain from can go unmade because only one side felt
the labor that set the price, no deception or poor taste required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the
labor honestly invested, the felt ownership it generates, the outside verdict that judges the same object plainly, the
impasse that follows when the two valuations cannot meet, the unfinished build that shows the effect requires
completion, and the true proportion of the effect, tracking task difficulty and not a flat, universal inflation, as
the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a builder too proud to see their work clearly.

It is a labor-inflates-value structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest neighbour.
It is distinct from Loath to Part, where mere possession, holding a thing simply because it is now one's own, lifts its
value above what any buyer will pay, no labor of any kind required, an owner of a mug or a ticket valuing it more than
a non-owner from the moment of acquisition alone: there the inflation is possession's alone, present the instant a
thing changes hands, with no construction, effort, or completion required to trigger it. Here the inflation comes
specifically from the effort of construction, and possession by itself is not enough to produce it: a builder who
abandons the task partway, and so possesses the unfinished object exactly as long and exactly as fully as a builder who
finishes it, shows none of the extra inflation the finisher shows, so the value added is the labor completed and not
the holding. The two effects can compound, a person who both builds and keeps a thing may show both possession's
ordinary lift and labor's added one, but they are separable in their own controlled comparisons, and the house stages
the labor and not the mere possession.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the builder as vain or the
  appraiser as unkind, because the source finding it stages is that both are honest: the builder's valuation is
  sincerely felt, generated by real labor and not manufactured for advantage, and the appraiser's plain judgment is not
  coldness but an honest read of an object it did not build. No one inflates a price to deceive and no one lowballs out
  of spite; the impasse is authored by no one, the predictable cost of a value that only one side's labor produced.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a fix that closes the gap. The remedy, an outside appraisal
  taken honestly, does not make the trade happen, since the builder's felt value is real and does not simply yield to
  being told it is not shared; and the boundary condition, that an unfinished build shows none of the inflation, is not
  a trick a builder can use to keep their valuation in check, since the whole point of building is to finish.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the case, the mechanism, and the documented bound belong to Norton, Mochon and
  Ariely, and the deeper theoretical grounding for why labor and not mere time or exposure carries the inflation
  belongs to the wider psychological-ownership and effort-justification literature. The mechanism is carried as
  carefully as the finding: this is an effect that requires a completed build and varies with task difficulty, and the
  house holds that not all effort breeds attachment regardless of outcome, only the effort that is felt as meaningfully
  one's own in something finished.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Labor**: carries the setup (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012), the effort honestly invested in building or
  assembling something, engaged for its own sake and not performed for show.
- **The Ownership**: carries the mechanism (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012), the felt sense that the finished object
  is meaningfully one's own because of the labor that made it, inflating its value independent of objective quality.
- **The Verdict**: carries the outside read (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012), an appraiser or buyer with no stake in
  the labor judging the same object plainly, at what it is objectively worth.
- **The Impasse**: carries the harm (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012), the trade both sides would gain from going
  unmade, since the builder's honestly felt price sits above what any buyer, feeling none of the labor, will pay,
  drawn distinct from Loath to Part's possession-alone inflation.
- **The Unfinished**: carries the bound's sharpest edge (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012), an abandoned build showing
  none of the inflation, since the effect requires a completed, successfully assembled object and not mere time spent
  on the task.
- **The Difficulty**: carries the hedge (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012; the psychological-ownership and
  effort-justification literature that followed), the effect's true proportion, varying with task difficulty and
  roughly tracking the labor felt as meaningfully one's own, not a flat or universal inflation.
