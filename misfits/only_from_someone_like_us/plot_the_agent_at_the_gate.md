## Tension

The officer is the only party in the play whose actual job is persuasion, and is the party least able to
perform it.
Every qualification for the post is a distance. The training, the degree, the salary from an institution,
the car, the accent from three counties over: each is reasonable, each is why they were appointed, and
each is a dimension on which they are not one of the people in front of them.
So **improving the appointment makes it worse.** A better-trained officer, a more fluent speaker, a larger
budget, all move the post further from the channel that carries, and none of them is a mistake anybody
could be told to stop making.
And the officer knows the agronomy better than anybody in the field. That is not the difficulty. The
difficulty is that the room accepts knowledge from anywhere and accepts a decision from one person, and
the officer cannot be that person by being better at this.
