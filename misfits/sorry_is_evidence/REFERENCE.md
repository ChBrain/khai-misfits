# Sorry Is Evidence: Reference

The research warrant for Sorry Is Evidence.
The term is the house's; the play runs on why harmed patients litigate, the admissibility of apology and
explanation, the measured effect of apology statutes, and the communication and resolution programmes that
pair disclosure with compensation, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the withheld explanation as **a request that costs nothing in money and everything in liability**:
a person harmed by an institution asks what happened, whether anybody is sorry, and whether it will happen
to the next person, and that is the one thing the institution cannot give them, because an account of what
went wrong is an account of fault. The play models the documented mechanics, the request that is mostly not
about money, the clinician who is willing and is not the constraint, the counsel whose advice is accurate
and is the whole of the obstruction, the litigation used as an information service because nothing else
compels an answer, the statute that protects the sentence and cannot protect the fact, and the programme
that works by paying, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of callous institutions
or opportunistic claimants.

The governing law is that the explanation and the admission are the same sentence. There is no form of
words that conveys what happened without conveying who is answerable for it, so any instrument that makes
the telling safe must either withhold the content or absorb the liability.

What removes the easy reading is that the willing party is not the constraint. The clinician who was in the
room usually wants to explain and often carries the event worse than anybody notices, and removing every
reluctance they might hold changes nothing at all, because the obstruction is the admissibility of what
they would say. Nor is counsel behaving badly: the advice that an account of fault is admissible is simply
correct, and the duty runs to the institution and to the fund that every future claimant draws on.

What makes it a loop rather than a hardship is where the silence leads. When claimants are studied rather
than assumed, the reasons they give are the need for an explanation, a desire that others be protected, a
sense of accountability, and compensation, and the decision to take action turns not only on the original
injury but on insensitive handling and poor communication afterwards, with fewer than fifteen per cent of
the explanations that were given considered satisfactory. So the advised silence produces the claim that
the advice exists to prevent, and litigation becomes the only mechanism in the system capable of compelling
an account.

And the obvious statutory repair reaches the wrong half. Apology laws make expressions of sympathy
inadmissible so that saying sorry is safe, and the measured effect across ninety per cent of single
specialty physicians over eight years is that on balance they increase liability risk: no substantial
effect for surgeons, and for non-surgeons a higher probability of facing a claim and a higher average
payment. The mechanism identified is that an apology conveys information the patient did not have, so the
knowledge rather than the admissibility produces the claim. No drafting could have fixed this, because the
information is what the apology is for.

It is distinct from the house's **Saving Face**, where a courteous team keeps every mistake unspoken so it
fixes what it can name and never learns what it cannot say: there the silence is internal, cultural, and
costs the organisation its own learning, and a braver culture would dissolve it. Here the silence is
external, legally advised, and the willing speaker is already present: courage is not the missing
ingredient, and adding it changes nothing.

It is distinct from the house's **Plausible Deniability**, where a principal wants a deed, will not order
it and arranges not to know, so blame cannot reach them: there ignorance is manufactured deliberately to
defeat responsibility. Here nobody arranges not to know. The knowledge is complete, held by people who want
to share it, and what is withheld is its transmission rather than its acquisition.

That cut is on the engine and not on the quantity, so the two share the axis `blame-attachment`, and this
play is its fourth member. **The dial in all four is how readily answerability attaches to whoever can be
shown to have known, decided or said**, and what differs is what a party builds against it. A clinician
builds a record so no fault can attach. A principal builds an ignorance so no knowledge can. A body builds
a venue so no stated position can. **Here nothing is built at all**, which is what makes this the mildest
and the hardest of the four: the knowledge is complete, the speaker is willing, the hearer is waiting, and
counsel's advice is correct, so the only thing standing between them is that saying it would make it
admissible.

It is distinct from the house's **The Frozen Middle**, where a bold change is softened on the way down and
the hard truth smoothed on the way up: there the message degrades as it passes through layers, each of them
adjusting it. Here the message does not degrade or pass through anybody. It is complete, accurate, and
simply not delivered.

It is distinct from the house's **Nothing to Lose**, where an actor whose harm exceeds its assets treats
the excess as free and under-guards: there the incentive is to cause harm cheaply. Here the harm has
already occurred, the institution would often rather resolve it, and the obstruction is to the remedy
rather than to the precaution.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the
  institution as callous or the claimant as opportunistic, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to
  get wrong: the harmed party is asking for an explanation and mostly not for money; the clinician wants to
  give it and is frequently the person most distressed by the event; counsel's advice that an account of
  fault is admissible is simply accurate, and the duty genuinely runs to the institution and to the fund
  future claimants depend on; the legislature that passed the apology statute identified the problem
  correctly and legislated at it directly. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that the explanation and the admission are one sentence, and no arrangement
  of willing people can separate them.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Apology statutes make
  candour safe in form and leave it fully consequential in substance, and the measured result is an
  increase in liability risk rather than a reduction. Protecting the internal incident report so that staff
  will write frankly is right, and it is the same protection that keeps the frank account from the family
  who asked. Communication and resolution programmes do reduce litigation and require an institution to be
  its own tribunal, to decide its own liability without one, and to pay some claims that would never have
  been brought, out of a fund never sized for them.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the studies, the statutes and the programmes are
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the 1994 study is of claimants rather than of all harmed
  patients, so it describes the reasons of people who did sue and not the counterfactual of those who did
  not; apology statutes vary considerably in scope, with some protecting only sympathy and others also
  covering admissions of fault, and the measured effects differ accordingly, so the finding quoted here is
  not a claim about every such law; the evidence on communication and resolution programmes comes largely
  from institutions that chose to adopt them, which limits what can be concluded about programmes imposed
  elsewhere; duties of candour now require disclosure in several systems, which changes the position
  materially where they apply; and the settings differ enough between jurisdictions that the mechanism
  transfers better than the numbers do. **The play makes no claim that institutions should not take legal
  advice**, only that a system in which the courtroom is the only place an answer exists will produce more
  courtrooms.
