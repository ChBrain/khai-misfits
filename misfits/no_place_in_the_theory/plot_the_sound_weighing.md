# Plot: The Sound Weighing

## Taxonomy

[No Place in the Theory](play_no_place_in_the_theory.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The anomalous claim reaches the Society's judgment, set beside a theory with a great weight of confirmed prediction behind it.

## Action

At [the Society](place_the_society.md), [the Skeptic](persona_the_skeptic.md) from [the skeptic's seat](position_skeptic.md) on [Trust the Weight](plan_trust_the_weight.md) through [Weighing](process_weighing.md) takes up [the Sound Default](piece_the_sound_default.md), weighing the anomaly against the theory's accumulated confirmed prediction.

## Stage

The Skeptic compares the surprising claim to the theory's long accumulation of confirmed prediction and judges the established account more credible, the ordinary weighing by which a mature paradigm absorbs a strange result without discarding what it has earned (Kuhn). This is the right default in the overwhelming majority of cases: most claims that contradict a well-supported theory really are mistaken, artifacts of a flawed study or a coincidence read as a pattern, and a field that abandoned a good theory at every surprising result would abandon good theories constantly (the philosophy and sociology of science literature). The Skeptic applies this rule to the anomaly exactly as it would apply it to any other unexplained claim, without singling it out and without extra suspicion, only the standard measure a mature discipline uses on every result that arrives without a recognized mechanism. No villain: the weighing is sound by the field's own best methods and correct nearly every time it is used, so applying it here is diligence, not closed-mindedness. What the weighing cannot do, from inside a single application, is tell this claim apart from the many others the same rule rightly turns away.

## Tension

A correct method about to meet its rare wrong case. The Sound Default is right almost every time it is used, and using it here is about to be the exception it was never built to catch.
