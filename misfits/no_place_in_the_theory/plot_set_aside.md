# Plot: Set Aside

## Taxonomy

## Owner

## Cue

The weighing done, and a verdict following from it: the anomaly rejected, its discoverer discredited.

## Action

At [the Society](place_the_society.md), [the Skeptic](persona_the_skeptic.md) from [the skeptic's seat](position_skeptic.md) through [Weighing](process_weighing.md) meets [the Rejection](piece_the_rejection.md), the anomaly set aside and its discoverer discredited among peers.

## Stage

The weighing completes and its verdict follows: the anomaly is set aside, the practice unadopted, and the one who found it discredited among peers who judge the finding an error dressed as a result. Semmelweis and his chlorinated hand wash met exactly this reception, doubted and largely dismissed by the medical establishment of his day, not from malice but from a correct-seeming inference applied to a claim that happened, this once, to be true (the historical record). The inference is not different in kind from the one that rightly turns away the great majority of anomalous claims a field encounters: it is the same reasoning, arriving this time at the wrong case. No villain: the rejection is authored by no one in particular, no conspiracy and no refusal born of pride, only the predictable cost of a sound general policy meeting its rare true exception. The Skeptic has not erred by the standards that usually serve the Society well, and the Discoverer has not exaggerated a claim to provoke this reception; the outcome follows cleanly from two honest positions and one correct-seeming rule.

## Tension

A truth turned away by reasoning that was, by every ordinary measure, right.
