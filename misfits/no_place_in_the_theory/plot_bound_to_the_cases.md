# Plot: Bound to the Cases

## Taxonomy

[No Place in the Theory](play_no_place_in_the_theory.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The pattern plain across the historical record, and the question of how far it truly reaches.

## Action

At [the Society](place_the_society.md), [the Historian](persona_the_historian.md) from [the historian's seat](position_historian.md) through [Reading](process_reading.md) meets [the Conditions](piece_the_conditions.md), the scope of the pattern held to documented cases of genuine anomaly.

## Stage

The Historian holds the pattern to its true scope: a documented sequence traced through specific historical cases of genuine anomaly, Semmelweis's discovery chief among them, and not a measured general rate at which theories wrongly reject true findings (the historical record). The same sound default that occasionally buried a true discovery rightly rejects the great majority of anomalies that really are error, so the pattern is not a license to doubt every consensus that turns away a surprising claim (Kuhn; the philosophy and sociology of science literature). No villain: the Theory earned its standing honestly on a long accumulation of confirmed prediction, the Discoverer reported an honest and repeatable finding, the Skeptic reasoned soundly by a rule that ordinarily serves the field well, and the Historian records the truth of it without turning the exception into an indictment of the caution around it, every actor doing well by the standards available at each moment. The pattern held here is distinct from On Good Authority, where a weak or unsupported claim, cited and re-cited in good faith, accretes an authority the evidence never gave it, a claim over-accepted into the look of settled fact, while here a claim with genuine support, a repeatable and true finding, is under-accepted for lacking a place in the reigning theory, the mirror image of the same trust in the record, misapplied to opposite effect; and it is distinct from Too Sure to Be True, where an improbable agreement among many independent observers indicts a shared hidden bias behind their unanimity, while here the signal is a single dissenting finding rejected by an otherwise reasonable consensus, no improbable agreement to question and no hidden common error to allege.

## Tension

A pattern real in its documented cases, and not a license to doubt every consensus.
