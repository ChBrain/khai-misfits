# It Has to Start Somewhere: Reference

The research warrant for It Has to Start Somewhere.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the default effect and on the finding that no arrangement of a
question is neutral, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging choice architecture as **an act with no null option in it**: a form has to be printed, and the box on it
is either ticked or it is not. Whichever way it goes to press, the answers come back different, from the same
people, with the same stated views, freely given and with nothing hidden. There is no third way to print it,
and the arrangement that looks like no arrangement is one of the arrangements. The play models the documented
mechanics, the initial state that must hold some value before anybody arrives, the effect that is larger than
the policy it sits beneath, the forced choice that is a design of its own, the running order that survives the
removal of the setting, the person who leaves a box as it came and has chosen nothing wrongly, and the appeal
to evidence that runs through the arrangement it would judge, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not
as a tale of anybody setting out to steer anybody.

The governing law is that a question has to arrive in some condition, every condition moves the answer, and
there is no null condition to fall back on, so the seat chooses which influence and never whether to have one.

**Nothing is concealed, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The box is visible, the
alternative is printed beside it, the change costs a moment, and the person is free to make it. This is not a
mechanism that operates by hiding an option or by pressing anybody, which is why it survives full disclosure:
telling somebody that the default is a default does not supply them with a preference about the question, and
where they have none there is nothing for the disclosure to release.

What removes any possibility of blame from the registrar is that abstention is not on the list. Presuming one
way, presuming the other and demanding an answer all produce different distributions, and there is no fourth
condition, so a request for a form that leaves the question entirely to the public is a request for an object
nobody has ever printed. The seat cannot be relieved by being more careful, because care selects among the
arrangements rather than escaping them.

What removes it from the person filling it in is that what is missing is a preference and not attention. A
setting an official body chose reads as a recommendation and frequently is one; the effort of forming a view
exceeds the stake on most of the questions a form asks; and on a question they have no view about, leaving the
answer where it is reports that accurately. No degree of literacy, care or time would produce a different
return.

And the legislator closes the case against the last party, because silence is not available there either. A
statute that says nothing about the setting leaves it to whoever prints the form, which is a decision about who
decides rather than an abstention, so a body is obliged to fix in law a thing that ought to have been nobody's
business, and every available wording moves the outcome by more than most of the policy the bill is about.

It is distinct from the house's **An Angle and a Count**, which is the nearest structure in the house and is
named in this misfit's own slate line for that reason. There two requirements are both correct and conflict, so
a seat must break one and the whole difficulty is which. Here nothing conflicts. There is one requirement, that
the question be put, and the difficulty is that no way of putting it is neutral, so the seat is not adjudicating
between goods but selecting from a list that has no zero on it. One misfit is a conflict without a resolution;
this one is a decision without an abstention.

It is distinct from the house's **Loath to Part**, which holds the endowment effect: there mere ownership raises
what a thing is worth to the holder, and that is one of the reasons a setting sticks, so the two are genuinely
adjacent. The difference is what remains when the stickiness is removed. Suppose a population with no
attachment to the status quo whatever: the form must still be printed in some arrangement, the options must
still be in some order, and position would still move the answer. So the endowment effect explains part of the
adhesion and none of the necessity, and this misfit rests on the necessity.

It is distinct from the house's **The Third Option**, which holds the decoy effect: there an option is **added**
to a set and shifts the preference between two others, so the mechanism is a comparison the new option makes
available. Here nothing is added and the option set is unchanged. What does the work is the condition the
existing set arrives in, which is why the remedy that closes the decoy, keeping the option set honest, leaves
this untouched.

It is distinct from the house's **Kept for the Questions We Had**, where an archivist must select and the
decision destroys the evidence by which it could be judged: both stage a seat that cannot abstain, which makes
them worth holding apart carefully. There the evidence is **destroyed** and no method reaches it afterwards.
Here the evidence exists in quantity, is clean, and is **contaminated**: every observation of what people want
was produced on a form that already carried an arrangement. Running the arrangements against each other is
available and is the right thing to do, and it establishes which setting produces which outcome without
establishing which outcome anybody wanted.

## Origin

The finding that no design is neutral, the size of the effect, the mechanism inside one firm, the remedy of
requiring an answer, the setting that survives the remedy, and the dispute that is still open.

| **Richard Thaler & Cass Sunstein**    | _Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness_ (2008), and the choice-architecture argument          | **The spine**: there is no neutral way to present a choice, so whoever arranges it is a choice architect whether or not they intend to be, and the question is which arrangement rather than whether to have one.             |

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the body that
  issues the form as steering anybody or the person who leaves a box as careless, because nobody in the
  sequence has a decision to get wrong: the page has to be finished and the list of arrangements has no zero on
  it; the alternative is printed, visible and free to take; a setting chosen by an official body reads as a
  recommendation and frequently is one; the effort of forming a view exceeds the stake on most questions a form
  asks, and where there is no view, leaving the answer is the honest report; requiring an answer is a design of
  its own with a real cost paid by people who have none; the running order still moves the answer once the
  preselection is gone, and randomising it distributes the effect rather than removing it; and a statute silent
  on the setting delegates it rather than abstaining. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that a question has to arrive in some condition and no condition is null.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Removing the preselection is
  the real remedy, is used, and installs a requirement with its own wording, its own distribution and its own
  cost. Disclosing that the default is a default is right and does not reach it, since disclosure cannot supply
  a preference where there is none. Setting the default to what most people choose is the reasonable proposal
  and reads a preference off a population the previous setting produced. Asking people directly does not
  escape it, because a survey has an order and a wording. Randomising the order spreads the effect rather than
  deleting it, and is a policy somebody chose. And knowing the whole structure, which every party may, still
  leaves a page that has to go to print on Friday.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the choice-architecture argument, the donation comparison, the
  enrolment study, the account of active choosing, the ballot-order estimate and the ethical critique are
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether the inevitability of an arrangement licenses setting it
  deliberately is genuinely contested**, and the play carries the argument rather than settling it or endorsing
  any policy; the cross-country donation comparison is observational and the countries differ in more than the
  setting, in registries, in how families are approached and in clinical capacity, so it is cited for the
  presence and rough scale of the effect and never as a measurement of one cause; the enrolment result is drawn
  from particular firms and periods and the magnitudes do not transfer unchanged; the ballot-order estimate is
  carried as evidence that position matters where it can be measured cleanly, and not as a rate applicable
  elsewhere; and the account treats a form as one question set once, which is the play's own simplification of
  systems that ask continuously and adapt. **The play makes no claim that any default was wrongly set, or that
  defaults should be set one way or another**, only that the question has to arrive in some condition, and that
  there is no condition that leaves it alone.
