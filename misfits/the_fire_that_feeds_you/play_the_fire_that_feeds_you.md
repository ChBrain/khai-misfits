# Play: The Fire That Feeds You

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

The Fire That Feeds You.
A body is not the default state of its matter. The default is the cold, still, low-energy equilibrium that everything drifts toward, the rest all matter keeps in the end. A living body holds itself away from that default only by burning fuel without pause, a fire of metabolism that powers every beat and repair and thought, and the moment the fire goes out the body returns to the cold it came from. To live is to burn.
But the fire that fights the cold is not clean. Producing energy throws off reactive byproducts, the reactive oxygen species of oxidative metabolism, that damage the very cell that made them, so the fire that warms the body also scorches it, an idea running from Pearl's rate of living to Harman's free radical theory (Pearl; Harman). And beneath the chemistry there is an economy, and it is the deeper turn. A body has a finite budget of energy and material, and it must divide that budget between two ends: living and reproducing now, and repairing and maintaining the soma for later. Because the world kills bodies anyway, by predation and accident and cold, natural selection does not favour a body built to last forever, since most will die before forever comes; it funds only enough repair to carry the body through its expected span of reproduction, and then lets the soma go. This is Kirkwood's disposable soma: ageing is the evolved consequence of spending on the living what might have gone to the repair, the body disposable once its copies are made (Kirkwood).
And here is the misfit. It is not that some enemy wears the body down. The wearing is the living. The fire that holds off death is the fire that consumes, and the economy that funds the living is the economy that starves the repair, so a body ages not despite staying alive but because of it, worn by the very warmth that keeps it from the cold.
And here is why no one in it is a villain, and nothing is. The metabolism doing the damage is the same metabolism keeping the body alive, so there is no wearing to be rid of that would not also be the life. The cold default is no attacker; it only waits, the rest all matter keeps. And the under-investment in repair is not a fault or a miserliness but an evolved and reasonable allocation, since a body the world will end regardless is not worth building to last forever. Nothing wears the body down but its own necessary living, and the loss is authored by no one.
And here is the way out, and why it is bought at a price. A body can burn a little slower and spend a little more on maintenance, as caloric restriction and the repair pathways it engages extend life in many creatures, and buy itself time. But the time is bought at the cost of the growth and the reproduction the budget would otherwise fund, the very trade the disposable soma names (Kirkwood). And the tempting shortcut, to simply scavenge the damage away with antioxidants, is refused by the evidence, since antioxidant supplements do not extend life and some reactive byproducts are signals the body needs. The escape works against the grain of the living the body is built to prioritise, and none of it lets the fire burn free of cost.
And here is the bound that keeps it honest, and this misfit is hedged harder than most. The mechanism is genuinely contested. The crude free radical and rate-of-living accounts are much challenged, since antioxidant trials show no life extension, some reactive species act as beneficial signals, and a serious rival holds that ageing is less accumulated damage than the runaway continuation of growth programmes past their use. The rate-of-living link is Pearl's, the free radical mechanism Harman's, and the disposable-soma economics Kirkwood's, and the house stages the robust core they share and not the contested specifics: that ageing is a cost intrinsic to the living process and the evolved allocation of a finite budget, and not that oxidative damage is the one true cause. The house stages the structure and holds the bound in view: that the fire which feeds a body is the fire that wears it, and not that ageing is a simple rust a better hygiene would prevent.

## Arc

The bend is this: the metabolism that keeps a body alive is the same process that damages and ages it, and the body's economy is evolved to spend on living and reproducing what it might have spent on repair, so to live is to be worn by the living, the fire that fights death the fire that consumes.
It runs on a warmth that wears the thing it warms.
A body is not the default of its matter: the default is the cold, still equilibrium of death, waiting.
And a living body holds off the cold only by burning, the fire of metabolism powering every beat and repair (Pearl).
And the fire is not clean. It throws off reactive byproducts that damage the very cell that made them (Harman).
And the budget is finite and spent on the living. Evolved to fund reproduction over repair, since the world ends bodies anyway (Kirkwood).
And so the body wears. Worn by the warmth that keeps it from the cold, the repair too starved to keep the pace.
And it is authored by no one. The metabolism that harms is the metabolism that sustains, so the loss is the structure's and not a fault.
And the way out is a slower fire. Burning less and repairing more buys time at the cost of growth, and the bound held, the mechanism contested and ageing not simply rust (Kirkwood).

## Stakes

Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward the body worn and the fire spent, and away from the untouched wholeness it began with, because to hold off the cold default it must burn, and the burning throws off the damage, and the evolved budget funds the burning over the mending. The structure cannot both keep the body alive and keep it from wearing, because the wearing is not an accident of the living but the cost of it: the same fire that holds off the cold is the fire that scorches, and the same economy that funds the reproduction is the economy that starves the repair, so a body that stayed perfectly whole would be a body that had stopped burning, which is a body returned to the cold. The way out, to buy time with a slower fire and more maintenance, spends the growth and reproduction the budget would otherwise fund, and it is not the clean scavenging of damage the shortcut promises and the evidence refuses. It can only show that no one is a villain and nothing is, that the metabolism doing the harm is the metabolism giving the life and the cold is no attacker but only the waiting rest, and that the loss is the body worn by its own necessary living and not anyone's fault. And it holds the bound in view, hedged harder than most, because the mechanism is genuinely contested, the crude damage account much challenged and the disposable-soma trade-off the more robust reading (Kirkwood), so the whole of the misfit is that the fire which feeds a body is the fire that wears it, and to live at all is to be consumed by the living.
