# The Fire That Feeds You: Reference

The research warrant for **The Fire That Feeds You** (play and company not yet staged; this warrant is drafted first, to
prove the science and the distinction before authoring). The title is provisional, a reading of a family of theories of
biological ageing, the **metabolic and disposable-soma account of senescence**: the very metabolism that keeps a body alive,
burning fuel to hold off the cold default of death, is the same process that damages and ages it, and the body's economy is
evolved to spend on living and reproducing what it might have spent on repair, so to live is to be worn by the living, all
cited below, held with the strong finding that the mechanism is genuinely contested, the crude damage account much
challenged and the evolutionary trade-off the more robust reading.

## Line of Work

Staging the metabolic and disposable-soma account of ageing as a **living-is-what-ages-you structure**: a body is not the
default state of its matter. The default is the cold, still, low-energy equilibrium that everything drifts toward, and a
living body holds itself away from that default only by burning fuel without pause, a fire of metabolism that powers every
beat and repair and thought. But the fire that fights the cold default is not clean. Producing energy throws off reactive
byproducts, the reactive oxygen species of oxidative metabolism, that damage the very cell that made them, and the faster
and longer a body burns, the more of this wear it takes on, an idea running from Pearl's rate of living to Harman's free
radical theory (Pearl; Harman). And there is a deeper turn beneath the chemistry, an economic one. A body has a finite
budget of energy and material, and it must divide that budget between two ends: living and reproducing now, and repairing
and maintaining the soma for later. Because the world kills bodies anyway, by predation and accident and cold, natural
selection does not favour a body built to last forever, since most will die before forever arrives; it favours one that
invests just enough in maintenance to stay whole through its expected span of reproduction, and then lets the soma go. This
is Kirkwood's disposable soma: ageing is the evolved consequence of spending on living and reproducing what might have been
spent on repair, the body disposable once its copies are made (Kirkwood). So the misfit is not that some enemy wears the
body down, but that the wearing is the living: the fire that holds off death is the fire that consumes, and the economy that
funds the living is the economy that starves the repair. It is authored by no villain and by no external cause: the
metabolism doing the damage is the same metabolism keeping the body alive, and the under-investment in repair is not a fault
but an evolved and reasonable allocation given a world that ends bodies regardless. No one and nothing wears the body down
but its own necessary living. The governing law is not that ageing is a disease inflicted from without, but that **the
metabolism that keeps a body alive is the same process that damages and ages it, and the body's economy is evolved to spend
on living and reproducing what it might have spent on repair, so to live is to be worn by the living, the fire that fights
death the fire that consumes, no villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the cold default a living
body spends to escape, the fire of metabolism that escapes it, the damage the fire throws off, the disposable-soma economy
that funds living over repair, the wearing that follows, and the bought time that slower burning and greater maintenance can
win at a real cost (Pearl; Harman; Kirkwood), as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a body simply
rusting or an ageing that better hygiene would cure.

It is a living-is-what-ages-you structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest neighbours,
which is important since the house already stages several ageing and wear traps. It is distinct from Early and Uncounted,
the antagonistic pleiotropy in which a gene favoured for an early benefit is never billed for its late cost because too few
of the cohort survive to pay it: there the engine is one gene's trade-off across the life, sheltered by the declining force
of selection, whereas here the engine is the metabolic cost of life itself and the disposable-soma allocation of the whole
body's budget, not a single gene's unbilled bill, and the two are complementary evolutionary accounts of ageing cut to
different vectors. It is distinct from The Load It Always Carried, the fatigue in which increments of mechanical damage sum
until a part fractures under its ordinary load: there the damage is the summing of load cycles on a structure, whereas here
the damage is the chemical byproduct of the life process and the starving of repair, a metabolic and economic wearing and
not a mechanical one. And it is distinct from In Its Own Defence, the immunopathology in which the body's defence escalates
correctly against an invader but its weapons wreck the host: there the harm is a defence response overshooting a threat,
whereas here the harm is the ordinary running of the body with no threat and no response, the plain cost of being alive.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast ageing as a disease inflicted
  from without or a body merely rusting through neglect, because the source finding it stages is that the wearing is the
  living: the metabolism doing the damage is the same metabolism keeping the body alive, and the under-investment in repair
  is not a fault but an evolved and reasonable allocation of a finite budget given a world that ends bodies regardless of how
  well they are maintained. No enemy and no error wears the body down but its own necessary living. The loss is authored by
  no one: it is the body worn by the very fire that sustains it, and the misfit is that the process which fights the cold
  default of death is the process that consumes the one it warms.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out that exists is real but
  costly and partial: to burn a little slower and invest more in maintenance, as caloric restriction and the maintenance
  pathways it engages extend life in many model organisms, buys time, but at the price of slower growth and reduced
  reproduction, the very trade the disposable soma names (Kirkwood). And the tempting hero, to simply scavenge the damage
  away with antioxidants, is refused by the evidence, since antioxidant supplements do not extend life and some reactive
  species are beneficial signals the body needs (mitohormesis). The escape works against the grain of the reproduction and
  growth the body is built to prioritise, and none of it is a clean cure that lets the fire burn free of cost.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the rate-of-living link is Pearl's, the free radical mechanism Harman's, the
  disposable-soma economics Kirkwood's, and the challenges to the damage account the work of the mitohormesis and
  hyperfunction lines. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding, and this warrant hedges harder than most,
  because the science is genuinely unsettled: the crude free radical and rate-of-living accounts are substantially
  challenged, antioxidant trials do not show the life extension a pure damage theory predicts, some reactive species act as
  hormetic signals, and a serious rival holds that ageing is less accumulated damage than the runaway continuation of
  developmental and growth programmes past their use (Blagosklonny; Gems). What the house stages is the robust core the
  accounts share, that ageing is a cost intrinsic to the living process and the evolved allocation of a finite budget, and
  not the specific and contested claim that oxidative damage is the one true cause. The scope is held to ageing as an
  intrinsic cost of metabolism and the disposable-soma trade-off, and not stretched into a settled mechanism or a counsel
  that life is merely a slow burning to be doused.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the proposed load-bearing
pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Cold Default**: carries the setting, the still, low-energy equilibrium of death that all matter drifts toward, the
  natural state a living body spends without pause to hold itself away from.
- **The Fire**: carries the life process, the metabolism that burns fuel to power every beat and repair and thought and so
  holds off the cold default, the fire that feeds the body (Pearl).
- **The Ash**: carries the mechanism of damage (Harman), the reactive byproducts the fire throws off, the oxidative wear
  that the very making of energy lays into the cell that made it, carried with its serious challenge.
- **The Ledger**: carries the economic driver (Kirkwood), the finite budget split between living and reproducing now and
  repairing the soma for later, evolved to fund the first and starve the second since the world ends bodies regardless.
- **The Wearing**: carries the harm, senescence, the body worn down by the very living that sustains it, the fire that
  fights death consuming the one it warms and the repair too starved to keep pace, drawn distinct from Early and Uncounted,
  The Load It Always Carried, and In Its Own Defence.
- **The Bought Time**: carries the remedy and the bound (Kirkwood; the mitohormesis and hyperfunction lines), the slower
  burning and greater maintenance that win time at the cost of growth and reproduction, and the refusal of the antioxidant
  hero, and the marker of the deep hedge, since the damage account is contested and ageing is not simply rust to be
  scavenged away.
