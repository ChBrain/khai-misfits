# As Good as New: Reference

The research warrant for As Good as New.
The term is the organizational ecologists'; the play runs on structural inertia as a product of selection, and
on what a reorganisation does to an organisation's mortality, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the reliable organisation as **a body whose competence and whose rigidity are one purchase**: reliability
and accountability require the capacity to reproduce structure with high fidelity; that capacity is inertia under
another name; selection rewards the first two and therefore manufactures the third; and the attempt to change,
made correctly and on a sound answer, spends the reproducibility record and returns the body to the elevated
mortality of a newly founded one. The play models the documented mechanics, the fidelity that both valuable
properties rest on, the environment that selects for dependability and produces rigidity without ever selecting
for it, the turn seen correctly and agreed throughout, the rebuild that succeeds and empties the account doing
it, the hazard that rises at the change and declines with time since it, and the momentum by which a body that
has changed once is likelier to change again, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a
complacent organisation or a timid board.

The governing law is that the properties which make an organisation worth keeping are bought with reproducibility,
so the capacity to be relied upon and the incapacity to be quickly remade are the same property read in two
directions.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The structure
reproduces because that is what makes delivery dependable. The environment rewards dependability and nothing else.
The organisation sees the turn and is right about it. The rebuild is competent and succeeds. The mortality that
follows is a rate, not a verdict on anyone.

**And the competence is real, which the play establishes before anything else.** An organisation that can be
relied on to deliver, and that can render an account afterwards of what it did, holds two things most bodies do
not, and both rest on low drift between one running of the structure and the next. **A promise is a claim about a
repeat and an account is a claim about a procedure actually followed**, so a body without the fidelity can neither
promise nor explain. The sameness is the mechanism of the competence and not a symptom of complacency, which is
what keeps this from resolving into a complaint about conservatism.

**Then the inversion, which is the finding and is easy to get backwards.** Inertia is not a flaw that selection
failed to remove: it is what selection **produced**. An environment that keeps choosing organisations which
deliver dependably and can account for themselves is choosing organisations built for reproducibility, and
reproducibility is inertia. So the pressure does not relieve the rigidity, it manufactures it, and a harsher
environment yields bodies that are harder to change rather than easier. The sources of the inertia are ordinary
and defensible in themselves: commitments sunk in plant, equipment and personnel, coalitions that had to be
assembled for anything to get done, precedents that became standards because following them was how the thing
was reliably done, and external constraints including the legitimacy and institutional support that radical
change puts at risk.

**Then the hinge, which is where this misfit is cut and where it must not be cut anywhere else.** The organisation
**sees the turn**, is right about it, and agrees internally on the form it must take. Perception is eliminated,
which spends every remedy addressed to awareness before the play opens. What remains is the act: the structure
that reproduced is the structure being dismantled, so during the work the body cannot come out the same way each
time, the account it renders stops describing the thing rendering it, and the legitimacy accrued over decades
rests on a claim about a body being replaced. **The attempt did not fail. Succeeding is what cost this.**

**And the cost is actuarial rather than atmospheric.** What is spent is position on a mortality curve: the body
emerges carrying the elevated hazard of a newly founded organisation, which is the liability of newness returned
rather than resembled. That makes the claim measurable, and it was measured on a population rather than a case.

**And the finding has two halves, both of which the play carries.** The hazard **rises** when the change is made
and then **declines with time since the change**, in the same shape age-dependence always has, so this is a window
and not a doom: an organisation left alone long enough earns its reliability back. That is the play's own limit,
stated by the play, and a finding that includes the case where it relents is stronger than one that does not.
The second half removes the last exit: an organisation that has changed once is **more likely to change again**,
so the same account that makes change disruptive makes it, over time, more available. Waiting the window out is
therefore not reliably open, because the next correct move arrives, is still correct, and is now likelier to be
taken.

It is distinct from the house's **Right for the Last World**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages structural
contingency, where a form genuinely matched to its environment is held after the environment turns and nobody
inside is positioned to see it. Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating that the organisation sees the turn
perfectly and immediately, understands exactly what it must become, and agrees on it throughout, and that misfit
is defused completely, because its whole hinge is that the turn is not visible from inside. This one stands at
full strength on the same stipulation: **seeing was never the problem here**, and the harm is in the act the
seeing obliges. Run it the other way for the confirmation: remove **this** engine, by stipulating that
reorganising costs no reliability and no legitimacy and carries no mortality, and this misfit collapses entirely,
because the body would simply adapt, while Right for the Last World is untouched, since it still cannot see. The
two are the same family and separate on **which half of the sequence fails**: there the knowing, here the doing.

It is distinct from the house's **The Success Trap**, which stages the innovator's dilemma, where an incumbent
climbs the margin upward and is undercut from below by an entrant serving the customers it rationally declined.
Remove that engine, by stipulating no entrant anywhere, no low-margin segment and no market shape at all, and this
stands entire, because **there is nobody competing with it.** The mortality here comes from the organisation's own
transition and would be identical in a market with a single occupant. That play needs a rival to arrive; this one
needs only the body to move.

It is distinct from the house's **The Done Thing**, which stages institutional isomorphism, where organisations
converge on a common form for legitimacy rather than efficiency. Remove that engine, by stipulating that no
organisation ever imitates another and there are no peers to copy, and this stands whole, because **nothing here
is copied from anybody.** The structure is whatever reproduces reliably and could be unique to this body; what
selection rewards is dependability, not resemblance.

It is distinct from the house's **Path Dependence**, which stages lock-in, where a standard is held by switching
costs and coordination and the better option is known and cannot be reached alone. Remove that engine, by
stipulating no network, no coordination requirement and no other party whose choice matters, so that the
organisation can reach any form it likes unilaterally, and this stands, because **the organisation can reach the
new form alone and reaching it is precisely what harms it.** There the better option is unreachable; here it is
reachable, is reached, and the reaching is the cost.

It is distinct from the house's **Held Still to Keep It**, which stages safeguarding as codification, where
protecting a living practice requires naming it and naming it fixes it. Remove that engine, by stipulating that a
practice can be protected without ever being described, inventoried or fixed, and this stands entire, because
**nothing here turns on description.** That misfit's fixity is imposed from outside by the instrument of
protection; this one's is generated from inside by the requirement to be dependable, and would occur with no
observer, no inventory and nobody protecting anything.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the organisation as
  complacent, the board as timid or the environment as cruel, because nothing in the sequence has a decision to get
  wrong: the structure reproduces because that is what makes delivery dependable and conduct explicable; the
  environment applies two criteria that any environment worth operating in applies, and holds no view whatever about
  internal structure; the organisation sees the turn, is right about it and is agreed throughout; the rebuild is
  competent, supported and successful; and the mortality that follows is a rate read off a population rather than a
  judgement about anybody. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that
  the properties worth keeping are the properties that cannot be carried across a rebuild.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Seeing more clearly recovers
  nothing, since the organisation already sees perfectly and agrees. Not changing leaves the body as it was in a
  world that is not. Changing carefully changes the size of the payment rather than its kind, because the currency
  spent is the reproducibility record itself and there is no other. Changing gradually lengthens and flattens the
  window rather than removing it. Waiting the window out genuinely works and is the play's own limit, and the same
  study closes it, because a body that has changed once is likelier to change again and may reopen the window
  before it has shut. And loosening the fidelity in advance, so that change would be cheaper, spends the
  reliability and accountability it was buying, which is the thing that made the organisation worth preserving.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the account of structural inertia as a product of selection is Hannan
  and Freeman's, the liability of newness is Stinchcombe's, and the dynamic measurement of change against failure
  is Amburgey, Kelly and Barnett's. The bounds are carried with them: **whether inertia is selected or is instead
  substantially open to deliberate managerial adaptation is genuinely contested**, and the play carries that
  argument rather than settling it; the measurement is one population, Finnish newspaper organisations, and a
  single industry over a long span is a strong design and not a universal one; the two halves of that finding pull
  in different directions and the play states both rather than choosing; the account is stated for changes to core
  structure rather than for every adjustment an organisation makes; and the play treats the reorganisation as
  succeeding, which is the case it is about and not the only case. **The play makes no claim about any
  organisation, any sector or any board, and gives no managerial, strategic or organisational advice of any kind**,
  only that reliability is bought with reproducibility, and that spending the reproducibility is how an
  organisation becomes something else.
