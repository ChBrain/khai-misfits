# Persona: The Payer

## Taxonomy

[Payer](position_payer.md). The one who, meaning well, attaches a reward or a fine to a willing behaviour to get more of it, in the ordinary expectation that a price added to a motive raises the act.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Projection

Practical, responsible, well-meaning.
Wants more of a good behaviour, or less of a bad one, and reaches for the obvious lever, a payment for doing the thing or a penalty for not.
No cynicism in it: the move is the sensible one, the one that ordinarily works, and the Payer offers the incentive to strengthen a motive, not to replace it.

## Action

Pays for more on [Pay for More](plan_pay_for_more.md), through [Pricing](process_pricing.md) at [the Counter](place_the_counter.md), setting [the Incentive](piece_the_incentive.md), the fine for lateness or the pay for donating, expecting the behaviour to rise.
Cannot see, at the counter, that the price will reframe the act rather than reinforce it, because the incentive looks purely additive, a reason laid on top of the reasons already there, and nothing at the moment of setting it shows that it will displace them.
Does what a reasonable manager or policymaker does: it prices the behaviour it wants more of, and the pricing is exactly what turns the willing act into a transaction.

## Shadow

Not a fool for pricing the act and not a villain for wanting more of it: the Payer's move is the ordinary one and usually the right one, since incentives ordinarily do raise behaviour, so its error, where there is one, is invisible in advance.
Because it cannot know from the counter whether the motive it is pricing was intrinsic and strong, it cannot tell whether the incentive will add or displace, so the one trying hardest to strengthen the act is the one who may quietly undo it.
It is the one who meant to buy more of a good thing, and the one whose price bought less.

## Tell

Says a fine will surely reduce lateness and a payment will surely raise donation, and is stating the ordinary rule, which is usually true.
Cannot be blamed for the attempt, because pricing a behaviour to strengthen it is sensible and mostly works, so it acts soundly and is caught only where the motive it priced was one a price displaces.
