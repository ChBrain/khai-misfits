# Position: Steward

## Taxonomy

[A Reason Too Many](play_a_reason_too_many.md). The seat that knows the bound, weighing whether to price at all, how much, and how to frame it, withholding an incentive where a motive already carries the act and paying enough where it does not.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Has

The knowledge that an incentive is not always additive: that laid on an intrinsic or moral motive it can subtract more than it adds, so a price is not to be reached for by reflex.
A set of moves that bound the harm: withhold where a motive carries the act, pay enough rather than too little where it does not, frame a reward as recognition rather than control, and act before the motive hardens (Frey and Jegen; Gneezy and Rustichini).
No certainty in time, because whether a motive is strong enough to be worth protecting is hard to know before the price is set, so its knowledge is real but rarely sure when it is needed.

## Orders

Know when not to pay.
Weigh the motive before pricing it: withhold the incentive where an intrinsic or moral reason already carries the act, and where you must pay, pay enough that the price outweighs the crowding and frame it as thanks, not control.
Hold the bound honestly: know that incentives ordinarily work and crowd out only under particular conditions, and that the judgement of which case you face is the hard part and may be wrong.

## Loses

The certainty it would need to apply its own knowledge cleanly.
Because the conditions that decide whether an incentive adds or displaces are hard to read in advance, the seat cannot turn its understanding into a rule, so it spends judgement against uncertainty and may misjudge the strength of the motive it means to protect.
It keeps the theory and loses the guarantee, the one who best understands the trap still not sure of avoiding it in time.

## Drives

Toward the one who could keep the reward from crowding the motive out and cannot always know, in time, whether it will,
pricing sparingly and framing carefully, holding the conditional bound that keeps crowding-out from being a law,
so the seat that understands the trap best is the seat whose remedy is judgement under uncertainty, real and costly and never a formula applied.
