# Whoever Is Not Involved: Reference

The research warrant for Whoever Is Not Involved.
The arrangement is the service-management frameworks'; the play runs on external change approval, and on what
happens when the property that qualifies a reviewer is the property that removes their knowledge, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the change advisory board as **a control disqualified by its own qualification**: nothing should enter a
live service unexamined, so a body is constituted to approve changes; it is made independent of the teams
proposing, because a reviewer who benefits from approval is not a reviewer; independence means nobody in the room
has operated the system being changed; so the board examines what it can see, which is the paperwork, and records
an approval that certifies the documentation. The play models the documented mechanics, the sound principle that
produces the composition, the honest submission that loses the risk in translation, the reasonable questions that
are real and are not the question, the failure rate that is entirely unmoved, the remedy that is itself a change
and queues behind the same gate, and the peer review that works by being what the board was forbidden to be, as
the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a bureaucratic board or a reckless engineer.

The governing law is that the knowledge required to judge a change lives in the particulars of the system, and
selecting a reviewer for independence selects against involvement, which is where that knowledge is produced.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The board is
competent and diligent. The principle that constituted it is true. The author submits honestly and wants the
review. The questions asked are worth asking. The approval is correctly recorded.

**And the intention is right, which the play establishes before anything else.** Putting a change into a live
service with no review at all is indefensible and everybody involved knows it, so a body is created; and it is
made independent, because a reviewer who benefits from approval is not performing a review. **That sentence is
true.** It is the reason the arrangement exists and it is what any responsible organisation would write, which is
what keeps this from resolving into an argument for abolishing review.

**Then the substitution, performed by everybody and chosen by nobody.** The board cannot assess whether this
change will break this system, because that requires knowing the system, and not knowing the system is the
qualification for sitting. So it assesses what is visible: whether the form is complete, whether a rollback is
described, whether the window is agreed, whether the right names are on it. **Those are real questions and they
are not the question.** A rollback plan genuinely matters, which is exactly why the substitution holds: what
replaces the question is not nonsense, and asking it well is, from inside the room, indistinguishable from doing
the job. **No participant experiences the meeting as theatre**, which is why the arrangement survives every
review of itself.

**Then the measurement, which settles what argument could not.** Four years of survey research across the industry
finds that approval by a body external to the team has **no correlation with the rate at which changes fail**.
Not a weak relationship and not a contested one: none. The same data finds it correlates **negatively** with lead
time, with deployment frequency, and with **time to restore service**. So the control costs three measured things
and buys nothing measurable of the thing it exists for. **The gate and the fault never intersected**, the gate
having examined the documentation and the fault being in the system.

**And the last measure is the turn.** The remedy for an outage is itself a change, and a change goes to the board.
So the control that did not prevent the failure now stands in front of the recovery from it. The emergency path
exists and is also a procedure, needing approvers, and approvers at two in the morning are asleep. The fix waiting
is **the best-informed change anybody will write all year**, produced by people who have just learned precisely
what is wrong, and it is held by a process designed for changes made in ignorance of any particular fault.
**The delay is longest exactly where delay costs most**, which is why the harm surfaces in time to restore
service.

**And the settlement is the same review by different people.** Handing the change to a peer who knows the system
works, on the same evidence that finds the board does not. What it spends is the independence: the reviewer now
has a stake, works on the system, and wants the change to go in. **The organisation must give up a sentence that
is true** in order to buy a review that reaches the risk, and the production offers no reconciliation, because
the property that makes a reviewer trustworthy and the property that makes a reviewer able are one property with
opposite signs.

It is distinct from the house's **Vetocracy**, which is a near incumbent and stages the proliferation of veto
points, where so many parties can block that nothing passes. Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating a single
approver, no proliferation of any kind, and a body that has never refused anything, and this misfit stands whole,
because **nothing here turns on the count of blockers or on refusal at all.** Every change in this play is
approved. What fails is the content of the judgement rather than the number of people entitled to make it, and a
single independent approver who says yes to everything produces the identical measured result.

It is distinct from the house's **No Idle Hands**, which stages the utilisation trap, where waiting time climbs as
a server approaches saturation. Remove that engine, by stipulating instant approval with no queue anywhere and a
board that sits the moment it is asked, and this stands, because **the approver still cannot judge the risk.**
That play is about how long a thing waits; this one is about what the wait was for, and the zero correlation with
failure rate is unaffected by making the meeting immediate.

It is distinct from the house's **Five or a Layer**, which stages the span-of-control trade, where supervision is
bought with layers and attention and fidelity are spent against each other. Remove that engine, by stipulating a
single layer, a tiny span and a supervisor with unlimited attention for every change, and this holds, because
**the defect is whose knowledge and not how much attention.** An undivided attention that has never operated the
system reaches the same verdict as a divided one.

It is distinct from the house's **The Empty Seat**, which stages automation bias, where an operator over-trusts an
automated judgement and stops checking. Remove that engine, by stipulating no automation anywhere in the process
and a wholly human review at every step, and this stands entire, because **there is nothing here being
over-trusted.** The board is not deferring to a machine; it is a human body that has been correctly constituted
and thereby made unable to see the thing it is looking for.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the board as
  bureaucratic or the engineer as reckless, because nothing in the room has a decision to get wrong: the body
  exists because unexamined changes are indefensible; its independence is the correct expression of a true
  principle; its members are competent and their questions worth asking; the author submits honestly, wants the
  review, and explains the change as well as it can be explained to somebody who has not operated the system; and
  the approval is correctly recorded. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the
  misfit is that the property qualifying a reviewer is the property removing their knowledge.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. A more diligent board changes
  nothing, since diligence was never what was missing. A better change form describes the change more clearly to
  people who still cannot evaluate it. Meeting more often removes the queue and leaves the judgement exactly where
  it was. Abolishing review is indefensible and the production never asks for it. An emergency path is itself a
  procedure and needs approvers who are people. And peer review genuinely works, at the price of a reviewer with a
  stake, which means giving up a sentence that is true.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the measurement of external approval against delivery and stability
  outcomes is Forsgren, Humble and Kim's, reporting the State of DevOps research. The bounds are carried with
  them: **the finding is survey-based and correlational across a surveyed population**, and is offered as what was
  measured rather than as a law of committees; how far it transfers to regulated sectors, where an approval record
  may be a legal requirement independent of what it prevents, is genuinely contested and the play adjudicates none
  of it; what a control is worth as evidence rather than as prevention is a real question the play does not
  settle; and the account is stated for approval by a body external to the team, which is not every review.
  **The play makes no claim about any organisation, any board or any change process, and gives no governance,
  operational or engineering advice of any kind**, only that a reviewer chosen for having no stake has, by the
  same act, been chosen for not knowing.
