# The Shares to Say It With: Reference

The research warrant for The Shares to Say It With.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the expiration of IPO share lockups and on the short-sale constraint
that keeps a known date out of the price, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the lockup expiry as **a foreseeable price move that cannot be traded, because the trade would need the
shares the move releases**: a company with no trading history binds its existing holders not to sell for a
stated term, which is what makes the offering credible to strangers, and prints the term, the count and the day.
For that interval the shares that may actually be sold are a fraction of the shares that exist, and a price is
made from the first quantity and not the second. On the stated day the promise ends, the holders sell because a
whole working life standing in one company's paper is what any adviser would tell them to reduce, and the price
falls on a morning when no accounts are published and nothing is learned. The play models the documented
mechanics, the commitment the offering requires, the float that makes the price, the disclosure that is
complete and inert, the borrow that is drawn from the float and from nowhere else, the sale that is prudence,
and the day with no information in it, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of insiders
dumping on the public.

The governing law is that a price is made by the shares that can trade rather than by the shares that exist,
and a lockup makes those two quantities differ by a disclosed amount for a disclosed interval. Closing the gap
would mean trading the shares that are not tradable, which is the one thing the instrument forbids, so the
information is perfect and the instrument for expressing it is absent.

What removes any possibility of blame from the underwriter is that the commitment is the answer to a real
problem and has no weaker form. Buyers of an untested company need its insiders bound, and a promise that
leaves the shares saleable is not a promise, so the clause that earns the offering its credibility is the same
clause that publishes an exact future date and freezes every share that could be sold against it. There is no
version of the instrument that does one job and not the other.

What removes it from disclosure is that disclosure is already complete. The term, the count and the date are
filed months ahead, plainly, and are available to every participant, so nothing is vague, late, buried or
selective, and no better wording or earlier filing would change a price. The obstacle was never anybody's
ignorance, which is what separates this from every failure a disclosure remedy reaches.

**The correcting trade is made, and that is the finding rather than an objection to it.** Short selling in
these stocks rises into the expiry date, so the play does not claim the market sits on its hands. It claims the
trade is rationed: a share must be borrowed before it can be sold short, a locked share can no more be lent
than sold, and the lendable pool is therefore exactly the free float, small for precisely the reason the fall
is coming. Ofek and Richardson tested the anomaly for exploitability and found it did not violate arbitrage,
which is the two halves of the misfit in one result, a price that is not efficient and a trade that will not
pay to make it so.

And the holder closes the case against a second party. The term is honoured in full, nothing is taken early,
reducing a single undiversified position is what a competent adviser would counsel, and the first lawful day is
the same day for everybody who signed one, so there is no less conspicuous morning to choose. The conduct is
correct at every step and conspicuous only because the instrument that made the offering possible scheduled it.

It is distinct from the house's **All at Once**, where a popular cached value expires at one shared instant so
every request misses together and the correct recomputation arrives as a flood: the calendars look identical
and the engines are not. There the engine is **simultaneity**, and de-synchronising the expiry dissolves the
failure, which is exactly what jittered time-to-live does. Here simultaneity is incidental. Lockups are
routinely released in tranches now, and each tranche runs the mechanism entire, because the shares of a tranche
still cannot be borrowed before the tranche exists. What is load-bearing is not that the parties act at once
but that the instrument which would price the event is the object the event delivers.

It is distinct from the house's **Fair Warning**, where an announced future cut in demand accelerates present
extraction, and is its exact inverse on the same variable. There the announcement **reaches** the present, and
the harm is done by its reaching: the owner of the resource can act now on a date set for later, so the earlier
and more credible the warning, the worse the outcome. Here the announcement is at least as early and at least
as credible and reaches nothing, because the party who would act on it has no asset to act with until the date
arrives. One misfit is announcement working too well and the other is announcement that cannot work at all.

It is distinct from the house's **Priced In**, where a price that reveals costly research for free destroys the
incentive to produce it, so a fully informative price is self-defeating: there the scarce thing is the
**information**, and the equilibrium keeps a degree of disequilibrium so gathering still pays. Here the
information is free, complete, public and early, and costs nobody anything to acquire. What is scarce is the
share, and the price fails to carry a fact that every participant already holds.

It is distinct from the house's **Nobody Reads It**, where standard-form terms degrade because only a minority
read them and that minority is too small to discipline the drafter: there the disclosure exists and fails at
the reader. Here it succeeds at the reader. The buyer reads the date, believes it, and buys anyway, because
understanding a fact and holding an instrument that prices it are different possessions and only the first was
on offer.

It is distinct from the house's **Sold to Find the Price**, which stages the same company weeks earlier, where
underpricing is information bought with the asset it is meant to value: there the price does not exist yet and
is assembled by asking the people who will bid, so the cost is a discount paid for candour. Here the price
exists, is quoted continuously, and is correct for the quantity available. The difficulty is not that the
number had to be found but that the number is made from a float the calendar will multiply.

It is distinct from the house's **The Beauty Contest**, which carries the limits of arbitrage as a supporting
row: there the arbitrageur can trade and may be forced out before the price corrects, so the constraint is
**risk and capital** on a position that is available. Here the position is not available in size at any capital,
because the shares to take it with do not exist as tradable objects until the date, and the constraint tightens
rather than loosens as the case for the trade becomes clearer.

is still open.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the insider as
  dumping on the public or the underwriter as engineering a squeeze, because nobody in the sequence has a
  decision to get wrong: the commitment is what makes an untested offering saleable and has no form that binds
  a holder without freezing the shares; the price during the term is made correctly from the quantity a market
  actually has to clear; the disclosure is complete, plain, early and universal and reaches every participant;
  the party who would trade the event forward is doing the right trade and is rationed by a lendable pool that
  is the free float and nothing else; and the holder honours the term in full and then does what any competent
  adviser would counsel, on the only day it is lawful to do it. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges
  from the structure, so the misfit is that the instrument which publishes a foreseeable fall is the instrument
  that withholds the shares to price it with.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Selling short into the date
  is the right trade, is made, and is capped by the free float and priced at what a scarce borrow costs, so it
  tightens exactly as the case for it strengthens. Staggering the release into tranches spreads the event and
  runs the mechanism once per tranche, because a tranche's shares cannot be borrowed before the tranche exists.
  Shortening the term shortens the interval without removing it and weakens the commitment the offering needed.
  Abolishing the lockup outright removes the trap and the moral-hazard remedy together, which is a trade and
  not a fix. Disclosing more is not available, since the disclosure is already complete, and that is the point
  of the production. And knowing the mechanism perfectly, which every party in the play does, supplies nobody
  with a share to trade it with.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the volume and return measurement, the supply account and the
  exploitability test, the divergence-of-opinion theory, the commitment finding and the short-selling evidence
  are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the measured effect is **small and heterogeneous**, absent
  altogether in the private-equity-backed part of one sample, so the play stages the structure and never a
  rate; **whether the fall is a supply effect or an information effect is genuinely argued**, and the play
  takes the supply account because that is what the anticipation puzzle turns on rather than because the
  argument is settled; the finding that the anomaly is not exploitable is a result about net-of-cost
  profitability under stated conditions and not a claim that nobody can trade it, which is why the play stages
  a trade that is made; the estimates are drawn from United States issues in particular eras and lockup design
  has since moved toward staggered tranches, performance-triggered early releases and listings with no lockup
  at all, none of which the play stages; and treating the release as a single date is the play's own
  simplification. **The play makes no claim that any party acted wrongly, or that buyers of new issues are
  systematically harmed**, only that a price is made from the shares that can trade, and that the clause
  fixing a public date is the clause that withholds them.
