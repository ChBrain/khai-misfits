# Plot: One Roof

## Taxonomy

[Over a Barrel](play_over_a_barrel.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

At [The Table](place_the_table.md), the two seats that faced off through [The Squeeze](plot_the_squeeze.md) are pulled in under a single ledger.

## Action

[The Owner](persona_the_owner.md), from [the owner's seat](position_owner.md), runs [Own Both Sides](plan_own_both_sides.md) through [Integrating](process_integrating.md): folding investor and buyer into [The Merger](piece_the_merger.md), one owner holding both, so no separate counterparty is left standing to threaten the walk that opened [The Squeeze](plot_the_squeeze.md).

## Stage

Under one roof, the plant beside the factory and the factory itself answer to the same balance sheet, so redrawing terms against one arm of the business only moves money from one pocket to the other, nothing left worth squeezing for (Williamson). The remedy has a price: a firm runs on administrative direction where a market ran on price, and the sharp incentive that made an independent supplier watch its own costs dulls once that supplier is just a department, its bureaucracy its own cost. A long contract or a hostage posted in earnest can buy some of the same protection without a merger, but a contract this long still cannot see every contingency, and integrating one side's ownership secures that side's incentive to invest only by weakening the other's stake in the outcome (Grossman and Hart).

## Tension

The hold-up is gone from this table, and in its place sits the standing cost every remedy here carries: the bureaucracy of the merged firm, the blind spots of the long contract, or the incentive quietly traded away on whichever side integration did not favor.
