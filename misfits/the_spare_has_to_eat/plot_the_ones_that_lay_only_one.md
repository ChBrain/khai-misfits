## Stage

[The Single-Egg Cousin](persona_the_single_egg_cousin.md), on [Lay One, Because One Is Enough](plan_lay_one_because_one_is_enough.md), hatches its single egg nearly always and fledges it, with no sibling to remove and no premium to pay.

## Tension

This is the test, and it is what makes the account evidence rather than a story about one bird.
The prediction is specific and was made before it was checked: females invest about the same in each egg, so the **cost** of a spare is roughly constant across species, while the **benefit** depends entirely on how often first eggs fail. The trait should therefore appear where the failure rate is high and be absent where it is low. And that is what the colonies show: roughly fifty-one to sixty-one per cent first-egg hatching where the second egg is laid, at least eighty-five per cent where it is not. **The species that does not need the insurance does not buy it**, which is not restraint and is arithmetic.
