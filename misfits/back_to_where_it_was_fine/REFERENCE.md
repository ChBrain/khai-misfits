# Back to Where It Was Fine: Reference

The research warrant for Back to Where It Was Fine.
The term is the house's; the play runs on alternative stable states and hysteresis in shallow lakes, and on
the failure of external nutrient reduction alone to restore the clear-water state, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging ecological hysteresis as **a repair specified from a record that contains only the way in**: a
shallow lake holds two self-maintaining arrangements, clear with rooted vegetation and turbid without it,
and nutrient loading eventually switches it from the first to the second. The obvious remedy, returning the
loading to the level at which the lake was demonstrably clear, is correct on every piece of evidence
anybody has and does not work. The play models the documented mechanics, the two states each stabilised by
their own feedbacks, the lawful loading that no single input is responsible for, the switch that is not
proportional to the push, the internal store that turns the lake into its own supplier, the regulator whose
reasoning is right at every step, and the catchment that delivers in full and receives nothing, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of weak regulation or of a catchment that cheated.

The governing law is that a system with alternative stable states does not retrace its path. The loading at
which the clear state fails and the loading at which the turbid state fails are different, and the second
lies well below the first, so reducing the load along the upper branch produces a moderate decrease in
turbidity and no return until a much lower threshold is reached.

What removes any possibility of blame is that the return threshold has never been an observable. Everything
ever measured about the lake was measured while it was on the way in, so the record is long, accurate,
complete, and contains no information about the quantity that now governs recovery. A target derived from
the historical loading is derived correctly from the only data in existence, and setting it lower would
have meant imposing cost on a catchment on the strength of a threshold nobody had measured.

What makes the loading unattributable is the resistance of the state it was wearing down. A stable system
absorbs disturbance and looks unchanged while doing it, so each year of no visible harm reads as evidence
that the loading is safe, and is in fact a year of the buffer being spent. The two are the same
observation. And the increment that finally crosses the switch is no larger than the hundreds before it, so
there is a real, cumulative, wholly permitted cause with no member that caused it.

And the remedy is superseded at the moment it succeeds. Phosphorus accumulated in the sediment over decades
is released back into the water column and kept in circulation by fish feeding in the bed, so the turbid
state is sustained internally: reductions in external load frequently fail to deliver the return of rooted
vegetation, because the load that now matters is already inside the lake and every instrument the catchment
holds acts on what flows in.

It is distinct from the house's **The Sort**, where mild preferences about neighbours tip past a threshold
into a segregation nobody chose: there the mechanism is a threshold crossing driven by aggregated
preference, and the interest is in how a small preference produces a large outcome. Here the crossing is
only the first half. The whole of this play is the asymmetry of the return, which that misfit does not
carry: a segregated neighbourhood does not require the preferences to be pushed far past their original
value before it unmixes, and this lake does.

It is distinct from the house's **Dry Enough to Build On**, where a wall keeps the small floods out so the
plain fills up and the flood it cannot stop finds a town: there the protection invites the exposure, and
the harm arrives as an event. Here nothing is being protected and nothing is invited: the harm is a change
of state that persists, and the difficulty is not that a bigger disturbance eventually arrives but that
removing the disturbance entirely leaves the system where it is.

It is distinct from the house's **The New Normal**, where each small deviation is tolerated once and becomes
the standard so the line of acceptable creeps: there a judgement is made repeatedly and a standard
degrades. Here no standard moves and nobody tolerates anything: the consents were the consents throughout,
and what changed was the lake.

It is distinct from the house's **Chesterton's Fence**, where a safeguard whose purpose is forgotten is
cleared away and the quiet harm returns: there something protective is removed in ignorance. Here nothing
is removed at all, the protective structure is the lake's own feedback, and it is not cleared but worn
through by inputs that were each permitted.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the regulator
  as lax or the catchment as evasive, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: every
  discharge held a consent and every field was fertilised at the recommended rate; no single input crossed
  the threshold, since the last increment was no larger than the hundreds before it; the regulator derived
  its target from four decades of monitoring and could not have justified a lower one, the return threshold
  never having been observed; the catchment changed its practice, rebuilt its works and met the number in
  full. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the record
  of how a system came to be broken contains no information about how to put it back.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Cutting the external load
  further works and means charging a catchment that has already delivered in full, on the strength of a
  threshold nobody had measured when the first bill was issued. Removing the fish works, clears the water
  within a season, and imposes a switch by force rather than allowing a system to return, so it holds only
  while the new arrangement can sustain itself and fails if the loading is still too high. Dredging or
  capping the sediment removes the store outright and costs more than the whole catchment programme, at
  scales where it is possible at all. Accepting the turbid state is also available, and is what happens by
  default.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the ecology, the models and the restoration
  literature are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: alternative stable states are best evidenced in
  shallow temperate lakes and the strength of the hysteresis varies greatly with depth, size, residence
  time and fish community, so this is not a general property of all water bodies; the extent to which real
  systems show true bistability rather than a steep but continuous response has been argued over for
  decades, and some restorations do succeed on external load reduction alone; the persistence of internal
  loading varies from a few years to several decades by site; and biomanipulation has a mixed record, with
  durable successes and reversions both well documented. **The play makes no claim that reducing nutrient
  loading is wasted**, since it is a precondition for anything else working, only that the level which
  preceded a change is not the level that reverses it.
