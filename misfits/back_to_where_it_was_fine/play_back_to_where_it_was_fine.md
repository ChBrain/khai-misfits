# Play: Back to Where It Was Fine

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Back to Where It Was Fine.
A shallow lake was clear for a very long time: weed on the bed, water you could see through, the fish that go with that. Then phosphorus came in, from fields and from a treatment works, decade after decade, every discharge consented and every field fertilised at the rate the guidance recommended. At some point the lake stopped being clear. And the obvious repair, the one any reasonable person would specify, is to put the phosphorus back to what it was when the lake was fine. So that is done, at great cost, and it is achieved, and the lake stays green.

## Arc

The bend is this: a system with two settled states does not retrace its path, so the level that was safe on the way in is not the level that is safe on the way out.
It runs on a lake that has two ways of being.
The Two States are both stable and both self-maintaining. Clear water holds weed on the bed, the weed holds the sediment down and takes up the nutrients and shelters the animals that graze the algae. Turbid water has no weed, because there is not enough light on the bed for it, and without weed the sediment stays stirred and the light stays out. Each arrangement keeps itself, which is what makes them states rather than moments.
The Load That Went In was lawful the whole way. Every discharge held a consent, every field was fertilised to the recommended rate, and no single input was the one that did it. The lake absorbed the loading for years while looking entirely fine, because a system in a stable state resists being moved, which reads from outside as evidence that the loading is harmless.
Then it went over, and the crossing was not proportional to the push. The last increment was no larger than any of the hundreds before it, and it arrived when the arrangement holding the clear state had been worn thin, so a small addition produced a large and rapid change: not a slope but a switch.
And now The Sediment holds the new state without any help from the catchment. Phosphorus stored in the bed over decades is released back into the water, the fish that feed in the bed keep it stirred, the light stays out and the weed cannot re-root. The lake is loading itself, so the pipes are no longer the thing to fix.
So The One Who Sets the Target does the correct arithmetic and gets the wrong number. Choosing the historical loading, the level at which the lake was demonstrably fine, is exactly what the evidence supports and exactly what any inquiry would endorse: and coming back down that far only moves the lake along the turbid branch. The backward switch sits far below the forward one, which is a property of the system and not a fact anybody could have read off the record.
And The Ones Who Paid for It meet the worst part. The catchment spends years and a great deal of money, the monitoring confirms the target has been met in full, and the lake looks precisely as it did before the work began. Nobody defaulted, nobody cheated, and the programme delivered everything it promised.

## Stakes

Whether a repair can be specified from a record, when the record contains only the way in.
Every plot moves the same way: toward consented discharges, recommended rates, targets drawn from evidence and programmes that deliver exactly what they promised, and away from any instrument that could have told the catchment how far down the road back begins, because the only measurement anybody had was of a lake that had never been on this branch. The production cannot make the sediment forget forty years, and it cannot ask a regulator to set a target the evidence did not support. What is left to fight over is not whether to cut the loading, that had to happen, but who owns the distance between the level that was fine on the way in and the level that would be enough on the way out.
