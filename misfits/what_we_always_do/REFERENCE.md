# What We Always Do: Reference

The research warrant for What We Always Do.
The term is the organisational behaviour literature's; the play runs on the threat-rigidity effect, and on what a
correctly perceived threat does to the range of what a body considers and to who considers it, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the response to threat as **two correct moves that invert together**: a real threat is perceived
accurately; the body restricts its information processing, attending to fewer cues through the channels that have
carried the important thing before; it constricts control, moving the decision upward to whoever is accountable;
and both of those are the well-learned answer, installed by training and confirmed by every previous occasion,
which is wrong exactly when the threat is a kind the repertoire does not contain. The play models the documented
mechanics, the threat seen exactly and denied by nobody, the practised response that arrives before the
alternatives by being ready rather than by winning an argument, the restriction that buys the capacity to act at
all, the concentration that buys speed and accountability, the cue set aside by the rule that correctly sets aside
the others, and the novelty that is established only by the weighing which has already stopped, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a panicking room or a rigid manager.

The governing law is that threat narrows the range of responses considered and the number of people considering
them, so a body meets an unfamiliar situation with less variety of response than it brings to a familiar one.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The threat is
assessed correctly. The practised response has a genuine record of working. The narrowing is what makes acting
possible under load. The concentration is what makes the answer fast and answerable. The cue that mattered was
available to everyone and looked like every other marginal thing.

**And both moves are right, which the play establishes before anything else.** Attending to fewer cues is how
anything gets done under load: a body weighing every marginal signal equally in a crisis would never act at all.
Concentrating the decision is how an organisation answers quickly and stays accountable for what it did. **Neither
is a vice**, both are what training installs, and a room that did the opposite, widening its attention and
dispersing its authority the moment things got serious, would be worse in almost every emergency it will ever
face. The production never asks for either to be abandoned, which is what keeps the misfit from resolving into a
complaint about rigidity.

**Then the condition that turns them, which is the whole play.** The moves are correct when the threat is one the
repertoire already contains, and wrong when it is not. The two things that would establish which case this is are
a wider sweep of cues and more people with the standing to say so, and both have just been withdrawn, by the
moves themselves. **The narrowing removes the means of noticing that the narrowing is wrong**, so there is no
moment inside the sequence at which anybody holds what they would need in order to object.

**And the cue is not a warning that was ignored.** It was in the room, unhidden and unsuppressed, and it looked
exactly like the many marginal things a correct rule sets aside. What would have distinguished it is the
knowledge that this threat is a new kind, which is precisely what the weighing was going to produce. So the
failure is one of weighing rather than of availability, which is what makes it structural rather than accidental.

**And the evidence is honest about where it fails, which the play carries rather than closing.** Twenty-four
groups placed under manipulated threat in a management simulation showed **restricted information processing and
raised stress**, as the account predicts. They did **not** show the other half: influence inside the groups did
not concentrate. So the restriction is well supported by that test and the concentration is not, standing instead
on the theory and on other evidence, and the play states the gap rather than papering it.

It is distinct from the house's **Good Money After Bad**, which is by the same scholar and stages escalation of
commitment, where a decision-maker defends a prior investment by committing further to it. Remove that engine
entirely, by stipulating a body that has spent nothing, holds no prior commitment and has nothing whatever to
justify, meeting a threat on its first day of existence, and this misfit stands at full strength, because
**nothing here is being defended.** The narrowing is a response to the threat rather than to a record, and it
fires identically in a body with no history of decisions at all. The two are the same scholar on different works,
which is expected, and they separate on what the actor is protecting: there a previous choice, here nothing.

It is distinct from the house's **Closing Ranks**, which stages groupthink, where a cohesive group's drive for
concurrence overrides its realistic appraisal of alternatives. Remove that engine, by stipulating a group with no
cohesion at all, members who dislike each other and want no agreement, or by removing the group outright and
leaving **one person alone**, and this stands entire, because the restriction of cue utilisation operates on a
single individual under pressure with nobody to agree with. That play needs a group and its wish to stay one;
this one needs only a threat and a trained response.

It is distinct from the house's **The Simpler Lever**, which stages the law of requisite variety, where a
controller with less variety than its disturbance cannot regulate it. Remove that engine, by stipulating a
controller that ordinarily holds ample variety, more than sufficient for anything the environment does, and this
stands, because **the harm here is the change and not the level**: the body sheds variety precisely as the
disturbance rises, so what fails is a correlation rather than a shortfall. There the insufficiency is standing;
here it is manufactured at the moment of greatest need by a body that had enough a minute earlier.

It is distinct from the house's **Watermelon Reporting**, which stages the distortion of bad news as it travels
upward, dressed green by each teller. Remove that engine, by stipulating that every signal is transmitted
faithfully and nobody softens anything at any level, and this stands whole, because **nothing here is distorted
in transmission.** The cue arrives accurately and is simply not weighed: there the sender changes the message,
here the receiver narrows the set of messages it will consider.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast anybody as panicking,
  rigid, autocratic or inattentive, because nothing in the room has a decision to get wrong: the threat is assessed
  accurately and denied by nobody; the practised response is what the drills installed and what every previous
  occasion confirmed; the narrowing is what buys the capacity to act under load and feels like focus because it is
  focus; the concentration of the decision is handed upward rather than seized, and supplies the accountability a
  dispersed decision cannot; and the cue that would have mattered was available to everybody and looked exactly
  like the many marginal things a correct rule sets aside. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that the response which makes a body reliable against familiar threats is the
  response that is wrong against a new one.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Widening attention in a crisis
  costs the speed the crisis is demanding, and there is no rule that keeps the useful margin while shedding the
  rest, since what distinguishes it is the very knowledge the weighing was to produce. Dispersing authority costs
  both the speed and the accountability, and leaves nobody answerable for what was done. Seeing the threat more
  clearly recovers nothing, because it was seen exactly. Rehearsing the novel case converts it into a familiar one
  and works to precisely the degree that the right novelty was guessed in advance. And abandoning the repertoire
  makes the body worse in almost every emergency it will actually meet, in exchange for being better in the rare
  one it might.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the multilevel threat-rigidity account is Staw, Sandelands and Dutton's,
  the narrowing of cue utilisation under arousal is Easterbrook's, the group experiment is Gladstein and Reilly's,
  and the account of crisis contracting authority in organisations is Hermann's. The bounds are carried with them:
  **the group-level test found one half of the account and not the other**, which the play states rather than
  smooths, so the concentration half rests on the theory and on other evidence and not on that experiment;
  a management simulation buys the manipulation of threat under control and costs the stakes of a real emergency,
  which is a fair objection and is carried; whether rigidity is general, and how much of it is appropriate rather
  than pathological, is genuinely contested and the play adjudicates none of it; and the account is stated for
  threats a body's repertoire does not contain, which is not most threats. **The play makes no claim about any
  organisation, any emergency or any decision-maker, and gives no managerial, operational or crisis-response
  advice of any kind**, only that the range narrows as the pressure rises, and that this is right until the
  situation is one the range does not cover.
