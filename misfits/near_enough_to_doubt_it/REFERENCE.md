# Near Enough to Doubt It: Reference

The research warrant for Near Enough to Doubt It.
The term is the house's; the play runs on grant and manuscript evaluation and on what closeness to a subject does
to the score it receives, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging peer evaluation as **an instrument whose depth varies across the things it ranks**: a proposal has to be
judged by somebody competent to judge it, which means somebody close enough to follow what it claims, and every
reform of the last century has pushed toward supplying that closeness. Being close is also being able to see how
the work could fail: which step is the hard one, which assay has never run at that scale, why the obvious version
has not been done. Those difficulties are real, are what expertise exists to produce, and each is a reason for a
lower score. The reader further away assesses significance, argument, plan and team competently and cannot see
them. Both readings are honest, they are not the same instrument, and they are written in the same units and
added up. The play models the documented mechanics, the assignment that must place expertise where it is needed,
the proximity that produces competence and doubt together, the difficulty that is genuinely there, the honest
reading taken from outside, the scale with no column for depth, and the line drawn at a percentile, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a reviewer defending territory.

The governing law is that the reader able to judge a novel proposal is the reader able to see why it might not
work, and a score is a rank, so being understood costs it.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The programme
officer matches expertise where it is needed. The near reviewer names real difficulties. The far reviewer reports
accurately what is visible. The chair converges the panel on the better-informed account. The applicant describes
the approach honestly, including the hard step.

What removes any possibility of blame from the near reviewer is that the lower score follows from perception
rather than from interest, and the evidence settles it rather than the play asserting it. In a grant process built
for the purpose, evaluators and proposals were **assigned at random** across more than two thousand pairings, and
closeness to a proposal's own field still predicted a less generous evaluation of it, particularly where the
proposal was novel. Randomisation removes the competitive relation as an explanation. In a separate body of
unblinded evaluations, harshness tracked the reviewer's **own assessment of their expertise**, and the reading the
authors favour is that bounded rationality rather than social influence is doing the work: a better-informed
reader sees more that could go wrong.

What removes it from the far reviewer is that the reading is accurate about everything within reach. Whether the
question matters, whether the design follows from it, whether the group has done comparable work: all of that is
assessed competently. What is out of reach is that the third step has never worked at that scale, and knowing it
requires having been in the room where it failed. Declining the assignment moves the proposal to a reader further
away still, and the panel must cover the call either way.

**And the assignment that causes it is good administration, which is where the structure becomes concrete.** A
call spans more subfields than any panel holds; the readers closest to a proposal are the ones most likely to be
conflicted out of it; those who agree to serve are a self-selected fraction of those asked. Matching as well as
possible concentrates the deepest readers on the proposals that most need them, which is correct in every
particular, and it is what makes the depth of reading uneven across the round.

**Then every available procedure carries the difference through, and the best one carries it most cleanly.** Panel
discussion is genuinely useful and it converges the room on the near reviewer's account, because that account is
better informed and everybody present can tell. So the deeper reading propagates, correctly, and the proposal that
had one falls relative to proposals whose difficulties nobody in the room could see. Averaging instead would
weight the reading that saw less equally, which is worse. There is no third procedure.

It is distinct from the house's **Marked the Same by Anyone**, which is the nearest incumbent, staging the
reliability ceiling: an instrument's correlation with what it is measuring is bounded by how consistently it
measures anything, so inconsistency caps validity. That misfit runs on **disagreement within one item**. Remove
its engine, by stipulating perfect inter-rater agreement everywhere, and this misfit stands entire: three experts
agreeing exactly on a four still sink a proposal that three generalists would have agreed exactly was a seven, and
the rank compares the two numbers. The difference is that there the trouble is reviewers coming apart, and here
the reviewers of any one proposal may agree perfectly while the **depth of reading differs between proposals**.

It is distinct from the house's **The Best on Paper**, which stages the optimizer's curse, where selecting on
noisy estimates guarantees that the winner's estimate was flattered by its noise. Remove that engine, by
stipulating scores with no noise at all, each exactly right given what its reader could see, and this stands: the
difficulty is not variance but a systematic difference in what each reader was able to see, which is a bias with
a known sign and not an error term.

It is distinct from the house's **The Curse of Knowledge**, where an expert cannot model what a novice does not
know and so communicates past them. Nobody here is communicating badly: the near reviewer's account is clear, is
understood by the panel, and is correct. What differs is not the transmission but what each reader was in a
position to perceive.

It runs directly against the house's **Whoever Is Not Involved**, on the same axis and with the opposite
sign. There a change is sent to a board selected for independence, and selecting for independence selects
against the involvement where the knowledge lives, so the distance this play treats as a defence is that
play's harm.

**What sets the sign is what the reviewer is being asked to produce.** Where the output is a judgement
about whether this particular thing will work, proximity supplies the knowledge the judgement needs and
its absence is the harm: an independent board is independent of the particulars. Where the output is a
**rank** against other proposals, proximity supplies the specific doubts that lower a score, and the
reviewer who cannot see the difficulties returns the higher number. A gate asking whether this is sound
wants the near reader; a competition asking whether this beats those penalises them. Same dial, opposite
sign, and the discriminator is whether the answer is a verdict or a position.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the expert reviewer
  as a rival protecting territory, because the evidence is against that reading and because nobody in the
  sequence has a decision to get wrong: the requirement to assign competent readers is the one thing every
  reform of review has agreed on; the difficulties a close reader names are real, and a reviewer who suppressed
  them to be encouraging would fail both the panel and the applicant; the far reviewer assesses accurately
  everything within reach and cannot reach what needs having been in the room; the officer's good matching is
  what makes the depth uneven; the chair's convergence on the better-informed account is correct and completes
  the penalty; and the applicant cannot write the difficulty out without misconduct or the novelty out without
  writing a different proposal. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit
  is that being understood and being weaker arrive as the same number.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Assigning only distant readers
  removes the competence the whole system exists to supply. Statistical adjustment for reviewer severity
  estimates a reviewer effect and cannot separate a harsh reader from a well-informed one, because under this
  mechanism they are the same reader. Blinding the applicant does not touch it, since the distance is between
  the reader and the **subject** rather than between the reader and the person. Panel discussion is a real
  improvement that propagates the deeper reading rather than discounting it. Lotteries above a quality threshold
  move the decision off the scale and give up the ranking the budget was meant to buy. Separate novelty scoring
  asks the same reader to rate novelty and feasibility, and it is the same knowledge that raises one and lowers
  the other. And knowing the whole structure, which the field does and has documented at length, leaves a panel
  meeting on Thursday and a budget that funds down to a line.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the randomised evaluation study, the expertise-and-severity
  analysis and the peer-review literature are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether an expert's
  lower score records a risk correctly perceived or discounts unfamiliar approaches beyond their real risk is
  genuinely contested**, and the play carries that argument rather than settling it; a novel proposal is in fact
  riskier, so the near reviewer's lower score is not shown here to be wrong, only to be differently informed
  from the score it is ranked against; the studies cited come from particular fields, institutions and periods
  and the size of the effect elsewhere is not established here; the account treats grant panels and manuscript
  review as one structure, which is its own simplification, since the aggregation step differs sharply between
  them; and the play makes no claim about the overall accuracy of peer review, which its own sources treat as a
  separate question. **The play makes no claim that any decision was wrong, that any reviewer acted improperly,
  that any applicant was treated unfairly, or that any funder should change its procedures**, only that the
  reader who can judge a novel proposal is the reader who can see why it might fail, and that a rank has no
  column for how far away each reader stood.
