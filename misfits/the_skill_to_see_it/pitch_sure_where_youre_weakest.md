# Pitch: Sure Where You're Weakest

## Taxonomy

The single hook of [The Skill to See It](play_the_skill_to_see_it.md): the default key the misfit is played in, that because you judge your skill with your skill, your confidence runs highest exactly where your skill runs lowest.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Measured, fair, sympathetic. An ordinary person doing an ordinary task and rating, in good faith, how well they did. Nothing here is boasting or bluff; the self-assessment is honest, and honestly wrong.

## Undertow

To tell a good answer from a bad one draws on the same skill that makes a good answer, so the skill to do and the skill to judge the doing are one skill (Kruger and Dunning). A person short of it is short of both halves: they do poorly and cannot tell, since the faculty that would tell them is the one they lack, so they read themselves with a broken ruler and land near the top (Ehrlinger and colleagues). The expert, finding it easy, marks down (Dunning).

## Nerve

That the overconfidence is blindness and not conceit, and that no amount of telling can lift it, since the person must judge the telling with the very faculty they lack, so only becoming more skilled makes them more doubtful. It is held against its limit, the effect contested and much of it perhaps regression and the better-than-average tendency.

## Echo

Because you judge your skill with your skill, you are surest exactly where you are weakest.
