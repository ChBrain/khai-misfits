# Piece: The Lesson

## Load Bearing

The same training that builds competence at a task builds, alongside it, the
metacognition needed to see where one's own earlier attempts fell short
(Kruger and Dunning): self-insight is not delivered by a separate lesson, it
arrives bundled with the skill itself and by no shorter road. Telling a person
outright that they are unskilled does not reliably work, since the message
bounces off the very faculty that is missing; the words can be heard, but the
judgement they require cannot yet be made.

This piece also carries the bound the house holds it to. The effect is
heavily contested: much of the classic pattern is reproducible by regression
to the mean together with the ordinary better-than-average tendency, even run
on random data with no real skill difference behind it at all (Krueger and
Mueller; Gignac and Zajenkowski), and that same regression is what the house
elsewhere stages as its own piece, the bounce. So the size of the effect, and
even its status as a distinct metacognitive deficit rather than a statistical
artifact, remain disputed; the lesson is offered as the remedy the science
points to, not as a settled proof standing beyond question.

## Apparent

Looks like a simple correction: a mentor could just say it in a sentence, and
the gap would close.
