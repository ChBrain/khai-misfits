# More Mouths Than Meat: Reference

The research warrant for More Mouths Than Meat.
The term is the house's; the play runs on predator-prey population dynamics, from Lotka and Volterra's
independent formulation through Gause's experiments to the long boreal record and its modern rereading,
all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the predator-prey cycle as a **coupled system with a lag in it**: two populations, each the other's
control, and a delay between eating well and there being more of you that carries each past the turn it
should have made. The play models the documented mechanics, the coupling in which prey feed predators and
predators thin prey so neither is governed from outside, the reproductive delay that is physiological and
not decisional so nothing anywhere chooses to overshoot, the overshoot in which predator numbers keep
climbing on a prey stock already falling because the young now being born were funded by a plenty that has
already gone, the starvation of a predation pressure that outlives the prey base that paid for it, the
recovery of prey into a world briefly emptied of predators, and the inherited throttle by which the stress
of being hunted lowers breeding and is carried into the next generation, so the rebound is slowed by what
was done to a mother, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of gluttony or
improvidence.

The governing law is that a control acting through a delay cannot stop at the right level. Each population
is regulated by the other, and each responds to a state that has already passed, so the system does not
settle at a balance but circles one, and the circling is not a disturbance to be damped but the ordinary
behaviour of the arrangement.

It is distinct from the house's **Chasing the Price**, whose lag sits in a **decision**: a producer plans
this season on last season's price, and the cycle is made of choices taken on stale information. Here
nothing decides anything at all. The delay is reproductive, a matter of gestation and growth, and it would
run identically in a system with no minds in it.

It is distinct from the house's **Limits to Growth**, where a reinforcing engine meets a balancing limit
through a delay and settles against it: there the limit is a fixed ceiling the growth arrives at, and the
system comes to rest. Here the limit is itself alive, lagged and responsive, so the two chase each other
and never settle.

It is distinct from the house's **The Bullwhip Effect**, which needs a chain of separate actors each adding
a prudent cover to an order: here there is no chain, no order and no actor, only two stocks and the rate
between them. It is distinct from the house's **Safety in Numbers**, where density dependence is positive
and thinning is terminal below a line: here density dependence is negative and the system cycles rather
than collapsing. And it is distinct from the house's **Room to Grow**, where a cull is made deliberately
and the survivors compensate: here nothing is culled on purpose and nobody is managing anything.

## Origin

The phenomenon in practice, and the research spine beneath it.

| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Lotka**                      | _Elements of Physical Biology_ (1925), from the 1920 study                                | **The naming**: two interdependent species can oscillate permanently with no external forcing.                                                              |
| **Volterra**                   | _Variazioni e fluttuazioni_ (1926)                                                        | **The independent formulation, and its occasion**: d'Ancona's question about why the share of predatory fish in Adriatic catches rose when fishing stopped. |
| **Volterra's principle**       | derived from the same model; later the paradox of the pesticides                          | **The remedy that backfires**: a suppression applied to both parties releases the prey, because the predator recovers more slowly than the thing it ate.    |
| **Gause**                      | _The Struggle for Existence_ (1934); _Paramecium bursaria_ on _Schizosaccharomyces pombe_ | **The experimental test, and its condition**: cycles appeared, but only where the prey had a refuge; without one the system ran to extinction, not a cycle. |
| **Elton and Nicholson**        | the Hudson's Bay lynx and snowshoe hare records (1942)                                    | **The long record**: a ten-year cycle held across a continent and two centuries of fur returns.                                                             |
| **Krebs, Boutin and Boonstra** | the Kluane Project (1986 onward); Krebs (2018)                                            | **The rereading**: excluding lynx for ten years did not stop the hares cycling, only changed the amplitude, so the pair alone does not explain it.          |
| **Boonstra; Sheriff**          | predator-induced stress (1998); maternal transmission of that stress                      | **The inherited throttle**: being hunted lowers breeding, and the effect is carried maternally into the next generation.                                    |

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the predator
  as greedy or the prey as feckless, because the source finding is precisely that no behaviour anywhere in
  the loop is excessive: eating is what a predator does and there is no rate at which it could eat
  responsibly, breeding is what a prey population does and it has no way to hold numbers back against a
  hunger it cannot perceive. The overshoot is authored by no appetite and emerges from the structure, a
  control that acts through a delay, so the misfit is that a system in which every party behaves exactly as
  its kind behaves cannot come to rest, and starves itself on a schedule.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The obvious lever is the
  wrong one: Volterra's principle holds that a suppression falling on both parties, a pesticide, a general
  harvest, releases the prey rather than restraining it, because the predator rebuilds more slowly than the
  thing it feeds on, so acting on the numbers makes the next peak higher. What would actually damp the
  cycle is a refuge, the condition Gause found the laboratory system needed to persist at all, and a refuge
  is capacity deliberately withheld from use, defended in the years when it is visibly doing nothing.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the coupled equations, the principle, the experiments
  and the boreal record are theirs. The contested edge is carried explicitly rather than smoothed: the pure
  model is neutrally stable, so its amplitude is set by where it started, and structurally unstable, so a
  slight change to the equations destroys the cycling altogether. The lynx and the hare are the phenomenon's
  most cited illustration and also its most awkward, since the hares go on cycling when the lynx are
  removed. The play therefore stages the mechanism and its delay, not the textbook two-species idealisation,
  and takes the inherited stress the Kluane work found as a vector rather than a footnote.
