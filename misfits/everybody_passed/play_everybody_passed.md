# Play: Everybody Passed

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Everybody Passed.
Ships are inspected in port, the deficiencies are recorded, the detentions are counted over three rolling years, and the flags are ranked from best to worst and published. It works. The registries that carry the world's tonnage answer it, because a place on the white list means fewer inspections and easier business, and the answer shows in the numbers: the three largest open registries are all on the white list, and the best-performing flag in the world belongs to an offshore financial centre. Then a shipowner sits down to choose a flag, looks at the safety column, and finds every serious candidate reading the same. The column is accurate. The improvement it produced is real. And it cannot tell them anything, because a number that is the same everywhere does not choose between anywhere, so the decision is made entirely on the columns nobody publishes.

## Arc

The bend is this: a measure that succeeds well enough to be met everywhere stops being able to distinguish anywhere, so the decision it was built to inform is settled by whatever went unmeasured.
It runs on the difference between a quantity being **good** and a quantity being **informative**, which are not the same property and move in opposite directions here.
Start with the instrument, which is sound. Port state control looks at what an inspector standing on a deck can verify, counts detentions, and publishes a three-year rate by flag. Nothing about it is a proxy that decayed and nothing in it is gamed: the deficiencies are real, the detentions are real, and ships got safer.
And the registries respond correctly, which is the whole point of publishing it. A white-list flag is inspected less and trades more easily, so staying white is worth money, and the ones with the tonnage stay white. That is the regime working exactly as designed, and it is not a race to the bottom: **the flags at the bottom of the table are small, and the flags carrying the fleet are at the top.**
Then the success closes the instrument's own usefulness for choosing. When every candidate a serious owner would consider sits in the same band, the safety column has no variance left, and a column with no variance cannot decide between two options. **The better the regime works, the less flag safety weighs in the choice of flag**, which nobody intended, nobody can be blamed for, and no amount of running the regime better will reverse, since running it better is what causes it.
So the choice goes to the dimensions with variance still in them, and those are the ones nobody ranks: what a registration costs, how fast it is granted, which nationalities may crew the vessel, and how much of the ownership behind it has to be disclosed to anybody. There is no white list for those. There was going to be a rule that reached them, and it is in the treaty: a ship's flag state must have a **genuine link** with the ship. Nobody agreed what that meant, because any definition would have suited some maritime states and cost others, and a convention that defined it would not have been signed. So it is binding and undefined, which is a different thing from absent: the place where the check would go is occupied.
Reach for the fix and each one turns. Publish a second list for the other columns, and you need a party with standing to inspect them, which port state control has for what is on the deck and nothing has for what is in a share register. Enforce the genuine link and you must first agree the definition that was avoided to get agreement at all. Widen the safety index until it discriminates again, and you have asked an inspection regime to stop succeeding.

## Stakes

Whether a measure can go on informing a choice after it has succeeded at the thing it measures.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the inspection regime is any good, because it is good and it worked, but whether success and usefulness can be held at once by the same column. The production cannot restore the variance, because the variance was the harm: a spread in detention rates meant ships were being detained, and closing it is the outcome everybody wanted. It can only show that a quantity being good and a quantity being informative are different properties, that the regime's own success is what emptied its column of information, that the decision then falls to whatever nobody ranks, and that the rule written to reach those columns was left undefined on purpose, because defining it was the price of never agreeing it at all.
