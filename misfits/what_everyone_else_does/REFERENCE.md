# What Everyone Else Does: Reference

The research warrant for What Everyone Else Does.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the customary standard of care in professional negligence, its
qualification, and the doctrine that industry custom is not conclusive, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the customary standard of care as **a ruler made of the thing it measures**: a court deciding
whether a professional was careless needs a standard and holds none of its own, so it asks whether a
responsible body of practitioners would have done the same. That deference is right, because the
alternative is judges settling contested clinical questions on their own reading, and because it protects
careful practitioners from being judged by outcome and by hindsight. It also means the profession's own
practice becomes the legal floor. The play models the documented mechanics, the modesty that is genuine
competence, the practitioner who is blameless and whose blamelessness closes the case, the seal by which a
universal practice cannot be negligent, the early adopter who funds a protection they cannot claim, the
claimant who proves everything except that somebody should have acted otherwise, and the corrections that
raise the floor and install a ceiling in the same motion, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not
as a tale of a captured judiciary or a self-serving profession.

The governing law is that the test asks what is done, so what is done becomes what is required, and the
mechanism contains no input through which the answer could be told it is too low. A method nobody uses is
not required, because no responsible body uses it. A method everybody uses cannot be negligent, because a
responsible body does. The seal closes at both ends.

What removes the easy reading is that no participant is at fault. The practitioner did what any colleague
would have done and what their training required, so there is no departure to identify. The judge is right
to defer, and the deference is bounded rather than absolute. The experts are honest, and every witness
qualified to answer the question is a member of the profession being judged.

What sharpens it is the direction of the incentive. Protection under this test comes from what others do
rather than from what you do, so adopting a safer method confers none, while declining is fully covered for
as long as enough others also decline. The method becomes compulsory at the moment it stops being an
improvement and becomes the usual thing, which means it protects everybody who adopts it afterwards and
nobody who adopted it before: the people who make the standard rise are the only people it never covers.

And the crack is old. In 1932 Learned Hand held that a whole calling may have unduly lagged in the adoption
of new and available devices, and that courts must in the end say what is required, there being precautions
so imperative that even their universal disregard will not excuse their omission. The principle is
quotable, correct and available, and in most of the settings where this misfit operates the working
standard is still what a responsible body accepts, because using the principle means a tribunal overruling
the only witnesses qualified to speak to the question.

It is distinct from the house's **The Done Thing**, where organisations adopt the recognised form for
standing so a field locks onto a shape chosen for legitimacy rather than fit: there the mechanism is
imitation for the sake of appearing proper, driven by what confers status. Here nobody is imitating for
standing, and the lock is not social but legal: an external tribunal has adopted the practice as the test,
so the practice acquires the force of a duty rather than the comfort of a convention.

It is distinct from the house's **The New Normal**, where each small deviation is tolerated once and becomes
the standard so the line of acceptable creeps until it fails: there the standard degrades through repeated
judgements, each one a departure that is let pass. Here nothing degrades and nothing is a departure. The
practice may be perfectly stable and perfectly performed, and the failure is that it cannot be raised.

It is distinct from the house's **Path Dependence**, where a lead won by accident compounds as each adopter
picks what others picked until a worse standard locks in: there adoption is self-reinforcing through
network effects and switching costs, and the lock is economic. Here the better method may be cheap,
available and obviously superior, and it is still not required, because the barrier is a legal test rather
than a cost of switching.

It is distinct from the house's **Clear from Here**, where hindsight makes a sound decision with bad luck
look like negligence: there the danger is a court judging by outcome, and the customary standard is the
protection against it. Here that same protection is the mechanism, so the two misfits are opposite faces of
one rule, and removing the danger in that one is what creates this one.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the court as
  captured or the profession as self-serving, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: a
  judge without clinical training who declined to defer would be deciding contested professional questions
  on their own reading, which is worse in every case anybody has proposed, and would expose careful
  practitioners to judgement by outcome; the practitioner followed the method they were taught and their
  colleagues use, competently and without shortcut; the experts are honest, and every witness qualified to
  answer belongs to the profession under examination; the guideline body is doing the most effective thing
  available. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the
  duty is read off the conduct it is supposed to be measuring.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Rejecting an opinion that
  will not withstand logical analysis is a real bound and requires a judge to overrule a body of expert
  evidence, which is done rarely and cautiously and for good reason. Holding that availability creates
  obligation ahead of adoption is exactly what was decided in 1932 and has not displaced the customary
  standard in the settings where this misfit lives. Codifying best practice raises the floor immediately
  and then freezes it, since the document becomes what a responsible body follows, errors and omissions
  included, and departing from it to follow newer evidence becomes the risky act rather than the careful
  one, on an update cycle measured in years.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the judgments and the doctrine are theirs. The bounds
  are carried with them: the strength of the customary standard varies enormously by jurisdiction and by
  profession, and several systems have moved substantially away from it, including in the distinct area of
  advice and consent where a patient-centred test has largely replaced the professional one; the
  qualification in Bolitho is a genuine change and courts do apply it, so the seal described here is
  tighter in the account than in every courtroom; the T.J. Hooper principle is live law in its own
  jurisdiction rather than a dead letter, and custom is generally evidence of the standard rather than
  conclusive of it in ordinary negligence, with medical negligence the field where deference has run
  furthest; and the incentive argument about early adoption follows from how the test allocates protection
  rather than from a study measuring adoption rates. **The play makes no claim that courts should decide
  clinical questions for themselves**, only that a standard read off the average was never going to raise
  the average.
