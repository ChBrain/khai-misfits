# Pitch: When It Comes Back

## Taxonomy

[Back to Even](play_back_to_even.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Sensible and disciplined.
An investor who takes a gain when they have one and will not sell a good holding at a loss, each move looking prudent on its own.
Nothing false in it: taking a sure gain is reasonable, and not dumping a sound company in a dip is reasonable too.

## Undertow

Under the prudence, a line drawn by the price paid.
A holding above what was paid is cashed to lock the sure gain, and a holding below it is kept to gamble on a rebound, the same reflected value function, risk-averse over gains and risk-seeking over losses, seen on its two sides (Tversky and Kahneman; Shefrin and Statman).
No greed and no folly: the gain is real and taken, the loser is held for a recovery that may come, and yet the winners sold climb on and the losers kept sink further.

## Nerve

The plainness of taking gains and holding through a dip, and the catch that the pattern cuts gains short and rides losses long:
that the price paid, which is sunk and should not weigh, is the line the sale turns on, and selling the loser means realising the loss and admitting it,
so the resolve is to sell only once it is back to even, waiting on a price the market has no reason to return to, while the account quietly falls behind.

## Echo

After the run: the winners sold that went on to beat the losers kept, the account bleeding return and, through the tax, a little more, worn as bad luck because no single trade was foolish, only the pattern (Odean).
And the remedy, at a price: judge holdings by prospects and not by what you paid, cut losers on their merits, lean on the year-end tax-loss selling that turns the effect around, each asking the investor to drop the purchase price and admit the losses it hid.
And the bound held in view, because the effect is robust but not a law of the ledger: it is reversed at year-end and lessened by rules and sophistication, and holding a fallen holding is not always wrong, only the systematic asymmetry is.
