# Back to Even: Reference

The research warrant for **Back to Even**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon the literature
calls the **disposition effect**: Shefrin and Statman's finding that investors sell winners too
early and ride losers too long, Odean's evidence from tens of thousands of accounts that the winners
sold go on to beat the losers kept, and Tversky and Kahneman's value function beneath it, all cited
below, held with the finding that the effect is robust but bounded, reversing where tax or
sophistication or a rule cuts across it.

## Line of Work

Staging the disposition effect as a **reference-point-splits-the-sell-decision structure**: an
investor holds a portfolio, and the price they paid for each position becomes the reference point
that divides it into winners, now worth more than was paid, and losers, now worth less. Faced with
which to sell, the investor sells the winners and holds the losers, the opposite of what returns and
taxes would counsel. The reason is the shape of the value function: people are risk-averse in the
domain of gains, so a winner tempts them to lock in the sure gain and sell, and risk-seeking in the
domain of losses, so a loser tempts them to gamble on a recovery rather than accept the sure loss,
and realising a loss means closing the account at a loss and admitting it, which loss aversion and
regret make painful (Shefrin and Statman; Tversky and Kahneman). So gains are cut short and losses
are nursed in the hope of getting back to even, and the cost is real: Odean, tracking tens of
thousands of brokerage accounts, found investors sold winners at a far higher rate than losers, and
the winners they sold went on to outperform the losers they kept, so the disposition to sell winners
and hold losers lowered returns, and selling winners rather than losers raised taxes besides (Odean).
The governing law is not that anyone is careless, but that **because the price paid becomes the
reference point and people are risk-averse over gains but risk-seeking over losses, and realising a
loss is painful, investors sell holdings that have risen and hold holdings that have fallen, so gains
are cut short and losses are ridden in hope of a rebound, and returns and taxes both suffer**. The
play models the documented mechanics: the disposition to sell winners early and ride losers long and
its roots in prospect theory, mental accounting, and regret (Shefrin and Statman), the account
evidence that the sold winners beat the held losers so the behaviour costs return (Odean), and the
reference-dependent value function, risk-averse in gains and risk-seeking in losses, beneath it
(Tversky and Kahneman), as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a foolish
investor who simply picked wrong.

It is a decision-under-a-reference-point interaction no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully
distinct from its nearest neighbours. It is distinct from Good Money After Bad, the escalation of
commitment, where fresh resources are poured into a failing course because of what has already been
sunk: there new money is committed and the sunk cost pulls it, whereas here no new money is put in,
only a losing position left unclosed and a winning one closed too soon, the lever the aversion to
realising a loss and not the weight of a sunk cost. It is distinct from Loath to Part, the endowment
effect, where merely owning a good lifts its value so it will not be sold at all: there ownership
inflates the price of any holding and blocks trade in both directions, whereas here a holding is
readily sold if it is a winner and stubbornly kept if it is a loser, so the reference point decides
not whether to trade but which positions to shed and which to hold. It is distinct from The
High-Water Mark, the ratchet of spending that each crisis lifts and no calm lowers: there the lever
is a floor that only climbs, whereas here the lever is the purchase price as a reference point and
the reversed appetite for risk above and below it, and nothing ratchets, the winner is sold and gone.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the
  investor who holds the loser as weak or the one who sells the winner as greedy, because the source
  finding it stages is that both moves follow from the shape of the value function: over a gain, the
  sure thing is tempting and selling the winner is the risk-averse choice, and over a loss, the gamble
  on recovery is tempting and holding the loser is the risk-seeking choice, and realising a loss is
  genuinely painful to admit, so the behaviour is a systematic bias and not a lapse of character
  (Shefrin and Statman; Tversky and Kahneman). The loss is authored by no one: it is the return given
  up when winners are cut short and losers ridden, and the misfit is that the reference point of what
  was paid, which is sunk and should not bear on the decision, is exactly what rules it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The forces that undo
  it exist, a rule that closes positions on their prospects and not their entry price, the discipline
  to realise a loss, the tax-loss selling that at year-end reverses the effect for a season, and the
  sophistication that lessens it in professionals (Odean; Shefrin and Statman), but none is free or
  general: a rule must be set and kept against the pull of the reference point, realising a loss stays
  painful each time, and holding a loser is not always wrong, since sometimes a fallen price truly does
  recover, so the discipline cannot be a blanket sell any more than a blanket hold. The reckoning is
  that the purchase price is a reference point the mind will not easily drop, so the pull to sell
  winners and ride losers returns whenever the guard is down.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here,
  never claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the disposition effect and its four-part
  explanation are Shefrin and Statman's, the account evidence that the sold winners beat the held
  losers is Odean's, and the reference-dependent, reflected value function beneath it is Tversky and
  Kahneman's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: that the effect requires a
  reference point set by the purchase price and the reflection of risk appetite across it, that the
  harm the play centers is the return and the tax given up and not merely a feeling, and that the
  effect is robust but bounded, reversing under tax-loss selling and weaker among sophisticated
  traders and where a rule governs the sale (Odean). The scope is held to the sell decision under a
  purchase-price reference point, and not stretched into a claim that holding a fallen asset is always
  wrong or that every sale of a winner is a mistake.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Purchase Price**: carries the reference point (Shefrin and Statman), the price paid that the
  mind keeps as the baseline, dividing each holding into a winner above it or a loser below, a figure
  that is sunk and should not bear on the sell decision yet rules it.
- **The Turn**: carries the reflection in the value function (Tversky and Kahneman), the flip from
  risk-averse over gains to risk-seeking over losses across the reference point, so a winner is a sure
  gain to lock in and a loser is a gamble to hold, the engine of the whole asymmetry.
- **The Sold Winner**: carries the winner cut short (Odean), the position that rose and is sold to
  bank the gain, its further climb foregone and a tax on the realised gain incurred, the sold winners
  in the evidence going on to beat the losers kept.
- **The Held Loser**: carries the loser ridden long (Shefrin and Statman; Odean), the position that
  fell and is kept, the loss unrealised in the hope of getting back to even, the realisation deferred
  because admitting it is painful.
- **The Cost**: carries the reckoning (Odean), the return given up when winners are cut and losers
  held and the tax worsened by realising gains and not losses, the measurable price of letting the
  purchase price rule the sale.
- **The Bound**: carries the boundary and the hedge (Odean; Shefrin and Statman), the tax-loss
  selling that reverses the effect at year-end, the sophistication that lessens it, and the rule that
  can override it, so the trap is robust but not a law, and holding a fallen asset is not always the
  error, only the systematic asymmetry is.
