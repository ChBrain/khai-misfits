# Everything Got Through: Reference

The research warrant for Everything Got Through.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the coordinated attack problem, on common knowledge as the
precondition for simultaneous action, and on the strategic discontinuity between almost common
knowledge and common knowledge, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging coordination over an imperfect channel as **an agreement that cannot be reached by sending**:
two parties who both want the same thing, who never mislead each other, whose messages all arrive, and
who cannot commit to acting together. The play models the documented mechanics, the plan whose
simultaneity is a fact about the ground rather than a preference, the channel that delivers every time
and reports nothing, the acknowledgement that improves the position and never closes it, the exposure
that changes address rather than shrinking, and the discontinuity that makes near certainty a
different substance from certainty, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of
timid officers or of a bad road.

The governing law is that acting together requires more than both parties knowing the time. It
requires each to know that the other knows, and to know that the other knows that they know, without
end, and every message that arrives raises that tower by exactly one floor. The requirement is the
whole tower; the supply is one floor per crossing; **the shortfall is therefore constant at every
height**, which is why a protocol that has run a thousand times is exactly as far from the top as it
was at the start.

What removes any possibility of blame is that nothing fails. Every crossing in the production succeeds,
every note is correct, every reply is prompt and unreserved, and both officers reason validly and reach
true conclusions. The play deliberately never rests on a lost message: what the result requires is that
a message **might** not arrive, and a channel crossed successfully a hundred times still might not
deliver the hundred and first. The condition is the possibility of loss and not its rate, which is why
better riders do not help and why a channel known with certainty to be perfect, which no commander has,
would close the tower in a single crossing.

What makes it structural is that the harm has an address and moves. At every moment exactly one party
holds a message whose arrival they cannot establish, and the other can see them holding it, so sending
again does not empty that seat but relocates it. Effort here is genuine, is always reasonable, and is
not cumulative: it looks like progress from either side and is stationary from above.

And the correction removes the requirement rather than meeting it. Agreeing in person, before the
parties separate, works completely and is unavailable for anything arising afterwards. A standing order
to act at a named time regardless of confirmation dissolves the need for common knowledge by dissolving
the need to know anything, and means acting alone if the other side never received the time. A rule
naming which party proceeds on an unconfirmed message assigns the risk instead of removing it. And
waiting for certainty is the deadline arriving with nothing decided, because the messages can be sent
without limit and the hour comes once.

It is distinct from the house's **Both Take the Crown**, where a partitioned system must choose between
consistency and availability: there the quantity is the partition, and stipulating a network that never
partitions removes the harm entirely. Here the impossibility holds for a channel that merely may lose a
message and in a production where nothing is ever lost, so a network that never partitions leaves this
untouched.

It is distinct from the house's **The Common Object**, where parties over-estimate how much of their
understanding is shared: there the quantity is the misestimate, and stipulating perfectly calibrated
parties removes the harm. Here both officers estimate the other's position exactly right, and being
right is what stops them: **the failure is not a misestimate but a theorem**.

It is distinct from the house's **The Ear It Was Heard With**, where meaning is settled by what the
receiver takes from a message rather than by what the sender put into it: there the content is at
issue. Here the content is unambiguous, agreed and identical on both hills, and what is missing is not
what the note said but whether it arrived.

It is distinct from the house's **After You**, where a coordination game has more than one stable point
and the difficulty is which one the parties land on: there the quantity is the multiplicity of
equilibria, and stipulating a single obvious point removes the harm. Here there is exactly one point,
both parties have chosen it, and they still cannot move to it.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast either
  officer as timid or the riders as unreliable, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get
  wrong: the plan's simultaneity is a fact about the enemy rather than a preference of the planners; the
  hour is well chosen and sent at once; the confirmation is prompt, correct and unreserved; every
  crossing succeeds; and both officers reason validly to true conclusions about each other. The failure
  is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that agreement is not the kind
  of thing that can be reached by sending.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Agreeing in person
  before the parties separate works completely and cannot be applied to anything that arises after.
  A standing order removes the need for common knowledge by removing the need to know anything, at the
  price of acting alone if the other side never received the time. A rule naming which party proceeds
  on an unconfirmed message assigns the risk rather than dissolving it. Weaker notions of shared
  knowledge, of the eventual and approximate kinds, are what working systems settle for and are
  genuinely useful, and each of them buys tractability by giving up the simultaneity the plan required.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the epistemic logic and the game theory are
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the impossibility is a result about **simultaneous** action,
  and a great many coordination problems that look like this one do not in fact require simultaneity and
  are solved routinely by protocols that tolerate one party acting first; the practical distributed
  systems literature has built a large body of agreement protocols that work by weakening the
  requirement rather than by defeating the result, and their success is not a counterexample to it; and
  the two generals framing is the literature's own image for the structure rather than a claim about
  military history. **The formal statements are deliberately not quoted here**, because the papers
  themselves could not be read in the pass that cited them: publisher hosts and the article archive are
  blocked to it, so the bibliography, the results and their directions were verified by search and
  corroborated across independent reports, which is the treatment a citation survives and a theorem
  statement is not. **The play makes no claim that coordination is impossible**, only that agreement to
  act at the same instant is not among the things a channel can deliver.
