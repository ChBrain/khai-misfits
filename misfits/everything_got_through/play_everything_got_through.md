# Play: Everything Got Through

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Everything Got Through.
Two forces on two hills, a valley between them held by the enemy, and an attack that succeeds only if both go at once and fails badly if one goes alone.
A time is proposed. The messenger crosses. The confirmation crosses back. The confirmation of the confirmation crosses again.
Every single messenger arrived. Nobody lied, nobody delayed, nobody misread anything, and the two generals cannot agree to attack.

## Arc

The bend is this: the obstacle is not that messages are lost. It is that they might be, and the parties are honest.

It runs on one relation from logic and one from the ground. **Acting together requires common knowledge of the time**, which is not merely that both know it, but that each knows the other knows, and knows that the other knows that they know, without end; and **each message that arrives raises that tower by exactly one floor and never completes it**, because the party who sent the last message cannot know it landed.

Take the messages first, because the play has to establish that the channel is **working**. Every crossing in this production succeeds. There is no interception, no forgery, no delay, no misreading and no ambiguity in any order. If the play needed a lost message it would be a play about an unreliable valley, and it is not: the valley in this production delivers everything it is given.

Then the fact that inverts it. **The sender of the last message is the one who is exposed.** Whoever wrote the most recent note knows what they meant and does not know it arrived, so they cannot go; whoever received it knows the sender is in exactly that position, so they cannot go either. Send another and the exposure moves to the other hill. It does not shrink, it changes address, and it will do that for as long as either general has a messenger left.

Then the sharper half, which is why more effort is not the answer. **Almost is a different thing from all the way.** With enough crossings each general is nearly certain, and near certainty is not what the plan requires: a coordination that only pays when both move gives no party a reason to move on a near certainty, because the other party is reasoning about the same near certainty one floor down. So the behaviour under ninety-nine confirmations is the behaviour under none, which is the part that reads as absurd and is the part that is proved.

And nobody has done anything wrong. The first general proposed a sound time. The second confirmed it immediately. The messengers ran, and arrived, and ran again. The plan is a good plan and the reason it requires simultaneity is that the enemy in the valley is strong enough to beat either force alone.

Reach for a settlement and each one gives up the thing that was wanted. Agree in advance, in person, and you have moved the coordination to a moment when there was no channel to fail, which works and cannot be done for anything that arises later. Attack on a schedule regardless of confirmation and you have replaced agreement with a standing order, which is a real answer and means going alone if the other did not receive it. Accept a rule that says attack if you have sent or received a message and you have chosen who bears the risk rather than removed it. And wait for certainty and you are on the hill in the morning with the messenger's tracks in the snow.

## Triggers

**[A Time Is Named](plot_a_time_is_named.md)**
The plan established as sound rather than as the villain: the enemy in the valley beats either force alone, so simultaneity is a requirement of the ground and not a preference of the planners. Closes when the hour is proposed, is a good hour, and the first messenger goes out.

**[It Arrives](plot_it_arrives.md)**
Cue: a messenger on the far slope, unhurt. Closes when the channel is shown to be working perfectly and the problem to be untouched: the note is delivered, is read, is correct, and the first general still does not know any of that.

**[Confirm the Confirmation](plot_confirm_the_confirmation.md)**
Cue: the second general's reply, in hand. Closes when the regress is in the open: each crossing raises the tower by one floor, the exposure moves from hill to hill without shrinking, and there is no crossing after which either party may go.

**[Almost Is Not Enough](plot_almost_is_not_enough.md)**
Cue: the ninety-ninth confirmation. Closes when near certainty is shown not to be the currency the plan takes: each party is reasoning about the other's near certainty one floor down, so the behaviour under many confirmations is the behaviour under none, and that is a result rather than a failure of nerve.

**[And Somebody Has to Move First](plot_and_somebody_has_to_move_first.md)**
Cue: the settlements. Closes when each is found to give up what was wanted: agreement in person moves the problem to a moment with no channel, a standing order replaces agreement with going alone, a rule about who attacks assigns the risk rather than removing it, and waiting for certainty is the morning with tracks in the snow.

## Stakes

Whether two honest parties who both want the same thing can ever be sure enough of each other to act together.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the messengers are reliable, because every one of them arrives, and not whether either general is willing, because both are, but whether agreement is the kind of thing that can be reached by sending. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the plan is sound, that the channel delivers, that the last sender is always the one exposed, that the exposure changes address rather than shrinking, and that near certainty is a different substance from the certainty the plan asks for.
