# Plot: What You Mean to Keep

## Taxonomy

[Loath to Part](play_loath_to_part.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

At [The Floor](place_the_floor.md), the trader's ease sits beside the ordinary owner, who cannot summon it.

## Action

The bound is drawn. [The Exchange](piece_the_exchange.md) dissolves the gap for seasoned traders and for goods held to be sold on, but a house, an heirloom, a thing held for keeping and not for trade, still clings to its owner, and [The Reference Point](piece_the_reference_point.md) re-forms around whatever is now held. The scene closes with the effect bound but not banished: real still, for the ordinary owner of the things they mean to keep.

## Stage

The effect fades with market experience and for goods held for exchange (List), and part of the original gap is contested as an artifact of elicitation (Plott and Zeiler). Yet for ordinary owners, of ordinary goods held to keep rather than to trade, the effect is robust (Thaler; Kahneman, Knetsch, and Thaler). The trap is bounded, not a law of all ownership: no claim is made that owners can never sell, only that most, of most things, do not sell as easily as a frictionless market would predict.

## Tension

The fix is seasoning and holding-for-exchange, and that is exactly what the things people most mean to keep never get.
