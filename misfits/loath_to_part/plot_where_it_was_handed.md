# Plot: Where It Was Handed

## Taxonomy

[Loath to Part](play_loath_to_part.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

At [The Draw](place_the_draw.md), the same good could have been handed to either party to begin with.

## Action

[The Allocator](persona_the_allocator.md), from [the allocator's seat](position_allocator.md), runs [Trust the Trade](plan_trust_the_trade.md): assigning the good and expecting trade to move it afterward to whoever values it most. Through [Freezing](process_freezing.md), the allocation sticks wherever it was first handed, because the endowment makes the first holder loath to part with it. The scene closes with the final owner decided not by who values the good most, but by who was handed it first.

## Stage

The Coase theorem holds that, transaction costs aside, the final allocation of a good is independent of who first holds it: trade moves it to its highest-valuing user regardless of the starting point. The endowment effect breaks this: the first assignment is sticky, and in the experiments the predicted volume of trade does not occur (Kahneman, Knetsch, and Thaler). Keep the cut clean: this is not the Anticommons. A single owner will trade the good, and can deliver the whole of it, only at a price that ownership has inflated.

## Tension

Who happens to hold a thing first decides where it comes to rest, though trade was supposed to make that irrelevant.
