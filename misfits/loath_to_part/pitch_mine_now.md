# Pitch: Mine Now

## Taxonomy

[Loath to Part](play_loath_to_part.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Ordinary and fair.
An owner who will not sell for a low price and a buyer who will not pay a high one, each naming an honest figure, neither being greedy or a fool.
Nothing false in it: valuing what you own, and not letting it go cheap, is ordinary and reasonable, and the buyer who will not overpay is reasonable too.

## Undertow

Under the fair prices, a worth that rose for nothing but the having.
Hand someone a mug and their price to give it back is about twice what a matched buyer will pay for the same mug, because owning it made keeping it the baseline and parting a loss, and a loss weighs more than the equal gain of acquiring (Kahneman, Knetsch, and Thaler; Tversky and Kahneman).
No greed and no error: the owner's higher price is what the good is now worth to them, not a ploy, and the buyer's lower price is honest too, so the two honest prices simply do not meet.

## Nerve

The plainness of not letting your things go cheap, and the catch that the not-letting-go is what keeps them from moving:
that the value and the reluctance are the one reference point seen from two sides, the good dearer to its holder precisely because it is theirs to lose,
so a trade both would have gained from goes unmade, and the good comes to rest where it first fell rather than where it is wanted most.

## Echo

After the run: the mug unsold though a buyer wanted it more, the good stuck with whoever was handed it first, the Coase theorem broken and a surplus foregone that no one can see, a loss authored by no one because nothing was taken (Kahneman, Knetsch, and Thaler).
And the bound that keeps it from being a law: a seasoned trader who holds goods for exchange shows little of the effect, so market experience wears it away (List), and part of the measured gap is contested (Plott and Zeiler).
But the seasoning that dissolves it is exactly what the ordinary owner of a home, an heirloom, a thing held to keep, never has, so for them the reference point re-forms around whatever they hold, and what is theirs stays dearer to them than to anyone.
