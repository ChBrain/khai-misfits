# Persona: The Owner

## Taxonomy

[Owner](position_owner.md). The one who holds the good and, once it is theirs, values it above what any buyer will pay, because parting with it is felt as a loss and a loss weighs more than the equal gain of acquiring it.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Projection

Reasonable, attached, honest.
Holds an ordinary good that became worth more the moment it was theirs, and would not dream of selling it for a price they would not themselves have paid to get it.
No greed in it and no calculation: the good simply feels dearer now that it is theirs to lose, and the price they would take reflects that dearness truthfully.

## Action

Holds what's mine on [Hold What's Mine](plan_hold_whats_mine.md), receiving [the Holding](piece_the_holding.md) through [Endowing](process_endowing.md) at [the Draw](place_the_draw.md), where ownership sets [the Reference Point](piece_the_reference_point.md) so that keeping the good is the baseline and parting would be a loss.
Cannot value the good as a non-owner would, because the reference point has moved to include it, so surrendering it now registers as a loss that a buyer, who never held it, does not feel.
Does what a reasonable owner does: it names, honestly, the price at which it would accept the loss of the thing, and that price sits above what the same thing is worth to anyone who does not own it.

## Shadow

Not greedy for asking a high price and not foolish for loving what it owns: the Owner's price is what the good is genuinely worth to them now that it is theirs, so the figure is honest and the attachment is real, not a bargaining pose.
Because the value and the reluctance are the same reference point, the Owner cannot want to keep the good and price it to sell at once, so the one most attached to the thing is the one least able to let it move to whoever wants it more.
It is the one who values its own the most, and the one whose valuing is what keeps the good from reaching a buyer who would value it still.

## Tell

Says it would never sell for that, though that is more than it would have paid, and hears no contradiction, because parting and acquiring feel different from where it stands.
Cannot be blamed for the price, because the loss it would feel is real and the figure names it truly, so it holds its own soundly and the trade that would have bettered both goes undone.
