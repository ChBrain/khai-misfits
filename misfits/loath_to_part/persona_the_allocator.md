# Persona: The Allocator

## Taxonomy

[Allocator](position_allocator.md). The one who assigns who first holds the good, the experimenter dealing mugs, the law fixing an entitlement, the default that lands a holding, trusting trade to move it to whoever values it most, and unwittingly deciding where it comes to rest.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Projection

Fair, unworried, mistaken.
Hands the good to someone to begin with, by lot or by rule, believing the choice of first holder does not matter, since trade will carry the good to whoever wants it most.
No carelessness in it: the belief is the Coase theorem, a respectable one, that free trade makes the initial allocation irrelevant to the final.

## Action

Trusts the trade on [Trust the Trade](plan_trust_the_trade.md), assigning [the Holding](piece_the_holding.md) at [the Draw](place_the_draw.md), and through [Freezing](process_freezing.md) the allocation sticks wherever it was first handed, because the endowment makes the first holder loath to part.
Cannot make the first assignment irrelevant, because ownership itself changes what the good is worth to its holder, so the good does not reshuffle by trade to who values it most but stays with whoever was handed it.
Does what a fair allocator does trusting a sound theorem: it assigns and steps back, and the stepping back is what lets the arbitrary start become the final rest.

## Shadow

Not careless for trusting trade and not cynical in the assigning: the Allocator relies on the Coase theorem, which is correct when ownership does not change value, and is caught only because the endowment effect breaks that premise.
Because the good will not move once held, the choice the Allocator thought idle is the choice that decides everything, so the one who believed the first assignment did not matter is the one whose first assignment settles the outcome.
It is the one who trusted the market to correct any starting point, and the one whose starting point the market then failed to correct.

## Tell

Says it does not matter who gets it first, the market will sort it out, and is stating a theorem that is true only where ownership leaves value unchanged.
Cannot be blamed for the trust, because the theorem is sound on its own terms, so it assigns in good faith and finds the good frozen where it fell, the allocation it thought provisional made final by a reluctance it did not reckon on.
