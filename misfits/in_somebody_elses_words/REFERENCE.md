# In Somebody Else's Words: Reference

The research warrant for In Somebody Else's Words.
The term is the house's; the play runs on court interpreting and on what happens when the manner a room reads
credibility from is produced by a second speaker, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging interpreted testimony as **a signal authored twice**: an interpreter is required by a code to convey
what was said without addition or omission, and to remain a conduit rather than a participant. Both
requirements are right and a code missing either would be worse. But hedging, hesitation, politeness and the
length of an answer are not ornament on the content: they are a second channel telling a listener how firmly a
thing is held, and it is the channel a room reads credibility from. No rendering carries that channel and the
fluency at once, so the interpreter chooses several times a sentence, and the fact-finder weighs the choice as
though it were the witness. The play models the documented mechanics, the two duties that diverge, the
pragmatic layer that is genuinely informative, the rendering with no neutral version, the room performing its
instructed task on merged material, the record that keeps one language, and the experiment that demonstrates
the effect and closes the clean repair, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a
careless interpreter or an indifferent court.

The governing law is that the party being assessed for credibility has no access to the object being assessed,
because the manner reaching the room was produced by somebody else and nothing marks the seam.

**Every seat performs its own task correctly, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.**
The interpreter is certified, the terminology is right, and no error is detectable in the room. The fact-finder
is doing exactly what the role instructs. Counsel presses on the pause and the qualifier, which is what
cross-examination is for. The transcript omits nothing said in the language it keeps.

What removes any possibility of blame from the profession is that the two duties are the ones anybody would
write. An interpreter licensed to summarise would be deciding the case; one licensed to advocate would be a
party. Accuracy and invisibility are each indispensable, and they pull apart on every sentence with a hedge in
it, because a hedge is content in one sense and manner in another: preserving it is an addition to fluency and
dropping it is an omission from the answer.

What removes it from the fact-finder is that reading credibility from delivery is the instruction rather than a
lapse. No direction tells anybody to disregard hesitation, and none could, since hesitation is genuinely
informative and a witness who hedges honestly is telling the room something true. The seat has no way to know
whose pauses these were, no way to ask, and receives a single stream in one language that the setting offers no
means of decomposing.

**And the effect is demonstrated rather than argued, which is what makes this stageable.** The monolingual
baseline was established first: with the substantive evidence held fixed, a witness using hedges, hesitation
forms and intensifiers is rated less convincing, competent and trustworthy than the same evidence delivered
without them. Then the interpreted case was measured on the same logic, with recordings of real proceedings and
matched versions differing only in the pragmatic layer, and the ratings moved. So the interpreter is not
inventing an effect: they are unavoidably authoring a variable already known to move verdict-relevant
judgements.

**And the same result closes the obvious repair.** If style always does work, there is no styleless rendering to
fall back on, so the remedy cannot be to strip the layer out. What is left are partial measures, each of which
costs something.

**The record is the part that outlasts everything.** A court transcribes the language it acts in, which is
correct and is not an omission: everything said in that language appears. But the original utterance is not
kept, so an appeal, a review or a later reader has one text and no second text to set beside it, and the
question of whose hedge it was cannot be asked afterwards either. A two-language record would need a second
transcriber, a second certification and its own disputes.

It is distinct from the house's **The Same Print Twice**, where an examiner's judgement is contaminated by
context that could be withheld, so the difficulty is information reaching somebody who did not need it and the
repair is available. Here nothing is withheld from anybody and no party knows anything they should not: the
distortion is produced by the act of rendering, which is the thing the room exists to have done, and there is no
version of it to withhold.

It is distinct from the house's **Marked the Same by Anyone**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages the
other shape of the same family. There a scoring instrument's demand for agreement between markers is what
suppresses the property being measured, so the constraint is reliability and the casualty is the divergent
candidate. Here nothing demands agreement, no instrument is being scored, and the property is not suppressed
but **re-authored** by a second person in the seconds available. One misfit is about what a standard of
consistency cannot reward; this one is about whose performance a room is weighing.

It is distinct from the house's **The Front**, where a person manages a presented self and pays for the
managing. The witness here manages nothing and is not performing: the hedges are honest and unmanaged, and what
reaches the room is a presentation the witness did not give and cannot inspect. One misfit is the cost of
holding a front, this one is being read off a front somebody else held for you.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the interpreter as
  careless or the court as indifferent, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the code's
  two duties are each indispensable and a code missing either would be worse; the witness's hedges are honest
  and are doing real work; the interpreter is the only party who can see the problem and has no move that
  avoids it; counsel presses on the pause and the qualifier, which is what the role requires; the fact-finder
  weighs manner with matter because that is the instruction and no direction could sensibly say otherwise; the
  transcript omits nothing said in the language it keeps; and the researcher establishes the effect completely.
  The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the person being
  assessed for credibility has no access to the object being assessed.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Team interpreting and
  briefing improve the rendering and do not make it neutral, since there is no neutral rendering to converge
  on. Recording both languages preserves the comparison and requires a second transcriber, a second
  certification and its own disputes about which version governs. Instructing juries about interpretation
  raises a difficulty they cannot act on and may simply shift how the witness is discounted. Training
  interpreters to preserve pragmatic force is real and is being done, and buys coherence for fluency at the
  point where a hearing is hardest to follow. Conducting proceedings in the witness's language moves the
  problem to everybody else in the room. And knowing the whole structure, which the profession does, leaves a
  witness in the box on Tuesday and a question that has to be answered now.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the courtroom recordings and mock-juror experiments, the
  speech-style studies, the discourse analysis of interpreting and the interactional account of the role are
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **how far mock-juror results carry to real juries is genuinely
  contested**, and the play rests on the direction of the effect rather than on any effect size; the conduit
  and participant models of the interpreter's role are an open argument inside the profession, which the play
  carries rather than settles; the studies cited are largely from particular language pairs and jurisdictions
  and the account generalises further than any one of them does; and the play treats codes of conduct as one
  standard where they differ between jurisdictions and between spoken and signed language interpreting. **The
  play makes no claim that any interpreter erred, that any verdict was wrong, or that interpreted testimony
  should be treated with suspicion**, only that the manner a room reads credibility from is produced by a
  second speaker, and that nothing in the room, the code or the record marks where one ends and the other
  begins.
