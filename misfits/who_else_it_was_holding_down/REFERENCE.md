# Who Else It Was Holding Down: Reference

The research warrant for Who Else It Was Holding Down.
The term is ecology's; the play runs on mesopredator release and on what happens when an intervention
succeeds completely on its target, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging predator control as **an intervention that works and inverts**: an apex predator takes stock, the losses
are tallied, and it is removed. The removal succeeds. What the tally has no line for is that the same appetite
had been killing and harrying every smaller predator in the range, so those rise, range further, forage by day
and breed up, and they take nests, eggs and small vertebrates in numbers the large one never did. The play
models the documented mechanics, the loss that is real and competently relieved, the suppression that is the
same act as the offence, the release produced by a subtraction rather than by any agent, the third party whose
cost exceeds what was prevented, the ledger with no column to record it in, and the restoration that is right in
principle and contested in effect, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of an
exaggerating stockman or a careless agency.

The governing law is that the removal of a predator can raise total predation, because the removed animal was
suppressing more predation than it performed, and the party that pays is not represented anywhere in the
transaction.

**The programme succeeds, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The target is reduced,
the reduction is verified, and stock losses fall. Every figure in the return is correct and would survive any
audit, because an audit checks the columns that exist against the world and never checks the world for columns.

What removes any possibility of blame from the stockman is that the losses are real and the question asked was
what was lost. Apex predators do take stock, the takes are counted, and the person carrying that cost is
entitled to ask for relief. What the animal does to smaller predators happens away from the stock and is not
that seat's to observe, so a report that is entirely accurate is also, by the shape of the question, partial.

What removes it from the apex predator is that the service and the offence are one act. Predation on stock and
suppression of mesopredators are the same behaviour aimed at different bodies, which is what the literature
calls intraguild predation: potential competitors that eat each other. The suppression was never a favour, was
never intended and was never contracted, and there is no version of the intervention that ends the taking of
lambs and keeps it.

And the mesopredator closes the case against the last party, because nothing was applied to it. No poison, no
bounty and no incentive reaches it. What reaches it is the absence of the thing that was pressing down, which is
a subtraction, and a subtraction is not an act any programme records as a cause. The seat responds as any
released population does.

**And the arithmetic runs against the intuition, which is what makes this stageable rather than merely
regrettable.** The apex largely ignored nests and eggs. The mesopredators do not, they are far more numerous,
and released they hunt over more ground and for more of the day. So one large predator removed is replaced by
many small ones whose combined take on the third party exceeds anything the apex was doing to it, and removing a
predator increases predation.

**The gap is in the schema rather than in the entries.** The programme has a column for stock lost and a column
for animals taken, and both are filled correctly. A scrub-breeding bird is neither: not the subject of the
request, not the object of the intervention, not a stakeholder and not a cost centre. So the cost does not
appear as a small figure or a disputed one. It does not appear, and there is no position in the arrangement
from which it is a number anybody is responsible for producing.

It is distinct from the house's **Room to Grow**, which the slate line required it to be tested against before
authoring, and the test was run in the sweep. There a culled population is held down by its own
density-dependence, so killing a fraction relaxes the competition or cannibalism among the survivors and the
**same** population rebounds past where it was. Remove that engine, by supposing a target that does not rebound
at all, and this misfit stands entire: the apex predator here stays removed, which is the premise rather than a
complication, and the rise is in a **different** species that nobody acted on, with the cost landing on a
**third** that was never in the sequence. One misfit is one species and its own regulation; this one is a chain
of three and an accounting that has room for two.

It is distinct from the house's **Never Aimed at You**, where an agent applied to one organism selects on
another it was not aimed at, since the mechanism there is exposure to something administered. Nothing is
administered to the mesopredator here and nothing reaches it at all: what changes is the removal of a
competitor, so the cause is an absence rather than a stray dose.

It is distinct from the house's **The Cobra Effect**, where an incentive offered to reduce a pest is farmed for
the reward, so the mechanism runs through somebody deciding how to respond to a payment. Here nobody responds to
anything and no party in the sequence changes its behaviour: the animals do what animals do when a suppressor
leaves, and the intervention is not gamed but simply succeeds.

It is distinct from the house's **The Balloon Effect**, where pressure on a banned trade in one place bulges it
to the next, so the same problem relocates. Here the problem does not move. A different problem grows in the
same place, and it is larger than the one that was solved.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the stockman as
  exaggerating or the agency as careless, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  losses are real, are counted, and the seat carrying them is entitled to relief; the question put to that seat
  is what it lost, and it answers accurately; the control programme reduces its target, verifies the
  reduction, and reports figures that would survive any audit; the apex predator is guilty of exactly what the
  tally says, and its suppression of smaller predators is the same act rather than a separable service that
  could have been retained; the mesopredator has nothing applied to it and responds to a subtraction; and the
  restorer proposes the repair the structure implies. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that an intervention succeeding completely on its target raises the total of the
  thing it was for.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Restoring the apex predator
  is the real remedy, is what the structure implies, and has a contested effect. Controlling the mesopredators
  instead means running a second and larger programme against far more numerous animals, indefinitely.
  Compensating stock losses rather than removing predators works and moves the cost rather than removing it,
  and depends on a scheme that pays promptly and is trusted. Fencing and guardian animals are real and are
  bought at a capital cost that falls hardest on the smallest holdings. Adding a column for the third party
  requires knowing in advance which party it will be, which is the thing nobody knew. And knowing the whole
  structure, which the ecologists do, leaves a lamb taken next week and a stockman entitled to an answer.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the canyon study, the quantified test, the global range analysis
  and the theory of intraguild predation are theirs. The bounds are carried with them and they are unusually
  heavy: **whether mesopredator release is general is genuinely contested**, most of the evidence is
  correlative rather than manipulative, reviews find roughly half of studied pairings behaving as predicted,
  and one large fenced removal experiment found no mesopredator increase at all, so the play stages a
  structure demonstrated in specific systems and makes **no claim that predator control generally backfires**;
  the strength of the effect depends on productivity, on other human pressure and on which mesopredator is
  present, none of which the play models; the accounting described is a simplification of arrangements that
  differ by jurisdiction and by species, many of which already carry wider conservation duties; and the play
  makes **no recommendation about any control programme or any restoration**, only that a suppressor removed
  releases what it suppressed, and that the party which pays for it was never in the ledger.
