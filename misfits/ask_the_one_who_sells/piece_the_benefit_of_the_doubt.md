# Piece: The Benefit of the Doubt

## Taxonomy

[Ask the One Who Sells](play_ask_the_one_who_sells.md). The sincere belief
that more is safer and more thorough, so the induced care feels like good
practice and not like selling.

## Load Bearing

An imperfect agent need not know they are acting as one; a provider can
believe, honestly, that the extra test or visit is the cautious and
responsible call, and recommend accordingly (McGuire, 2000). This is the
reason no fraud is required for demand to be induced: belief and incentive
point the same way, so the provider never has to choose between them. Guard
against reading the sincerity as an excuse that lets the provider off: it is
not a defence but the mechanism, the very thing that makes the over-supply
reliable and invisible, since a provider who believes more is better will
induce demand as surely as one who knows it is not, and more comfortably.

## Apparent

It looks like conscience: a careful professional erring toward caution,
exactly what a buyer would want from someone entrusted with their care.
