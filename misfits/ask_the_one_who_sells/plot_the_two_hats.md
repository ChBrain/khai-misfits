# Plot: The Two Hats

## Taxonomy

[Ask the One Who Sells](play_ask_the_one_who_sells.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

[The Clinic](place_the_clinic.md), where one person does the advising and the selling in the same visit.
Nothing here is hidden: the same hands take the history, read the chart, and write the order for the very service those hands will render.

## Action

[The Provider](persona_the_provider.md), from [the provider's seat](position_provider.md), holds
[the Dual Role](piece_the_dual_role.md) through [Recommending](process_recommending.md): asked to judge
what the patient needs, and paid for whatever is recommended, the Provider advises the very purchase they
profit from, in one recommendation, in one breath.

## Stage

The physician is at once the patient's agent, trusted to recommend what the patient needs, and the supplier
of the care recommended, paid for each unit supplied (Evans, 1974; McGuire, 2000). The conflict needs no lie
to bite; it is structural, one person carrying both roles at once. This is a specific instance of the
principal-agent problem, the agent who is also the seller, distinct from Moral Hazard's hidden action, where
the party who conceals is not the one being trusted to advise.

## Tension

The person you trust to say what you need is the person paid for what you buy, and both hats are worn in
good faith.
