# Pitch: Just Being Careful

## Taxonomy

[Must Be a Catch](play_must_be_a_catch.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Ordinary and cautious.
Two sides at a table, an offer on it, a negotiator weighing terms with the due diligence any deal deserves before a signature goes on it.
Nothing false in it: a rival's offer really does deserve scrutiny, and reading it carefully is exactly what a competent negotiator should do.

## Undertow

Under the careful reading, a discount attached before the reading even starts.
The very same terms are worth less for no reason but that the other side proposed them, since a party assumed to act in its own interest is assumed to have offered only what favors it, so the concessions most likely to close the deal are the ones marked down hardest (Ross; Stillinger, Epelbaum, Keltner and Ross).
No sucker and no cheat: suspecting a rival's motive is the reasonable response to a rival, and the same suspicion falls on every offer a rival could make, sound or not.

## Nerve

The comfort of due diligence properly done, and the catch that diligence here cannot tell a good offer from a bad one:
that the inference protecting each side from a bad-faith offer is the very inference that marks a good-faith one as suspect, the discount and the caution the one attribution seen twice,
and just being careful is not neutral scrutiny but a verdict already reached by the name at the bottom of the page.

## Echo

After the run: an arms-reduction proposal rated as fair when attributed to one leader and as a trap when the identical text is attributed to the other, a labor concession read as a hidden agenda, a plea offer discounted for coming from the prosecutor rather than a judge (Stillinger, Epelbaum, Keltner and Ross).
And the remedy, at a cost and not always available: a trusted mediator carrying the same terms stripped of their source restores much of what the discount took, but an adversarial relationship rarely has one ready to hand, and a contested mediator only moves the discount, it does not remove it (Ross and Stillinger).
And the bound held in view, because the mechanism is real and conditional at once: it needs an adversarial relationship and a traceable source, and it lifts where the offer is relayed blind, a trusted party carries it, or the relationship itself stops being adversarial, so the whole of it is that the surest way to make a good offer look bad is to be the one who makes it.
