# Play: You Have to Mean It

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

You Have to Mean It.
A threat deters only if the other side believes it will be carried out. Belief cannot be bought with words, because the other side is asking the one question words cannot answer: would you actually do it. So the threat has to be meant. And if it is meant well enough, the attack does not come, and the thing meant is never done. What was produced, and the only thing that was ever produced, is a settled intention to do something that on the day would be pointless and monstrous.

## Arc

The bend is this: the intention is the whole product, and the intention is the harm.
It runs on a threat that cannot be faked.
[The Deterred](persona_the_deterred.md) is not reading the words. It is estimating a person, and it has every reason to look hard, because the whole question for it is whether this is a posture. A bluff that is examined by somebody with that much at stake does not survive being examined.
So [The One Who Must Mean It](persona_the_one_who_must_mean_it.md) has to form the thing for real. Not perform it, not rehearse it: hold it, as a settled conditional intention, of a kind that would carry through. Which is the point at which the ordinary rule stops working, because the ordinary rule is that it is wrong to intend what it would be wrong to do, and here the intending is what prevents the thing anybody is worried about.
And [The Reason That Expires](piece_the_reason_that_expires.md) closes the trap from the other side. Deterrence has exactly one purpose, to stop the attack. On the morning after an attack, that purpose is gone: the deterring failed, and carrying out the threat buys nothing at all, so the very success condition that made the intention worth forming is the condition whose absence removes every reason to act on it. The threat is rational to make and irrational to keep.
So the hand is tied. [Tying the Hand](process_tying_the_hand.md) is not theatre and not a trick: the only way to hold an intention that the moment will argue you out of is to arrange, in advance, that the moment cannot reach you. Standing orders, delegated release, a system that proceeds unless stopped. Every one of them works by removing the capacity to reconsider from the person who would otherwise reconsider.
Then, if all of it works, [The Act That Never Comes](persona_the_act_that_never_comes.md) is the outcome, and there is nothing to point at. No attack, no retaliation, no event. The peace is real and it is invisible, and the only thing that ever actually existed is the state of mind, which is the part that was wrong.
And nobody in this can be told to do otherwise. Meaning less means being read as meaning less. Meaning it and reserving the right to reconsider is not meaning it. Declining the whole arrangement is available and hands the decision to whoever is willing to make it.

## Stakes

Whether a state of mind can be adopted as an instrument without becoming a fact about the person holding it.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a threat that must be meant to work, an intention that is the entire deliverable, a moment that would strip away every reason to act on it, and a hand tied in advance so that the moment cannot, and away from any arrangement in which the peace is bought with something other than the meaning. The production cannot ask the deterred to believe less carefully, since believing carefully is the whole of its position, and it cannot ask the threatener to mean it provisionally, since a reserved intention is not one. What is left to fight over is not whether the threat was justified, it may well have been, but what to call a person who has correctly reasoned their way into holding something they hope never to act on and must not be able to drop.
