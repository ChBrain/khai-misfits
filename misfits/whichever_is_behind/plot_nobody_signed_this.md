## Tension

Here the harm lands, and it lands with nobody at fault.
The realised policy is a sequence of corners: full development, then full condition, then full
development again. **The organisation has never been at the split**, and the split was available at
every meeting, tabled, sensible, and privately agreed to be roughly right.
It cannot pass because passing it means minuting a rate of exchange between a family housed and a
home kept dry. The officers have no basis for that number and the members would have to defend it
to whichever side it went against, so the paper is carried forward, correctly, every year.
Both constituencies are right. Each was served, each was dropped, and each is accurate about having
been dropped. And the order was never anybody's preference: it is a property of the channels that
put items in front of the room, so a different chief executive with opposite views inherits the
same sequence and is right to. **There is no seat in the building from which a different answer
was available.**
