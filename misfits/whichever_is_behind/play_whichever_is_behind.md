# Play: Whichever Is Behind

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Whichever Is Behind.
A housing authority has two standing goals and both are real: build homes for the people on the list, and keep the homes it already has in condition.
It does not weigh them. It looks at which one is behind, and it attends to that one.
Ask anybody in the building and they will tell you the organisation is doing both. It is. Just never at the same time.

## Arc

The bend is this: the organisation is never wrong about which goal to attend to, and it is never at the point either side would have chosen.

It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing. Attention is singular: a committee that meets monthly carries one thing of substance, and what it carries is settled by the channels that put items in front of it rather than by anybody's preference in the room. And a goal here is not a weight, it is a threshold: the list is behind or it is not, the stock is behind or it is not, and behind means measured against the peer figure and against last year.

Take the sequence first, because the play has to establish that it is **right**. An organisation with two real goals and no common scale between them cannot weigh them, and the honest thing to do with a goal you cannot weigh is to treat it as a constraint and satisfy it. That is what the behavioural account of the firm found organisations actually do: goals attended in an order of priority rather than traded off against one another, with attention moving on once the active goal is met. It is cheaper than bargaining, it spills less blood, and it is genuinely responsive, because whatever is actually going wrong is the thing that gets the room.

Then the fact that inverts it. **The trigger to attend is a shortfall, so the trigger to stop attending is success.** The list leaves the agenda the month it clears the peer figure: not when it is safe, but when it stops being behind. And the stock, which has been ageing quietly through three years of development, reaches the top of the agenda already in the condition that three years of not being looked at produces. Each goal is taken up in the state that made it pressing and put down in the state that made it comfortable, and comfortable is where the decay starts.

Then the sharper half. Because the switch is fired by a threshold and not by a plan, the realised policy is a sequence of corners: full development, then full condition, then full development again. **The organisation has never once been at the split**, and the split was on the table at every one of those meetings. A paper proposing sixty and forty is tabled, discussed and not adopted, every time, because adopting it means somebody writing down what a family housed is worth against a home kept dry, and nobody in the building can write that sentence.

And the turn is not free. Standing a development programme down and back up costs money that neither goal pursued steadily would have spent: a contractor released and re-procured, a design team dispersed, a site held and insured. **So the alternation is dearer than either policy and dearer than the split**, and it was chosen by nobody, because at no meeting was the alternation the item on the agenda.

And nobody has done anything wrong. Every decision was correct on the paper in front of it and would be correct again on the same paper. Both constituencies are right, about their own goal and about having been dropped. Even the order is not anybody's view: it is a property of how the organisation's channels present issues, so a different chief executive with different opinions inherits the same sequence.

Reach for a settlement and each one costs the thing the sequence was for. Weighing needs a rate of exchange nobody holds and no constituency has signed. Fixing the split removes the responsiveness, so a genuine emergency in one goal cannot pull anything toward it. Adding attention, a second committee or a longer agenda, reproduces the sequence inside each new body, because attention is singular wherever it is exercised. And lengthening the aspiration so the switching slows does not remove the switch: it means only that each goal is neglected for longer before it is behind enough to be seen.

## Triggers

**[Both Are Real](plot_both_are_real.md)**
The conflict established as genuine rather than as a failure of will: two goals, both owed to somebody, and one pot of money and one agenda between them. Closes when it is clear the play is not asking anybody to care less about either, because nobody in it does.

**[The One That Is Behind](plot_the_one_that_is_behind.md)**
Cue: the comparison, tabled. Closes when the selection rule is in the open: the goal below its threshold takes the agenda, which is responsive, cheap and correct, and is a rule about shortfall rather than about worth.

**[Cleared, and Dropped](plot_cleared_and_dropped.md)**
Cue: the list clearing the peer figure. Closes when the inversion lands: attention is released by success, so a goal stops being watched at the moment it stops being behind, and the other goal arrives at the top of the agenda in the condition that not being watched produces.

**[Nobody Signed This](plot_nobody_signed_this.md)**
Cue: the split paper, tabled again. Closes when the harm is in the open with nobody at fault: the realised policy is a sequence of corners nobody chose, the compromise was available every time and adopted never, and both constituencies are correct that they were dropped.

**[And the Turn Costs More](plot_and_the_turn_costs_more.md)**
Cue: the settlements. Closes when each is found to cost what the sequence was for: weighing needs a rate of exchange nobody has, a fixed split removes the responsiveness, more attention reproduces the sequence one level down, and slower switching only lengthens the neglect.

## Stakes

Whether an organisation can hold two goals at once, when the only thing it has to hold them with is attention, and attention is one thing at a time.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the officers are weak, because each decision was right on its own paper and would be right again, and not whether the money is enough, because the play runs identically on a larger pot and takes no position on the size of it, but whether a body that can only ever attend to one thing can be said to hold two goals at all. The production cannot settle that. It can only show that the conflict is real and owed to two different sets of people, that attending to whichever is behind is responsive and cheap and correct, that the trigger to stop attending is success rather than safety, that the split was on the table at every meeting and adopted at none because no one can price one goal in the other, that the alternation costs more than either policy it alternates between, and that the order was set by the channels rather than by anybody in the room.
