# Play: Anyone Can See It

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Anyone Can See It.
Two people watch the same meeting, read the same email, sit through the same argument, and come away with opposite accounts of what happened, each certain its account is simply what happened. Not a spin, not a case they are making: the plain facts, obvious, the kind of thing anyone with clear eyes would see. And each says, in effect, the same line: anyone can see it. The trouble is that they say it about opposite things.
And here is the shape beneath the quarrel. What each of them is seeing is not the situation but a reading of it, a construal, made through a lens of interest, history, and expectation that does its work unnoticed, so that the reading arrives feeling like perception and not like interpretation (Ross and Ward). From that felt plainness, three steps follow without anyone taking them on purpose: I see this as it objectively is; a reasonable person, given the same facts, would see it as I do; therefore the one who disagrees is not seeing a different truth but failing to see the truth, from ignorance, or muddle, or bias. Each of them, in good faith, reaches that verdict about the other.
And so the disagreement is misfiled. It is a difference of vantage, two honest readings of a genuinely ambiguous thing, and each party files it instead as a defect in the other: you would agree if you were not biased. And because the bias-read is invisible to the one who makes it, each detects the slant in the other and never in itself, so the charge runs one way from each seat and is never turned inward (Pronin, Lin, and Ross). Both are sure they are the fair one in the room, and both are equally sure the other is not.
And here is why talking makes it worse. The natural fix is more contact and more facts, and it backfires, because each construes the new facts through the same lens and reads the other's persistence as fresh proof of the bias already inferred. Bring in a neutral account, balanced and fair, and both sides find it slanted against them, since from each seat a fair account gives the other side too much (Vallone, Ross, and Lepper). So the gap does not close with engagement, it widens: every exchange adds evidence, to each, that the other is exactly as biased as suspected, and two reasonable people end further apart than they began (Robinson, Keltner, Ward, and Ross).
And the loss is authored by no one. Neither is lying and neither is the fool the other takes them for: each really does see its reading as the facts, because a construal cannot be seen from inside as a construal, and each really does infer the other's bias, because from a seat that takes its own view as objective, a reasonable person's disagreement can only look like distortion. So the widening is a harm with no wrongdoer, and it reads as the other's stubbornness when it is the structure of seeing itself.
And here is what actually closes it, and why it is dear. Not more insistence on the plain facts, since the plainness is the illusion, but perspective-taking: being brought to construe the situation as the other does and to grant that a reasonable person could land there. It works, and it is effortful and resisted, because each already believes it sees clearly and that it is the other who needs correcting, so the one move that would help is the one each is surest it does not need (Ross and Ward). A neutral arbiter cannot supply it, since both distrust the arbiter; only the costly act of trying on the other's eyes can.
And so the misfit is not that people disagree but that each mistakes its own eyes for the world. The three tenets are Ross and Ward's, the overestimated gap is Robinson, Keltner, Ward, and Ross's, the neutral account both sides call biased is Vallone, Ross, and Lepper's, and the bias seen only in the other is Pronin, Lin, and Ross's. The house stages the misattribution and holds the bound in view: that it needs a disagreement over construal and little insight into one's own, and lifts under a checkable fact, a real act of perspective-taking, or a disagreement that genuinely is the other side's error, so the trap is real and common and bounded, and the whole of it is that anyone can see it, said honestly by two people about opposite things, is the sound of a vantage taken for the view.

## Arc

The bend is this: where two parties disagree over a matter of construal and each mistakes its own reading for unmediated reality, each attributes the other's disagreement to bias rather than to a different vantage, so good-faith opponents talk past each other and more contact widens the gap, with no one lying and no one the fool the other takes them for.
It runs on a situation read through a lens.
The same events, evidence, and words are construed differently by two parties, each through an interpretive lens it does not notice it is using, so the reading feels like perception and not interpretation (Ross and Ward; Robinson and colleagues).
And each takes its own reading for the plain facts. A construal cannot be seen from inside as a construal, so the reading arrives as reality itself, obvious, the kind a fair mind would share (Ross and Ward).
And so each reads the other as biased. From a seat that takes its view as objective, a reasonable person's disagreement can only be ignorance, muddle, or bias, so the difference of vantage is misfiled as a defect in the other (Ross and Ward).
And the charge runs one way from each seat. Each detects the bias in the other and denies it in itself, so no one turns the bias-read inward, and both are sure they are the fair one (Pronin, Lin, and Ross).
And more contact widens the gap. Each construes the new facts too and reads the other's persistence as proof of the bias, and a neutral account is called slanted by both sides, so engagement adds evidence of the other's bias rather than closing the difference (Vallone, Ross, and Lepper).
And the widening is authored by no one. Neither lies and neither is the fool the other takes them for, so the deepening discord is a harm with no wrongdoer, easily misread as the other's stubbornness.
And perspective-taking can close it, at a cost. Being brought to construe the situation as the other does and to grant that a reasonable person could dissolves the bias-read, but it is effortful and resisted, since each believes it is the other who needs correcting (Ross and Ward).
And the whole is held against its own limit. The effect is conditional: it needs a construal disagreement and low self-insight, and lifts under a checkable fact, a genuine act of perspective-taking, or a disagreement that truly rests on one side's error (Ross and Ward).

## Stakes

Whether two reasonable people can close a disagreement of vantage, when each mistakes its own reading for reality and takes the other for biased.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward agreement, which two fair-minded people ought to reach, and away from it, because each takes its own construal for the plain facts and can read the other's honest disagreement only as a defect. The production cannot make more contact close the gap, because the contact is construed too: the facts each brings are read through the same lens, and the other's persistence is filed as proof of the bias already inferred, so the evidence sought and the widening it produces are the one construal seen twice. The remedy, perspective-taking, being brought to see as the other sees and to grant that a reasonable person could, spends real and resisted effort, and is not certain, since each believes it is the other who needs correcting and a neutral arbiter is distrusted by both.
