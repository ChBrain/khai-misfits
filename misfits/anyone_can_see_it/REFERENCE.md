# Anyone Can See It: Reference

The research warrant for **Anyone Can See It**, a staging of the phenomenon the literature calls
**naive realism**: that each party takes its own reading of a shared situation as objective reality
rather than as one construal among possible ones, so a reasonable person who disagrees is read not as
seeing from a different vantage but as uninformed, irrational, or biased, and two good-faith parties
each certain the other is the distorted one talk past each other and widen. It is held with the
finding that the effect is conditional on the disagreement being over construal and on each side's low
insight into its own, since a plainly checkable fact, a genuine act of perspective-taking, or a
disagreement that really does rest on one side's error each changes the case.

## Line of Work

Staging naive realism as a **construal-mistaken-for-reality structure**: two parties look at the same
situation, the same events, the same evidence, and read it differently, each through an interpretive
lens it does not notice it is using, so each experiences its own reading not as an interpretation but
as the plain facts. From this follow three moves the literature names: I see the situation as it
objectively is; a reasonable person given the same facts will see it as I do; so one who disagrees must
be uninformed, or irrational, or biased (Ross and Ward). The ordinary expectation is that more contact
and more facts will bring two reasonable people together. But where the disagreement is over construal,
each construes the new facts too, and each reads the other's persistence not as a sign of a different
vantage but as proof of the bias it had already inferred, so the neutral account is seen as slanted by
both sides and the gap hardens with engagement rather than closing (Vallone, Ross, and Lepper; Robinson
and colleagues). And it is powered by an asymmetry: each party detects the bias in the other and not in
itself, since one's own construal is invisible from inside as construal, so the bias-read runs one way
from each seat and neither turns it on itself (Pronin, Lin, and Ross). The governing law is that
**where two parties disagree over a matter of construal and each mistakes its own reading for
unmediated reality, each attributes the other's disagreement to bias rather than to a different
vantage, so good-faith opponents talk past each other and more contact widens the gap, with no one
lying and no one the fool the other takes them for**. The play models the documented mechanics: the
three tenets of naive realism and their yield in conflict (Ross and Ward), the overestimated gap
between one's own and the other side's construal (Robinson, Keltner, Ward, and Ross), the hostile-media
effect in which a neutral account is seen as biased against them by both sides (Vallone, Ross, and
Lepper), and the bias blind spot that keeps the attribution one-directional (Pronin, Lin, and Ross), as
the load-bearing vectors of the structure, and not as a claim that no disagreement is ever the other
side's fault.

It is a misperception no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest neighbours.
It is distinct from Closing Ranks, groupthink, where a cohesive team converges on one view and closes
ranks against internal doubt: there the failure is intra-group conformity toward a shared position,
whereas here the engine is inter-party and no shared position is ever reached, two sides each certain
the other is biased and no group closing around either. It is distinct from The Trip to Abilene,
pluralistic ignorance, where a group agrees its way into a choice no member wanted, each misreading the
others' assent: there the parties converge on a false agreement by misreading agreement, whereas here
they entrench in a real disagreement by misreading each other's honest vantage as bias, the mirror
case, false discord rather than false accord. It is distinct from Nobody Was Looking at Your Shirt, the
spotlight effect, where one's own salience is projected outward: there the projection is of how much
one is noticed, whereas here it is one's own construal that is projected as objective reality, a
different projection with a different yield. It is distinct from The Filter Bubble, where a feed
narrows exposure toward the agreeable: there the divergence is fed by what each is shown, whereas here
two parties looking at the very same account still diverge, because the lens is in the eye and not in
the feed.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast either
  disputant as the bad-faith one, because the finding it stages is that both are in good faith: each
  really does see its own reading as the plain facts, since a construal is invisible from inside as a
  construal, and each really does infer the other's bias, since from a seat that takes its own view as
  objective, a reasonable person's disagreement can only look like distortion (Ross and Ward; Pronin,
  Lin, and Ross). The neutral party that both sides distrust is not slanted, and the one who bridges the
  gap is not naive. The loss is authored by no one: it is two honest vantages each mistaken for reality,
  so that the very reasonableness of each is what convicts the other of bias.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The gap can be closed,
  but not for free: genuine perspective-taking, being brought to construe the situation as the other
  does and to grant that a reasonable person could, can dissolve the bias-read, but it is effortful and
  is resisted precisely because each already believes it sees plainly and the other is the one who needs
  correcting; more facts alone do not do it, since each construes the facts; and a neutral arbiter is
  read as biased by both, so appeals to a shared authority can deepen the distrust rather than settle it
  (Ross and Ward; Vallone, Ross, and Lepper). The reckoning is that a disagreement over construal cannot
  be closed by insisting harder on the plain facts, because the plainness is the illusion, and undoing
  it costs the one thing each is surest it does not need.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the three tenets of naive realism are Ross and
  Ward's, the overestimated construal gap is Robinson, Keltner, Ward, and Ross's, the hostile-media
  effect is Vallone, Ross, and Lepper's, and the bias blind spot is Pronin, Lin, and Ross's; the
  biased-assimilation neighbour is Lord, Ross, and Lepper's and is drawn to mark the line and to support.
  The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: that the effect needs a disagreement over
  construal and low insight into one's own, that the bias-read is an honest inference from a seat that
  takes its view as objective and not a slur, that the asymmetry keeps it one-directional, and above all
  that it is conditional, since a plainly checkable fact, a real act of perspective-taking, or a
  disagreement that genuinely rests on one side's error each changes the case, and not every
  disagreement is symmetric construal. The scope is held to construal disagreements between good-faith
  parties, and not stretched into a claim that no one is ever actually biased or actually wrong.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Construal**: carries the interpretive lens each party reads the situation through (Ross and
  Ward; Robinson and colleagues), the reason the same events, evidence, and words yield two honest and
  incompatible readings, the perspective that does the seeing and is not itself seen.
- **The Plain Sight**: carries the first tenet, the conviction that one's own construal is not a reading
  but the unmediated facts (Ross and Ward), the felt transparency that makes a view feel like reality
  and a different view feel like error.
- **The Bias Read**: carries the third tenet, the attribution of the other's disagreement to bias,
  ignorance, or irrationality rather than to a different vantage (Ross and Ward), the honest inference
  from a seat that takes its own view as objective.
- **The Blind Spot**: carries the asymmetry that keeps the attribution one-directional (Pronin, Lin, and
  Ross), the bias readily seen in the other and denied in the self, so each seat runs the bias-read
  outward and never turns it inward.
- **The Widening**: carries the phenomenon whole, the escalation by which each reads the other's
  persistence and the neutral account as further proof of bias and hardens, so contact and evidence
  widen the gap rather than close it (Vallone, Ross, and Lepper; Robinson and colleagues), a discord
  deepened by the very good faith on both sides.
- **The Boundary**: carries the condition and the hedge, that the effect needs a construal disagreement
  and low self-insight and lifts under a plainly checkable fact, a genuine act of perspective-taking, or
  a disagreement that truly rests on one side's error (Ross and Ward), so the trap is real but bounded
  and not a claim that no one is ever biased.
