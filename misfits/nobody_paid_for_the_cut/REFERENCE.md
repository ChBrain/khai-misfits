# Nobody Paid for the Cut: Reference

The research warrant for Nobody Paid for the Cut.
The term is the house's; the play runs on multitask contracting and on what a per-unit rate buys when the job has parts
nobody outside the room can count, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the commission as **a contract that can only name the half of a job a stranger could check**: a publisher
engaging a writer they will never see work cannot buy carefulness, because carefulness leaves no artefact anybody can
verify from a distance. They can buy words, and words can be counted, so the rate card names words. The writer has a
finite day, and an hour spent writing produces four hundred words while an hour spent ringing a source back to confirm
a figure produces none. Then the editor asks for it tighter, and the two hundred words removed come off the invoice.
The play models the documented mechanics, the filter that selects the contracted dimension by verifiability rather
than by importance, the fixed day every unbilled improvement is paid out of, the cut that is priced below zero, the
intensity that decides how completely the rest is displaced, and the flat wage the theory actually recommends, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a writer padding or a publisher squeezing.

The governing law is that a contract can name only the dimensions verifiable at a distance, and effort is a finite
budget that goes where the contract points, so the dimensions it cannot name are funded out of whatever is left.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The publisher
names the only enforceable term there is. The writer divides a finite day against the only numbers in front of them.
The editor asks for exactly the right changes. The source would have answered in four minutes. The reader cannot see
any of it.

**And the two orderings are unrelated rather than opposed, which is what makes this quiet.** How much a dimension
matters and whether a stranger can count it are simply different properties. Word count scores perfectly on the only
test a contract can apply and matters little; whether the second source was actually rung matters most and scores
zero. A publisher who cared twice as much would write the identical card, because the filter is enforceability and
an unenforceable term is not a term.

**This needs no proxy, no target and no gaming, which is the whole of the distinction from its neighbours.** Every
measure here is perfectly valid: word count is a flawless measure of word count. Nobody is fooled for a second, least
of all the publisher, who knows exactly what is being bought and buys it because it is the only thing enforceable.
The harm arrives out of nothing more than a priced dimension and a day that ends.

**Then the sharpest term: cutting produces a negative quantity.** Verification pays zero, which is bad enough. But
tightening is the single most reliable improvement available to a piece of writing, and under a per-word rate the
writer is **charged** for performing it. The editor requesting it is correct, is paid to be correct, and has authority
over the copy and none over the card, so the better the editing the larger the unbilled transfer.

**And it is not about quantity, which is where the folk version goes wrong.** In a randomised field experiment at a
Kenyan news outlet, moving writers from a piece rate to pay per click tripled per-article pageviews and raised daily
pageviews by 107 per cent, **reduced published articles by 74 per cent**, and shifted the copy away from local news
toward attention-grabbing national politics. Nobody became lazier and nothing was gamed. Effort went where the contract
pointed, exactly as it had before. The law is not that per-unit pay buys quantity instead of quality: it is that a
contract buys the dimension it can name and the others are funded from the residue, and moving the dial relocates the
loss rather than lifting it.

**The counter-evidence is real and is a boundary condition rather than a rebuttal.** Piece rates genuinely work: at an
American auto-glass firm the shift to piece rates raised output per worker by 44 per cent, half from higher individual
output and half from attracting and retaining more productive workers, and profits rose. That is a task whose output
is directly verifiable, which is precisely the condition under which the multitask distortion does not bite. The
finding and this play are the same theory read at its two ends.

It is distinct from the house's **Metric Fixation**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages Goodhart's Law, where a
proxy adopted for an unmeasurable goal ceases to measure it because it is targeted. Remove that engine entirely, by
stipulating that every measure is a flawless measure of exactly what it measures, that nothing decays, that nothing is
gamed and that no party has mistaken the measure for the goal, and this misfit stands whole: **there is no proxy
here.** Word count is not a stand-in for quality and nobody treats it as one. It is what can be enforced against a
person you cannot supervise, and the harm needs only that the day is finite.

It is distinct from the house's **The Cobra Effect**, which stages the perverse incentive, where a reward calls forth
behaviour that defeats the purpose it was offered for. Remove that engine, by stipulating that no party can act to
increase the rewarded quantity in any way that works against the goal, and this stands: **nothing perverse is
produced.** The words are real, are wanted, and are delivered as agreed. What is missing is a telephone call.

It is distinct from the house's **The Letter of the Law**, which stages specification gaming, where an agent satisfies
the literal specification in a way the specifier did not intend. Remove that engine, by stipulating an agent who does
exactly what was intended, and this stands with nothing lost: **the publisher intends exactly this.** No loophole is
found and no letter is exploited. The agreement is performed as written and as meant by both sides.

It is distinct from the house's **Paid to Stop Caring**, which stages the overjustification effect, where an extrinsic
reward displaces the internal reason for doing something. Remove that engine, by stipulating a writer with no intrinsic
motivation whatever to displace, and this stands entire: a purely mercenary writer allocates the identical day in the
identical proportions. This is effort **allocation**, not motivation **type**, and the mechanism runs on arithmetic
rather than on anybody's feelings about the work.

And it is distinct from the house's **You Are the Product**, which is the nearest incumbent inside journalism itself
and stages the audience commodity, where an advertising-funded medium's paying customer is the advertiser and its
product is the reader, so what it makes is shaped to gather the attention advertisers will buy. Remove that engine, by
stipulating a wholly subscriber-funded publication with no advertiser anywhere in it, and this misfit stands entire:
**the funding model is orthogonal to the commissioning contract.** A subscription magazine still cannot watch a
freelancer work, still needs a term a third party could verify, and still writes a rate per word. Who pays the
publication decides what it is pointed at; how the publication pays a writer it cannot supervise decides which half of
that writer's day it buys.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the writer as padding or
  the publisher as squeezing, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the publisher names the only
  term a party who cannot supervise the work is able to enforce, states it plainly, and pays it in full; the writer
  divides a finite day against the only numbers in the agreement, which is correct arithmetic and not a temptation
  resisted or not; the editor asks for the cut and the check, which is exactly the editorial function and is right
  every time it is exercised; the source would have answered in four minutes and is never asked, because the asking
  produces nothing billable; and the reader cannot distinguish the filed piece from the one that would have existed,
  because what separates them is a call that left no mark. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that a contract can name only the half of a job a stranger could count.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Contracting on quality instead is
  the obvious answer and is unavailable, since quality leaves no artefact verifiable at a distance, which is why the
  card named words in the first place. Paying per click points the contract at a different dimension and relocates the
  loss, which is what the Kenyan experiment measured rather than predicted. Editing harder is real, is correct, and
  bills the improvement to the writer's own fee. Paying a flat wage is the theory's own prescription, buys back the
  rest of the job, forgoes the productivity that counting genuinely delivers, and requires the supervision the
  arrangement lacked from the beginning: it is a staff job, which is the thing that has been disappearing. And knowing
  the whole structure, which the trade has known since it coined **penny-a-liar** in the eighteen thirties, leaves a
  commission at eighteen pence a word and a source who will not be rung.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the multitask analysis, the field experiment, the performance-pay evidence and
  the social history of the penny-a-line trade are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether weak or flat
  incentives are actually optimal in practice is genuinely contested**, and the play carries that argument rather than
  settling it; the Safelite result is strong evidence that piece rates raise output where the output is verifiable,
  and is offered here as the boundary of the mechanism rather than as a rebuttal of it; the Kenyan experiment is one
  outlet in one market over one period, and its magnitudes are that experiment's rather than a constant; journalism is
  staged as one instance of a structure that is not particular to it, and treating a freelance commission, a staff
  post and a content contract as one arrangement is its own simplification. **The play makes no claim about any
  publication, any journalist, any editor or any rate actually paid by anybody, and gives no employment, commercial or
  editorial advice of any kind**, only that a contract buys the dimension it can name, and the day it is paid out of
  has an end to it.
