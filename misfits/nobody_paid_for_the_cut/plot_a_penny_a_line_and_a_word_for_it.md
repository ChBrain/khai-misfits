# Plot: A Penny a Line, and a Word for It

## Taxonomy

[Nobody Paid for the Cut](play_nobody_paid_for_the_cut.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

London, the eighteen thirties. The same arrangement, in its first documented form.
Men connected with the journals who lived by the press without belonging to it, paid by the line for whatever ran.

## Action

The identical [rating](process_rating.md) and the identical [allocating](process_allocating.md), two centuries earlier: a countable unit, a per-unit rate, no supervision, and copy filed at the length the unit rewarded.

## Stage

[The Publisher](persona_the_publisher.md)'s seat sets a penny a line, for the same reason it sets eighteen pence a word now: it is what can be counted by somebody who was not there.
[The Writer](persona_the_writer.md)'s seat files at length, for the same reason, and receives sometimes nothing at all, the rate being contingent on what ran.

## Tension

The verdict is not a modern complaint. It is in the dictionary.
The trade named its own people penny-a-liners, and within a few years the language had produced **penny-a-liar** from it, which fixes two things at once: that the effect on the work was obvious to everybody at the time, and that two hundred years of it being obvious have changed nothing. This is the play's argument against reading the misfit as a failure of anybody's standards. The standards were not the variable. The unit was.
