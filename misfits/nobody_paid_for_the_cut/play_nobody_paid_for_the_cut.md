# Play: Nobody Paid for the Cut

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Nobody Paid for the Cut.
The rate is eighteen pence a word, or forty pounds a piece, or a fraction of a penny a click, and whichever it is, it is the only term in the agreement that can be checked by somebody who is not in the room. A publisher commissioning a writer they will never watch work cannot buy carefulness, because carefulness leaves no artefact anybody can count from a distance. They can buy words, and words can be counted. So the contract names words. The writer has a day, and the day is finite, and every hour of it spent ringing a source back to confirm a figure produces no words at all, while an hour spent writing produces four hundred. Then the editor asks for it tighter, and the writer cuts two hundred words out, and the invoice goes down. Nobody in this is cheating anybody. The arithmetic is simply doing what arithmetic does.

## Arc

The bend is this: a contract can only name the parts of a job that can be verified at a distance, and effort goes to the parts the contract named, so the job's other parts are funded by whatever is left of a finite day.
It runs on two properties that have nothing to do with each other: how much a dimension matters, and whether a stranger can count it.
Those two orderings are not opposites, which is what makes this quiet rather than scandalous: they are simply **unrelated**. Word count is trivially countable and matters little. Whether the second source was actually called matters enormously and is countable by nobody outside the room. The contract has no way to prefer the second, so it does not mention it, and a thing unmentioned in an agreement is not thereby free: it is paid for out of the residue.
And the residue is the writer's own day. This is the whole of the mechanism and it needs nothing else: no proxy that decayed, no measure that was gamed, no target anybody pushed on, no motive that was crowded out. Every measure here is perfectly valid. Word count is a flawless measure of word count. Nobody is fooled for a second, least of all the publisher, who knows exactly what they are buying and buys it because it is the only thing they can enforce.
Then the sharpest term in it: **cutting produces a negative quantity.** Verification produces zero, which is bad enough, but tightening a piece produces less than nothing, so the one revision that most reliably improves a piece of writing is the only act in the arrangement that the writer is charged for performing. The editor requesting it is right, is paid to be right, and has no line on the rate card with which to make the request whole.
And it is not about quantity, which is the part everybody gets wrong. Change the contract to pay for attention instead and the same law applies to a different dimension: in a randomised trial at a Kenyan outlet, moving writers from a piece rate to pay per click tripled the pageviews an article got, and cut the number of articles by nearly three quarters, and moved the copy off local news and onto national politics. Nothing became lazier. Effort simply went where the contract pointed, again, exactly as it had before.
Reach for the fix and it is already known and nobody wants it. The theory's own answer is that where dimensions differ in how measurable they are, the correct incentive on the measurable ones is **weaker**, and can be flat: pay them a wage and stop counting. That is a staff job, it is what has been disappearing for thirty years, and it costs the thing piece rates genuinely deliver, which at one American glass company was a forty-four per cent lift in output per worker. The arrangement is two hundred years old and the language settled its verdict long ago: a man paid a penny a line was a penny-a-liner, and within a few years of the phrase existing, people were saying penny-a-liar.

## Stakes

Whether a job with parts nobody outside the room can count can be bought by the piece at all.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether anybody is shirking, because nobody is and the arithmetic is correct at every desk, but whether the countable half of a job can be priced without the other half being funded out of somebody's remaining hours. The production cannot make carefulness countable, because if it were countable the contract would have named it and there would be no play. It can only show that the two orderings are unrelated rather than opposed, that the residue is always a finite day, that the single most reliable improvement to a piece of writing is the one act the arrangement charges the writer for, that pointing the contract at a different dimension moves the loss rather than removing it, and that the answer the theory actually gives is to pay a wage and stop counting, which costs the gain that counting really does buy and is the arrangement that has been going away.
