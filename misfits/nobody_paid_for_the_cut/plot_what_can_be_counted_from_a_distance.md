# Plot: What Can Be Counted from a Distance

## Taxonomy

[Nobody Paid for the Cut](play_nobody_paid_for_the_cut.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

A commission to a writer nobody will ever see work.
[The Rate Card](place_the_rate_card.md), blank, and a question about what to put on it.

## Action

[Rating](process_rating.md): [The Publisher](persona_the_publisher.md), holding [the payer's seat](position_payer.md), selects a unit a third party could count without being present, and sets a price against it.

## Stage

[The Publisher](persona_the_publisher.md), on [Buy What Can Be Verified](plan_buy_what_can_be_verified.md), names words, or pieces, or clicks, knowing perfectly well that none of them is the thing wanted and that a term neither side could enforce is not a term.
[The Writer](persona_the_writer.md), holding [the filer's seat](position_filer.md), reads the rate and agrees it is fair, which it is.

## Tension

The filter is verifiability, and verifiability has nothing to do with importance.
This is where the whole production is decided and nothing has gone wrong yet. Word count scores perfectly on the only test a contract can apply, and matters little. Whether the second source was actually rung matters most and scores zero, because it leaves no artefact anybody outside the room could check. The two orderings are not opposed, which would be a scandal: they are simply **unrelated**, so the card ends up naming a dimension chosen by a property that has no bearing on value, and a publisher who cared twice as much would write the identical card.
