# Whatever You Call It: Reference

The research warrant for Whatever You Call It.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the euphemism treadmill, on pejoration by association as
historical linguistics has long described it, and on the X-phemism framework that locates the difference
between two words for one thing in their connotation, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging a kindness as **a shield that is consumed by being carried**: a word for something people find
shameful or frightening has become an insult, so a better one is chosen, carefully, by people acting on
behalf of those it names; the new word is adopted, and for a while it does exactly what it was chosen to
do; and then, through nothing but honest use, it comes to mean the thing, and the thing is what people
feel about, so the word acquires the feeling, and one day a child uses it in a playground as an insult
and the whole exercise begins again. The play models the documented mechanics, the choice made in good
faith, the interval in which the word genuinely works, the ordinary uses that teach the association, the
connotation that travels from the referent to whatever points at it, and the replacement that is proposed
before anybody has finished learning the last one, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a
tale of squeamish committees or of coarse speakers.

The governing law is Pinker's, and it is a sentence: concepts, not words, are in charge, so give a
concept a new name and the name becomes coloured by the concept. **A word has no defence against its own
referent.** The connotation is not stored in the syllables, where a naming committee could put a better
one; it is stored in what people feel about the thing, and any expression that reliably points at the
thing will collect it. The more reliably the new word does the job it was chosen for, the faster it is
contaminated, because pointing at the thing is exactly how the feeling arrives.

What makes it structural rather than a failure of taste is that every requirement pulls the same way. A
replacement must be **clear**, or it does not communicate; it must be **adopted widely**, or the old word
survives beside it; and it must be **used in earnest**, by clinicians, officials and neighbours, about
real people, or it is not a replacement at all. Those three are also the complete recipe for building the
association that will spoil it. There is no version of a successful euphemism that is not, by the same
motion, a euphemism on its way to becoming the thing it replaced.

What removes any possibility of blame is that nobody in the sequence has anything to get wrong. The
people who chose the word were acting for those it names, usually at their request, and chose well. The
people who adopted it were being considerate. The clinicians who wrote it on forms were being accurate.
The child in the playground learned what the word points at from adults using it correctly, which is how
every word is learned, and then used it for the feeling that attaches to the thing. **Nobody spoiled the
word. The word did its job.**

And the only variable anybody controls is the interval. Choosing a longer, more technical or more
recently coined expression buys time, because unfamiliarity slows the association, and it buys that time
by being harder to use, which is the same property read as a cost. The remedy for the treadmill is a word
people cannot quite say, and a word people cannot quite say is not yet a replacement.

It is distinct from the house's **The Hollow Word**, where a term keeps circulating after the practice it
named has changed, so the field speaks the old language and does the new: there the quantity is the drift
of the **practice** away from a stable word, and stipulating that the practice hold still removes the
harm. Stipulate that here, hold the referent perfectly fixed for a century, and the harm is **worse
rather than absent**, because a stable referent is the ideal condition for a stable association, and it
is the association that spoils the word.

It is distinct from the house's **Prevalence-Induced Concept Change**, where a category widens as its
instances grow scarce: there the quantity is the prevalence of the thing, and the boundary of the concept
is what moves. Here the boundary does not move at all, the concept is exactly what it was, and what moves
is the **connotation of the expression that points at it**.

It is distinct from the house's **Metric Fixation**, which is the resemblance to check rather than the
neighbour to fear: a measure adopted as a target ceases to be a good measure, which sounds like a word
adopted as a shield ceasing to be a good shield. There the quantity is the **reward attached** to the
measure, and the degradation is produced by parties optimising against it. Stipulate that nobody is
optimising anything and that no reward is attached to any word, and this harm survives untouched, because
the contamination is done by **ordinary sincere reference** and not by anybody gaming a target.

It is distinct from the house's **More to Stand Still**, which stages hedonic adaptation: there repeated
exposure erodes the return on a stimulus, by habituation. Here nothing is habituated to. The new word
does not become less noticeable; it becomes **the wrong word**, having acquired a specific content it did
not start with, which is an addition rather than a wearing-off.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the people
  who choose the words as squeamish or the people who spoil them as coarse, because nobody in the
  sequence has a decision to get wrong: the word was chosen for those it names and usually at their
  asking, adopted out of consideration, written on forms for accuracy, and learned by a child from adults
  using it correctly. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is
  that the three properties a replacement needs, clarity, adoption and sincere use, are the complete
  recipe for spoiling it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. A longer or more
  technical replacement lasts longer and is harder to say, which is the same property twice. Declining to
  replace at all leaves people addressed in terms that have already turned, which is a real cost borne by
  real people and not a philosophical position. Reclaiming a word by the group it names works, sometimes,
  and is not available to anybody else and cannot be organised. And addressing the stigma itself is the
  only remedy that removes the engine, which is to say it is the thing everybody has been trying to do
  all along and the reason the words keep being changed.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the linguistics is theirs. The bounds are carried
  with them. **The treadmill is a tendency and not a law of every euphemism**: many stay stable for
  centuries and never turn over, the rate varies enormously by how charged the referent is, and the
  generalisation is drawn from a small number of highly charged domains, so a term that has not turned is
  not a counterexample to anything the play claims. **The naming source is a newspaper essay** rather
  than a research finding, which is stated here rather than dressed up: it named a pattern the historical
  linguistics had already described, and the play leans on the description for its authority and on the
  essay for its formulation. **The play deliberately does not list the successive terms** for any
  stigmatised condition, because printing that sequence performs the harm it is about, and the mechanism
  is fully visible in the lavatory. **No magnitudes or rates are carried here**, because the works
  themselves could not be read in the pass that cited them: publisher hosts and the article archives are
  blocked to it, so the bibliography, the formulation and its direction were verified by search and
  corroborated across independent reports, which is the treatment a citation survives and a reported
  figure is not.
