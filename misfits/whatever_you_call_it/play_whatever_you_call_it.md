# Play: Whatever You Call It

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Whatever You Call It.
A word for something people find shameful or frightening has turned into an insult, so a better one is chosen, carefully, by people acting for those it names.
It is adopted. It is written on forms and said in waiting rooms and taught to children. For a while it does exactly what it was chosen to do.
Then a child uses it in a playground as an insult, and everybody agrees a better word is needed, and one is chosen.

## Arc

The bend is this: the feeling is attached to the thing and not to the word, so any word that reliably points at the thing collects the feeling, and pointing reliably is the whole job.

It runs on one relation from linguistics and one from ordinary life. **Concepts are in charge**, so a concept given a new name colours the name rather than the other way round; and **a replacement has to be used**, clearly, widely and in earnest, or it has not replaced anything.

Take the choosing first, because the play has to establish that it was done well. The word was not picked by a committee protecting itself. It was picked for the people it names, usually at their asking, after the previous one had become something you could be called in a corridor. It was accurate, it was plain, and it was better.

Then the fact that inverts it. **The three properties a replacement needs are the complete recipe for spoiling it.** Clarity means everybody knows what it points at. Adoption means everybody uses it. Sincerity means it is used about real people, by clinicians and officials and neighbours, in earnest. Each of those is a way of building the association, and the association is what carries the feeling across.

Then the sharper half, which is why the usual answers do not reach it. **Nobody spoiled the word: the word did its job.** The child in the playground learned what it points at from adults using it correctly, which is how every word is learned. There is no misuse anywhere in the chain, no moment of carelessness to prevent, and no version of a successful replacement that is not, by the same motion, on its way to becoming the thing it replaced.

And the only thing anybody controls is the interval. A longer, more technical, more recently coined expression lasts longer, because unfamiliarity slows the association, and it lasts longer by being harder to say. The remedy for the treadmill is a word people cannot quite manage, and a word people cannot quite manage has not replaced anything yet.

Reach for a settlement and each one gives back the thing that was wanted. Choose something technical, and you have bought time with awkwardness. Refuse to change at all, and you are addressing people in terms that have already turned, which is a cost paid by them and not by you. Reclaim the word, which does sometimes work, and you have used the one move that is available only to the people it is used against and cannot be organised for them. Fix the stigma itself, which is the only remedy that removes the engine, and you have named the thing everybody was trying to do when they changed the word.

**[It Works, For a While](plot_it_works_for_a_while.md)**
Cue: adoption. Closes when the word is in the style guide, on the form and in the waiting room, when it is genuinely doing what it was chosen to do, and when the interval is understood as real rather than as an illusion.

**[The Word Learns the Thing](plot_the_word_learns_the_thing.md)**
Cue: ordinary sincere use, repeated. Closes when the association is built, when every step that built it is seen to be correct, and when it is clear that the three properties a replacement needs are the three that spoil it.

**[And Then It Is the Insult](plot_and_then_it_is_the_insult.md)**
Cue: the playground. Closes when the child is understood to have learned the word from adults using it properly, when no misuse can be located anywhere in the chain, and when the word has become the thing it was chosen to spare people from.

**[And Somebody Proposes a New One](plot_and_somebody_proposes_a_new_one.md)**
Cue: the settlements. Closes when each is found to give back what it was for: a technical word that lasts by being hard to say, a refusal that costs the people addressed, a reclamation available only to them, and a cure for the stigma that is the thing everybody was already attempting.

## Stakes

Whether a kindness that expires was a kindness.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the word was well chosen, because it was, and not whether anybody misused it, because nobody did, but whether an effort that must be repeated for ever is an effort that failed. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the choosing was right, that the interval was real, that the association was built by correct use, that the child learned the word properly, and that the only lever anybody holds makes the word harder to say.
