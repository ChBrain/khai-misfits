# Missing From the Count: Reference

The research warrant for Missing From the Count.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the differential undercount in the decennial census, the
demographic analysis that measures it, and the statutory bar on using statistical sampling for
apportionment, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the census undercount as **a measured error that the law requires to be carried**: a census is
required to be an actual enumeration rather than an estimate, and the requirement is a genuine safeguard,
because a number that may be adjusted may be adjusted by whoever holds the machinery, and what is being
distributed is political representation. The count is then wrong in a stable direction about the same
people every decade. The play models the documented mechanics, the guarantee that nobody wants withdrawn,
the households missed for reasons that are individually innocent and jointly concentrated, the enumerator
whose diligence is not the binding constraint, the independent estimate that establishes the gap to a
decimal place, the statute that keeps that estimate out of the apportionment room, and the outreach sized
by the very count that did the missing, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a
lazy agency or a rigged process.

The governing law is that the error is not random. Random error cancels and leaves the shares intact, but
every mechanism that produces this one concentrates in particular households, ages and streets, so the
count comes out tilted rather than fuzzy, and a tilt does not average away across a decade.

What makes it structural rather than remediable is that the shortfall is known precisely. Demographic
analysis builds a population estimate from birth records, death records and migration, owing nothing to the
enumeration, and comparing them yields the gap state by state and county by county. On the 2020 count it
put the net undercount of children under five at about 5.5 per cent, roughly a million children, the
largest undercount of any age group and the highest rate recorded since tracking began in 1950. This is not
an allegation or a contested inference: it is a measurement published by the counting agency itself,
including the parts that embarrass it.

And it may not be applied where it matters most. The Census Act provides that no sampling or other
statistical procedure may be used in determining population for apportioning representatives, and in 1999
the Supreme Court held the Bureau's plan to do exactly that unlawful. So the correction and the protection
compete for the same slot, both are right, and the cost of keeping the protection falls entirely on the
people the count already missed.

The remedy that remains is scaled by the error. Hard-to-count designations and the population denominators
behind field staffing, advertising and partnership funding are derived from the previous census, so an area
understated last time is allocated less effort this time, in proportion to how badly it was missed. And
administrative records, the other route, miss disproportionately the same people the enumeration missed.

It is distinct from the house's **The Survivors**, where the evidence is read off what came back because
what did not was destroyed: there nobody selects, the missing cases are gone beyond recovery, and the error
is the reader's for not thinking of them. Here the missing are alive, locatable in principle, and their
number is published: the failure is not that nobody realises, it is that everybody does.

It is distinct from the house's **The Proxy**, where a market unable to see an individual prices them by
their group's average and the discount makes the average come true: there a statistic is applied to a
person and the loop is self-fulfilling through belief. Here no belief operates at all. A person is simply
absent from a list, and the loop runs through budget arithmetic rather than through anyone's expectations.

It is distinct from the house's **The File Drawer**, where only the studies that worked reach the record so
science reads surer than it is: there the omission is invisible and its size unknown, which is the whole
problem. Here the omission's size is the best measured quantity in the play, and knowing it changes nothing.

It is distinct from the house's **Treated and Counted**, where a person is cared for and measured in the
same hour by a design never built around them: there the instrument reaches the person and distorts them.
Here the instrument does not reach them at all.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the agency as
  negligent or the enumeration rule as an obstruction, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get
  wrong: the requirement for a head count is a real safeguard against a number being shaped by whoever is
  positioned to shape it, and is defended by people who are right about the danger; the enumerator returns
  six times, asks the neighbours and records honestly, and cannot enumerate somebody no one will name; the
  households that omit a person are frequently exercising a choice they are entitled to make, sometimes for
  reasons they have earned; the planners target the hard-to-count areas deliberately and competently; and
  the demographer publishes the finding that embarrasses their own agency. The failure is authored by
  nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the error is measured, published and
  undisputed, and legally irrelevant in the one room where it becomes seats and money.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Adjusting the totals works
  arithmetically and requires a method somebody chooses, which is the hazard the enumeration rule exists to
  prevent. More outreach works and is sized from denominators produced by the count that did the missing,
  so the areas needing it most are allocated least. Administrative records work for some purposes and miss
  disproportionately the same people the census missed. And declining to act carries a known shortfall into
  a decade of apportionment and funding formulas that nothing reopens before the next census.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the constitutional provision, the litigation, the
  coverage measurements and the operational research are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: net
  undercount figures are estimates with ranges and revisions, and demographic analysis rests on
  administrative data whose own coverage varies, particularly for migration, so the published gap is itself
  a measurement with uncertainty rather than a hard count; coverage error also includes overcounts, and the
  net figure conceals gross errors in both directions; the 1999 ruling concerned the use of sampling for
  apportionment specifically rather than for every purpose; the census is one country's and its
  arrangements do not transfer unexamined; and the loop this play stages between an understated count and
  the resources allocated to counting it again follows from how the planning denominators are derived
  rather than from a study measuring that feedback end to end. **The play makes no claim that the count is
  dishonest or that the enumeration requirement should be abandoned**, only that an error known to a decimal
  place is being carried because the instrument that measured it may not be used.
