# Play: Told What to Watch For

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Told What to Watch For.
A drug is going to be prescribed, and before it is, the patient has to be told what it may do to them.
So they are told: the ache, the tiredness, the nausea, each one real, each one on the label because it happened to somebody.
They leave knowing what to watch for, and they watch for it, in a body that aches sometimes anyway.

## Arc

The bend is this: the disclosure that makes the decision the patient's is also a cause of the thing it discloses.

Take the duty first, because the play has to establish that nothing here is optional. Consent is what makes a prescription the patient's decision rather than the prescriber's, and a clinician who left the risks out would not be sparing anybody: they would be choosing on someone else's behalf, for reasons the patient never got to weigh. **The naming is not a formality and not defensive medicine.** It is the thing that makes a refusal possible, and a refusal that is not possible is not a consent.

Then take the list, which is true in every line. Each named effect is on the label because it was observed in somebody, so the disclosure can be complete, accurate and honest, and nothing in it is exaggerated for caution. **There is no false statement anywhere in this play.**

Then the watching, which is the only sensible response to being told. A person warned about muscle ache attends to their muscles. That is not credulity; it is what a warning is for, and a patient who ignored it would be the one behaving oddly. **The attention is the correct response and it is the mechanism.**

Then the body it lands on, which was never silent. Adults ache. They are tired some weeks and not others, and most of it goes unremarked because there is nothing to remark it against. Now there is. **The mechanism needs no new sensation, only a new reason to notice an old one**, and a cause standing ready to receive it.

Then the attribution, which is the one false thing and the one thing nobody can check. The ache is real and reported honestly; what is not true is that the tablet caused it. And the patient cannot know that, because a body does not label which of its sensations came from where, and neither can the prescriber, who has one patient and no counterfactual.

Then the evidence that separates them, which is stronger here than the shape usually allows because it is **within-patient**. Give the same people the drug, an identical placebo and no tablet at all, in randomised months, and have them score their symptoms daily: most of the burden recorded on the drug is recorded on the placebo too, and the placebo months are clearly worse than the months with no tablet. The same person, the same body, the same scale. What differed was whether they had taken something.

And then the cost, which is not the ache. The patient stops the drug. They stop it for a harm the tablet did not do, and they now carry the risk the drug was preventing, which is a real risk and was the point of prescribing it. **The symptom was minor and reversible; the discontinuation is neither.**

Reach for a settlement and each one gives back the thing that was wanted. Say less, and have a patient who did not consent to what they took. Frame the numbers kindly, and decide something about a person's beliefs without telling them you are doing it. Judge case by case how much truth this particular patient can carry, which is the discretion that consent exists to remove. Or explain the nocebo effect itself, and hand over one more thing to watch for.

## Triggers

**[The List](plot_the_list.md)**
Cue: a prescription about to be written, and a duty that says the risks must be named first. Closes when the effects have been named accurately and in full, when it is clear that the naming is what makes a refusal possible, and when nothing false has been said by anybody.

**[Watching for It](plot_watching_for_it.md)**
Cue: the patient at home with the leaflet and a body. Closes when the attention has been correctly directed at the thing they were told to attend to, and when it is clear that a patient who ignored the warning would be the one behaving oddly.

**[There It Is](plot_there_it_is.md)**
Cue: an ache, in the third week, of the kind that adults have. Closes when the sensation has been assigned to the tablet, when the assignment is the only false thing in the play, and when it is established that neither the patient nor the prescriber has any means of checking it.

**[The Tablet Stops](plot_the_tablet_stops.md)**
Cue: a symptom reported in good faith, and a clinician who takes it seriously because taking it seriously is right. Closes when the drug has been discontinued for a harm it did not do, and when the risk it was preventing has come back, which is the cost and is not the ache.

**[Saying Less](plot_saying_less.md)**
Cue: the settlements, tried by somebody who has understood the problem exactly. Closes when each gives back what it was for: a patient who did not consent, a decision about their beliefs taken without them, a discretion consent exists to remove, and one more thing to watch for.

## Stakes

Whether a person can be told what a drug may do to them without that telling being one of the things that does it.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the prescriber was careful, because the disclosure was accurate and complete and owed, and not whether the patient was honest, because the symptom is real and reported truthfully, but whether an accurate description of a possible harm can be delivered to the person it concerns without becoming a cause of it. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the duty is what makes the decision the patient's, that the list is true in every line, that watching for what you were warned about is the correct response to a warning, that the body being watched was never silent, and that the one false thing in the room is a link neither party can check and both act on.
