# Told What to Watch For: Reference

The research warrant for Told What to Watch For.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the nocebo literature, on the within-patient trial evidence that
separates the drug's effects from the expectation of them, and on the bioethics argument about what a
prescriber owes a patient when the telling is itself a cause, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging informed consent as **a duty that manufactures the harm it discloses**: before a prescription is
written, the prescriber is required to say what the drug may do, and to say it in terms the patient can
understand and act on. The requirement is not a formality and not defensive medicine. It is the thing that
makes the decision the patient's, and a prescriber who withheld it would be deciding for them. So the
effects are named: the muscle ache, the fatigue, the nausea, each one real, each one documented, each one
on the label because it happened to somebody. The patient leaves knowing what to watch for. And then they
watch for it, which is what a person told to watch for something does, and the body they are watching is
one that aches sometimes anyway. The play models the documented mechanics, the duty that requires the
naming, the list that is true in every line, the attention it directs, the ordinary background of sensation
that attention lands on, the cause now available to attach that sensation to, the symptom reported in good
faith, and the treatment stopped for a harm that the tablet did not do, as the load-bearing vectors of the
structure, not as a tale of a suggestible patient or a careless doctor.

The governing law is that **an expectation of a symptom is itself a cause of that symptom**, so a
disclosure that is accurate, complete and owed produces some of the outcomes it accurately describes, and
the more precisely it describes them the more it produces.

What makes it structural is that **the duty is not optional and not a formality**. Consent is what makes a
prescription the patient's decision rather than the prescriber's, and the alternative to naming the risks
is not a quieter patient but an uninformed one who cannot refuse. The prescriber is not choosing between
telling and not telling. They are discharging an obligation whose discharge is the mechanism.

What makes it costly is that the symptom is real. **Nothing here is imagined and nobody is malingering**:
the ache is felt, the fatigue is felt, and the patient reporting them is telling the truth about their own
body. What is not true is the attribution, and the attribution is the one part of it that neither the
patient nor the prescriber can check, because a body has no way of labelling which of its sensations the
tablet caused.

And the evidence that separates the two is unusually strong for a claim of this kind, because it is
**within-patient rather than between-group**. In an n-of-1 crossover design, patients who had stopped a
statin for side effects took the statin, an identical placebo, and no tablet, in randomised months, scoring
their symptoms daily. Most of the symptom burden recorded on the drug was also recorded on the
placebo, and symptom scores on the placebo were clearly higher than on no tablet at all. The same
person, the same body, the same scale: what differed was whether they had taken something.

It runs with the house's **The Fix Tells Them Where** on the axis `disclosure-detail`, at the same end and
the same sign. There a security patch states precisely what was wrong, and the precision that lets every
defender fix it is the precision that lets an attacker build the exploit. Here a consent conversation states
precisely what the drug may do, and the precision that lets the patient decide is the precision that
produces what it names. **In both, the harm scales with how exactly the disclosure specifies its subject,
and in both the disclosure is owed.** What differs is who is harmed by it: **there the disclosure is turned
against a third party who reads it, and here it acts on the person it was owed to.** An axis shares a dial
rather than a mechanism, and the two mechanisms are as far apart as an adversary and a nervous system.

It is distinct from the house's **You Would Have to Tell**, where a duty to pass information on chills what
is said to the professional in the first place: there the disclosure that does the damage is the
professional's onward one, the harm is that something never gets said, and the party who withholds is acting
strategically. Here nothing is withheld by anybody, the information flows exactly as intended, and the harm
is produced by its arrival rather than by its suppression.

It is distinct from the house's **Treated and Counted**, the therapeutic misconception, where a participant
misunderstands what a trial is for: there the patient holds a false belief about the purpose of the
procedure and better explanation is the remedy. Here the patient's beliefs are correct in every particular,
they have understood the explanation exactly as intended, and **understanding it better is what makes it
worse**.

And it is distinct from the placebo's mirror image in the house's **A Lower Bar Set by a Higher One**, which
stages the evidentiary asymmetry between drugs and procedures. That play is about what a trial is allowed to
compare against. This one is about what a conversation does to a body, and it would run identically in a
world where every procedure had been sham-controlled.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the prescriber
  as careless or defensive and the patient as suggestible or dishonest. The prescriber names the effects
  because consent requires it and because a patient who is not told cannot refuse. The patient watches for
  what they were told to watch for, which is the only sensible response to being told, and reports what they
  feel, which is true. **The symptom is real and the attribution is the only false part**, and it is the
  part nobody in the room can check. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so
  the misfit is that **an expectation of a symptom is itself a cause of that symptom**.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into better bedside manner. Saying less works and
  produces a patient who did not consent to what they took. Framing the numbers positively works, moves the
  rate, and is a decision about the patient's beliefs taken without them. Contextualised consent works, is
  what the literature proposes, and asks a clinician to judge case by case how much truth a particular
  person can carry, which is a discretion consent exists to remove. Open-label approaches work in some
  settings and are not available for a first prescription of a drug the patient has never taken. And telling
  the patient about the nocebo effect itself works, and is one more expectation handed to somebody who will
  now watch for it.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the nocebo research and the bioethics argument are
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them. **The effect is documented most cleanly for subjective,
  self-appraised symptoms** and the play stages those rather than claiming it for objectively measured harm.
  **The magnitudes those sources report are deliberately not carried here**, and the play states the
  direction of the trial's result and not its numbers: most rather than a percentage, and worse rather than
  a score. The result also belongs to that trial in that population, patients who had already stopped a
  statin for side effects, and is not a general claim about all drugs or all patients. **The works themselves could not be read in
  the pass that cited them**, because publisher hosts and the article archives are blocked to it, so the
  bibliography, the findings and their directions were verified by search and corroborated across
  independent reports, which is the treatment a citation survives and a reported figure is not.
