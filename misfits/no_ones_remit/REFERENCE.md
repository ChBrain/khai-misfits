# No One's Remit: Reference

The research warrant for No One's Remit.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the politics of artifacts and the literature on exclusionary or
unpleasant design, from Winner's naming through the empirical studies of defensive street furniture, all
cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging exclusionary design as an **assembled system**: an object that excludes a group is produced by a
chain of decisions, each answerable within its own remit, none of which is a decision to exclude. The play
models the documented mechanics, the complaint that is specific and truthfully made by someone entitled to
make it, the officer whose remit is the complaint and not the condition behind it, the brief that asks for
a surface which discourages lying down and is a legitimate instruction to a designer who would lose the
work by refusing it, the measure that counts what the scheme was asked to reduce and finds it reduced, the
displaced party who appears in no measure of the scheme because they were never a party to it, and the
harm that lands intact in a ward that commissioned nothing, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure,
not as a tale of cruelty. The governing law is that a remit narrow enough to be answerable is narrow enough
to exclude the person the answer lands on, so an exclusion no one authored is assembled from decisions each
of which is correct at the scale it was taken.

The house's standing refusal of villains is the analytic instrument here, not a softening of it. An account
that stops at hostile designers has stopped at the first actor it could blame, and in stopping there it
loses the mechanism: it cannot explain why the bench recurs across cities with no shared author, why
removing any one actor changes nothing, or why the people who commissioned it can each describe a
reasonable job. The villain is what the account shows when it has not yet widened far enough to see the
system, and widening is the work.

It is distinct from the house's **Plausible Deniability**, where a principal wants a result that cannot be
asked for aloud and a chain is built to carry intent without an order: there the intent exists and is
deliberately laundered; here there is no such intent anywhere in the chain to launder. It is distinct from
the house's **The Tyranny of Small Decisions**, where a large outcome assembles from small choices each
about itself: there the choosers are the same kind of party choosing repeatedly and the loss falls on them;
here the choosers are different offices with different remits and the loss falls entirely outside all of
them. It is distinct from the house's **Vetocracy**, where legitimate checks sum to an inability to act:
there the failure is that nothing happens; here something is built, and works.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the officer as
  callous or the designer as cruel, and this refusal is the finding rather than a courtesy. The complaint is
  real and the shopkeeper who makes it is not lying; the officer is employed to answer complaints and has no
  housing budget, no housing power and no housing remit; the designer is asked for a bench meeting a written
  brief and is one of several who could take the contract; the manager reports the loitering figure it was
  asked to report, and it has genuinely fallen. The exclusion is authored by no one because authorship would
  require a scale at which someone was asked to weigh the displaced against the complaint, and no seat in
  the chain has that scale. The misfit is that the exclusion is not the failure of any of these decisions
  but the sum of all of them working.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The correction is a seat
  with a remit wide enough to hold both the complaint and the displaced, which means an office that can be
  told it has failed for a harm no one asked it to prevent, funded against a benefit that shows up as an
  absence and a cost that shows up as a line. Widening a remit is expensive, unpopular and hard to measure,
  so the cure for an exclusion nobody authored is a seat that can be blamed for it.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the politics of artifacts, unpleasant design and the
  field record of defensive street furniture are theirs. The contested edge is carried explicitly: the
  Moses parkway bridges are the field's most cited example and also its most disputed, and the play stages
  the mechanism rather than resting on that case.
