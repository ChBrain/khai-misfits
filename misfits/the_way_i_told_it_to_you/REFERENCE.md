# The Way I Told It to You: Reference

The research warrant for The Way I Told It to You.
The term is the literature's; the play runs on the saying-is-believing effect, on audience tuning, on the
shared-reality account of why the tuning leaves a residue in the teller, and on the audience-design work that
makes the tuning a competence rather than a compromise, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging an ordinary account of an ordinary event as **a version made for one listener and kept by the
speaker**: a person who was there describes what happened to a person who was not, and describes it to
_them_, which means selecting, among the descriptions the material honestly admits, the ones that will land
with this listener, whose view of the matter is already known. That selection is not a concession and not a
performance. It is what makes an utterance interpretable at all: a hearer arrives at one reading rather than
another precisely by assuming the speaker built it so they could. The message goes over, the listener takes
it, and the speaker gets back the small signal that it was understood. And afterwards, when the speaker
reaches for the event, what comes back is the version they told. The play models the documented mechanics,
the known audience attitude, the ambiguity in the material that makes more than one true account available,
the tuning, the uptake that tells the speaker it worked, the epistemic trust that makes the tuned version
worth holding, and the later recall that returns it, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a
tale of a flatterer or a liar.

The governing law is that **the version a speaker builds for a listener is the version the speaker keeps**,
so the act of being understood by one person is the act of editing one's own record of what happened.

What makes it structural is that **the tuning is the competence**. A message not shaped to its recipient is
not a more honest message; it is a less usable one, and often not interpretable at all. Definite reference,
anaphora and word meaning are all resolved by the hearer on the assumption that the speaker designed the
utterance for them, so a speaker who declined to tune would not be delivering the unvarnished truth but
failing to communicate. There is no undesigned version to have told instead. The remedy that would remove
the drift removes the message.

What makes it costly is the boundary condition, which runs the wrong way round. The memory bias does not
follow the tuning by itself: it follows the tuning **when the communication succeeded**. Where feedback
signalled that the audience had identified the target, the communicator's memory moved toward the tuned
version; where identification failed, it did not, and the difference was mediated by the communicator's
epistemic trust in the audience's view of the world. So the residue is a function of how well the exchange
went. **What makes the message good is what makes the memory move**, and a speaker aiming at being
understood is aiming, exactly, at the condition under which their own account of the event is replaced.

And nobody in the room loses anything. The listener received a true, well-built account of what happened and
was not misled. The speaker asserted nothing false: the material admitted several honest readings and the
telling chose among them. Nobody argued for anything, and the audience's attitude was never even put as a
claim, only known. The whole cost falls on the one party who was actually there, and falls after everyone
has gone home.

It is distinct from the house's **The Front**, where each member shows the resolved version and edits the
raw away. There the edit is driven by how the speaker will be read, the material held back is what would
cost them standing, and the loss lands on the group, which never receives the burning thing; the speaker's
own belief about the matter is untouched. Here the edit is driven by what the listener can use, nothing is
held back for the speaker's sake, and the group gets everything it needed. The loss is inside the teller and
nowhere else. Stipulate away every consideration of standing, every stake, every audience the speaker cares
about impressing, and this play survives whole, because tuning to a listener is not something a speaker does
under observation but something a speaker does to be understood.

It is distinct from the house's **The Illusion of Shared Understanding**, where a receiver fills an
underdetermined message with their own meaning and believes it shared, and from **The Ear It Was Heard
With**, where one sentence carries several sides at once and each party stops at the first that fits. Both
of those run on a gap between what was meant and what was taken. Here there is no gap: the message is
designed for this hearer, the hearer takes it as designed, and the two parties end in genuine agreement
about what was said. The harm needs the communication to have worked perfectly, which is the condition those
plays are about failing to reach.

It is distinct from persuasion and from conformity. **Nobody was persuaded**, because the audience made no
case, offered no evidence and did not argue; their view was background knowledge the speaker held, not a
claim put to them. And the speaker's initial private judgement of the matter does not have to be absent for
the effect to run, so this is not a person filling a vacuum with someone else's opinion. It is a person with
a view of their own, telling what happened accurately, to somebody in particular.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the speaker as
  a flatterer and the listener as a manipulator. The speaker asserted nothing false, selected among
  descriptions the material honestly admits, and did so in order to be understood rather than to be liked.
  The listener made no case, offered no evidence and asked for nothing; their attitude was known to the
  speaker rather than pressed on them. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so
  the misfit is that **the version a speaker builds for a listener is the version the speaker keeps**.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into better discipline. Telling it flat, to nobody in
  particular, works and produces an account no particular person can use, because designing for a hearer is
  how a hearer gets a unique reading at all. Writing it down first works and moves the tuning into the
  record, since the note is also written for somebody. Telling it to several audiences works and multiplies
  the tuned versions rather than cancelling them. Knowing about the effect works least of all, because the
  speaker has no unedited copy to compare against and the drift is invisible from inside. And declining to
  care whether the listener understood works, and gives up the thing communication is for.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the social-cognition and psycholinguistics research is
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them. **The effect is reported for evaluatively ambiguous material**,
  where more than one accurate description is available, and the play stages that case rather than claiming
  the drift wherever anybody speaks. **The shared-reality account is the one the play runs on** and is
  carried as the account that makes the structure legible, with the literature's own contingency kept in
  place: the bias tracks successful, trusted communication and not tuning as such. **The magnitudes those
  sources report are deliberately not carried here**, because the works themselves could not be read in the
  pass that cited them: publisher hosts and the article archives are blocked to it, so the bibliography, the
  findings and their directions were verified by search and corroborated across independent reports, which
  is the treatment a citation survives and a reported figure is not.
