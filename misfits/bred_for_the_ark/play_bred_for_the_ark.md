# Play: Bred for the Ark

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Bred for the Ark.
When a wild population is going to zero, you take some of them in. You feed them, you treat their parasites, you keep the predators out, you pair them by pedigree so the genetics stay broad, and you keep them alive while somebody works on the reason they were dying. That is the right thing to do and often the only thing left to do. And an enclosure is not a pause. It is a place, with its own conditions and its own advantages, and every generation that lives there is a generation selected for living there.

## Arc

The bend is this: selection has no setting for held in trust, so a population kept safe is a population being adapted to safety.
It runs on the fact that captivity is an environment.
The Selection That Does Not Stop is not doing anything unusual. Animals that tolerate proximity to people, that breed on the schedule the enclosure allows, that thrive on what is provided and do not waste effort on vigilance, leave more descendants than animals that do not. That is not neglect, mishandling or a failure of standards: it is what happens when you keep animals alive somewhere, and it would happen in a perfectly run facility.
And the traits it removes are the expensive ones. Wariness costs energy and yields nothing where there are no predators. A broad and difficult foraging repertoire is worthless where food arrives. The behaviours that are load-bearing in the range are precisely the ones that are pure cost in the enclosure, so the enclosure discounts them, quietly, without anybody selecting for anything.
The Ones Who Keep Them Alive have no version of this to avoid. They cannot introduce predators, they cannot let animals starve to keep foraging sharp, and every hardship they might impose is measured against a population that may be the last one. Their duty is to the animals in front of them and it is discharged properly.
The Ones Who Manage the Line do the one thing that can be done, and it does not reach the problem. Studbooks, computed pairings, equalised family sizes and crossed replicate populations all slow the drift and hold the diversity, which is real work and worth doing: and equalising family sizes reduces the rate of genetic adaptation to captivity without reducing its deleterious effect on the population once it is put back.
And the effect grows with the thing that is going well. Adaptation to captivity accumulates with generations held, so the longer the insurance policy runs the less it insures, and a programme that succeeds at keeping a species alive for thirty years has spent thirty years making it less able to go home. The advice is to release within a few generations, into the habitat that is usually still the reason they were taken in.
Then The One Released meets a range that did not wait. The population was held still on purpose while the place they came from went on changing, so they return adapted to an enclosure, out of practice at a world that is no longer the world they left.

## Stakes

Whether a thing that is alive can be held in reserve, when being alive is the property that makes it change.
Every plot moves the same way: toward keepers discharging a duty to the animals in front of them, geneticists doing the only work that can be done, and a population that exists at all because somebody intervened, and away from any arrangement that could hold a species still, because standing still is not a state available to anything that breeds. The production cannot introduce predators into an enclosure, and it cannot ask a keeper to let the last of something starve for the sake of its foraging. What is left to fight over is not whether to take them in, that decision is usually made by the alternative, but who owns the difference between the animals that went in and the animals that come out, and whether an ark that must be lived in was ever a way of pausing anything.
