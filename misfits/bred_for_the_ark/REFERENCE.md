# Bred for the Ark: Reference

The research warrant for Bred for the Ark.
The term is the house's; the play runs on genetic adaptation to captivity in conservation breeding
programmes and its effect on reintroduction, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging ex situ conservation as **preservation that cannot hold a living thing still**: when a wild
population is going to zero, animals are taken into managed care, kept from the hazards that were killing
them, and bred under a pedigree designed to hold the genetics broad. That is right and is often the only
option remaining. The play models the documented mechanics, the intervention that is not a choice between
captivity and the wild, the enclosure that is an environment rather than a pause, the selection that runs
identically in a perfectly managed facility, the keepers whose correct discharge of duty is the mechanism,
the genetic management that reaches the rate and not the harm, and the range that goes on changing while
the population is held still, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of bad husbandry
or misguided zoos.

The governing law is that selection has no setting for held in trust. A population that breeds is resampled
every generation, and resampling under conditions is selection whether or not anybody intends it. So the
property that makes a lineage worth saving, that it is alive rather than a specimen, is the property that
guarantees it will not be the same lineage when it is handed back.

What removes any possibility of blame is the direction of the pressure. Animals that tolerate people, breed
on the available schedule and thrive on what is provided leave more descendants than animals that do not.
The traits the range requires, wariness and a broad foraging repertoire, cost energy and return nothing in
an enclosure, so they are discounted quietly and without anybody selecting for anything. This would happen
in a perfectly run facility, because it is the arithmetic of who leaves descendants applied to a place.

What makes it structural is that the keepers cannot act otherwise. Predators cannot be introduced, animals
cannot be left hungry to keep their foraging sharp, and every such measure is weighed against the
possibility that these are the last of them. So the duty to the animals present is discharged properly, and
discharging it is the whole of the effect: each improvement in care makes the divergence larger rather than
smaller.

And the correction reaches the rate rather than the consequence. Equalising family sizes reduces how fast
adaptation to captivity accumulates and does not reduce its deleterious impact on the population once
reintroduced, because a studbook allocates parentage and cannot allocate an environment. The measures that
do work are fragmenting the population and crossing replicates, and releasing within a few generations,
which spends precious founders into a habitat where the original threat usually persists.

Finally, the target moves. The population is held still by design and the range is under no such
arrangement, so the animals are returned into a second environment that is not the one their founders left:
a double mismatch, once because the lineage changed and once because home did.

It is distinct from the house's **Right for the Last World**, where a structure fitted to its environment is
rewarded, hardens into how things are done, and executes on after the environment moves: there the form is
obsolete and nobody has noticed, and the harm is its continued correct execution. Here everybody knows the
environment is temporary, holding still is the explicit purpose, and the population is not hardened at all:
it is changing continuously, which is the problem.

It is distinct from the house's **Exactly One Kilogram**, where a reference with no outside drifts and
exports the error to everything measured against it: there the artefact is inert, the drift is physical
contamination, and the difficulty is that nothing can measure it. Here the drift is measurable, expected
and published, and it happens because the thing being preserved is alive and therefore cannot be inert.

It is distinct from the house's **The Lie of the Land**, where a cue that once meant good habitat still
fires after the ground goes bad, so settlers fill the sink: there the animals are misled by a signal and
choose wrongly. Here nothing is misleading anybody and no choice is being made by the animals at all.

It is distinct from the house's **Worth More Nearly Gone**, where rarity raises a species' price and the
value it gains from scarcity finishes it: there the mechanism is a market and the harm is exploitation.
Here there is no market, the intervention is protective, and the loss is a by-product of the protection.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the programme
  as misguided or the keepers as careless, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  intervention is usually a choice between captivity and nothing rather than between captivity and the
  wild; the keepers cannot introduce predators or impose hunger on what may be the last of a species, and
  are judged on survival, correctly; the geneticists apply the best methods the field has and preserve real
  diversity with them; the selection would operate identically in a perfectly managed facility, because it
  is not a symptom of husbandry. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the
  misfit is that keeping something alive is a thing you do to it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Equalising family sizes
  slows the accumulation and does not lessen the harm on release. Fragmenting the population and crossing
  replicates helps and requires more space, more institutions and more money than most programmes have.
  Releasing within a few generations is the one measure that reaches the mechanism and spends precious
  founders into a habitat where the original threat usually persists, at mortality rates that are high and
  expected. Cryopreserving gametes stops the adaptation completely by stopping the life, and fixes the
  lineage at one instant of a world that goes on moving.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the conservation genetics is theirs. The bounds are
  carried with them: the size and speed of genetic adaptation to captivity vary greatly between taxa,
  facilities and husbandry regimes, and much of the quantitative work rests on laboratory model organisms
  whose generation times and selection pressures differ from those of the species programmes actually hold;
  behavioural deficits on release also reflect learning and rearing environment rather than genetics alone,
  and the two are hard to separate in practice; many reintroductions have succeeded, and the field has
  improved reintroduction outcomes substantially through soft release, pre-release training and habitat
  work, so the picture is not uniformly bleak; and modern programmes are frequently designed from the start
  around minimising generations held. **The play makes no claim that captive breeding should not be
  attempted**, since the alternative is usually extinction, only that an ark has to be lived in, and living
  in a place is how a lineage becomes fitted to it.
