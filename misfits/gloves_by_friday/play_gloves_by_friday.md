# Play: Gloves by Friday

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Gloves by Friday.
The profession has a ladder and it is right. Get rid of the hazard, or swap it for a milder one, or build a guard around it, or write a rule about it, or put equipment on the person: five rungs, ranked by how well they work, taught everywhere, agreed by everybody. The ranking is not in dispute and never has been. What nobody put on the same page is the second list, which runs the other way: getting rid of the hazard needs the person who designed the process, who worked for another company in another country nine years ago, and it needs capital, downtime and requalification. A guard needs a change to installed plant and a shutdown to fit it. A rule needs a meeting. Gloves need a purchase order. Something happened on Tuesday, the coordinator has until Friday, and the only rungs inside a week are the bottom two.

## Arc

The bend is this: the ladder is ordered by how well a control works, and it is very nearly the reverse order of how fast a control can be put in place and how far up the organisation you have to reach to get it.
It runs on two correct rankings that nobody laid side by side.
Every rung is genuinely better than the one below it, for the reason the profession gives: the top three work without needing anybody to do anything, and the bottom two work only for as long as a person keeps doing something. That is not a slogan, it is the whole of the difference, and it is why the ladder is in that order.
But the same property that makes a control strong is what makes it slow. A control that works without human effort has to be built into the thing, and building it into the thing means the drawing board, and the drawing board closed years ago somewhere else. A control that needs human effort can be introduced on any afternoon by anybody with a budget code, because all it requires is the human, who is already there.
So the person holding the pen after an incident faces a ladder whose top is real and out of reach and whose bottom is weak and available, and they are not weak or unserious: in the field study the coordinators were the successful ones, they put 280 measures in, and they were stopped seventy-one times. What they landed on was administrative more than half the time. That is not what a lazy safety function looks like. It is what the shape of the ladder looks like when somebody good climbs it under a deadline they did not set.
And the bottom rungs audit beautifully, which closes it. A respirator issued is a line in a log, a signature, a countable thing, verifiable next quarter. An eliminated hazard is an absence, and absences are hard to point at in a review. So the control that can be shown to have been implemented is the control that requires a person to keep performing it forever, and the log fills up with real, correct, completed actions while the thing on the floor is exactly where it was.
Reach for the obvious fix and it turns. Mandate the top of the ladder and you have mandated a capital programme against a deadline set by an incident. Train harder and you have chosen the second-weakest rung and called it rigour. Blame the coordinator and you have blamed the person who was blocked seventy-one times trying. The ladder is right, the ranking is right, the people are right, and the gloves go out on Friday.

## Triggers

**[Something Happened on Tuesday](plot_something_happened_on_tuesday.md)**
An exposure, a near miss, a hand in the wrong place. The Board wants to know by the end of the week what has been done, which is the correct thing for a board to want. The clock that will decide everything starts here, and it is set by the incident rather than by the engineering. Closes when the corrective action is due and the assessment has begun.

**[The Ladder Says Eliminate](plot_the_ladder_says_eliminate.md)**
Cue: the hazard identified and the hierarchy applied, properly, in the order it is meant to be applied in. The answer comes back at the top: remove the hazard, or swap it for something milder. The Coordinator writes that down, because it is right. Closes when the correct control has been named.

**[Nobody in the Building Can Do That](plot_nobody_in_the_building_can_do_that.md)**
Cue: the correct control costed. Elimination needs the process redesigned, which needs The Designer, who is nine years and one company away. A guard needs installed plant altered and a line stopped. The Coordinator's authority reaches the fourth rung and the fifth, and stops. Closes when the reach has been measured against the ladder and found short.

**[The Two Rungs That Fit](plot_the_two_rungs_that_fit.md)**
Cue: Friday. The rungs that fit inside a week are a written procedure and equipment on the person, and both go in: a revised method statement, a toolbox talk, and gloves. Every one is real, approved, hazard-assessed and correctly issued, and every one works only for as long as The Worker keeps performing it. Closes when the measures are in place and the hazard is where it was.

**[Counted as Closed](plot_counted_as_closed.md)**
Cue: the review. The Auditor can verify a signature on an issue sheet and cannot point at an absence, so the measures that audit are the measures that require somebody to keep doing something. The log records corrective actions complete, the tally comes out administrative more than half the time, and the residue is on the floor, permanently, in the form of a person having to be careful. Closes when the action is signed off and nothing on the floor has changed.

## Stakes

Whether a ranking by effectiveness can survive being applied by people who are ranked by availability.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether anybody chose badly, because the ladder is right and the coordinator is good and the board is entitled to an answer, but whether a correct ranking is any use to somebody who can only reach the bottom of it. The production cannot put the top rungs within reach, because what makes them strong is that they are built into the thing, and building into the thing is a decision that was taken elsewhere and finished. It can only show that the two orderings run opposite, that the successful coordinators land where they land and are blocked seventy-one times getting there, that the weakest controls are the ones that can be counted and therefore the ones that get credit, and that the difference between the ladder and the log is a person on the floor being careful, every shift, forever.
