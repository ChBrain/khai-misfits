# Play: The Crowded Middle

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

The Crowded Middle.
Picture a long beach with sunbathers spread evenly along it, and two ice cream carts deciding where to stand. Each customer walks to the nearer cart. If the two carts sat at the quarter points, a third of the way in from each end, every sunbather would have a short walk and the beach would be served as well as it could be. But that is not where they end up. Look at either cart alone: if it edges toward the middle, it keeps all the customers behind it, who still have no one nearer, and it steals a slice of the customers between the two carts, who now find it closer. Moving inward wins the whole span between the rivals and gives up only the thin tail behind. So each cart, doing nothing but reading the same beach, slides toward the center, and the other answers, until the two stand side by side at the middle, and the sunbathers at the far ends have the longest walk of all (Hotelling).
And here is what the two carts have done to the beach. They are together now, all but touching, selling the same thing at the same spot, when the whole beach would have been better served by their staying apart. Neither blundered. Each move was the right reply to where the other stood, and neither can pull back toward its end without handing the rival everything in the middle, which is most of the beach. The sameness is not laziness and not a conspiracy: it is the equilibrium, the place two rivals competing for a spread-out market are driven to, and Boulding gave it a name, the principle of minimum differentiation.
And here is the same shape away from the sand. Two parties in a one-dimensional field of voters, each wanting to win, each moves toward the position of the median voter, the one in the middle with as many to the left as to the right, because that is the position that beats any other under majority rule, the point no majority can defeat (Black). So the parties converge, and come to sound alike, and the voter who wanted something at the edge is offered two versions of the center (Downs). The customer at the end of the beach and the voter at the edge of the spectrum are the same figure: courted before the rivals converged, abandoned after, offered nothing near what they wanted, and their number is exactly the variety the market has lost.
And here is the cost, and why it never shows. Nothing was made worse. No price was cut to the bone, no standard lowered, no effort wasted standing on tiptoe. The two rivals are exactly where each should be, given the other. The loss is not borne by them: it is borne by the ends, the customers served by no one and the variety never offered, and it never appears in any ledger because the thing that would have revealed it, a cart at the quarter point, a party at the edge, was never there to be missed. The harm is a coverage foregone, and a coverage foregone leaves no mark.
And here is the one lever the abandoned hold, and its limit. The voter at the edge can refuse to vote at all, and if enough of them stay home, a party feels the ground shift and edges back toward its end to hold them, so the convergence is not quite complete (Downs). But the lever only works where walking away is possible and where the walk is felt, and it pulls the huddle open only a little. The ends can discipline the middle only by refusing it, and only so far.
And here is what breaks the huddle, and what it costs, and why the whole thing is held lightly. Add a third cart and the two cannot both sit dead center. Let the carts sell genuinely different flavors and each can hold its own ground. Reward parties for staying distinct, as proportional representation does, and the field spreads out again. Each of these restores variety, and each spends something to do it or changes the game itself. And the deepest correction is that the pull to the center was never a law. Hotelling's own claim that the two carts settle stably at the middle was shown to be wrong: with the travel cost rising in a straight line with distance, there is no stable price equilibrium once the carts are close, and if the travel cost rises with the square of the distance the carts fly apart to the very ends, maximally differentiated (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse). So the crowded middle is what competition reaches under one set of assumptions, position the only choice and the pull of the mass unopposed, and it comes undone under another. The spatial competition and the drive to the center against the quartile optimum are Hotelling's, the name is Boulding's, the median voter as the point majority rule selects is Black's, the two-party convergence and the abstention caveat are Downs's, and the correction that makes it all conditional is d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse's. The house stages the vector and holds the law lightly.

## Arc

The bend is this: when competitors position themselves to capture a market spread across a dimension of preference, each is drawn by its own best reply toward the center where the most customers lie, so they converge on the median and cluster alike, and the variety and coverage the dispersed market wanted is lost, every reposition locally right and the sum a huddle at the middle.
It runs on a market spread across a range of tastes.
Customers are distributed along a dimension of preference, a street, a flavor, a policy axis, and each buys from the nearest offering, so where a competitor stands decides how much of the spread it wins (Hotelling).
And it turns on where the winning move points. For each competitor alone, edging toward the center keeps every customer behind it, who still have no one nearer, and takes a share of those between it and the rival, so moving inward wins the whole span for the loss of only the thin tail (Hotelling).
And so both rivals move the same way. Each best reply is a step toward the middle, the other answers, and the two converge on the median, the point that captures the larger side of any split and that majority rule selects under single-peaked preferences (Black; Downs).
And at the middle they are alike. Two offerings sit together where the market wanted them spread, the principle of minimum differentiation, the sameness an equilibrium neither chose and neither can leave without ceding the middle (Boulding).
And the ends are abandoned. The customers at the extremes are served by no one, the variety and coverage the spread market wanted is lost, and the quartiles that would have minimized the customers' travel are never reached, the loss borne by the edges and by no one's fault (Hotelling).
And the whole is authored by no one. Neither rival schemed and neither blundered, each is exactly where it should be given the other, and the variety the market loses never appears because the thing that would have carried it was never offered.
And the ends hold one lever, and it is small. The alienated extreme can refuse to be served, and where enough of them walk a rival edges back to hold them, so the convergence is not quite complete, but only where the walk is possible and felt, and only so far (Downs).
Add a third rival, or real difference, or a rule that rewards distinctness, and the huddle opens at a price. Each restores variety by spending a cost or changing the game, and the deepest correction is that the pull was never a law: under a steep enough travel cost the rivals fly apart to the ends instead, maximally differentiated (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse), so the crowded middle is what competition reaches on one ground and comes undone on another.

## Stakes

Whether a market spread across a range of tastes can be served across that range, when for each competitor alone the winning move is to abandon its own ground for the crowded center.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward the center, because the center is where the mass is and the position that beats any other, and away from the variety the spread-out market wanted, because holding a distinct position means ceding the middle to a rival who will not. The production cannot make the rivals both distinct and safe at once, because the same pull that makes the center each one's best reply is what empties the ends: the value of the middle and the cost to the edges are the one convergence measured two ways. The levers that break it, a third entrant, real differentiation, proportional representation, each restore variety by spending a cost or changing the game, and the median the two converge on may serve the majority perfectly well, so the correction trades the ends' variety against the middle's mass and cannot make every position both distinct and secure. It can only show that no one schemed and no one blundered, that the rivals who cluster are each exactly where they should be and the customers at the ends are abandoned by no one's fault, that the sameness is an equilibrium and not a failure, and that the variety the market loses never appears in any reckoning because the thing that would have carried it was never built. And it holds even this lightly, because the pull to the center is not a law but a result under assumptions, and where the assumptions change the rivals fly apart instead (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse).
