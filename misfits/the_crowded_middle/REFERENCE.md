# The Crowded Middle: Reference

The research warrant for **The Crowded Middle**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon the
literature calls the **principle of minimum differentiation**: Hotelling's account of two sellers on
a line converging on the center, Black's median voter as the point majority rule selects, and
Downs's two vote-maximizing parties drawn toward the median voter, all cited below, held with the
correction that the convergence result is not general but conditional on the model's assumptions
(d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse).

## Line of Work

Staging the principle of minimum differentiation as a **reposition-to-the-center structure**: a set
of competitors sells to a market spread out along some dimension of taste, position on a street,
flavor, policy, time of day, with customers distributed across it and each buying from the nearest
offering. Each competitor chooses where to stand, and given where the other stands, the position
that captures the most customers is the one nearer the center, because moving toward the middle wins
the whole span between the two competitors while giving up only the sparse tail beyond. So each,
best-replying to the other, edges inward, and the two settle together at the median, alike where the
market wanted them spread. Nothing is colluded and nothing is copied: each move is an independent
best reply to the distribution of customers, not an imitation of the rival. The governing law is not
that anyone conspires, but that **when competitors position themselves to capture a market spread
across a dimension of preference, each is drawn by its own best reply toward the center where the
most customers lie, so they converge on the median and cluster alike, and the variety and coverage
the dispersed market wanted is lost, every reposition locally right and the sum a huddle at the
middle**. The play models the documented mechanics: the spatial competition that drives two sellers
to the center against the social optimum of the quartiles (Hotelling), the median as the point
majority rule selects under single-peaked preferences (Black), and the two-party convergence on the
median voter (Downs), as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, carried with the finding that
the result holds where position is the choice and price or platform is fixed, and reverses or
dissolves where it is not (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse).

It is a competitive-positioning interaction no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from
its nearest neighbours. It is distinct from The Arms Race, where each party's escalation is matched
by the other and the moves cancel, so the net is effort wasted with no advantage gained: here the
moves do not cancel, each step toward the center captures real share from the rival and the center is
each one's genuine best reply, and the loss falls not on the players, whose positions are optimal,
but on the dispersed ends the convergence abandons, so the harm is a misallocation of position and a
variety foregone, not a resource dissipated on tiptoe. It is distinct from Information Cascade, where
each agent infers from the observed actions of those ahead and discards its own signal to copy the
crowd: here no one copies anyone and no one reads another's position as a signal of what is true,
each competitor independently computes that the center maximizes its own catchment given the
distribution of customers, so the convergence is parallel best-reply, not sequential imitation, and
it would occur even if neither could see why the other stood where it did. It is distinct from The
Lowest Bar, the race to the bottom, where mobile demand drives each jurisdiction to lower a standard
until all sit at the floor: here nothing is lowered and no standard falls, the competitors converge
on the median that may be exactly what the largest block of customers wants, and the cost is borne by
the spread-out ends and the variety lost, not by a degraded level, so this is a convergence in
position, not a slide in quality.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the
  competitors who converge as cynics selling out their distinct offerings, because the source finding
  it stages is precisely that each move to the center is the correct best reply to where the other
  stands, not a betrayal: a competitor that stayed at its end would cede the whole middle to the rival
  and lose, so the huddle is the sum of individually optimal positions, reached with no collusion and
  no imitation (Hotelling; Downs). The loss is authored by no one: it is the variety and coverage of
  the dispersed ends, abandoned not by any competitor's failure but by the pull of the median on each
  of them at once, and the misfit is that the market's own logic makes its sellers alike where its
  customers wanted them spread.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The forces that
  restore differentiation exist, more than two competitors spreading out along the line, product or
  brand differentiation that lets each hold its own ground, high enough transport or switching costs
  that under a quadratic penalty push the sellers apart to the extremes, proportional representation
  that rewards parties for staying distinct (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse; Downs), but none is
  free and none is universal: each changes the game rather than dissolving the pull, differentiation
  spends real cost to sustain, added entrants may still cluster in pairs, and the median the two
  converge on may serve the majority well, so the correction trades the ends' variety against the
  middle's mass and cannot make every position both distinct and safe at once. The reckoning is that
  the convergence is an equilibrium, not an error, and every guard against it rewrites the conditions
  that produced it.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here,
  never claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the spatial competition and the drive to
  the center against the quartile optimum are Hotelling's, the "principle of minimum differentiation"
  label is Boulding's, the median voter as the Condorcet winner under single-peaked preferences is
  Black's, and the two-party convergence on the median voter with its multiparty and abstention
  caveats is Downs's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: that the convergence
  requires competition on position along a dimension of preference with demand that goes to the
  nearest, that Hotelling's own claim of a stable price equilibrium at the center was shown wrong and
  that under quadratic transport costs the result reverses to maximal differentiation, so the play
  stages the position-convergence vector and holds the price-stability claim lightly, crediting the
  correction (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse). The scope is held to competitors positioning on a
  single dimension of taste with captured demand, and not stretched into a claim that all competition
  breeds sameness or that firms never differentiate.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Line**: carries the dimension of preference the market is spread across (Hotelling), the
  street, the flavor, the policy axis, the span along which customers are distributed and each buys
  from the nearest offering, the space in which position is chosen.
- **The Median**: carries the attractor (Black; Downs), the point that captures the most given the
  distribution and that majority rule selects under single-peaked preferences, the center each best
  reply pulls toward and the Condorcet winner no majority can beat.
- **The Move**: carries the individually rational reposition (Hotelling), each competitor's best
  reply to where the other stands, edging inward because the center wins the whole span between them
  while giving up only the sparse tail, share-capturing and locally correct.
- **The Huddle**: carries the clustered outcome (Hotelling; Boulding named it), both competitors
  together at the middle, minimum differentiation, the sum of the moves, alike where the market wanted
  them apart.
- **The Ends**: carries the social cost (Hotelling; Downs), the dispersed extremes left unserved and
  the variety and coverage the spread-out market wanted, the gap between the center the competition
  reaches and the quartiles that would have minimized the customers' travel.
- **The Spread**: carries the remedy and its price (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse; Downs),
  product differentiation, more entrants, quadratic transport costs that push the sellers to the
  extremes, proportional representation that rewards distinctness, each restoring variety by changing
  the game and each at a cost, the reversal to maximal differentiation the correction that keeps the
  convergence conditional.
