# Piece: The Spread

## Place

Taken back out along [The Street](place_the_street.md), away from [The Center](place_the_center.md), toward the ground [The Ends](piece_the_ends.md) left open.
It is a place reached only by leaving the Median's pull deliberately, which is why so few competitors reach it on their own.

## Load Bearing

Not free and not universal. Hotelling's own claim that a stable price equilibrium sits at the center is wrong: with linear transport costs, no price equilibrium exists once the sellers stand close together (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse). Under quadratic transport costs the pull reverses entirely and sellers are driven to maximally differentiate, taking opposite ends of the Line instead of its middle. Downs carries the same conditional into politics: many parties, or the abstention of the alienated extremes, pulls the two-party convergence back apart.
So the Spread is the correction that keeps convergence from being read as an iron law: it holds only where position is the free choice and price or platform is fixed, and it reverses once that condition changes.
Guard against reading the remedy as a costless fix or the convergence itself as universal law. Differentiating spends real cost, whether in narrower reach, higher price competition, or the risk of ceding the center to a rival who stays; and the reversal under quadratic costs is a different game, not proof the center was never an attractor at all. Each cure changes the conditions that produced the Huddle; none simply cancels it for free.
