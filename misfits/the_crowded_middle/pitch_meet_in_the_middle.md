# Pitch: Meet in the Middle

## Taxonomy

[The Crowded Middle](play_the_crowded_middle.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Ordinary competition, fair and open.
Two rivals on the same ground, each simply trying to win more of the market, nothing hidden, no one cheating, no standard cut.
Nothing false in it: the rivals are genuinely competing, and reading the customers and moving to where they are is exactly what competing well looks like.

## Undertow

Under the fair competition, a pull that draws both rivals to the same spot.
For each of them alone the winning move is to edge toward the center where the most customers lie, because moving inward wins the whole span between the two while giving up only the sparse tail behind, so each keeps moving, the other answers, and the two end up together at the middle (Hotelling).
No scheme and no blunder: each move is the right reply to where the other stands, and the sameness they arrive at is the equilibrium, the place competition drives them, not a failure in either of them (Boulding).

## Nerve

The plainness of two rivals competing honestly, and the catch that competing well means abandoning your own ground for the crowded center:
that the pull which makes the middle each one's best reply is the same pull that empties the ends, the value of the center and the cost to the edges the one convergence measured twice,
so the market spread across a range of tastes is offered two versions of the middle, and the customers at the ends are served by no one, a variety lost that never shows because the thing that would have carried it was never there.

## Echo

After the run: two offerings alike where the market wanted them spread, the far end served by no one, the variety foregone and never seen, a loss with no culprit and no mark (Hotelling; Downs).
And the levers that break it, each at a price: a third entrant the two cannot both crowd out, real differentiation that lets each hold its ground, proportional representation that rewards staying distinct, each restoring variety by spending a cost or changing the game.
And the law held lightly, because the pull to the center is a result under assumptions, not an iron rule: under a steep enough travel cost the rivals fly apart to the ends instead, maximally differentiated (d'Aspremont, Gabszewicz, and Thisse), so the crowded middle is what competition reaches on one ground and comes undone on another.
