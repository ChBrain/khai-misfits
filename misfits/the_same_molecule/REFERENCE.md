# The Same Molecule: Reference

The research warrant for The Same Molecule.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the documented economics of generic sterile injectable
shortages, on certified therapeutic equivalence as the condition that makes them possible, and on the
market's inability to observe and reward manufacturing quality, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging a drug market as **an achievement that consumes its own supply**: a medicine comes off patent,
several manufacturers enter, a regulator certifies that their products are interchangeable and it is
true, buyers award volume to the lowest bid because interchangeable is what interchangeable means, the
price falls to something close to the cost of the glass, and the oldest and cheapest and most necessary
medicines are the ones that stop being made. The play models the documented mechanics, the equivalence
that is real, the award that has no line for the plant, the margin that cannot fund a sterile line's
replacement, the concentration that leaves two or three makers of anything, the stoppage that is a
national absence, and the exit that is permanent because re-entry has to be paid for, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a greedy manufacturer or a careless regulator.

The governing law is that a market can only reward what it can see. Two vials of the same generic
injectable are, at the moment of purchase, the same thing, and this is not an illusion: it is what
approval means and it is the achievement that made the drug affordable. What differs between them is
not in either vial. It is the condition of the line that will make next year's, and no buyer purchases
that, no contract prices it, and no label carries it. So competition runs at full strength on the one
dimension that is visible and at no strength at all on the one that determines whether the drug exists,
and manufacturers economise exactly where nobody is looking, which is the only place left to economise.

What removes any possibility of blame is that every party did the thing it is for. The regulator
certified equivalence, which is its job and which is correct. The buyer awarded on price, which is what
a buyer of an interchangeable good is supposed to do and what drove the price down in the first place.
The manufacturer bid a price it could meet and then met it, which is what a bid is. Nobody chose to run
an old line; the line was adequate, and the money that would have replaced it was competed away by the
same mechanism that made the drug cost almost nothing. **Success at making a medicine affordable is
what makes it disappear.**

What makes it structural is that the failure is an absence rather than a defect, so nothing in the
market can select against it. An adverse-selection market unravels because bad units arrive and buyers
learn to expect them. Here the units are good, every time, exactly as certified, and then one day there
are none. There is no bad instance to observe, no warranty to claim, no reputation to lose in the
ordinary way, and a hospital that wanted to pay more for reliability has nothing to pay it into and no
way to be sure it bought anything. Reliability is a property of a **future absence**, and a future
absence is not a feature of a good.

And the market is too thin to lose anybody. Very few manufacturers make any given old injectable, and
sterile capacity is qualified line by line and product by product, so the survivors cannot simply make
more: the capacity that exists is approved to make something else. A single suite going down for
remediation is therefore not a share shift but a national shortage, and the maker who stops has every
reason not to return, since coming back means paying for the line the price never funded.

It is distinct from the house's **The Market for Lemons**, which is the nearest relative and is not the
same engine. There the quantity is the buyer's ability to tell one **unit** from another, and
stipulating that every unit's quality is visible at sale removes the harm, because the good sellers
then stay. Stipulate exactly that here: every vial's quality is fully visible, which it already is,
because the vials are equivalent and certified so. **The harm is untouched**, because what is
unobservable is not in the vial. Lemons unravels a market into bad goods; this one delivers good goods
at a very low price until it delivers none.

It is distinct from the house's **Kept for the Worst**, where a drug's reward is suppressed by the duty
to conserve it: there the quantity is the restriction on use, and stipulating no conservation
requirement with reward tied freely to volume removes the harm. Stipulate that here, sell every vial
that can be made and pay for every one, and the harm survives entirely, because the exit is driven by a
margin competed away on a visible dimension and not by a restriction on how much may be sold.

It is distinct from the house's **Nobody Reads It** and **Cheaper Not to Know**, where the information
exists and the reader rationally declines to acquire it: there the quantity is the cost of looking, and
stipulating a free and diligent reader removes the harm. Stipulate an infinitely diligent buyer here and
the harm is reduced and not removed, because the thing they would need to buy is next year's reliability
and there is no instrument that sells it: the tender awards this year's vials, and the premium a single
hospital pays does not keep a national line open.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the
  manufacturer as greedy, the buyer as negligent or the regulator as asleep, because each did the thing
  it exists to do: equivalence was certified and is real, the award went to the lowest bid because the
  goods are interchangeable and that is what makes them cheap, the bid was met, and the line was
  adequate on the day it was inspected. The money that would have replaced it was competed away by the
  mechanism that made the medicine affordable. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that a market can work perfectly on every dimension it can see and lose
  the good entirely on the one it cannot.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. A stockpile buys
  months, expires, and is paid for by whoever holds it. Qualifying a second source is the right answer
  and takes years, because sterile capacity is approved line by line and product by product. Paying a
  premium for reliability is available to any buyer and is individually irrational, since one hospital's
  premium does not keep a national line open and the buyer cannot verify what it purchased. Contracting
  for supply with a penalty prices the penalty and not the shortage. A publicly backed or non-profit
  manufacturer is a real answer that exists and is one more participant in the same market on the same
  terms.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the health economics, the market analysis and the
  regulatory account are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: shortages have many proximate causes
  and manufacturing quality is one of several, so the play stages the mechanism that makes the class of
  drugs vulnerable rather than the explanation of any particular shortage; the pattern is documented
  most fully for the United States and the procurement arrangements differ elsewhere, so the play should
  not be read as a claim about every health system; and real remedies exist and are working, among them
  contracting that pays for reliability, publicly supported manufacture and regulatory attention to
  supply resilience, none of which is nothing. **The magnitudes those sources report are deliberately
  not carried here**, because the papers themselves could not be read in the pass that cited them:
  publisher hosts and the article archives are blocked to it, so the bibliography, the findings and their
  directions were verified by search and corroborated across independent reports, which is the treatment
  a citation survives and a reported figure is not. **The play makes no claim that generic competition
  should not have happened**, since it is what put these medicines within reach at all, only that the
  dimension it cannot price is the one that decides whether they exist.
