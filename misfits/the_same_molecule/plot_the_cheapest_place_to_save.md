## Tension

Here the economising has to go somewhere, and there are only two kinds of cost.
Anything the regulator inspects is fixed and cannot be cut, and anything the customer cannot see is
indistinguishable from efficiency when it is.
So the money that does not get spent is the money on the line: the replacement deferred, the redundant
suite not built, the second site not kept warm.
**Nobody decides to run a fragile line.** A working suite is not replaced, this year and then the next,
out of a margin that no longer exists, and each of those decisions is defensible on the day it is taken
and reviewed by people doing their jobs properly.
And there is no counterparty for the alternative, because a manufacturer that spent the money could not
have charged anybody for it: the vial would have been the same vial, and the sheet would have shown a
higher number.
