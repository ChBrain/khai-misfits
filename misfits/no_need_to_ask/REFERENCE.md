# No Need to Ask: Reference

The research warrant for **No Need to Ask**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon named the
**political resource curse**, or the rentier state: where a state is funded by the rents of a
resource rather than by taxing its citizens, it need not seek their consent to raise its revenue, so
the old bargain that ties taxation to representation is severed and the accountability that bargain
forced quietly atrophies, all cited below, held with the finding that the effect is contested and
conditional on prior institutions, not a determinism, since states that entered the windfall with
strong accountable institutions have escaped it and the cross-national evidence is genuinely
disputed.

## Line of Work

Staging the political resource curse as a **severed-fiscal-link structure**: a state comes into a great flow of money from
a resource, oil, gas, a mineral, that it sells abroad, and the money arrives at the treasury without passing through the
pockets of its own people. This is the strange and load-bearing fact: the ordinary state must tax to live, and to tax it
must bargain with those it taxes, account for what it takes, and submit to being checked, so that historically the power to
tax and the power to hold power to account grew up together, no taxation without representation. The rentier state is freed
from that. It need not ask its citizens for money, and a state that need not ask need not answer. So it does the locally
sensible thing with the rents: it distributes rather than extracts, spending on subsidy and public jobs and patronage that
buy quiet, and on the means to keep order, rather than on the slow institutions, the capable bureaucracy, the courts, the
legislature that holds the purse, that a taxing state is forced to build. And the citizen does the locally sensible thing in
turn: untaxed and given the subsidy, they press no hard claim to hold the rulers to account, since the lever that ordinary
publics hold, the withholding of the revenue the state depends on, is not theirs to pull, the state depending on the ground
and not on them. Each move is reasonable on its own, and their sum is a state grown unaccountable, its checking institutions
withered for want of the pressure that builds them, its wealth leaving its governance weaker than no windfall would have.
It is authored by no villain: the tale is not of a wicked ruler alone, because the mechanism bites even a government of
ordinary good will, since it is the funding and not the character that severs the link, and the citizen who trades voice for
the handout is not a fool but is answering the incentives in front of them. The governing law is not that anyone is
malicious, but that **where a state is funded by resource rents rather than by taxing its citizens, it need not seek their
consent to raise revenue, so the fiscal bargain that ties taxation to representation and forces accountable institutions is
severed, and each party acts rationally, the ruler spending rents on patronage and subsidy rather than on the institutions
that would constrain him, the citizen taking the subsidy and, untaxed, pressing no claim to hold power to account, so
accountability and the institutions that check power atrophy and the windfall leaves governance weaker than no windfall
would have, no villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the rents that arrive without asking (Mahdavy),
the severed link between taxation and representation (Beblawi and Luciani), the distributive handout that buys quiet in
place of accountability (Karl), the untaxed and quiescent citizen, the withered institutions and the affordable repression
(Ross), and the strong prior institutions that alone let a state escape the curse (Mehlum, Moene and Torvik), as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a morality tale of a greedy despot.

It is a fiscal-and-accountability channel of the resource curse no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from
its nearest neighbours. It is distinct from Dutch Disease, which holds the economic channel of the same windfall: there the
resource money lifts the real exchange rate and prices the country's factories and farms out of their markets, hollowing the
tradable economy through prices and the currency, whereas here the money runs through the treasury and the citizen-state
bargain, severing the fiscal link that forces accountability, so that the harm is to governance and institutions and not to
the exchange rate and the tradables, and a state can suffer either channel without the other. It is distinct from
Rent-Seeking, where real resources are burned in the competition to capture a transfer that creates nothing: that
wasteful competition is one channel through which the curse can work, as elites contend for a share of the rents, but the
curse's load-bearing vector is not the dissipation of the transfer, it is the severed tax-link and the accountability that
withers with it, the citizen's silence and not only the elite's scramble. And it is distinct from any reading as mere
kleptocracy or a wicked ruler, because the structure bites a government of ordinary good will as surely as a greedy one:
what severs the link is the funding and not the character, so the curse is not the story of a thief in the palace but of a
treasury filled from the ground rather than from the people.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the phenomenon as the tale of
  a greedy despot, because the source finding it stages is that the curse runs through the funding and not through anyone's
  character: a state funded by rents rather than by taxes is loosed from the bargain that forces accountability whoever sits
  at its head, and the mechanism bites a government of ordinary good will as surely as a rapacious one. The ruler who spends
  rents on subsidy and patronage rather than on slow institutions is doing the locally sensible thing, and the citizen who,
  untaxed and given the handout, presses no claim to account is answering the incentives before them and is no fool. The
  loss is authored by no one: it is the accountability that never grew because the pressure that builds it, the state's need
  of its citizens' money, was never there, and the misfit is that the very wealth that should have freed a people leaves the
  power over them less answerable than poverty would have.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, to build or keep the
  accountable institutions before the windfall lands, to tax citizens even when the rents make it needless, to bind the
  rents in a transparent fund and publish the accounts, so that the fiscal link is held open by design, but it is neither
  free nor reliable: it asks a state to impose on itself the very discipline the rents were meant to spare it, it depends on
  a prior strength most petro-states did not have, and the pull to spend the rents on quiet rather than on checks returns
  with every boom. The reckoning is that the severed link is inherent in rent-funding, so wherever a state can live on what
  it sells rather than on what it taxes, the accountability that taxation forces has to be built against the grain.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the rentier state and its name are Mahdavy's, the rentier-state theory and the
  no-taxation-no-representation corollary Beblawi and Luciani's, the fiscal sociology of the petro-state Karl's, and the
  cross-national evidence and the channels Ross's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: the effect is
  genuinely contested and conditional, not a law. It is conditional on prior institutions, a windfall meeting
  producer-friendly institutions proving a blessing and grabber-friendly ones a curse (Mehlum, Moene and Torvik), so that
  states entering the boom with strong accountable institutions have escaped it (Norway, Botswana); and the very
  cross-national link between resource wealth and authoritarian rule has been challenged by work using long within-country
  series that finds little support for it (Haber and Menaldo). The scope is held to states heavily dependent on resource
  rents in place of taxation, and not stretched into a claim that every resource-rich state is cursed or that resources make
  bad government inevitable.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Rents**: carries the setup (Mahdavy), the great flow of money the state receives from selling the resource abroad,
  arriving at the treasury without passing through its citizens' pockets, revenue it need ask no one for.
- **The Severed Link**: carries the engine (Beblawi and Luciani), the broken fiscal bargain, that because the state need not
  tax it need not bargain, account, or submit to being checked, so the tie between taxation and representation is cut.
- **The Handout**: carries the distributive turn (Karl), the state spending the rents on subsidy, public jobs, and patronage
  that buy quiet, distributing rather than extracting, in place of the slow institutions a taxing state must build.
- **The Unasked**: carries the demand side, the citizen who, untaxed and given the handout, presses no claim to hold power to
  account, rationally trading the voice they cannot easily exercise for the subsidy in hand, the lever of withheld revenue
  not theirs to pull.
- **The Hollow Institutions**: carries the harm (Ross), the accountability institutions, the capable bureaucracy, the
  courts, the legislature that holds the purse, that wither for want of the pressure that builds them, while repression
  stays affordable to a state that need not tax.
- **The Prior Strength**: carries the remedy and the bound (Mehlum, Moene and Torvik; Haber and Menaldo), the strong
  accountable institutions that predate the windfall and alone let a state escape the curse, costly and rare and against the
  grain, and the marker of the hedge, since the curse is contested and conditional on institutions and not a determinism,
  and much resource wealth does no such harm.
