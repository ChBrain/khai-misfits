# The Weather We Make: Reference

The research warrant for **The Weather We Make**, a staging of the phenomenon the literature calls
**endogenous risk** (Daníelsson and Shin): that where every institution measures risk with the same
kind of model, estimated on the same history, and is required to act on what that measure says, the
model's founding premise, that prices are given from outside and do not respond to the holder's
reaction to them, is falsified by the fact that everyone holds it, so a shock makes every model say
reduce exposure at the same moment and the synchronised selling produces exactly the extreme move the
models had assigned a vanishing probability. It is held with the finding that no participant games
anything, that the failure is a property of compliance rather than of evasion, and that every
available remedy trades measured accuracy for aggregate stability.

## Line of Work

Staging endogenous risk as a **the-measure-makes-the-thing-it-measures structure**: the ordinary
expectation of a risk model is that it describes a world it does not disturb, in the way a barometer
describes weather it does not cause. Estimate the distribution of returns on history, read off the
loss that will be exceeded only rarely, hold capital against it, and cut the position when the number
says the risk has risen. Every step is prudent, and for one institution acting alone every step is
also true, because one bank's selling does not move the market. The finding is that this last clause
is the whole of the matter and it fails precisely when it is needed. Two conditions do the work
together and neither is sufficient alone. The first is **harmonisation**: supervisory regimes that
require a risk-sensitive, quantitative, auditable measure push every institution toward the same
class of model estimated on the same observable history, so the models do not merely resemble one
another, they agree, and they update together. The second is the **binding constraint**: a measure
tied to a capital or limit rule converts a number into a forced action, so agreement between models
becomes simultaneity of trade. When a shock arrives, measured volatility rises, every model reports
that risk has risen, every limit binds at once, and every holder reduces the same exposures into a
market where the only available counterparties are other holders doing the same thing. The price
falls because they are selling, measured volatility rises further because the price fell, and the
loop runs. The extreme move is not a failure of the models to foresee the world; it is the world the
models made. The play models the mandate that demands a number, the model that becomes common, the
exogeneity assumption baked into the estimate, the forced unwinding, the tail that the unwinding
produces, and the remedy that has to buy stability with accuracy, as the load-bearing vectors of the
structure, and not as a tale of reckless banks. The ordinary expectation is that better measurement
makes a system safer; the finding is that identical measurement, acted on under a common constraint,
is itself a channel of contagion. The governing law is that **where every participant measures risk
the same way and must act on what the measure reports, the measure's premise that prices are
independent of the response to it is false, so the responses arrive together and the market makes the
move the models had ruled out**.

It is a measurement-and-feedback structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from
its nearest neighbours. It is distinct from Metric Fixation, where a measure becomes a target and
effort flows toward the measured dimension until the number is a true account of the wrong thing:
there someone changes their behaviour to serve the metric, and the corruption is in the behaviour,
whereas here **nobody redirects any effort at all**, every institution acts honestly on a number that
is correctly computed and correctly reported, and the number is falsified after the fact by the
aggregate of honest responses to it, so this is a failure of compliance rather than of gaming and
would not be improved by anyone behaving better. It is distinct from The Long Calm, Minsky's
financial instability hypothesis, where a stretch of quiet endogenously shifts the financing
structure from hedge toward speculative and Ponzi: there the driver is **belief**, revised over years
as a calm validates itself, and the fragility is built into balance sheets slowly, whereas here the
driver is a **mandated mechanical rule** that needs no revision of anyone's beliefs, and the whole
loop can run in an afternoon; and Minsky has no counterpart at all to this play's load-bearing
vector, that everyone is holding the same model. It is distinct from The Bank Run, where symmetric
depositors face a sequential-service constraint on a finite fund and a guarantor selects the good
equilibrium: there the trouble is a queue for something that can be exhausted and the cure is a
credible third party, whereas here nothing is depleted, there is no queue and no first-come
advantage, and the selling is compelled by a limit rather than by any belief about what others will
do. And it is distinct from The Beauty Contest, where price is set by a regress of beliefs about
others' beliefs: there the mechanism is inherently about second-guessing, whereas here no participant
forms any belief about any other participant, the response is mechanical and mandate-driven, and the
loop would run in full even under common knowledge of fundamentals and of every other institution's
position.

It joins the house's **Debt-Deflation** on the axis `simultaneous-deleveraging`, at the same sign and from
a different cause. There the simultaneity comes from **a common shock and a common obligation**: many
borrowers are called on at once and each sells to reduce a debt, so the price falls and the real burden of
what is left rises. Here it comes from **a common measure**: harmonised, risk-sensitive models estimated on
the same history agree and update together, so a single reading turns many independent prudent decisions
into one sale. **Stipulate that no two holders sell at the same time** and this play has nothing left,
which is the test that puts it on the dial. What it adds is that the coordination needs no shared exposure
at all, only a shared instrument for looking at exposure.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast a
  reckless bank, a captured supervisor or a quant who knew and said nothing, because the finding it
  stages needs none of them. The risk officer runs a validated model, reports the number it produces,
  and would be removed for reporting any other. The supervisor requires a quantitative, auditable,
  risk-sensitive measure for excellent reasons, since the alternative is trusting discretion it cannot
  inspect and could not defend afterwards. The desk cuts exposure when the limit binds, which is the
  prudent and mandated response and the one every rulebook demands. Nobody games anything, nobody
  hides anything, and the whole loop runs on institutions doing precisely what they were told to do,
  which is why it is not reachable by enforcement: there is nothing here to enforce against.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction, and here every
  remedy is a purchase of stability with accuracy. Deliberate model diversity works, and means
  requiring some institutions to run models that are measurably worse at describing their own risk,
  which nobody can be asked to volunteer for and no supervisor can comfortably mandate.
  Countercyclical buffers work, and mean holding capital idle through good years and releasing it by a
  discretionary judgement that is hardest to make at the moment it is needed and politically hardest
  to defend afterwards. Risk-insensitive leverage caps work, and are crude by construction, so they
  penalise the genuinely safe balance sheet alongside the dangerous one and invite the migration of
  risk to wherever the cap does not reach. The reckoning is that the accurate measure and the stable
  system are in tension, and that a supervisor must choose how much of one to give up.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here,
  never claimed as the house's. The account of risk as endogenous to the system's response to its own
  measurement is Daníelsson and Shin's; the limits of statistical risk modelling and the warning that a
  harmonising supervisory regime raises systemic risk rather than lowering it are Daníelsson's, in the
  2002 paper and in the Academic Response to Basel II written with Embrechts, Goodhart, Keating,
  Muennich, Renault and Shin, all of whom are credited by name for it. Charles Goodhart is a co-author
  of that response and is a separate matter from Goodhart's Law, which the house stages elsewhere; the
  two are different contributions by the same person and the warrant does not conflate them. The scope
  is held to systems in which a common risk measure is coupled to a binding constraint, and is not
  stretched into a claim that risk measurement is useless, that capital regulation is a mistake, or
  that models should be abandoned, since the source literature argues for how such systems should be
  designed and not against measuring risk at all.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Limit**: carries the constraint that turns a number into an action (Daníelsson and colleagues,
  the Academic Response), the capital or exposure rule keyed to the measure, without which a shared
  model would produce shared opinions and no shared trade.
- **The Same Model**: carries harmonisation, the load-bearing vector and the one with no counterpart in
  the neighbouring plays: not that the models resemble one another but that they agree and update
  together, because a supervisory regime demanding an auditable risk-sensitive measure pushes every
  institution to the same class of model on the same observable history.
- **The Weather**: carries the exogeneity premise (Daníelsson and Shin), the assumption that the price
  is something that happens to you rather than something you help make, true for any one holder acting
  alone and false for all of them acting together, which is the one condition on which the whole
  apparatus rests.
- **The Rush**: carries the forced unwinding, every limit binding in the same hour and every holder
  reducing the same exposure into a market whose only available counterparties are other holders doing
  the same thing.
- **The Tail**: carries the outcome (Daníelsson and Shin), the extreme move the models priced as
  nearly impossible, produced by the models rather than missed by them, so the failure is not one of
  foresight and would not be repaired by a better estimate of the same distribution.
- **The Boundary**: carries the condition and the hedge, that the trap needs a common measure coupled
  to a binding constraint and lifts where either is absent, and that each remedy, model diversity,
  countercyclical buffers, or a risk-insensitive cap, buys aggregate stability with measured accuracy,
  so the choice is a real one and not an oversight to be pointed out.
