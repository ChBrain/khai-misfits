# Play: The Weather We Make

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

The Weather We Make.
A risk model is built the way a barometer is built. It looks at what the world has done, it reports what the world is likely to do, and it does not suppose that reading it changes anything. Estimate the distribution of returns from the history, read off the loss that will be exceeded only rarely, hold capital against that, and reduce the position when the number says the risk has risen. Every step is prudent. For one institution acting alone, every step is also true, because one bank's selling does not move a market.
And that last clause is the whole of the matter, and it fails exactly when it is needed.
And it fails because of two things that are each entirely reasonable and only dangerous together. The first is that everyone comes to hold the same model. Not similar models: the same one, in the sense that matters, because a supervisor asking for a risk measure that is quantitative, auditable and sensitive to conditions is asking for a particular kind of model estimated on a particular kind of history, and the good answers to that request converge. So the models do not merely resemble each other. They agree, and they update together (Daníelsson and Shin).
And the second is that the number is wired to an action. A measure tied to a capital rule or a trading limit is not an opinion, it is an instruction, so agreement between models becomes simultaneity of trade. Without the wire you would have a room full of people who think the same thing. With it you have a room full of people who do the same thing in the same minute.
And so the shock arrives, and nothing about what follows is a mistake. Measured volatility rises. Every model reports that risk has risen, correctly, because it has. Every limit binds. Every holder reduces the same exposure. And the only counterparties available are other holders reducing the same exposure, so the price falls because they are selling, and measured volatility rises further because the price fell, and every model reports that risk has risen again.
And the extreme move at the end of it was priced by those models as a thing that happens once in many lifetimes. It was not missed. It was manufactured. The models did not fail to foresee the weather; the weather was the sound of everybody reading the same forecast and reaching for the same door (Daníelsson and Shin).
And nobody in it is a villain. The risk officer runs a validated model and reports the number it produces, and would be removed for reporting any other. The supervisor asks for a measure it can inspect, which is the right thing to ask for, since the alternative is trusting a discretion it cannot audit and could not defend afterwards. The desk cuts exposure when the limit binds, which is the prudent response and the mandated one. There is nothing here to enforce against, because there is no rule that anybody broke.
And now the cruelty. The remedy is real and it costs the thing the whole apparatus was built to provide. You can require model diversity, which means telling some institutions to run models that are measurably worse at describing their own risk, and no supervisor can comfortably order that and no bank will volunteer. You can hold countercyclical buffers, which means capital sitting idle through the good years and released by a discretionary judgement that is hardest to make at the exact moment it is needed. You can impose a crude leverage cap that ignores risk altogether, which penalises the genuinely safe balance sheet alongside the dangerous one and pushes risk to wherever the cap does not reach. Every one of them buys a steadier system by giving up an accurate measure (Daníelsson).
And so the misfit is not that banks are reckless but that identical measurement, honestly performed and dutifully acted on, is itself a channel of contagion, and that better measurement is not the same thing as a safer system. Endogenous risk, risk generated by the system's response to its own measurement, is Daníelsson and Shin's; the limits of statistical risk modelling and the warning that a harmonising regime raises systemic risk rather than lowering it are Daníelsson's, and the Academic Response to Basel II that made the warning in advance was written with Embrechts, Goodhart, Keating, Muennich, Renault, and Shin. The house stages the mandate, the common model, the exogeneity premise, the forced unwinding, the tail and the priced remedy, and holds the bound in view: that it needs a shared measure wired to a binding constraint, that it lifts where either is missing, and that the corrections are purchases and not repairs, so the whole of it is that a barometer everybody obeys at once stops describing the weather and starts making it.

## Arc

The bend is this: where every participant measures risk the same way and must act on what the measure reports, the premise that prices are independent of the response to the measure is false, so the responses arrive together and the market makes the move the models had ruled out, with no rule broken and nothing to enforce against.
It runs on a number the supervisor can audit.
A risk-sensitive, quantitative, inspectable measure is the right thing to ask for, discretion being the thing that cannot be checked or defended after the fact.
And a convergence nobody ordered. The good answers to that request are the same answer, so the models do not merely resemble each other, they agree and they update together (Daníelsson and Shin).
And a wire from the number to the hand. A measure tied to a capital rule is an instruction and not an opinion, so agreement between models becomes simultaneity of trade.
And a premise that holds for one and fails for all. The model treats the price as weather, given from outside, which is true for any single holder acting alone and false for every holder acting at once.
And a shock that starts a correct chain. Volatility rises, every model reports it, every limit binds, every holder reduces the same exposure, and the only counterparties are the others doing the same.
And a tail the models made. The price falls because they are selling and volatility rises because it fell, and the once-in-many-lifetimes move is manufactured rather than missed.
And nobody to blame anywhere in it. The officer reports what the model says, the supervisor asks for what it can inspect, the desk obeys the limit, and no rule is broken.
And the whole is held against its own limit. It needs a common measure wired to a binding constraint, and every remedy, diversity, buffers, or a crude cap, buys a steadier system by giving up an accurate one (Daníelsson).

## Stakes

Whether a system can measure its own risk without the measuring becoming the risk.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward a step that is correct, mandated and inspectable, which each of them is, and toward a market in which the same instruction lands on every desk at once, because a measure that everybody shares and everybody must obey turns agreement into simultaneity. The production cannot let a better estimate rescue it, because the failure is not one of foresight: the extreme move was not missed by the models, it was made by them, so refitting the same distribution on more history buys nothing and refitting it on the crisis itself teaches the models to agree about the next one too. It can only show that nobody is reckless and nobody is captured, that the risk officer reports what a validated model produces and would be removed for reporting anything else, that the supervisor asks for an auditable number because a discretion it cannot inspect is the thing it may not accept, that the desk cuts exposure because the limit binds and the rulebook is unambiguous, and that there is nothing here to enforce against because no rule was broken by anyone. And it holds the bound in view, because the trap is conditional and not universal: it needs a common measure wired to a binding constraint and lifts where either is missing, and every correction is a purchase rather than a repair, since model diversity means mandating worse models, countercyclical buffers mean idle capital released on the hardest judgement to make, and a crude cap means penalising the safe alongside the dangerous (Daníelsson and Shin; Daníelsson), so the whole of the misfit is that a barometer everybody obeys at once stops describing the weather and starts making it.
