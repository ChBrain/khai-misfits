# Play: Longer Than Its Meaning

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Longer Than Its Meaning.
Someone knows something worth more than the one life they have to tell it in, so they cut it into the one thing built to outlast a body: rock, a slab, a marker meant to still be standing when no one who set it is left to explain it. The cutting works. The stone holds. What it holds is not the same as what was meant: to fit a surface that cannot be revised, the message is cut down to its hardest edges, and the softer parts, the ones that would have said why, or how much, or when to stop reading it as one thing and start reading it as another, do not survive the cut. A stranger finds it, later than anyone can say, and reads it the only way a reader can, in the frame they have, which is not the frame it was cut in. The care that went into the making is still there to see, and it says the wrong thing: something built this well, this heavy, standing this long, must be worth having, must be worth digging for, must be exactly what a warning is not supposed to say. Nobody on either end did anything wrong. The maker made the only choice that could outlast them. The stranger reads the only way a stranger can. The message is still standing, and still not received.

## Arc

The bend is this: the choice that lets a message outlive its maker is the same choice that keeps it from being understood, and there is no way to spend on one account without spending against the other.
It runs on a gap no living voice can cross. The maker is gone before the reader arrives, so the marks left behind must do, alone, what a person would do face to face: answer a question, add a caveat, catch a raised eyebrow and adjust. A cut in rock can do none of that, so what is cut to outlast a body must also outlast its own explanation, and nothing is left standing to give one.
Endurance sets the terms the whole way down. A message chosen to survive is a message reduced to what a hard surface will hold: cut the softening, cut the parts that only made sense at the time, cut the frame itself, since a frame is exactly the connective tissue that will not fit in the cut. The maker cuts in good faith, reducing only what the medium will not carry. The cutting is what erases the one thing a stranger will need first.
And the marks left behind do not read as neutral once the frame that made them is gone. Care shows as skill, skill shows as expense, expense shows as value, and a warning meant to repel a hand instead calls it closer, because nothing signals worth having to a stranger as plainly as something someone worked hard to make. Strip the skill to kill that invitation, and the same stroke strips the one quality that would have made a passerby stop, wonder, and leave it standing at all: an ordinary rock asks nothing of anyone, not even to be spared.
Either way, no report comes back. The reader who could confirm the marks worked, or confirm they did not, is exactly the reader the maker will never meet, so the message closes with no word at either end: sent in full and read, or unread, or read backward, and nobody on the sending side will ever learn which.

## Triggers

**[The Cut](plot_the_cut.md)**
The Maker holds knowledge worth more than a lifetime and cuts it into the one carrier built to outlast one: the Compressing runs the message down to what the stone will hold, and the Frame it leans on goes uncut and unnoticed, assumed rather than carved. Closes when the marker stands complete and the Maker is gone.

**[Nothing Worth Taking](plot_nothing_worth_taking.md)**
Cue: the same marker, finished but for one question, whether its own craft will get it carried off before it is ever read. The Warder strips it of the very quality that would draw a hand toward it, at the cost of the quality that would have made anyone want to keep it standing at all. Closes when the marker is made too plain to want and too plain to protect.

**[The Find](plot_the_find.md)**
Cue: the Site, no longer within reach of anyone who could explain it, found by a Stranger carrying a frame the Maker never met. The Inverting turns the marker's own signals against it: what reads as care reads as value, what reads as plain reads as nothing, and the warning is taken up as an invitation or set down as empty ground. Closes when the Stranger has read the marker in the only frame they have.

**[No Word Comes Back](plot_no_word_comes_back.md)**
Cue: the whole exchange laid end to end, the cutting, the stripping, the finding, set beside the one question none of them can answer. The Trade is stated whole and the Silence closes it: whichever way the marker was read, no word of it will ever reach the one who cut it. Closes when it is plain that the account never balances back to the sender.

## Stakes

Whether a message cut to outlast its maker ever reaches the reader it was cut for, or only ever reaches a reader, and the wrong one, reading it in a frame it was never built for.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a carrier that keeps the message standing and away from a meaning that keeps it readable, because the stone that survives the centuries is exactly the stone that cannot be revised once the centuries have run. The production cannot make the Maker meet the Stranger, because the whole design is built on the certainty that it will not happen, so it can only show that no one erred: cutting into rock is the right choice for a message that must outlive you, stripping it plain is the right answer to a warning that must not be carried off, and reading a found marker in the only frame on hand is the only thing a reader can do. What the run holds open, plot to plot, is which price gets paid where, mass read as treasure or plainness read as nothing, and whether the Maker ever learns which, which the Silence answers before anyone asks it: never.
