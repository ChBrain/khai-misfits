# Play: It Was There When I Wrote It

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

It Was There When I Wrote It.
A judgement, or a paper, resting part of its argument on a source, cited exactly: author, title, date, address. The author checked it. It said what they said it said.
Some years later a reader follows the reference and arrives at nothing. Not a contradiction, not a retraction, not a different claim: nothing.
The judgement is still in force and still authoritative, and the sentence that carried its support now carries a string of characters.

## Arc

The bend is this: the document survives perfectly and its support evaporates underneath it, and every party in the sequence behaved correctly at the moment they acted.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: a citation fixes an address rather than a thing, and nobody owes anybody a permanent address.
Take the citation first, because the play has to establish that it was **good**. It was precise, it was checked, and it was honest: the author read the source, represented it accurately, and recorded where it could be found. Nothing about the scholarship was careless, and doing it any other way was not available, because the address was the only handle the thing had.
Then the host, who is equally blameless. A site is reorganised, a department is renamed, a company is bought, a project ends, a server is retired, a domain lapses. **No host anywhere undertook to keep a URL alive for forty years**, none was asked to, and every one of those acts is ordinary administration that any reasonable body performs.
Then the asymmetry, which is the whole play. The citing document is durable by design: it is printed, deposited, indexed, and it will still be legible in a century. The cited target is durable by nobody's design at all. **So the two halves of a reference decay at completely different rates**, and the more permanent the citing document, the longer it spends pointing at nothing.
And the failure is silent, which is what lets it accumulate. A broken reference does not announce itself: the text reads exactly as it did, the footnote is still well formed, and nothing in the document is now false. Only a reader who actually goes looking discovers it, and most readers do not go looking, so a body of work can rot through without anybody noticing that it has.
The measurement is what makes this a finding rather than a complaint. More than **seventy per cent** of the addresses cited in one set of law reviews, and **fifty per cent** of those in United States Supreme Court opinions, no longer reach what was cited.
Reach for a settlement and there is a good one, and it has a hard condition. Archive the target at the moment of citing and cite the archived copy, which works and is done. But it must be done **then**: once the target is gone there is nothing left to preserve, so the remedy cannot be applied to any of the existing damage and protects only what is cited from now on. **The cure is available and is not retrospective**, and the corpus already written stays as it is.

## Stakes

Whether a record can outlive the things it rests on.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the author was careless, because the citation was checked and exact, and not whether the host was negligent, because nobody undertook to keep an address alive and every change was ordinary administration, but whether a durable document can rest on a support that nothing is keeping. The production cannot make it so. It can only show that a citation fixes an address rather than a thing, that the two halves of a reference are kept by different parties with different obligations and decay at different rates, that the failure is silent and therefore cumulative, that most of a measured corpus has already gone, and that the remedy is real, is in use, must be applied at the moment of citing, and reaches nothing that was written before it existed.
