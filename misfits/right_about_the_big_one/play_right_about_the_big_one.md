# Play: Right About the Big One

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Right About the Big One.
A forecaster reports what they actually believe, every day, for years. A scoring rule exists precisely so that this is the best thing they can do. Then the storm arrives, and the review is held on the storm, because that is the case anybody cares about. Scored over those days alone, the forecaster who has been quietly saying the true number comes out behind the one who leans toward the extreme, and the rule that made honesty optimal has stopped saying so. Nobody switched the rule. The review simply took it over a different set of days.

## Arc

The bend is this: a scoring rule is proper with respect to the cases it is averaged over, and the only average anybody computes is over the cases where the extreme happened.
It runs on a rule that does one job.
[The Proper Score](piece_the_proper_score.md) is built so that a forecaster maximises their expected score by reporting their honest belief and by nothing else. That is the whole of what propriety means, and it holds over the full distribution of cases.
And the review happens after the big one.
[The Reviewer](persona_the_reviewer.md) convenes in [the post-mortem](place_the_post_mortem.md), on the storm, the crash, the outbreak. This is correct: a forecast earns its keep at the extremes, nobody calls a review after an ordinary Tuesday, and nobody should.
Then [Conditioning on the Outcome Breaks the Rule](plot_conditioning_on_the_outcome_breaks_the_rule.md).
[The Extreme Subset](piece_the_extreme_subset.md) is selected by what happened rather than by what was forecast, so the average is taken over a different distribution from the one the rule is proper against. Scored there, the report that maximises the expected score is no longer the honest one, and the arithmetic does this on its own.
And [The One Who Called It Is the Worse Forecaster](plot_the_one_who_called_it_is_the_worse_forecaster.md).
[The One Who Called It](persona_the_one_who_called_it.md) is not cheating and need not have decided anything: leaning toward the extreme is simply the strategy the conditional review rewards, and a person who sincerely holds wilder beliefs wins by it without choosing to. What that record signals is a strategy that is worse over everything.
And [The Noise](persona_the_noise.md) sets how hard it bites. The lower the signal in a domain, the further the conditional ranking departs from the honest one, so the distortion is largest where the forecasts are hardest and matter most.
And [the weight](piece_the_weight.md) has to be chosen before. The remedy is published and it works, and it asks the reviewer to fix what counts as an extreme in advance, and to be the kind of audience that reads a weighted score at all.

## Triggers

**[A Score Is Only Proper Over All of It](plot_a_score_is_only_proper_over_all_of_it.md)**
Cue: an instrument built so that honesty is a forecaster's best answer. Closes when propriety has been located in the average over the full distribution of cases, so the guarantee is a property of the rule and the sample together and never of the rule alone.

**[The Review Happens After the Big One](plot_the_review_happens_after_the_big_one.md)**
Cue: a storm that was not forecast, and a room convened about it. Closes when caring about the extremes has been shown to be correct rather than lazy, since a forecast earns its keep there and no review is ever called after an ordinary week.

**[Conditioning on the Outcome Breaks the Rule](plot_conditioning_on_the_outcome_breaks_the_rule.md)**
Cue: an honest score, computed honestly, over the days the extreme happened. Closes when the report maximising the expected score has been found to be no longer the honest one, by arithmetic, with no error and no bad faith anywhere in the room.

**[The One Who Called It Is the Worse Forecaster](plot_the_one_who_called_it_is_the_worse_forecaster.md)**
Cue: the forecaster everybody remembers, the one who called the crash. Closes when that record has been read as a signal of a strategy that leans toward extremes and performs worse over everything, rather than as evidence of judgement.

**[It Bites Hardest Where the Noise Is](plot_it_bites_hardest_where_the_noise_is.md)**
Cue: two domains, one predictable and one barely. Closes when the departure from the honest ranking has been shown to grow as the signal falls, so the distortion is largest exactly where forecasting is hardest and matters most.

**[And the Weight Has to Be Chosen Before](plot_and_the_weight_has_to_be_chosen_before.md)**
Cue: a published remedy that restores propriety while still emphasising the extremes. Closes when it has been found to require a weight function fixed in advance and an audience willing to read a weighted score, which relocates the judgement rather than removing it.

## Stakes

Whether an evaluation that only ever looks at the cases that mattered can reward telling the truth.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a rule whose guarantee holds over all the cases, a review that is right to be held on the extremes, an average taken over a set selected by the outcome, a record that reads as judgement and signals a strategy, and a remedy that asks somebody to say in advance what will count, and away from any account in which a forecaster hedged or a reviewer was unfair. The production cannot fault a room for wanting to know why the storm was missed, and it cannot fault a forecaster for saying the number they believed. What is left to fight over is not whether the extremes matter, they do, but what to call an evaluation that is honest, arithmetically correct, and rewards the wrong answer.
