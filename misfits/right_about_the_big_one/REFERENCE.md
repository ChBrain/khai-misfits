# Right About the Big One: Reference

The research warrant for Right About the Big One.
The term is the field's; the play runs on the forecaster's dilemma and on the propriety a conditional
evaluation destroys, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging forecast evaluation as **a guarantee attached to a sample nobody has a reason to take**: a proper
scoring rule is built so that a forecaster maximises the expected score by reporting the belief they actually
hold, which is the whole of what makes an evaluation trustworthy. That guarantee is an expectation, and an
expectation is taken over a distribution of cases, so it is a property of the rule and the sample together. The
review, when it comes, is convened after the storm, the crash or the outbreak, because a forecast earns its
keep in the tail and no institution has ever called a room together about an ordinary Tuesday. Scored over
those days alone, the report that maximises the expected score is no longer the honest one. The play models the
documented mechanics, the rule whose promise is conditional, the review that is right to be held where it is
held, the subset selected by what happened rather than by what was said, the record that reads as judgement and
signals a strategy, the noise that widens the gap, and the weight that must be fixed before, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a forecaster hedging or a press pack being unfair.

The governing law is that propriety is a joint property of a scoring rule and the distribution it is averaged
over. Conditioning on the realised outcome substitutes a different distribution, so the rule is unchanged, the
arithmetic is unchanged, and the answer to the question _what report should I make?_ moves toward the extreme.
Nothing has to be violated for this to happen, which is why it cannot be fixed by insisting that anybody behave
better.

What removes any possibility of blame from the reviewer is that caring about the extremes is correct. A
forecast is commissioned, funded and argued over for what it says about the tail, so a review held there is the
serious review rather than a lazy one, and there is no version of a conscientious evaluation culture that
convenes after a quiet week. The rule they reach for is a proper one and they compute it properly.

**And the reversal is that a perfect evaluator produces the same ranking.** This is what separates the
production from every account in which somebody is fooled: an evaluator with no hindsight whatever, no interest
in the outcome and no error in the sums, using a strictly proper rule on the selected days, still ranks the
forecaster who leans toward the extreme above the one who reported the truth. The defect is arithmetic and
survives the elimination of every psychological cause.

What removes it from the forecaster who wins is that they need not have decided anything. Leaning is the
strategy the conditional review rewards, so adopting it is rational for anybody who wants to keep the job, and
somebody who sincerely holds wilder beliefs arrives at the identical record having optimised against nothing at
all. An accurate call on an extreme is therefore a signal about method rather than about judgement, and the two
occupants of that seat cannot be told apart from the record.

And the noise decides how much of this there is. The further a domain is from deterministic, the further the
conditional ranking departs from the honest one, so the distortion is largest in exactly the domains that
commission forecasts and act on them, and is negligible where nobody needed a forecaster.

It is distinct from the house's **Clear From Here**, where a known outcome makes a decision look inevitable so
a sound choice with bad luck reads as negligence: the room is the same room, convened after the same storm, and
the mechanism is not. There the distortion is **psychological**, sitting in how a knowing observer perceives a
past decision, and the remedy is to record the reasoning before the outcome. Here the distortion survives the
complete removal of that: an evaluator immune to hindsight, computing a strictly proper score correctly, still
ranks the exaggerator first, because the damage is done by which cases are averaged and not by how any of them
is seen. One misfit is about a reader; this one would run in a machine.

It is distinct from the house's **Only the Close Ones**, where rational settlement filters the trial pool
toward a fifty-percent win rate so inference from decided cases misleads: both turn on a sample selected rather
than drawn, and the objects differ. There two adversaries jointly select the predictable cases **out**, and
what is damaged is an observer's inference about the merits. Here one party selects the extreme cases **in**,
openly and for a good reason, and what is damaged is a rule's **incentive property**, so the casualty is not
somebody's conclusion but the forecaster's reason to be honest.

It is distinct from the house's **The False Trade-Off**, where conditioning on a common effect of two causes
opens a spurious association: it is the nearest formal relative, since both are damage done by conditioning,
and the repairs point in opposite directions. There the casualty is an association in data and the repair is to
reason about the selection that built the sample, which can be done afterward by anybody who thinks clearly.
Here the casualty is a decision-theoretic property, and reasoning about the selection does not restore it: a
different rule is required, and it has to be chosen before the outcome is known.

It is distinct from the house's **Metric Fixation**, where a measure becomes a target and is gamed until it
stops measuring: there a proxy diverges from the goal under pressure, and the remedy is to relieve the pressure
or change the proxy. Here the score is not a proxy at all. A proper scoring rule is the exact instrument, it is
not corrupted, and nobody need be optimising against it, since the seat that wins may be occupied by somebody
who simply believes wilder things. Relieving every pressure in the room leaves the ranking exactly where it
was.

It is distinct from the house's **The Firefighter's Medal**, where nobody gets credit for problems that never
happened: there the non-event is **invisible**, and the difficulty is that the averted crisis leaves no trace
to reward. Here the quiet weeks are fully visible, completely recorded, and available to anybody who asks for
them. Nothing is hidden and nothing is missing: the record is honest and consulted selectively, which is a
harder problem than an absent one, because there is nothing to supply.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the reviewer as
  lazy or the press as unfair, and refuses equally to cast the forecaster who wins as a charlatan, because
  nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the honest report is the one propriety was designed to
  induce and is made faithfully for years; the review is convened where a forecast earns its keep, which is the
  serious place to convene it and the only place anybody ever does; the rule chosen is proper and the sums are
  correct; the sample is selected openly, for a stated and good reason; and the seat that benefits may be held
  by somebody who has optimised against nothing and simply believes wilder things. The failure is authored by
  nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that a guarantee about honesty is attached to an
  average nobody has a reason to compute.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Weighted and censored scoring
  rules are the real remedy, are published and do work, and they require somebody to declare in advance which
  outcomes count and how much, before anybody knows what will happen, with different weight functions producing
  different rankings, and then require an audience that came to find out who called the crash to accept a
  weighted average instead. Evaluating over all the cases restores the guarantee exactly and is the review no
  institution has a reason to hold. Reporting distributions rather than points does not reach it, since the
  conditioning is on the outcome and applies to any score. Penalising exaggeration requires knowing the
  forecaster's true belief, which is the thing the score existed to elicit. And knowing the mechanism
  completely, which everybody in the play may, still leaves the room convened after the storm.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the dilemma and its demonstration, the definition of propriety,
  the success-as-a-signal result, the tail-scoring remedies and the point-forecast argument are theirs. The
  bounds are carried with them: the result is about the **conditioning**, and how much practical distortion
  follows depends on a signal-to-noise ratio that is rarely measured, so the play stages the structure and
  never a magnitude; the weighted and censored rules are **proposals with substantive design choices** rather
  than a settled answer, and different weight functions rank forecasters differently, which the play carries as
  the cost of the remedy and not as a defect in it; the Denrell and Fang result is a model with supporting
  evidence in a different literature and is cited to complement the mechanism, never as a measurement of any
  actual forecaster; attending to extremes is legitimate and often necessary for decisions, and nothing here is
  an argument against caring about the tail; and treating "the review" as one act is the play's own
  simplification of a verification practice that takes many forms. **The play makes no claim that any
  forecaster exaggerated or that any evaluation was conducted in bad faith**, only that propriety is a joint
  property of a rule and a sample, and that the sample anybody has a reason to take is the one selected by what
  happened.
