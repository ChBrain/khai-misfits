# Stranger Than True: Reference

The research warrant for **Stranger Than True**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon Vosoughi,
Roy, and Aral documented as the differential spread of true and false news: people share what they
find novel and surprising, and false stories, unconstrained by what is true, are on average more
novel than true ones, so falsehood diffuses farther, faster, and deeper than truth, driven not by
bots and not by any taste for falsehood but by the ordinary human preference for the novel, all
cited below, held with the finding that the tilt is a tendency strongest in political news and
driven by novelty as the authors' best-supported explanation, and not a claim that all falsehood
spreads or that people prefer to be lied to.

## Line of Work

Staging the differential spread of false news as a **novelty-selection-amplifies-falsehood structure**: what
travels on a sharing network is selected not for being true but for being worth passing on, and what is worth
passing on is above all what is novel and surprising. To share a surprising thing is rational: it carries social
value, marking the sharer as informed and in the know, and it gives the receiver something new. But truth is
constrained by what actually happened, while a falsehood is free to be as surprising as it can be made or happens
to be, so false stories are on average more novel than true ones, and novelty is exactly what the sharing filter
selects. So the same rational preference for the novel, applied by each sharer to each story, systematically
amplifies falsehood over truth: the false travels farther, reaches more people, and gets there faster, while the
true is still lacing its boots. It is authored by no deceiver: each person is simply passing on the interesting
thing, and the study's own control shows that automated accounts spread true and false alike, so the differential
is the work of ordinary humans sharing the surprising, not of bots and not of any wish to mislead. The governing
law is not that people prefer falsehood, but that **where people share the news they find novel and surprising,
because novelty carries social and informational value, and false stories, unconstrained by what is true, are on
average more novel than true ones, each rational share of the surprising amplifies falsehood over truth, so the
public information environment systematically favours the false, with no one intending to deceive and no agent
required, the tilt driven by the human preference for the novel and not by bots or by any taste for falsehood**.
The play models the documented mechanics: the surprise that pulls the share (Vosoughi, Roy & Aral; Berger &
Milkman), the social value of passing on the novel, the novelty edge a falsehood has over a truth bound to the
facts, the far, fast, deep reach the false thereby wins, the true story left lagging, and the friction that could
slow the sharing at a cost, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a liar deceiving a
gullible crowd.

It is a diffusion-selection asymmetry no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from The Availability Cascade, where a belief gains felt truth by being retold, alarm
outrunning the evidence as repetition makes it feel more likely: there the engine is repetition breeding
felt-truth, a single belief snowballing, whereas here the engine is the novelty of the first telling driving reach
across the whole ecosystem, and it is which stories out-spread which, the novel and false over the mundane and
true, not a frequency-driven rise in one belief's credibility. It is distinct from Information Cascade, where each
rationally follows the crowd and discards their own private signal, so the herd locks onto the first few noisy
guesses: there people ignore their own information and copy others' observed choices, whereas here no private
signal is discarded and no choice is copied, each shares what is novel to them, and the selection is on the
content's surprise, not on the behaviour of the herd. It is distinct from Sick Enough to Spread, where a parasite
selected only to spread settles at the harm that spreads best: there a transmissible agent's own harm level is
tuned by selection for transmission, the harm instrumental to spreading, whereas here there is no evolving agent
and no harm being tuned, the sharers select stories by novelty and falsehood wins that filter incidentally, for
being unbound by truth, not because anything made it harmful to travel. And it is distinct from The Filter
Bubble's personalised narrowing of one reader's feed, in that here the tilt is in what spreads across the network
by its novelty, not in what a recommender shows one person by their clicks.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the sharer as a liar
  or the crowd as gullible, because the source finding it stages is that the tilt follows from the preference for
  the novel and not from any wish to deceive: each person rationally passes on the surprising thing, the study's
  own control shows bots spread true and false alike, and false news wins the sharing filter not because anyone
  prefers falsehood but because a falsehood, unbound by the facts, can be more novel than a truth. The loss is
  authored by no one: it is the false story reaching farther and faster than the true because it is more
  surprising, and the misfit is that the very appetite for the novel that makes sharing worthwhile is what carries
  the false ahead of the true.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The ways out exist, friction on
  the share, a prompt to read or verify before passing on, fact-checking and prebunking, media literacy, but none
  is free or fast: the correction always lags, because refuting a surprising falsehood costs far more than telling
  it and the truth reaches its audience long after the lie has, and friction on sharing fights the grain of the
  human preference for the novel and costs the reach and engagement a platform lives on. The reckoning is that the
  selection for novelty is inherent in how people share, so wherever the surprising is what gets passed on, the
  false that is stranger than the true travels ahead of it.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the differential spread of true and false news, the novelty measure,
  the emotion profile, and the bot control are Vosoughi, Roy, and Aral's, the arousal-and-sharing account is
  Berger and Milkman's, and the lie-outruns-truth image and the bullshit asymmetry are the folk saying's and
  Brandolini's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: that novelty is the authors' best-supported
  explanation of a robustly measured differential and not a settled causal certainty, that the tilt is a tendency,
  strongest in political news, that falsehood is more likely to spread and not that all falsehood spreads or that
  truth never does, and that people are shown to prefer the novel and not to prefer the false. The scope is held to
  the differential spread on sharing networks, and not stretched into a claim that people cannot tell true from
  false or that the truth never catches up.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Surprise**: carries the selection criterion (Vosoughi, Roy & Aral), the novelty of a story that decides
  whether it is worth passing on, the filter the sharing network applies to everything that moves through it.
- **The Social Currency**: carries the sharing motive (Berger & Milkman), the value a sharer gets from passing on
  the surprising, marked as informed and first, so that sharing the novel is rational and not idle.
- **The Novelty Edge**: carries the reason the false wins (Vosoughi, Roy & Aral), that a falsehood, unbound by
  what actually happened, can be made or happen to be more novel than a truth constrained to the facts, so it beats
  the true story at the one thing the filter selects.
- **The Wide Reach**: carries the harm (Vosoughi, Roy & Aral), the far, fast, deep spread the false story wins,
  reaching more people and reaching them sooner than the true, the documented differential that skews the shared
  picture of the world.
- **The Boots**: carries the lag (the folk saying; Brandolini), the true story still lacing its boots while the lie
  is round the world, and the correction that arrives long after, because refuting the surprising costs far more
  than telling it.
- **The Brake**: carries the remedy and the bound (Vosoughi, Roy & Aral; the platform-design literature), the
  friction on sharing that could slow the false, a prompt to verify, a check before the pass, costly because it
  fights the appetite for the novel and forfeits reach, and the marker of the hedge, since the tilt is a tendency
  strongest in political news and driven by novelty, not a law that all falsehood spreads.
