# They All Sent Something: Reference

The research warrant for They All Sent Something.
The term is the house's; the play runs on post-disaster relief and on what happens when the response arrives as
objects, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the relief surge as **an abundance that has to be undone before it can be used**: a disaster is announced
and people give, in enormous numbers, within hours, at their own cost, toward exactly the right place. What they
give is largely things. Every carton has to be received, opened, identified, sorted, stored or discarded before
any of it reaches anybody, and the people, vehicles and hours doing that sorting are the same ones that would be
distributing. Of what arrives at a large disaster, roughly three-fifths is non-priority and should not have been
sent, a further third is low-priority and belongs in storage for later, and something between a twentieth and a
tenth is what was needed on the day it landed. The play models the documented mechanics, the generosity that is
real and immediate, the tangibility that is the condition of the gift rather than a mistake about it, the
convergence that follows from the announcement, the sorting that is the only route to the work and is what blocks
it, the refusal that exists on paper and not in the world, and the person waiting two hundred metres from
everything, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of foolish donors or incompetent
responders.

The governing law is that aid arrives in proportion to how much people care, and the capacity to sort it does
not, so the caring is what buries the response.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The donor
gives immediately and at their own cost. The broadcaster issues the only appeal defensible before an assessment.
The logistician works flat out at the task that leads to every other. The mayor accepts because refusing costs a
destroyed town its goodwill. The affected person waits.

What removes any possibility of blame from the donor is that the form of the gift is a condition of its
existence rather than a misunderstanding about what helps. Relief professionals have asked for money instead in
every large disaster for decades, so the explanation cannot be that donors have not heard. What moves somebody to
act within hours, at their own cost, for strangers is the prospect of a definite thing reaching a definite person,
and a transfer is neither definite nor visibly theirs once made. Part of the hesitation about cash is also well
founded, since a donor cannot verify that it arrives. **The argument against the form is an argument against the
motive**, and removing the tangibility removes a large part of the giving with it.

What removes it from the broadcaster is that a needs assessment takes days and the response must begin in hours.
Naming particular items before anybody has assessed anything would be guessing, and the guess would be shipped in
bulk. So the only honest appeal at the moment it must be made is a general one, and a general appeal is answered
generally, by everybody, with whatever they have.

**And the convergence follows from the announcement, which is where the structure becomes concrete.** A disaster
is announced once, to everybody, so everybody who responds does so within the same short window. There is no
register of who is sending what, no authority the senders are subject to, and no way for a person packing a box to
learn what has already been packed. Meanwhile the receiving capacity is smaller than usual rather than larger,
because the disaster removed some of it.

**Then the sorting is the only route to the work and is what blocks it.** The priority items are genuinely present
and are dispersed through a volume many times their size, in cartons nobody labelled because nobody was
coordinating. One set of people, trucks and hours does both jobs, so every carton opened is a delivery not made,
and deferring the sorting is unavailable because perishables set the clock and the queue is outside now.

It is distinct from the house's **Everybody's Business**, which is the nearest incumbent by inversion and stages
the bystander effect, in which the presence of others makes each person less likely to act, so nobody does.
Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating a population in which every single person acts, and this misfit is not
weakened but produced: **everybody acting is the cause here**, and the harm scales with how many people helped.
The two are the same collective-action axis read in opposite directions, and the house had three misfits about
people not helping and none about people helping.

It is distinct from the house's **The Loud Few**, which stages the logic of collective action, where
concentrated benefits and diffuse costs leave a public good under-provided because no individual's
contribution is decisive. Remove that engine, by
stipulating contributors who all give regardless of pivotality, and this stands: the difficulty is
over-provision, arriving unsolicited and in the wrong composition, which that misfit's mechanism does not reach.

It is distinct from the house's **The One in Front of You**, which stages the greater pull of an identified
life over a statistical one and so concerns **which** causes attract giving. Remove that engine, by stipulating
donors perfectly indifferent between identified and statistical victims, and this stands, because the surge
converges on the announced disaster for the ordinary reason that it is where the need was announced, and the
congestion is a matter of timing and composition rather than of selection between causes.

It is distinct from the house's **The Extra Road**, which stages Braess's paradox, where adding a road makes
every journey worse because self-interested drivers re-route to a new equilibrium. Remove that engine, by stipulating agents who make no
routing choice at all, and this stands: donors are not choosing between paths, are not self-interested, and the
loss is a processing bottleneck rather than an equilibrium shift.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast donors as foolish or
  responders as incompetent, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the giving is real,
  immediate, at the giver's own cost and aimed at the right place; the form of the gift is the condition on which
  it exists, and the argument against the form is an argument against the motive; part of the hesitation about
  cash is well founded, since a donor cannot verify that it arrives; the appeal must be general because an
  assessment takes days and the response takes hours; the convergence follows from a single announcement with
  nobody coordinating and nobody able to; the sorting is the only route to the priority items and consumes the
  capacity that would deliver them; and refusal costs a destroyed town its goodwill without moving the pallets.
  The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the response arrives in
  proportion to how much people care and is handled at the rate one runway allows.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Asking for money instead is
  correct, is broadcast in every large disaster, and does not stop the goods, because the tangibility it argues
  against is what produces the giving. Publishing a needs list works partially and arrives after the flow has
  started, since the flow begins in hours and the assessment does not. Refusing consignments is available on
  paper and costs a town its goodwill at the moment it most needs it, while the pallets stay on the tarmac.
  Pre-positioning supplies and standing agreements are what professionals do, work well, and do not touch the
  unsolicited flow. Cash-transfer programming is the answer where it can run and depends on markets and payment
  rails a disaster may have removed. And knowing the whole structure, which the field has documented since 1957,
  leaves a broadcast tonight and a cupboard full of things somebody could send tomorrow.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the convergence literature, the material-convergence estimates and
  the humanitarian-logistics research are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether the flow can be
  redirected at scale rather than only absorbed better is genuinely contested**, and the play carries that
  argument rather than settling it; the composition figures are estimates for large disasters and vary
  considerably by event, by country and by how the response is organised, and are offered as the documented shape
  of the problem rather than as a constant; convergence has a personal and informational form as well as a
  material one, and only the material form is staged here; and the account treats sudden-onset disasters of very
  different kinds as one structure, which is its own simplification. **The play makes no claim about any
  particular disaster, any relief organisation, any government or any donor, and gives no advice about how or
  whether to give**, only that the response arrives in proportion to how much people care, and has to be gone
  through before it can help.
