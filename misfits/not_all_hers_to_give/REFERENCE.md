# Not All Hers to Give: Reference

The research warrant for **Not All Hers to Give**, a staging of **parent-offspring conflict and its genomic
imprinting extension** (Trivers; Haig): that a fetus is related to itself completely but a mother is related
by half to it and by half to every offspring she will ever have, so the fetus is selected to demand more
investment than the mother is selected to give, and that within the fetus's own genome a paternally inherited
gene copy, unrelated to any half sibling the mother might have by a different father, silences its more
restrained maternal counterpart at loci that raise the mother's blood sugar and blood pressure, so real
pathology, gestational diabetes and preeclampsia, can follow with no side at fault. It is held with the
finding that the effect is conditional on a viviparous kin system with loci under imprinting control, and
that it lifts toward convergence wherever a mother's every offspring shares the one father.

## Line of Work

Staging parent-offspring and imprinting conflict as an **asymmetric-relatedness-presses-the-transfer
structure**: a fetus and a mother look to share one project, a healthy birth, and in the largest sense they
do, but they are not identically invested, since the fetus is related to itself by the whole and the mother
is related to this fetus by only one half, and by that same one half to every other offspring she carries
now or will carry later (Trivers). A resource the mother would give until her cost to a future offspring
balances her gain to this one is, by the fetus's own calculus, worth taking past that balance, since the
fetus discounts only what the taking costs a future sibling it is related to by half again, so the fetus is
selected to want more than the mother is selected to provide, from the arithmetic of relatedness alone and
not from any excess on either side. The play models the documented mechanics: the wider ledger the mother
keeps against every offspring present and future (Trivers), the intragenomic split by which a paternally
inherited gene copy, having no stake in a possible future half sibling by a different father, presses for
extraction while evolution silences the more restrained maternally inherited copy at the same locus (Haig;
Haig and Moore), the hormonal mechanism by which the placenta, built from the fetus's own tissue, raises the
mother's blood sugar and blood pressure, demonstrated at the molecular level by a paternally expressed
growth gene moving fetal growth in the laboratory (DeChiara, Robertson and Efstratiadis), the maternal cover
that meets the press in the ordinary case, and the real pathology, gestational diabetes and preeclampsia,
when the press outruns the cover, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, and not as a claim that
pregnancy is adversarial or that either party is at fault. The governing finding of the source literature
is the play's governing law: where relatedness runs asymmetrically, once between parent and offspring and
again between an offspring's own two gene copies, the resulting demand runs past what any single ledger
would set, and where it outruns what the other side can cover, harm follows that traces to no defection and
no design fault.

It is a kin-conflict structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from The Arms Race, where two unrelated rivals escalate a costly, zero-sum
competition for relative rank: there the parties share no genetic interest at all and the waste is pure,
whereas here the two sides are close kin with a large overlapping interest in the same birth, and the
conflict is a difference of degree within that shared interest, not a race between strangers. It is
distinct from Saved to Spread, where a leaky vaccine relaxes the trade-off between a pathogen's virulence
and its transmission across an unrelated host population: there the mechanism is host-pathogen coevolution
under an epidemiological trade-off, whereas here the mechanism is Hamiltonian relatedness asymmetry between
kin who are not adversaries and share most of their genetic interest, played out as an intragenomic tug and
not as a host-parasite one.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the fetus as
  greedy or the mother as withholding, because the finding it stages is that both act soundly by their own
  gene's calculus: the paternally inherited copy presses because pressing is exactly what its relatedness
  rewards, having no stake in a possible half sibling by a different father, and the mother's system covers
  because covering is the sound response to a fetus that is hers to carry, rationing across every offspring
  she is related to by the same half (Trivers; Haig). The loss is authored by no one: it is a relatedness
  asymmetry run twice, once between mother and fetus and once between the fetus's own two gene copies, so
  the press runs past what the mother's ledger alone would set, harm arising from two sound calculi and not
  from any defection.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Screening for gestational
  diabetes and preeclampsia, and treating the overshoot with insulin, monitoring, or early delivery, is real
  medicine and catches real risk, but it does not touch the imprint that produced the press, since the
  silencing at each locus is fixed in the genome before the pregnancy begins and no clinic visit reverses it
  (Haig). The reckoning is that the two ledgers, the mother's spread across every offspring and the fetus's
  held to itself alone, were never going to balance at the same point for both, and the clinic can only
  manage the acute overshoot, not remove the asymmetry beneath it.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The general parent-offspring conflict from asymmetric relatedness is Trivers's,
  built on Hamilton's relatedness math; its extension into an intragenomic tug of war between imprinted
  paternal and maternal gene copies is Haig's and Haig and Moore's; and the molecular demonstration that a
  single locus's parent-of-origin expression moves fetal growth is DeChiara, Robertson and Efstratiadis's.
  The scope is held to the structural contribution the kinship theory of genomic imprinting describes, and
  not stretched into a claim that every case of gestational diabetes or preeclampsia traces to this
  mechanism alone, since both are multifactorial in the clinic and the staged mechanism is one documented
  contributor among others, credited as a theory with real molecular support and not offered as medical
  advice or a complete account of either condition.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Ledger**: carries the relatedness asymmetry (Trivers; Hamilton), the mother related by half to this
  offspring and by that same half to every offspring she will ever have, the fetus related to itself
  completely, the structural mismatch the whole trap rests on.
- **The Imprint**: carries which parental gene copy is switched on or silenced at a given locus (Haig; Haig
  and Moore), set by whether that copy has a stake in a possible future half sibling, the mechanism that
  splits the fetus's own genome into two interests.
- **The Push**: carries the hormonal mechanism (Haig; DeChiara, Robertson and Efstratiadis), the placenta
  raising the mother's blood sugar and blood pressure, a paternally expressed growth gene shown moving it
  directly in the laboratory.
- **The Counter**: carries the maternal response (Haig), insulin and vasculature adjusting to meet the push,
  the cover that in most pregnancies meets the demand with room to spare, ordinary gestation.
- **The Overshoot**: carries the phenomenon whole (Haig), gestational diabetes and preeclampsia arising when
  the push outruns the cover, real pathology with no toxin, no infection, and no one to blame.
- **The Boundary**: carries the condition and the hedge (Haig and Moore; DeChiara, Robertson and
  Efstratiadis), that the trap needs a viviparous kin system with loci under imprinting control, and lifts
  toward convergence wherever a mother's every offspring, present and future, shares the one father, with
  screening catching the acute overshoot and never removing the imprint that presses it.
