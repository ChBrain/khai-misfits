# Position: Mother

## Taxonomy

[Not All Hers to Give](play_not_all_hers_to_give.md). The seat of the maternal system, related by half to this fetus and by that same half to every offspring she will ever have, present and future.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Has

A ledger spread across every offspring she will ever have, present and future, each held by the same one half of relatedness.
Insulin and vasculature to counter-regulate against whatever the placenta presses, sufficient in most pregnancies.
No visibility into the imprint that set the press, so she answers the demand as it arrives and not the mechanism behind it.

## Orders

Ration across the broods: give what a fair share of the whole ledger allows, not the full amount any single offspring's genes would ask for.
Counter-regulate: raise insulin, adjust the vasculature, meet the press with the resources the seat actually has to give.
Accept what the seat does: that a cover calibrated to the whole ledger will meet the demand in the ordinary case and strain against it in the harder one.

## Loses

Whatever the press draws past what her own ledger would have set, borne in her own blood sugar and blood pressure.
Because her cover is calibrated across every offspring she will ever have and the fetus's demand is calibrated to itself alone, the seat loses exactly the gap between the two ledgers, met without complaint in most pregnancies and unmet, at a real cost to her, in the rest.
She keeps the offspring she is carrying, and loses the margin the wider ledger was meant to protect.

## Drives

Toward the one whose sound rationing across every offspring she will ever have is read, by a fetus related to itself alone, as a shortfall to be pressed against,
covering because covering is the sound response to a fetus that is hers to carry and, by doing so, absorbing a demand no single ledger of her own would have set,
so the seat that only did well by rationing fairly is the seat through which the tug becomes gestational diabetes or preeclampsia when the press outruns what she can give.
