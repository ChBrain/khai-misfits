# Plan: Go by What I've Seen

## Taxonomy

A **standing plan**: the intent of [the learner's seat](position_learner.md), inside [Once Bitten](play_once_bitten.md).

## Owner

- Owner: [Learner](position_learner.md)

## Direction

Keep sampling what has served you and decline to repeat what has hurt you, since that is how experience teaches and prejudging is worse than learning from outcomes. Try an option, read the result, and let the good ones stay in your rotation while the bad ones drop out of it. Know that this fair rule reads from a record your own avoidance has censored, so the picture you form runs low through no fault of your judgement, only through what a sampler who never goes back to what burned it never comes back to see.

## Orders

([The Learner](persona_the_learner.md) takes [the Bad Draw](piece_the_bad_draw.md) through [Burning](process_burning.md) at [the Stove](place_the_stove.md) into [the Flinch](piece_the_flinch.md), never touching again what burned it, then reads its belief off [the Censored Sample](piece_the_censored_sample.md) at [the Ledger](place_the_ledger.md).)

## Implementation

([Burning](process_burning.md) at [the Stove](place_the_stove.md) turns one bad outcome into a hand withdrawn for good, because [the Flinch](piece_the_flinch.md) stops the sampling, so the low-side error is never corrected and [the Censored Sample](piece_the_censored_sample.md) the Learner reads is truncated below.)

## Targets

- [ ] what has served is kept in the rotation and what has hurt is dropped from it
- [ ] the avoidance of a bad draw is understood as rational, not prejudice
- [ ] the record read from is seen to be censored, missing what was dropped after one draw
- [ ] the downward bias is owned as a property of the sampling, not a failing of judgement
