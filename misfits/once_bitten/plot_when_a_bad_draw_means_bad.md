# Plot: When a Bad Draw Means Bad

## Taxonomy

[Once Bitten](play_once_bitten.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

[The Ledger](place_the_ledger.md), a reliable option's flat, unvarying column set beside a variable option's single low entry.
One record that would read the same on a hundred more trials, and one that might not, side by side on the same page.

## Action

The bound is drawn on the page itself: [the Downward Bias](piece_the_downward_bias.md) holds only where outcomes are variable and the sampling that stopped was the agent's own choice, and it softens where a bad draw truly means bad because the option is near-deterministic, where sampling is forced rather than chosen, or where another's experience of the option can be observed instead of drawn firsthand.

## Stage

The hedge, and it is load-bearing: the effect is a bias conditioned on noise and on endogenous sampling, not a general law that all avoidance is error, and most options a learner shuns really are bad (Denrell). Where the stove is hot every time, the Learner's one touch already told the truth, and no second draw would move the verdict.

## Tension

The trap is exactly as strong as outcomes are noisy and sampling is free, and it claims nothing past that line.
