# The Same Name on Both Registers: Reference

The research warrant for The Same Name on Both Registers.
The term is the house's; the play runs on common ownership and on what a proprietor of two rivals is and is not
served by, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging common ownership as **a structure that thins competition with no act in it**: two carriers compete on a
route, their shareholder lists are public, and the largest entries on both are the same handful of funds,
because those funds track an index and an index holds everything. A proprietor of both gains nothing when one
takes passengers from the other. Nobody has said this to anybody, no meeting has occurred, and every holding on
the page is disclosed and lawful. The play models the documented mechanics, the index whose completeness is
what makes it honest and cheap, the overlap that follows from arithmetic rather than intention, the executive
who declines a fare war having been told nothing, the pay design that carries the effect while containing no
message, the authority whose powers are built to find an act, and the magnitude competent people cannot agree
on, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of funds coordinating an industry.

The governing law is that a proprietor of two rivals is not served by a transfer between them, so the pressure
to compete weakens as the overlap rises, and it weakens without an agreement, an instruction or a conversation.

**The precondition is a genuine good, which is what stops this being a story about a bad instrument.**
Broad-market investing at a few basis points removed the fees, removed the stock picking that mostly did not
work, and put a spread portfolio within reach of anybody with a payslip. Its completeness is what makes it
honest: a version that omitted one competitor would be a judgement, would need a judge, and would be the
discretionary expensive thing the product was invented to replace. There is no reform of the object that keeps
what it is for and removes what follows from it.

What removes any possibility of blame from the fund is that holding the whole list is the definition of the
instrument rather than a decision inside it. A tracking fund leaving out a rival would breach the mandate it
sold and would be a worse product, so the overlap is not an investment view, a strategy or an oversight.

What removes it from the executive is that nothing was communicated and nothing in the conduct changed. No
shareholder asked for restraint, no meeting occurred, and the standing reasons against a fare war are unchanged
and were always partly good: they cost a great deal, they are matched by morning, and they usually end where
they began. What has moved is that the party the seat answers to would not be much better off if it worked, and
serving that party competently is what the seat is for.

**And the objection that nobody could have told anybody is answered without an instruction.** A remuneration
committee measuring an executive against their own results rather than against the rival's is arguing on its
own merits, that a person should not be punished for an industry-wide bad year nor paid for a good one they did
not cause, and that argument is sound and predates this question entirely. It also removes whatever reward
there was for taking share from the competitor. So the transmission is a design choice made for unrelated
reasons, which is the difference between a channel and a message.

And the authority closes the case against the last party, because its instrument is correct and aimed at an
object that is not there. Competition law is built around acts: an agreement, an understanding, a concerted
practice, something somebody did that can be proved. Here everything is either lawful or absent, and
restricting the holdings means taking a good product away from the people it serves.

It is distinct from the house's **Nobody Shops Around**, which holds the Diamond paradox and is the nearest
mechanism, since both stage competition failing among firms that never communicate. There the failure is on the
**buyer's** side: a small cost of comparing prices unravels the incentive to undercut, and the firms are
independently owned throughout, so the repair is anything that makes comparison cheap. Here comparison is free,
the fares are published and a passenger can see both in a second. What has changed is who owns the sellers, so
the two misfits agree that competition can fail without collusion and disagree entirely about where to look.

It is distinct from the house's **Nobody Votes for the Plant**, its immediate neighbour in the same field and
built alongside it. There the owner is diversified **across industries** and the failure is what a barely
exposed holder under-weights about one firm's continuation, which is a question about the vertical relation
between an owner and a company. Here the owner is diversified **across rivals** and the failure is what a
proprietor of both is indifferent about between two companies, which is a question about the horizontal
relation between them. A fund holding four hundred firms in four hundred unrelated industries produces the
first and not the second, so the quantities are genuinely different and the axes are declared apart.

It is distinct from the house's **The Race to the Bottom**, where jurisdictions competing for a mobile base
each shave their standards: there competition is the **engine** of the failure and there is too much of it,
where here there is too little, and the two are the same variable read from opposite ends of its range.

And it is distinct from any cartel, which is the reading the play exists to refuse. A cartel is an agreement,
its evidence is a communication, and its remedy is to detect and punish one. Here there is no agreement to
find, nothing was concealed, the registers are filed publicly, and full disclosure of the structure is already
in place and changes nothing, because a structure is not an act.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the funds as
  coordinating an industry or the executives as being told to go easy, because nobody in the sequence has a
  decision to get wrong: the saver buys the best cheap thing that happened to people on a payslip; the fund
  holds the whole list because holding the whole list is what the product is, and omitting a rival would breach
  the mandate and need a judge; the indifference of a proprietor of both follows from arithmetic rather than
  intention, so nobody wants prices high; the executive receives no instruction and declines a fare war for
  reasons that were always partly good; the remuneration committee argues its package on fairness to one
  person and predates the question; and the authority's powers are correct and built to find an act where there
  is none. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that a proprietor
  of two rivals is not served by the contest between them.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Restricting the holdings
  reaches the mechanism and takes cheap diversification away from the people it was built for, which is a real
  cost falling on savers rather than on funds. Requiring passivity is close to what index funds already do, and
  the effect does not run through instructions. Disclosure is already complete: the registers are public and
  the structure is visible to anybody, so there is nothing left to reveal. Pay design measured against rivals
  would reach the channel and would punish an executive for an industry-wide bad year, which is the objection
  the present design exists to answer. Competition law can find no act to prosecute, and no sharpening of an
  instrument built for conduct reaches a structure. And knowing the whole mechanism, which every party may,
  leaves the fund still obliged to hold the list.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the airline estimate, the partial-ownership framework, the
  incentive finding, the legal argument and the rebuttal are theirs. The bounds are carried with them:
  **whether the effect is real and how large it is are genuinely contested**, the dissent cited above is a
  direct rebuttal in the same journal on the same industry, and the play therefore stages the **structure**,
  which the disputants share, and never a magnitude, which they do not; ownership is not assigned at random and
  the concentration measures rest on assumptions about control that are themselves argued over, which the play
  carries rather than resolving; the airline setting is used because it is where the argument has been
  conducted and not as a claim about other industries; the account treats one contested route and one decision,
  which is the play's own simplification of markets with many carriers, routes and periods; and the legal
  reading is described as an argument that has been made and is not endorsed. **The play makes no claim that
  any fund acted unlawfully, that any fare was set improperly, or that index investing should be restricted**,
  only that a proprietor of two rivals gains nothing from the contest between them, and that nobody had to say
  so.
