# Pitch: Split the Difference

## Taxonomy

[The Price Neither Will Name](play_the_price_neither_will_name.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Reasonable and fair-minded.
A willing buyer, a willing seller, a real gain sitting between them, and the ordinary confidence that two parties who both want the deal will meet somewhere in the middle.
Nothing false in it: most trades with a gain in them do clear, and offering a little low or asking a little high is the plainest bargaining there is, not a trick.

## Undertow

Under the fair-minded meeting, a number each will not name.
The buyer knows its top price and the seller its floor, and each keeps its own back because to say it is to hand the gain away, so two honest, self-serving figures are put on the table and they need not cross though the true values would (Myerson and Satterthwaite).
No cheat and no fool: naming your value is the one move that costs you everything, so holding it is sound, and the sound move on both sides is exactly what leaves the deal unmade.

## Nerve

The plainness of splitting a difference, and the catch that the difference cannot be seen:
that the gain is real but the rent to reveal it is realer still, larger at the margin than the gain it guards, so no rule that pays itself can pull both true numbers into the open,
and the price that would have closed the deal is the one price neither will name, foreclosed not by anyone's greed but by the cost of proving what only you can know.

## Echo

After the run: the house that did not sell though buyer and seller both had a deal in reach, the business two partners could not split though both would gain from splitting it, a surplus gone to no one's pocket, a loss with no wrongdoer and easily misread as mere stubbornness (Myerson and Satterthwaite).
And the remedies, each at a cost and none free: an outside dollar that pays each side its rent from a purse that is not the gain, breaking the balance of the books (Vickrey; Clarke; Groves), or an equal ex-ante ownership that changes what each must reveal (Cramton, Gibbons, and Klemperer).
And the bound held in view, because the impossibility is conditional and not a law: it needs two-sided private value over overlapping ranges, and lifts under one-sided information, a known value, or guaranteed gains, so the whole of it is that the price neither will name is the price that would have made the deal.
