# The Price Neither Will Name: Reference

The research warrant for **The Price Neither Will Name**, a staging of the phenomenon the
literature calls the **Myerson-Satterthwaite impossibility**: that in a bilateral trade where a
buyer and a seller each hold a private value and their possible values overlap, no bargaining rule
can be at once efficient, incentive-compatible, individually rational, and budget-balanced, so some
trades both sides would gain from are never made, with neither party lying and no outside purse to
draw on. It is held with the finding that the impossibility is conditional on two-sided private
information over overlapping supports, since one-sided information, a known value, guaranteed gains,
or an outside subsidy each restores the efficient trade.

## Line of Work

Staging the impossibility as a **private-information-forecloses-the-gain structure**: a buyer values
a thing above what a seller would take for it, so a real surplus sits between them that a trade would
split and both would gain from. Each knows only its own number: the buyer its top price, the seller
its floor. To claim more of the surplus, the buyer states a figure below its true value and the
seller a figure above its true cost, each shading toward its own side, and neither is lying, since
under any rule that asks them to report, shading is simply the best reply. The ordinary expectation
is that a willing buyer and a willing seller with a gain between them will find a price. But the two
figures, each honestly self-serving, need not cross even when the true values do, and the deal dies
in the gap. And it cannot be designed away: to make truthful reporting the best reply, any rule must
concede each side an information rent, and the rents the two sides must be paid together exceed the
surplus on the marginal trade, so no rule that pays itself, taking in no more than it pays out, can
clear every trade the values warrant (Myerson and Satterthwaite). The governing law is that **where
two parties bargain over a gain and each privately knows only its own value across overlapping
ranges, no budget-balanced, voluntary, truthful rule can realise every trade both would gain from, so
some efficient trades are foreclosed by the rent that private information commands, with no one
cheating and no rule at fault**. The play models the documented mechanics: the revelation logic by
which any mechanism reduces to a truthful one and must therefore pay the rent (Myerson), the
efficient mechanisms that exist only by running a deficit an outside party covers (Vickrey; Clarke;
Groves), the bilateral impossibility itself over overlapping supports (Myerson and Satterthwaite),
and the special ex-ante structures that escape it (Cramton, Gibbons, and Klemperer), as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, and not as a claim that bargaining never clears.

It is a bargaining impossibility no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Over a Barrel, the hold-up problem, where a party that has sunk a
relationship-specific investment can be squeezed once the money is in the ground: there the barrier is
an appropriable quasi-rent created by a sunk, specific asset, whereas here nothing is sunk and the
trade is a single arm's-length one-shot exchange, the barrier being two-sided private information over
a value, not exposure after an investment. It is distinct from The Market for Lemons, adverse
selection, where one-sided hidden information about quality drives the good goods out and leaves the
bad: there the information is one-sided and about type, and the failure is a sorting of quality,
whereas here the information is two-sided and about each party's own willingness, the good is known,
and no quality is hidden or sorted. It is distinct from Loath to Part and A Little Crooked, and Mine,
the endowment and labor-inflation biases, where an owner genuinely values a thing above what any buyer
will pay: there the true valuations do not overlap and there is no real gain to be had, whereas here
the valuations are unbiased and a real surplus exists, foreclosed not by an inflated true value but by
the strategic concealment of a true one. It is distinct from After You, the two-sided cold start,
where each side waits for the other to arrive and the market never forms: there both sides are absent
and the failure is one of timing and coordination, whereas here both sides are present and willing and
a trade is attempted, foreclosed by the rent private information commands and not by anyone's waiting.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the
  buyer who offers low or the seller who holds high as a chiseller, because the finding it stages is
  that shading is not a cheat but the best reply under any rule that asks them to report: a party that
  names its true value hands the whole gain to the other side, so protecting its own number is
  rational and honest, not a lie (Myerson). The broker who runs the rule and the sponsor who weighs a
  subsidy act in good faith throughout. The loss is authored by no one: it is the information rent that
  every incentive-compatible, individually rational rule must concede to each side, a rent that on the
  marginal trade exceeds the surplus, so the trade that both would gain from is foreclosed by the very
  logic that keeps the reporting honest.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Efficient trade is
  reachable, but not for free: a mechanism can clear every warranted trade if an outside party covers
  the deficit it must run, so the Vickrey-Clarke-Groves rule buys efficiency by paying out more than
  it takes in (Vickrey; Clarke; Groves), and an equal ex-ante ownership can restore a budget-balanced
  efficient rule where a fixed buyer and fixed seller cannot (Cramton, Gibbons, and Klemperer). But an
  ordinary bilateral trade has no outside purse and no chosen ex-ante structure, so within it the
  choice is real: subsidise the trade and break budget balance, or keep the books balanced and lose
  some of the trades the values warrant. The reckoning is that private information commands a rent, and
  where two parties must split a gain that neither can prove, the rent can cost them the gain.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here,
  never claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the bilateral impossibility is Myerson and
  Satterthwaite's, the revelation principle and the rent that makes truth optimal are Myerson's, the
  efficient mechanisms that survive only on an outside subsidy are Vickrey's, Clarke's, and Groves's,
  and the ex-ante ownership that escapes the impossibility is Cramton, Gibbons, and Klemperer's; the
  contrast with one-sided hidden quality is Akerlof's and is drawn only to mark the line. The mechanism
  is carried as carefully as the finding: that the impossibility needs two-sided private values over
  overlapping supports, that shading is the best reply and not a fraud, that the rent on the marginal
  trade exceeds the surplus so no budget-balanced rule clears everything, and above all that the result
  is conditional, since one-sided information, a known value, guaranteed gains over disjoint supports,
  an outside subsidy, or the right ex-ante ownership each restores the efficient trade. The scope is
  held to two-sided private-value bargaining, and not stretched into a claim that markets never clear
  or that bargaining is always doomed.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Private Value**: carries each party's true reservation, the buyer's top price and the seller's
  floor, known only to itself and drawn from a range the other can only guess (Myerson and
  Satterthwaite), the hidden number the whole trap turns on.
- **The Overlap**: carries the real surplus, the range where the buyer's value exceeds the seller's
  cost so a trade would leave both better off, the gain that makes the miss a loss and not a nothing.
- **The Shade**: carries the strategic misrepresentation, the buyer stating below its value and the
  seller above its cost to claim more of the surplus, the best reply under any reporting rule and not a
  lie, since to name the true number is to hand the gain away (Myerson).
- **The Rent**: carries the engine of the impossibility, the information rent any incentive-compatible,
  individually rational rule must leave each side, the two sides' rents together exceeding the surplus
  on the marginal trade, so no budget-balanced rule can pay both out of the gain (Myerson and
  Satterthwaite).
- **The Unmade Trade**: carries the phenomenon whole, the efficient trade that does not happen and the
  surplus lost in the gap between the two shaded figures, no one having lied and no rule having erred.
- **The Boundary**: carries the condition and the hedge, that the impossibility holds only for
  two-sided private values over overlapping supports and dissolves under one-sided information, a known
  value, guaranteed gains, an outside subsidy (Vickrey; Clarke; Groves), or the right ex-ante ownership
  (Cramton, Gibbons, and Klemperer), so the trap is real but bounded.
