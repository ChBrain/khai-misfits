# Reasonable Either Way: Reference

The research warrant for Reasonable Either Way.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the garden of forking paths, named by Gelman and Loken, with the
researcher degrees of freedom that Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn demonstrated before it and the
many-analyst studies that measured it after, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging data-contingent analysis as a **multiplicity made of paths not walked**: an analyst who runs one
analysis, honestly, on a hypothesis posited in advance still carries the multiple-comparisons problem,
because the choices that analysis is built from were made after the data were seen, and different data
would have prompted different choices that would have been equally defensible. The play models the
documented mechanics, the choice that is reasonable and is made after looking, the branch not taken that
counts against the result anyway, the absence of any trace because nothing was repeated and nothing
concealed, the spread that appears the moment the same data reach other competent hands, the fresh sample
that does not reproduce the finding, and the corrections that work and are paid for, as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a cheat.

The governing law is Gelman and Loken's: multiplicity does not require multiple tests. It requires only
that the analysis be contingent on the data, so the count that matters is not how many analyses were run
but how many would have been run had the numbers come out otherwise. That count is invisible, because the
paths not taken leave nothing behind. An analyst can perform exactly one test, report it in full, and still
be standing in a garden.

Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn had already shown what the flexibility is worth by using it in the open,
demonstrating that undisclosed latitude in collecting and analysing can present very nearly anything as
significant. What Gelman and Loken added is the part that makes it structural rather than a matter of
conduct: the effect survives the removal of intent. No fishing expedition is needed, no p-value is examined
and discarded, the hypothesis may be stated ahead of time, and the problem is still there.

The many-analyst studies then measured it. Twenty-nine teams, sixty-one analysts, one dataset and one
question about football referees produced effect sizes from 0.89 to 2.93 in odds-ratio units, with twenty
teams finding a significant positive effect and nine finding none, across twenty-one distinct combinations
of covariates. Seventy teams given one brain-imaging dataset and nine stated hypotheses produced no two
identical workflows between them. None of these analysts was cheating. They were disagreeing, competently,
about which path was right.

It is distinct from the house's **The File Drawer**, whose warrant already names this as a cousin set
apart: there the filter falls on the record _after_ a study is finished, keeping the significant and
drawering the null, so the bias lives in which honest studies were published. Here nothing is filtered and
the study is not yet submitted: the bias is manufactured inside a single analysis, before anyone else can
see it, by choices that are each defensible.

It is distinct from the house's **Overfitting**, where a model tuned to a sample learns its noise as
signal: there the excess flexibility is in the model and can be counted, penalised and cross-validated.
Here the flexibility is in the _analyst_, spent once, and the branches it did not take are not in any
object anyone can inspect.

It is distinct from the house's **The Streetlight Effect**, where the search runs where the light is good:
there a whole region goes unexamined. Here the region is examined, correctly, and the route through it was
chosen with the data already visible.

It is distinct from the house's **Too Sure to Be True**, where independent judgments agreeing too closely
mark a shared hidden error: there the tell is unanimity. Here the tell is the opposite, a spread of
competent answers from one dataset, and the spread is not the failure but the evidence of it.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the analyst
  as a cheat or a fisher, because the source finding is precisely that neither is required: the hypothesis
  can be stated in advance, exactly one analysis can be run, every choice can be defensible on its own
  merits and disclosed in full, and the problem remains. The many-analyst record makes the point in the
  other direction, since sixty-one analysts working openly and in good faith on the same numbers still
  spread from 0.89 to 2.93, which is not sixty-one misconducts but one structure. The failure is authored
  by nobody and emerges from the order of events, that the data are seen before the path is chosen, so the
  misfit is that an analysis can be honest in every step and still owe a debt to the steps it never took.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Preregistration works by
  fixing the path before anyone knows what the data look like, which is exactly when the analyst knows
  least, so it buys its protection by forbidding the correct response to a genuine surprise. Reporting the
  whole multiverse works by showing every defensible path at once, and produces an object that answers the
  question honestly and answers it in a form almost no reader will consume, which hands the choosing back
  to whoever reads selectively. Both are real instruments and neither is free, and a field that adopts
  either still cannot recover what the paths not walked would have said.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the naming, the demonstration, the many-analyst
  designs and the reported figures are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the spread a many-analyst
  study finds is a property of that dataset and that question, and does not license a claim about any
  particular published result. **The play makes no claim that a data-contingent analysis is wrong**, only
  that its p-value is not the p-value it appears to be, which is a statement about what the number can
  bear and not about the analyst who produced it.
