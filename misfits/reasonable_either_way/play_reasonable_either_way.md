# Play: Reasonable Either Way

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Reasonable Either Way.
An analyst has a question, written down before the data arrived, and one dataset to answer it with. They open the file, look at what is there, and begin making the choices that any careful person has to make: these three records are plainly bad and come out, this variable is skewed and is better logged, this group is too small to stand alone and joins the next, this obvious confound must be controlled for. Each choice is defensible. Each is explained in the write-up. Exactly one analysis is run, and its result is reported in full. And every one of those choices was made with the data already on the screen, which means that had the numbers come out differently the choices would have come out differently too, and been just as defensible then.

## Arc

The bend is this: multiplicity does not need multiple tests, it needs only that the path be chosen after the data are seen.
It runs on the order of events.
The One at the Keyboard does nothing wrong at any point. The question was posited in advance, so there is no fishing. One test is run, so there is no discarded p-value sitting unreported. Every decision is written down, so there is nothing concealed. On every count by which we normally catch a bad analysis, this analysis is clean.
And the count that matters was never taken, because the things it would count are the turns that were not made. The Garden is made of them: the exclusion that would have been reasonable had the outliers sat the other way, the covariate that would have been obviously necessary had the groups looked different, the split that would have been the sensible one had the distribution been shaped otherwise. None of those analyses were run. All of them were available. And the result's claim to be surprising rests on how often a result like it would arise by chance, which depends on how many paths were open, not on how many were walked.
So there is nothing to audit. An auditor can ask what was run and be told the truth. They can ask what was excluded and be shown the rule and agree with it. What they cannot ask is what the analyst would have done with a different file, because the analyst does not know either, and would have found out only by seeing it.
The spread shows up the moment the same file reaches other hands. Twenty-nine teams and sixty-one analysts, one question about referees and red cards, produced effect sizes from 0.89 to 2.93 and split twenty to nine on whether there was an effect at all, using twenty-one different sets of controls. Seventy teams given one imaging dataset produced seventy different workflows, no two alike. Nobody in either study was cheating. They were disagreeing, competently, about the route.
Then The Next Sample arrives, and the finding does not come back, and the failure lands on the replicator, who is asked what they did differently. They did nothing differently that anyone can name. They walked a path from a garden that had rearranged itself around new numbers.
Every correction is real and every correction is paid for. Tie the analysis down in advance and you have fixed the route at the moment you know least, forbidding the honest response to a genuine surprise. Show every path at once and the report becomes a landscape rather than a claim, answered truthfully in a form few will read all of, which hands the choosing to whoever reads selectively.

## Triggers

**[Before Any Choice](plot_before_any_choice.md)**
The question is written down before the data exist, and the file arrives afterward. Closes when the analysis begins with its hypothesis already fixed, which is the condition under which everything that follows cannot be fishing.

**[A Reasonable Exclusion](plot_a_reasonable_exclusion.md)**
Cue: three records that are plainly bad, visible the moment the file is opened. The One at the Keyboard writes an exclusion rule that any careful person would write, and writes it down. Closes when the rule is set, defensible, and recorded.

**[The Paths Not Walked](plot_the_paths_not_walked.md)**
Cue: the same three records, imagined sitting the other way. A different exclusion would have been just as reasonable, a different covariate obviously necessary, a different split plainly sensible. Closes when it is clear that the branches exist, that none of them were run, and that nothing anywhere records them.

**[Twenty-Nine Desks](plot_twenty_nine_desks.md)**
Cue: the same file, sent out to other competent hands. The answers spread from 0.89 to 2.93, twenty teams find an effect and nine do not, and the sets of controls come back in twenty-one different combinations. Closes when the spread is on the table and no one in it has done anything wrong.

**[It Does Not Come Back](plot_it_does_not_come_back.md)**
Cue: a fresh sample, and the same analysis run on it. The finding is not there. The replicator is asked what they did differently, and has no answer, because they did nothing differently. Closes when the failure is charged to the replication rather than to the garden that produced the original.

**[Tie It Down](plot_tie_it_down.md)**
Cue: the problem understood, and the two instruments reached for. Fixing the path in advance, which forbids the honest response to a surprise, or showing every defensible path at once, which produces an answer almost nobody reads to the end. Closes when one is chosen and its price is paid, or neither is and the garden stays.

## Stakes

Whether a number that took one honest route through a dataset can mean what it claims to mean, when the route was chosen with the dataset already visible.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a choice that is correct and is made after looking, and away from any test that could catch it, because the evidence of the problem is the set of things that did not happen. The production cannot make the analyst choose before seeing, since choosing before seeing is what the corrections cost their price for, and it cannot make the paths not taken leave a trace, because that is what it means not to take them. What is left to fight over is not whether the analyst was honest, everyone agrees they were, but what a p-value is worth when the analysis that produced it was shaped by the data it was computed on.
