# Room for Three in a Hundred: Reference

The research warrant for Room for Three in a Hundred.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the trial penalty in criminal procedure, the capacity argument the
courts themselves make for plea bargaining, and the documented cases of innocent defendants pleading
guilty, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the trial penalty as **a throughput number wearing the clothes of a sentence**: a criminal court has
a fixed number of rooms, judges and hours, and cases arrive at a rate set by something else entirely, so
roughly three convictions in a hundred can be produced by trial and the rest must be produced some other
way. The mechanism that produces them is a price. Plead and the sentence is one figure, ask for the trial
the constitution guarantees and it is a much larger one, and the size of that gap is whatever it has to be
to move ninety-seven cases in a hundred. The play models the documented mechanics, the capacity constraint
nobody in the building can relieve, the discount priced from prospects rather than from culpability, the
offer that improves as the case weakens, the admission that must be spoken to collect it, the examination
that tests everything except the thing that matters, and the corrections that each charge somebody absent,
as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of prosecutorial cruelty.

The governing law is that the discount is computed from capacity, and capacity contains no term for who did
it. A number set by hours and rooms is then applied identically to the guilty and the innocent, because
there is nothing in its derivation capable of telling them apart. This is not a defect in the pricing. It is
what pricing is.

What sharpens it is the direction of the sorting. A charge that might fail at trial is worth less to the
prosecution and therefore settles cheaper, which is ordinary expected value and is not about the defendant
at all. But the effect is that the most attractive offer in the building sits in front of the defendant with
the strongest defence, so the pressure to plead is heaviest exactly where guilt is least certain.

What makes it structural rather than misconduct is that the discount is collected in words. The defendant
must state in open court that they did it, so an innocent plea does not enter the record flagged, doubted or
marked as exceptional: it enters as a voluntary, counselled, judicially examined admission of guilt,
indistinguishable in every particular from a true one, completed under oath by the only person who knew
otherwise. Voluntariness in law means free of improper coercion, and a large lawful discount is not improper
coercion, so the examination passes it exactly as it passes a free plea. The bench is thoroughly equipped to
catch a defective plea and holds no instrument whatever for a valid plea that is false.

It is distinct from the house's **The Next One Pays**, where a repeat player buys the cases that would make
bad law out of the record: there the person paid comes out ahead, the removal leaves no trace at all, and
the cost falls on a future stranger who was not in the room. Here the person paid is convicted, the removal
leaves a trace that asserts the opposite of the truth, and the cost falls on the person standing in front of
the judge.

It is distinct from the house's **Only the Close Ones**, where the predictable cases settle and the tried
record fills with toss-ups: there the sorting is by predictability, performed jointly by two adversaries,
and what it damages is inference from the record. Here the sorting is by a fixed price neither party set,
and what it damages is not a statistic but a population of people.

It is distinct from the house's **The Case It Destroys**, where growth outruns capacity and the standard
quietly drops: there the accommodation is unacknowledged, and the demand driven away reads as proof the
standard was right. Here nothing is quiet and nothing is driven away. The accommodation is explicit,
published, and named by the Supreme Court as an essential component of the administration of justice, and
the system processes the whole of its demand, at a price.

It is distinct from the house's **No Idle Hands**, where driving a resource to full use makes the queue
behind it swell without bound: there the shortfall shows up as waiting. Here the queue clears, completely
and on time, because the shortfall is settled by pricing people out of the service rather than by making
them wait for it, and a cleared list looks exactly like sufficient capacity.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the prosecutor
  as a bully or the defendant as a dupe, because the misfit does not need anybody to misbehave: an official
  disposing of a caseload no courtroom can absorb is operating the mechanism the Supreme Court has called
  essential, and did not choose the caseload, write the sentencing ranges or allocate the courtrooms.
  Defence counsel weighing two certain years against a fifteen per cent chance of twenty is required to
  recommend the certain thing, and would be the one found ineffective for doing otherwise. The judge asks
  every question the law provides and receives truthful answers to all of them. The defendant who takes the
  number is acting rationally, and so is the one who refuses. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges
  from the structure, so the misfit is that a number derived from hours and rooms is spent in years of
  people's lives, and the record that would show it is filled in by the person it happened to.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Closing the gap works and
  stops the list clearing, at a price the Court itself has already stated in judges and court facilities
  multiplied many times over. Capping the discount works and moves the pressure into the charging decision,
  where almost nothing reviews it. Putting judicial eyes on the bargaining works and makes the judge a party
  to the agreement they must later examine as voluntary. Funding the trials works and requires a sum
  somebody must vote for, while every case that goes to trial spends the time of victims and witnesses the
  plea was sparing them and lengthens the wait of everyone still held before their own.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the judgments, the reform research and the exoneration
  data are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the raw ratio between trial and plea sentences
  overstates the causal penalty, because the cases that go to trial differ systematically from those that
  do not, so part of the gap is selection rather than punishment for asking; the frequency of innocent
  pleas is unknown and unknowable by construction, since the record of a false plea is a valid confession,
  and the exoneration figures are a floor rather than an estimate; the characterisation is contested, with
  reform bodies describing the differential as coercion and the courts describing it as the ordinary
  give-and-take of negotiation; and practice varies enormously between jurisdictions, offence types and
  eras. **The play makes no claim that cases should not resolve without trial**, almost all of them must,
  only that a discount priced by throughput cannot tell who did it.
