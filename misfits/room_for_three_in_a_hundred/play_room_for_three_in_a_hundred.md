# Play: Room for Three in a Hundred

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Room for Three in a Hundred.
A criminal court is a building with a finite number of rooms, a finite number of judges and a finite number of hours, and the number of cases arriving at it is not set by any of those things. Roughly three in a hundred can be given a trial. The other ninety-seven have to be disposed of some other way, and the mechanism that disposes of them is a price: plead, and the sentence is one number, ask for the trial you are entitled to, and it is a much larger one. That gap is not a punishment anybody designed as a punishment. It is the size of the inducement required to clear a list that will not otherwise clear, which makes it a fact about the buildings.

## Arc

The bend is this: the discount that empties the list is priced by capacity, and capacity knows nothing about who did it.
It runs on a number that came from the estate department.
The gap has to be large, because it has to move ninety-seven cases in a hundred. At the federal level, sentences after trial run around three times the sentences after a plea for the same conduct, and sometimes eight or ten times. Nothing in that ratio was calculated from anybody's culpability. It was calculated, in effect, from the hours available.
The One Who Offers is doing the job. A prosecutor with a caseload no courtroom can absorb, offering a discount to resolve it, is doing exactly what the system requires and what the Supreme Court has repeatedly said is essential: the Court's own reasoning is that if every charge went to full trial, the states and the federal government would need to multiply by many times the number of judges and court facilities. The bargaining is not a workaround. It is the design.
The One Who Counts the Odds is obliged. Defence counsel weighing two certain years against a fifteen per cent chance of twenty is not free to prefer the principle: the duty runs to this client, the arithmetic is the arithmetic, and recommending the certain thing is competent advice. Advising otherwise, on these numbers, is the thing that gets a lawyer found ineffective.
And the sharpest turn is that the offer improves as the case weakens. A charge that might well fail at trial is worth less to the prosecution, so it settles cheaper. The defendant with the strongest defence is therefore looking at the most attractive number on the table, which means the pressure to plead is heaviest exactly where guilt is least certain.
Then The One Who Says It says it. The discount is not given for silence: it is given for an admission, spoken aloud in open court, that the defendant did the thing. So the one place where an innocent plea would show up is the one place it is definitionally erased, by the person harmed, as the condition of the deal. The record does not contain a wrongly convicted person who pleaded. It contains a voluntary, competent, counselled admission of guilt, which is what the record is designed to contain and what it truthfully reports.
And The One Who Asked is the warning. In 1978 a man charged over a forged cheque for eighty-eight dollars and thirty cents was offered five years, declined, and received life under a habitual offender statute the prosecutor had openly said he would seek if the offer was refused. The Supreme Court held that this was not vindictiveness but the normal give-and-take of plea negotiation, and constitutional. The case is not an outlier that got through: it is the rule, stated.
And every correction spends something. Close the gap and the list stops clearing, and the Court has already said what that costs in judges and buildings. Cap the discount and it moves into the charging decision, which is barely reviewable. Have a judge police the offers and the judge becomes a party to the bargain they must later accept as voluntary. Fund the trials and somebody must vote for it, while every case that goes to trial spends the time of victims and witnesses that the plea was sparing them, and lengthens the wait of everyone still held before their own.

## Triggers

**[The Building Only Holds So Many](plot_the_building_only_holds_so_many.md)**
A finite number of rooms, judges and hours, and an arrival rate set by something else entirely. Closes when the capacity to try is fixed at roughly three cases in a hundred, and the other ninety-seven must be disposed of by some mechanism that is not a trial.

**[A Number From the Buildings](plot_a_number_from_the_buildings.md)**
Cue: the discount required to move ninety-seven cases in a hundred. It runs at roughly three times the plea sentence and sometimes eight or ten. Closes when the size of the gap has been fixed by throughput rather than by anybody's culpability, and applied identically to everyone.

**[The Weaker the Case, the Better the Deal](plot_the_weaker_the_case_the_better_the_deal.md)**
Cue: a charge that might well fail at trial, worth less to the prosecution and therefore cheaper to settle. Closes when the most attractive offer in the building is sitting in front of the defendant with the strongest defence, and neither of them has done anything wrong to arrive there.

**[Say You Did It](plot_say_you_did_it.md)**
Cue: the discount conditional not on silence but on admission, spoken aloud in open court. Closes when the only record that could show a wrongful plea has been completed, correctly and voluntarily, by the person it would have shown.

**[Eighty-Eight Dollars and Thirty Cents](plot_eighty_eight_dollars_and_thirty_cents.md)**
Cue: a forged cheque, an offer of five years, a refusal, and a habitual offender charge the prosecutor had said openly he would bring. Closes when the sentence is life, the Court has called it the normal give-and-take rather than vindictiveness, and the price of asking is on the record as lawful.

**[Multiply the Courthouses](plot_multiply_the_courthouses.md)**
Cue: the structure understood, and the answers on the table: close the gap, cap it, police the offers, or fund the trials. Closes when the list stops clearing, or the pressure moves into charging where nothing reviews it, or the judge becomes a party to the bargain, or the bill arrives and nobody will vote for it.

## Stakes

Whether a right can be said to exist when the system is built on the assumption that almost nobody will use it.
Every plot moves the same way: toward dispositions that are lawful, counselled, voluntary and correctly recorded, and away from any instrument that could distinguish the people who did it from the people who said they did. The production cannot give the buildings more rooms than they have, and it cannot ask a defendant to spend eighteen extra years proving a point. What is left to fight over is not whether cases should resolve without trial, almost all of them must, but whether a discount priced by throughput should be offered to a person who did nothing, and what a record is worth when the price of the discount is filling it in.
