# Taken Apart to Be Read: Reference

The research warrant for Taken Apart to Be Read.
The term is the house's; the play runs on excavation as a destructive method and on the trade between reading a
site now and leaving it for better hands, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging excavation as **a method that consumes the object it reads**: the order of the layers is most of what a
site can tell anybody, and the order is established by taking them off one at a time. So the reading and the
destruction are one motion, the site can be asked once, and whoever holds the trowel that season is the only
person who will ever see it. The play models the documented mechanics, the stratigraphic method whose central
achievement works exactly once per site, the technique that improves in every decade and would have recovered
more, the deposit that decays whether or not anybody looks, the deferral that is a position rather than an
abstention, the timetable owned by a party who is not weighing the question, and the record that is complete
with respect to a question set rather than to the ground, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as
a tale of a hasty excavator or a negligent curator.

The governing law is that the case for waiting is correct and never expires, and the decay it is weighed
against never stops, so the two curves do not cross and there is no moment at which both favour acting.

**Every generation of the seat records more carefully than the last, and that is not the measure.** The trench
opened this season is the best excavation ever conducted on that ground, the recording is exhaustive, the
archive is properly deposited and the licence conditions are met in full. None of that touches the difficulty,
because the difficulty is not in how well the work is done but in the fact that doing it at all is the only way
to read the ground and consumes it.

What removes any possibility of blame from the excavator is that legibility here is a property of the sequence
and the sequence is consumed by being followed. There is no careful version that leaves the ground readable,
and no instrument that reads a stratigraphy without dismantling it, so the seat is not choosing between a
destructive method and a gentler one.

What removes it from the curator is that preservation in situ is the discipline's own answer, is written into
treaty and law, and defers to hands that will genuinely do better. What it cannot do is confirm that the
deposit is still there: the water table falls, oxygen reaches what was waterlogged, the ploughsoil deepens, and
the only instrument that would settle the condition is the excavation being deferred. A deferral right in
principle and unverifiable in fact is a different thing from a safe one.

And the planner closes the case against the last party, because the question no archaeologist can settle is
settled in practice by a seat that never puts it. Developer-funded work is a genuine protection and is why the
record exists at the scale it does; it also means the choice between now and later is made by whoever needs the
ground, correctly under the law, and the answer is always now.

It is distinct from the house's **Kept for the Questions We Had**, which the slate line required it to be named
clear of, and the test was run in the sweep before authoring rather than afterwards. That misfit runs on a
**criterion** built from the present's questions and applied to the only copy: an archivist selects what to
keep, and the selection is patterned by what is currently wanted. Remove that engine entirely, by supposing an
excavation conducted with no criterion at all, and this failure stands: the ground is still consumed by being
read, and the question here is **when** rather than **what**. The two do meet at one point, in the final plot,
where the record turns out to be complete only with respect to the questions of its decade, and that is the
incumbent's mechanism operating inside this one rather than this one restating it.

It is distinct from the house's **Not While You Are Watching**, where a target specified by the absence of an
observer is reachable only by an observer's presence: there the thing observed **survives** in an altered state,
a second observation is possible, and the residue can be argued about because the speaker is still there to be
recorded again. Here nothing survives to be observed a second time, so the two are the same joint at different
severities: one measurement alters its object, the other ends it.

It is distinct from the house's **Held Still to Keep It**, where inscribing a practice as heritage fixes a
living variability into one authorised form: there preservation **changes** the thing preserved and the change
is legible to anybody comparing the form with its history. Here preservation changes nothing and reveals
nothing, and what the curator cannot establish is not how the site has been altered but whether it is there.

And it sits beside the house's **The Last Layer**, which holds Tainter on the diminishing returns of complexity
and takes its evidence from the same discipline: that is a misfit about what archaeology **found**, and this is
one about what archaeology **is**, so the field appears twice in the house on opposite sides of its own method.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the excavator as
  hasty or the curator as negligent, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the sequence is
  the information and is consumed by being followed, so no careful version leaves the ground legible; the
  recording is exhaustive and the archive properly deposited; the case for waiting is a plain induction with no
  expiry; the deposit decays whether or not anybody looks and its condition cannot be checked without the act
  being deferred; the planning condition is a real protection and the timetable is a construction programme;
  and the record is complete about the questions of its decade, which is not a standard anybody could exceed.
  The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that reading the ground
  and destroying it are one motion, and the argument for postponing it is as unbounded as the loss from
  postponing it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Preservation in situ is the
  discipline's own answer, is law, and defers to better hands while leaving the knowledge unobtained and the
  deposit unverifiable. Sampling a fraction and leaving the rest is standard practice, is right, and keeps the
  part not sampled subject to the same decay while removing the part that was. Non-destructive survey, geophysics
  and remote sensing genuinely reduce how much must be opened and cannot establish a sequence, which is the
  information. Keeping the excavated soil for later analysis is done, is limited by storage and cost, and does
  not preserve the relation between contexts, which was the thing. Recording more comprehensively is what every
  decade already does. And knowing the whole structure, which every party does, leaves a trench that must be
  opened or not opened this season.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the analysis of fieldwork as construction, the stratigraphic
  method, the convention, the sequence of techniques, the planning frameworks and the monitoring literature are
  theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether preservation in situ actually preserves is genuinely
  contested**, the monitoring evidence on dewatering and plough damage is partial, and the play carries the
  argument rather than settling it or recommending any policy; the account treats a site as one decision taken
  once, which is the play's own simplification of practice that samples, phases and returns over decades; the
  advance of technique is offered as a pattern with no expiry and not as a prediction of any particular future
  method; the legal and planning arrangements described are those of one tradition and differ by jurisdiction;
  and nothing here is a judgement on any excavation, any curator or any development. **The play makes no claim
  that sites should be dug or left alone**, only that reading the ground is taking it apart, and that the
  reasons for waiting and the reasons for not waiting both grow without limit.
