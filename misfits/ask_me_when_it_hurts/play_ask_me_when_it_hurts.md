# Play: Ask Me When It Hurts

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Ask Me When It Hurts.
A person in a cool, calm state, free of hunger, pain, fear, or desire, makes a plan or a decision that depends on how they will feel or want later. The plan is sincere, reasoned from everything the calm state can access: what usually matters, what is usually wanted, what a reasonable person would choose with a clear head. It is not a weak plan or a careless one. It is the best plan the calm state can make.
But a visceral state is not fully imaginable from outside itself. Hunger cannot be recalled with its true pull once sated, pain cannot be simulated with its true urgency once it has passed, and desire cannot be weighed at its true strength before it is felt, so the calm state's forecast of the gripped state is honestly made and reliably wrong (Loewenstein, "Out of Control: Visceral Influences on Behavior", 1996). The gap runs in both directions: a calm person underpredicts how much a future visceral state will drive their choices, and a gripped person, once the state has passed, cannot fully recall how strongly it pulled either.
When the visceral state arrives, real and involuntary, it does not merely make the calm plan harder to keep. It changes what is wanted, narrowing attention onto the drive and its relief and crowding out the considerations the calm plan weighed, so the plan is abandoned or reversed by a self that is not weak-willed but is, in that moment, honestly a different self, wanting something the earlier self could not have wanted on its behalf (Loewenstein, 1996).
The gap carries real stakes outside the laboratory. A patient in a calm consultation room, not yet in labor and not yet in the grip of acute pain, honestly underpredicts how much a future pain state will drive their own choices, and so may decline in the calm state an intervention the gripped state will urgently want, or accept in the gripped state what the calm state would have refused, a mispredicted preference with consequences a clinic has to plan around, for consent, for treatment, for the directives a person leaves for a future self they cannot yet fully imagine (Loewenstein, "Hot-Cold Empathy Gaps and Medical Decision Making", 2005).
The mechanism indicts no one on either side of the gap. The calm self reasons soundly from what it can access, and the gripped self is not overridden by a worse impulse but is genuinely pursuing what it, honestly and in that moment, wants. Neither self is lying, neither is weak, and no one designed the gap between them; it is the ordinary shape of how a visceral state occupies a mind while it is present and recedes from memory and imagination once it is not.
And the gap is bounded. It is strongest for acute and unfamiliar drive states and weaker for mild or well-rehearsed ones, so the pattern is a documented and variable tendency, not a claim that self-prediction always fails or that a calm plan is never worth making (the visceral-influence and empathy-gap literature that followed Loewenstein).

## Arc

The bend is this: a visceral state changes the content of a preference and not merely its discount, so a plan honestly made in its absence cannot be trusted to survive its arrival, and no failure of will is required to explain why.
It runs on a plan meeting a state it could not foresee.
A plan is made, in the calm, honestly and well reasoned.
And the gap is found. The calm state structurally unable to simulate what the gripped state will want.
And the pull arrives. Real and involuntary, narrowing attention onto the drive and its relief.
And the plan is reversed. Not by weakness but by a want that has genuinely changed.
And the stakes are carried outward. Into a clinic, a directive, a decision that must be made across the gap.
And the true scope is held. Strongest for acute and unfamiliar states, weaker for mild or familiar ones, a documented tendency and not a general failure of self-prediction.

## Stakes

Whether a plan made in the calm can be trusted to survive the state it was made without, when the very state that will decide the outcome is the one the plan could never fully imagine.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward the reversal and away from the plan holding, because the calm self reasons as well as its access allows, and a visceral state, once present, is not a stronger version of the same want but a different want, narrowing what is weighed down to the drive and its relief. The structure cannot both let a calm plan be made honestly and guarantee it will hold, because the honesty is the vulnerability: a forecast built from what the calm state can access is, by the same fact, built without what the gripped state will feel, and there is no way to close that gap from inside the calm state alone. The stewardship offered, asking as close to the threshold as the moment allows, is real but incomplete, since the state cannot always be consulted in advance and the calm self that returns once it passes is, again, unable to fully recall what it just felt, so the same plan is remade no better informed than before. It can only show that no one is a villain, that the calm self forecasts honestly and the gripped self wants honestly, and that the reversal is authored by no one, the predictable cost of a preference that a state, and not a plan, controls. And it holds the bound in view, hedged, because this is a documented pattern that varies by domain and by the intensity and familiarity of the visceral state, strongest for acute and unfamiliar drive states and weaker for mild or well-rehearsed ones, not a claim that all self-prediction fails (Loewenstein, 1996; 2005; the visceral-influence and empathy-gap literature that followed), so the whole of the misfit is that the truest answer to what a person will want lives on the far side of a state their calm self can plan around but never fully enter, and the only honest fix is to ask again, closer to the hurt, knowing even that will not close the gap for good.
