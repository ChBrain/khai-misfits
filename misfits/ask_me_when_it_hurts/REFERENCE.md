# Ask Me When It Hurts: Reference

The research warrant for **Ask Me When It Hurts**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon named
the **hot-cold empathy gap**: people in a cool, calm state cannot accurately predict how a visceral
state, hunger, pain, fear, desire, will change their own preferences and behavior, so plans made in
the cold state are abandoned or reversed once the hot state arrives, all cited below, held with the
finding that the size of the gap varies by domain and by the intensity and salience of the visceral
state, strongest for acute drive states and weaker for milder or more familiar ones, not a claim
that all self-prediction fails.

## Line of Work

Staging the hot-cold empathy gap as a **state-blind-to-its-opposite structure**: a person in a cool, calm state, free of
hunger, pain, fear, or desire, makes a plan or a decision that depends on how they will feel or want later, and the plan
is sincere, reasoned from everything the cold state can access. But a visceral state is not fully imaginable from outside
itself: hunger cannot be recalled with its true pull once sated, pain cannot be simulated with its true urgency once it
has passed, and desire cannot be weighed at its true strength before it is felt, so the cold state's forecast of the hot
state is honestly made and reliably wrong (Loewenstein, "Out of Control: Visceral Influences on Behavior", 1996). When
the visceral state arrives, real and involuntary, it does not merely make the cold plan harder to keep; it changes what
is wanted, narrowing attention onto the drive and its relief and crowding out the considerations the cold plan weighed,
so the plan is abandoned or reversed by a self that is not weak-willed but is, in that moment, honestly a different
self, wanting something the earlier self could not have wanted on its behalf (Loewenstein, 1996). The same gap runs the
other direction and carries real stakes: a patient in a calm consultation room, not yet in labor or not yet in the grip
of acute pain, honestly underpredicts how much a future pain state will drive their own choices, and so may decline an
intervention in the cold state that the hot state will urgently want, or accept in the hot state what the cold state
would have refused, a mispredicted preference with consequences the clinic has to plan around (Loewenstein, "Hot-Cold
Empathy Gaps and Medical Decision Making", 2005). The mechanism is not a failure of intelligence or of resolve on
either side of the gap: the cold self reasons soundly from what it can access, and the hot self is not overridden by a
worse impulse but is genuinely pursuing what it, honestly and in that moment, wants. The governing law is not that
people are weak or that their calm plans are dishonest, but that **a visceral state changes the content of preference
and not merely its discount, so a plan honestly made in its absence cannot be trusted to survive its arrival, no failure
of will required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the plan honestly made in the cold state, the structural
gap that leaves the cold state unable to simulate the hot state's pull, the visceral state's real and involuntary onset,
the reversal that follows because what is wanted has changed and not only how urgently, the return to the cold state
once the visceral state passes and with it the plan remade no better informed than before, and the true scope of the
gap, strongest for acute drive states and weaker for milder or more familiar ones (Loewenstein, 1996; 2005), as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a planner too weak to keep their own word.

It is a state-blind-to-its-opposite structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbour. It is distinct from Starting Tomorrow, where a reward's value spikes as it nears in time and the ranking of a
smaller-sooner against a larger-later reward reverses purely as a function of proximity, the same preference discounted
more steeply and not altered in kind: there the planner wants the same thing throughout and only the weight given to
timing shifts. Here the preference itself changes: the cold self and the hot self are not weighing the same want on
different clocks, they want different things, since a person freezing hungry does not merely discount food's value less
steeply as mealtime nears, they come to want food in a way the sated self could not feel on their behalf. The two
structures can co-occur, a reward that is also the object of a visceral pull discounts steeply and changes in kind
together, but the mechanisms are separable, one a function of time-to-reward and the other a function of a state's
presence or absence, and the house stages the gap and not the curve.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the cold self as dishonest
  or the hot self as weak-willed, because the source finding it stages is that both are reasoning and wanting honestly
  from where they stand: the cold self forecasts as well as a state it cannot fully access allows, and the hot self is
  not overridden by an inferior impulse but is, in that moment, genuinely pursuing a want the earlier self could not
  have felt on its behalf. No one breaks a promise out of bad faith and no one is punished for a lack of resolve; the
  reversal is authored by no one, the predictable cost of a preference that a state, and not a plan, controls.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a plan sturdy enough to survive its own blind spot. The
  remedy, deciding inside the hot state itself or building a rule that does not depend on cold-state prediction, is real
  but incomplete: the hot state is not always available to consult in advance, and a rule built to bind the hot self can
  itself be misjudged from the cold state that designed it, since the same gap that misjudges a future preference can
  misjudge the rule meant to hold it. The cycle is not closed by one bad experience, since the cold state that returns
  once the visceral state passes is, again, structurally unable to fully recall the pull it just felt.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the framework and its name belong to Loewenstein, the applied medical case and
  its stakes are Loewenstein's own extension, and the boundary and domain-variation findings belong to the wider
  visceral-influence and empathy-gap literature that followed. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding:
  this is a documented tendency that varies by domain and by the intensity and salience of the visceral state in
  question, strongest for acute drive states and weaker for milder or more familiar ones, and the house holds that not
  all self-prediction fails, only the kind that reaches across a state it cannot access.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Plan**: carries the setup (Loewenstein, 1996), the decision honestly made in the cold state, reasoned from
  everything a state free of hunger, pain, fear, or desire can access.
- **The Gap**: carries the mechanism (Loewenstein, 1996), the cold state's structural inability to simulate a visceral
  state's true pull, an honest forecast that is reliably wrong and not a failure of intelligence.
- **The Onset**: carries the arrival, the visceral state itself, real and involuntary, narrowing attention onto the
  drive and its relief.
- **The Reversal**: carries the harm (Loewenstein, 1996), the plan abandoned or overridden because what is wanted has
  changed and not merely how urgently it is wanted, drawn distinct from Starting Tomorrow's discount curve.
- **The Return**: carries the applied stakes (Loewenstein, 2005), the visceral state passing and the cold state
  resuming, no better informed than before, a plan remade by a self that still cannot fully recall the pull it just
  felt.
- **The Conditions**: carries the bound (the visceral-influence and empathy-gap literature that followed), the scope
  held to a gap that varies by domain and by the intensity and familiarity of the visceral state, the marker of the
  hedge.
