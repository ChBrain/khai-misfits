# Keep Her Course and Speed: Reference

The research warrant for Keep Her Course and Speed.
The setting is a crossing under the collision regulations; the play runs on the fact that an avoidance problem
with two manoeuvring parties is not determinate and becomes determinate when one is held fixed, and on the fact
that the moment at which the held party is released cannot be made precise without returning the problem, all
cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the stand-on duty as **determinacy bought by suppressing one party's response**: the give-way vessel must
take early and substantial action, the stand-on vessel must keep her course and speed, and the second duty is
what makes the first one's calculation possible; _early_ is a range rather than an instant, so a lawful stretch
exists in every approach during which the give-way vessel has not acted and owes nothing; the stand-on vessel is
released _as soon as it becomes apparent_ that the other is not acting, which is a judgement and must remain one;
and the failure is two lawful manoeuvres arriving in the same few seconds. The play models the geometry that
makes holding necessary, the give-way duty and the gap inside its timing, the release condition and why precision
would destroy it, the room going while everybody complies, the two correct acts, and the executable rule that
asks for the one value the regulation keeps, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a
careless watch or a badly drafted regulation.

The governing law is that the arrangement's benefit is stored in one party's inaction and in the imprecision of
the condition that ends it, so making that condition decidable spends exactly what the inaction was bought for.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The stand-on
vessel keeps her course and speed, as required. The give-way vessel takes substantial action inside the range her
duty allows. The watchkeeper who judges that no turn is coming reasons soundly from an accurate picture. The
regulation's drafters left one word indeterminate for the reason that word has to be indeterminate. And the
engineers asking for a number want the rule obeyed more consistently than people obey it.

**And the holding is the achievement, which the play establishes before anything else.** An avoidance problem
with two moving parties is not determinate: each party's plan invalidates the other's. Fix one trajectory and the
encounter has an answer in it. So keeping course and speed is not passivity or deference but the thing that turns
the other bridge's guess into a calculation, and it resolves crossings tens of thousands of times a day without
anybody writing anything down. **Whatever the regulations had said, somebody would have had to stop responding**,
so the cost is a property of the geometry rather than of these particular words.

**Then the give-way duty, which is equally real and contains a gap by construction.** Action must be _early and
substantial_, and the two adjectives are not alike. Substantial is a size and can be checked, because a small
alteration is unreadable at a distance and the rule says so. Early is a range, because what counts as early
depends on the encounter and no rule covering every encounter could say otherwise. **So there is a stretch of
every approach in which the give-way vessel has not acted and is in breach of nothing**, and from the other
bridge a vessel that is late and a vessel that is not coming are the same picture.

**Then the release condition, which is where the play turns.** The stand-on vessel may act as soon as it becomes
apparent that the other is not taking appropriate action. _Apparent_ is a judgement, sitting between provisions
that are exact on either side, and almost everybody who meets it reads it as a drafting failure and reaches for a
criterion: a range, a time to closest approach, a closing rate. Any of them would serve as a criterion and none of
them can be used, because **a precise threshold is a moment at which the stand-on vessel's behaviour changes
discontinuously**, and the give-way vessel would then have to predict that moment, which is the unpredictability
the holding was purchased to remove. The vagueness is not where the rule leaks. **It is the last place the rule's
own benefit is stored.**

**Then the harm, which is composed entirely of obedience.** The give-way vessel turns, late and lawfully. The
stand-on vessel, having concluded on the evidence in front of her that no turn was coming, acts as the rule
permits. Neither did anything wrong at the moment they did it, and each manoeuvre is made against a picture the
other has already invalidated. And it is not a coincidence: **both acts are triggered by the same event, the
approach getting late, so they are correlated by construction** and arrive together far more often than
independence would put them there.

**And the room was spent on legibility before it was worth having.** The stand-on duty is heaviest while there is
most sea room and lightest when there is least, so the arrangement restricts most in the stretch where a response
would have cost least, and the reserve is gone by the part of the encounter that wants one.

**And the strongest settlement asks for the mechanism.** A vessel that steers itself would obey the rule more
consistently than people do, which would be a genuine improvement. Nearly all of the regulation converts: which
vessel gives way, which side to alter to, how large the alteration must be. Then the release condition, and an
algorithm cannot execute a judgement, so the implementing literature asks for the ambiguity to be removed and is
right about what an algorithm needs. **What is being asked for and what is being relied on are the same
quantity**, and any value chosen becomes a moment other vessels must predict, so the fill produces the problem it
was meant to remove at whatever value is chosen.

It is distinct from the house's **The Stag Hunt**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages the assurance game,
and the cut is worth writing out because a first reading of it nearly struck this candidate. The Stag Hunt's
quantity is not uncertainty in general: it is **strategic uncertainty in an equilibrium-selection problem**, where
a game has a payoff-dominant and a risk-dominant equilibrium, the safe choice depends on nobody, and doubt about
the others drives each player to hedge to the inferior outcome. Set that to zero and this misfit is unchanged,
because **no equilibrium is being selected here and neither party is hedging.** The stand-on vessel's holding is
not a safe option chosen out of doubt: it is a legal duty, it is the cooperative act rather than the cautious one,
and she would hold it against a partner she trusted completely. The failures are also opposite in sign: that play
ends with nobody moving, and this one ends with both moving at once. Generalising the incumbent's quantity to
_uncertainty_ makes this look annexed and is the wrong test, since the switch-off is run on the quantity a misfit
declares and not on a wider one it does not.

It is distinct from the house's **You Have to Mean It**, which stages the deterrence paradox, where it is rational
to intend what it would be irrational to carry out. Remove that engine, by stipulating that no party is ever asked
to form an intention it would be irrational to execute, and this stands entire, because **nothing here is
intended in advance.** The stand-on vessel forms no commitment about a future act: she is told to keep doing what
she is doing, and there is no moment at which executing her duty is against her interest while having adopted it
was in her interest.

It is distinct from the house's **Time Inconsistency**, which stages the reversal between what a party wants now
and what the same party will want later, and the value of binding the later self. Remove that engine, by
stipulating preferences that never change, and this is untouched, because **the stand-on vessel's preferences are
constant throughout.** She wants the same outcome at every moment of the approach and is not being protected from
her future self: she is prevented from responding, which is a restriction on her action and not on her
inclination.

It is distinct from the house's **Everybody's Business**, which stages the bystander effect, where responsibility
diffuses among many and each waits for another to act. Remove that engine, by stipulating exactly two parties with
duties assigned individually and in writing, which is the case here, and the mechanism is untouched. **Nobody here
is waiting to see whether somebody else will take it on.** The duties are allocated, known, and accepted by both,
and the failure comes from both discharging theirs.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast either watch as
  careless or the regulation as badly drafted, because nothing in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  stand-on vessel keeps her course and speed as required, the give-way vessel acts substantially inside the range
  her duty allows, the officer who judges that no turn is coming reasons soundly from an accurate picture, the
  drafters left one word a judgement for the reason it has to be one, and the engineers asking for a number want
  the rule obeyed more consistently than people manage. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that the determinacy is bought by suppressing one party's response and the price is
  paid where that suppression ends.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Quantifying the release
  condition makes the rule decidable and makes the stand-on vessel's behaviour predictable in a new place, which
  is the unpredictability the holding removed. Requiring the stand-on vessel to act earlier shortens the interval
  in which she is legible, which is the whole of what she contributes. Radio agreement between the two bridges is
  discouraged for reasons of its own and, where it happens, replaces a rule known to both with an arrangement
  known to two. And automation, which is the strongest remedy available and would improve consistency
  genuinely, needs the one value the rule is keeping.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the regulations are the International Maritime Organization's
  instrument, the critical analysis of what an algorithm can implement is García Maza and Poo Argüelles's, and the
  characterisation of practice from AIS data is Hagen, Knutsen, Johansen and Brekke's. The bounds are carried with
  them, and two are owed plainly: **neither cited paper was read in full**, both being behind publisher access, so
  each is cited for the question it addresses and its stated findings as reported, and **no numerical parameter
  from either is used anywhere in this production**. The others: the rules are summarised rather than reproduced,
  and their application in any particular case is a matter for a court and not for this house; the structural
  halves, that the geometry requires one party to be held and that a precise threshold would have to be
  predicted, are offered as descriptions and not as measured results; the claim that the two manoeuvres are
  correlated by their shared trigger is an argument and not a statistic; and how often this pattern accounts for
  collisions is not asserted here at all. **The play makes no claim about any vessel, any officer, any casualty or
  any administration, and gives no navigational, legal or regulatory advice of any kind**, only that a rule which
  makes one party legible by forbidding her to respond has to decide when she may respond again, and cannot.
