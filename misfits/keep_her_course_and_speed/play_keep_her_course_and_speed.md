# Play: Keep Her Course and Speed

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Keep Her Course and Speed.
Two ships cross. One is required to get out of the way. The other is required to do nothing at all: to hold what she is doing, exactly, while the distance closes.
That second duty is the strange one, and it is the one that makes the first duty possible. A pilot cannot plan a way round something that is also planning a way round them.
So one of them is made into a fixed object, on purpose, and told to stay that way until it is too late to matter.

## Arc

The bend is this: the rule buys the give-way ship a solvable problem by forbidding the other ship to respond, and the moment at which that prohibition lifts cannot be written down without destroying the thing it was bought for.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: an avoidance problem with two moving parties is not determinate, and becomes determinate the instant one of them is held fixed; and any precise trigger for releasing the held party is itself a change of behaviour that the other party would then have to predict.
Take the holding first, because the play has to establish that it is **right**, and that it is the achievement. The stand-on ship keeps her course and speed. That is not passivity and it is not deference: it is what turns the other ship's problem from a guess into a calculation, and it is why crossings resolve tens of thousands of times a day without anybody noticing.
Then the other duty, which is equally real. The give-way ship must take action early and substantially. **Early is a range and not an instant**, so there is a stretch of the approach during which she has not yet acted and is not yet in breach of anything.
Then the fact that inverts it. The stand-on ship, watching that stretch, cannot tell a ship that is late from a ship that is not coming. The rule anticipates this and releases her: she may act **as soon as it becomes apparent** that the other is not doing what she must. And apparent is a judgement, deliberately.
Then the reason it must stay that way, which is what makes this a structure rather than sloppy drafting. Replace apparent with a number, any number, and that number becomes a moment at which the stand-on ship's behaviour changes discontinuously, and the give-way ship must now predict **that**. **The vagueness is not a defect in the rule. It is the last place the rule's own benefit is stored**, and spending it buys back the unpredictability the whole arrangement exists to remove.
Then the harm, which is made of two correct acts. The give-way ship turns, late and lawfully. The stand-on ship, having concluded on the evidence in front of her that no turn was coming, turns in the same few seconds. Neither did anything wrong at the moment they did it. **Both of them obeyed, and the obedience arrived at once.**
Reach for a settlement and the strongest one is being built now and cannot get past the same word. A ship that steers itself needs the rule as a decision, and the rule does not contain one: the literature that tries to implement it asks for the ambiguity to be removed, which is right about what an algorithm needs and is a request to spend the thing that was protecting everybody. **What is being asked for and what is being relied on are the same quantity**, and nobody has found a way to have it twice.

## Stakes

Whether a rule can make one party legible without making them helpless.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether either watchkeeper is careless, because the production refuses to cast one and the trap runs on two ships that both comply, and not whether the regulation is badly drafted, because its vagueness is shown to be the deliberate and load-bearing part, but whether the determinacy that one party's fixed behaviour supplies can be bought without also fixing the moment at which that party may stop supplying it. The production cannot buy it. It can only show that the holding is what makes the other's problem solvable, that early is a range with a lawful gap inside it, that the release condition must stay a judgement or return the problem it solved, that the failure is two lawful turns in the same few seconds, and that the machine which needs the rule as a number is asking for the one thing the rule keeps in order to work.
