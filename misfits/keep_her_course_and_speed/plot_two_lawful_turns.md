## Action

## Stage

[Both Turns at Once](piece_both_turns_at_once.md) arrives at [the last two minutes](place_the_last_two_minutes.md), composed entirely of obedience.

## Tension

This is where the harm lands, and it is made of two correct acts.
The give-way ship turns, late and lawfully, exactly as her duty required. The stand-on ship, having concluded on the evidence in front of her that no turn was coming, acts as the rule permits her to. **Neither did anything wrong at the moment they did it**, and each manoeuvre is made against a picture the other has already invalidated.
And it is not a coincidence, which is the part worth having. Both acts are triggered by the same event, the approach getting late, so they are **correlated by construction** and arrive together far more often than chance would put them there.
The room, meanwhile, was spent on legibility. The stand-on duty is heaviest while there is most of it and lightest when there is least, so the arrangement restricted most where a response would have cost least.
