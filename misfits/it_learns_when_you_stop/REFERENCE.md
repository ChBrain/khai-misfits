# It Learns When You Stop: Reference

The research warrant for It Learns When You Stop.
The setting is pressure-release riding; the play runs on the fact that in negative reinforcement the reinforcing
event is the removal of the pressure rather than its application, and on what that does to a rider who responds to
an unanswered question by asking more firmly, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the ridden aid as **a signal whose teaching happens only when it stops**: the rein and the leg put a
question and supply a motivation, and neither is a fault; the horse's first answer arrives as a shift before any
movement; the reasonable response to an unanswered question is to increase the pressure and hold it, so the
horse's small offer arrives inside the holding and is met with pressure; and the horse, reading that accurately,
offers less often. The play models the ask that is correct, the release that carries the whole lesson, the try
that is too early to be seen, the firmness that is expressed as holding, the continuum with no felt boundary
between correction and punishment, the explanation that fits every observation, and the settlement that is
measured and takes years to acquire, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a harsh rider
or a difficult horse.

The governing law is that the instructional content of a pressure-release exchange lives entirely in the moment
the pressure ends, so increasing the pressure improves the question and degrades the answer at the same time.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The ask is a
legitimate signal. The firmer ask is the reasonable response to an unanswered question, and is what good teaching
recommends over nagging. The horse's offer is the correct response in its earliest form. The rider's conclusion
is ordinary reasoning from accurate observation. Nothing here needs a cruel rider and the production refuses to
supply one.

**And the asking is right, which the play establishes before anything else.** A rider who never asks teaches
nothing at all: the rein and the leg carry both halves of a signal, what is wanted and a reason to answer, and
that is how horses are taught, everywhere, every day. A play that begins by faulting the ask has nothing left to
say about the release.

**Then the inversion, which is the whole mechanism in one sentence.** In negative reinforcement the reinforcer is
the **removal** of the aversive stimulus, so the pressure asks and the release teaches. Everything before the
release is only the question, and the lesson is dated by when the rider stops rather than by how well the rider
asked. **The instrument a rider is taught to perfect is not the instrument that does the teaching.**

**Then the hinge, which is what makes this a structure rather than a lapse.** When the answer does not come, the
reasonable move is to ask more firmly, and there is only one way to make a sustained signal firmer: increase it
and keep it there. Firmness and **holding** are the same act described twice. The horse's first offer is a shift
of weight or a fractional softening arriving **before** the movement, so a rider waiting for the answer sees
nothing yet and goes on holding. If the pressure is still present when the offer comes, then what followed the
offer was pressure. **The try was punished**, and the rate of trying falls, and the rate falls because the horse
read its situation correctly.

**And no felt boundary separates the two.** The equitation-science account describes a continuum running from
poorly timed negative reinforcement into punishment, with nothing marking the crossing: the rider experiences one
continuous act of being firm, and the position on that continuum is set by when the horse offers, which the rider
does not know. **It is not a dial anybody is holding.** Its far end has a name, and the name is learned
helplessness.

**Then the explanation, which is the part that seals it.** The horse ignores the aids, resists more the firmer the
ask, and does nothing when asked politely. One word covers all three, it is available, and it is what a reasonable
person concludes. The competing account requires knowing that the reinforcer is the release and that a try was met
with pressure months ago, neither of which is visible from the saddle. And **acting on the wrong explanation means
asking harder, which produces more of the behaviour it predicts**, so it is confirmed daily by its own
consequences and never faces a test it could fail.

**And the settlement is real, measured, and slow.** Releasing at the horse's first attempt rather than at the
completed response was compared directly in trot-to-walk transitions with young horses, and the early release
produced lower rein tension and fewer conflict behaviours. So the exit exists and is documented. What it asks is
that the rider give up the pressure at an instant when the thing asked for is **still absent**, which means
feeling a shift that has not become a movement. **The rider has to stop asking precisely when they most want to
keep asking**, and what stands between them and that is a perception rather than a fact: knowing the whole
mechanism does not let a hand know when to open, so a rider who understands all of this will hold through the try
exactly as before, now with an explanation.

It is distinct from the house's **The Open Cage**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages learned helplessness,
where uncontrollable aversive events teach an animal that outcomes are independent of its behaviour. Remove that
engine entirely, by stipulating a perfectly contingent world in which the pressure always ends as a consequence of
what the horse does and never otherwise, and this misfit is unchanged, because **the contingency here is exact and
is teaching the wrong rule.** The release is reliably contingent, on the completed movement rather than on the
try, so the horse learns precisely and correctly that offering early is followed by pressure. That play needs
non-contingency; this one is a learning success at the wrong target, and the helplessness at the far end of the
continuum is where it arrives rather than what drives it.

It is distinct from the house's **Paid to Stop Caring**, which stages overjustification, where an external reward
displaces a motive the person already had. Remove that engine, by stipulating an animal with no self-supplied
motivation whatever and therefore nothing available to crowd out, and this stands entire, because **nothing here
is being displaced.** That play needs a prior internal reason to destroy; this one needs only a reinforcer that
lands a second late.

It is distinct from the house's **The Cobra Effect**, which stages the perverse incentive, where paying for a
proxy buys the proxy. Remove that engine, by stipulating that the reinforcer is aimed at exactly the right
behaviour with no proxy anywhere and no possibility of gaming, and this is untouched, because **the rider is
already rewarding exactly the right thing.** That play turns on _what_ is reinforced; this one turns on _when_,
and the two are independent: a perfectly specified target reinforced late still teaches the wrong lesson.

It is distinct from the house's **Alarm Fatigue**, which stages the cry-wolf effect, where a signal that is
frequently uninformative stops being answered. Remove that engine, by stipulating a signal that is true every
single time and never once a false alarm, and this stands, because **the rider's ask is a true signal and the
horse has not stopped believing it.** There the receiver discounts the signal because the signal was unreliable;
here the receiver has learned that answering it early is met with more of it, which is a lesson about the
consequence rather than about the message.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the rider as harsh,
  the horse as difficult, or the teaching as bad, because nothing in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  ask is a legitimate signal doing legitimate work, the firmer ask is the reasonable response to an unanswered
  question and is what good instruction recommends over nagging, the horse's early offer is the correct response
  in its first instalment, and the rider's conclusion is ordinary reasoning from accurate observation. The failure
  is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that effort runs against instruction.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Explaining the mechanism changes
  nothing, because what stands between the rider and the remedy is a perception rather than a fact. Asking more
  softly is not the answer, since a question that cannot be read teaches nothing either. Watching harder does not
  help, because the try happens before the movement and the boundary on the continuum is not felt. And releasing
  at the try, which genuinely works and has been measured working, requires giving up the pressure at the instant
  the thing asked for is still absent, which is the moment a rider most wants to keep asking, and the years it
  takes to feel are not shortened by wanting or by understanding.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the continuum from poorly timed negative reinforcement into punishment
  is McGreevy and McLean's, the comparison of release at the first attempt against release at the completed
  response is Egenvall, Eisersiö and Roepstorff's, and the operant definition the whole inversion rests on is
  Skinner's. The bounds are carried with them: **the transition comparison is a pilot study with a small number of
  young horses**, so its magnitudes are indicative rather than settled; the account is stated for pressure-release
  work under saddle and is not offered as a general theory of animal training; how much ordinary training
  difficulty this mechanism accounts for, and how far knowing it shortens the skill, are genuinely contested and
  the play adjudicates neither; and the conflict-behaviour measures are observational categories rather than
  direct readings of an animal's experience. **The play makes no claim about any rider, any horse, any trainer or
  any discipline, and gives no training, veterinary or welfare advice of any kind**, only that when the teaching
  lives in the stopping, trying harder means stopping later.
