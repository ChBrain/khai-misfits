# Built Before the Rule: Reference

The research warrant for Built Before the Rule.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the prospective application of building codes, the grandfathering
of existing structures, the substantial improvement threshold in floodplain management, and mandatory
seismic retrofit ordinances for unreinforced masonry, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging code grandfathering as **an exemption granted by the same fact that predicts the hazard**: building
requirements are written after failures, because nobody knows what to demand until something demonstrates
it, and they apply forward, because applying them backward would condemn a jurisdiction's entire existing
stock on the day of enactment. So relief is granted by date of construction, and date of construction is
what predicts the absence of the protection. The play models the documented mechanics, the code that could
only arrive late and reach forward, the lawfully nonconforming building that is both exempt and lethal, the
improvement threshold that turns repair into a condemnation notice, the estimate specified to land beneath
it, the denominator that concentrates the deterrent on the poorest stock, and the retrofit mandate that
reaches the building by charging the tenant, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of
lax regulators or negligent landlords.

The governing law is that the exemption and the danger are one fact read twice. A rule that exempted fewer
buildings would be a rule that condemned more occupied housing, so the overlap cannot be narrowed from
either end without displacing the people the code exists to protect.

What turns it is the trigger. Grandfathered status survives only while the building is left substantially
alone. Under the national flood programme, work costing fifty per cent or more of the structure's market
value before the damage requires the whole building to be brought to current standard, and communities may
set the line lower, at thirty or forty per cent. Since compliance routinely costs more than the building is
worth, the rational, lawful and often the only affordable course is to specify work that lands underneath
the threshold. The documented result is that buildings decline or sit empty rather than be improved: the
rule written to stop the stock being patched and never upgraded is answered by patching the stock and never
upgrading it.

And the denominator sorts. The threshold is a percentage of market value, so the cheapest buildings reach it
with the least work, which means the owners with the least capacity to fund full compliance are the ones
most often required to and therefore most often deterred from repairing at all. Nothing in the rule intends
this and nothing in it could avoid it, since a threshold must be proportionate to something and an absolute
figure would fall harder still on the cheap building.

It is distinct from the house's **The New Normal**, where each small deviation is tolerated once and becomes
the standard, so the line of acceptable creeps until it fails: there the standard itself moves, quietly and
by repeated judgement. Here the standard does not move at all. It rises, publicly and correctly, and simply
does not apply to the buildings in question.

It is distinct from the house's **The Unmarked Edge**, where cost and effort push the working point toward a
safety boundary nobody can see: there the system drifts across a line through accumulated pressure. Here
nothing drifts and nothing is hidden from the regulator: the buildings are catalogued in the city's own
records, their hazard is known, and they are exempt on the face of the rule.

It is distinct from the house's **The Spent Margin**, where a safety measure hands a margin that the
protected party spends back as risk: there the intervention works and its benefit is consumed by changed
behaviour. Here the intervention never reaches the party at all, and the behaviour it changes is the
decision to repair.

It is distinct from the house's **Path Dependence**, where a lead won by accident compounds until a worse
standard locks in: there the inferior thing persists because adoption is self-reinforcing. Here nothing is
adopted and nothing competes. The old building persists because the law deliberately protects it, for
reasons that are good.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the code as
  captured or the owner as a slumlord, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  requirement could not have been written before the disaster that demonstrated it, and prospective
  application is the only version that does not condemn a jurisdiction's whole stock on the day of
  enactment; the building complies with everything that existed when it went up; the owner who reduces the
  scope is following the incentive the regulation handed them, on correct professional advice, falsifying
  nothing; and the official who applies the threshold evenly is doing the thing that makes the rule fair,
  since a line that bent for a sympathetic applicant would bend for a well-connected one. The failure is
  authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the fact granting the exemption
  and the fact predicting the danger are the same fact.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Mandatory retrofit
  ordinances are the right answer and the only instrument that reaches an exempt building, and the work
  routinely costs more than the building is worth, so it is recovered from the rent or the building is sold
  and the tenants it protects are displaced by it. Lowering the threshold catches more buildings and deters
  more repairs. Raising it lets more damage accumulate uncorrected. Demolition and rebuilding meets the
  standard by removing the cheapest housing in the district. And because the cost falls locally and
  immediately while the earthquake is a probability, doing nothing remains the cheapest course and is
  indistinguishable from prudence until it is not.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the codes, the programme rules and the retrofit
  ordinances are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: thresholds, definitions of market value,
  cumulative-improvement provisions and enforcement practice vary substantially between jurisdictions, and
  some communities adopt cumulative or lower thresholds specifically to close the gap staged here; grant
  and buyout programmes exist in some places and materially change the arithmetic for some owners; the
  deterrent effect is well attested in practice and its magnitude is not something this play quantifies;
  and the distributional point about market value as the denominator follows from the construction of the
  rule rather than from a measurement the house is citing. **The play makes no claim that codes should apply
  retroactively**, which would empty occupied buildings at a stroke, only that the exemption is handed out
  by the property that marks the danger.
