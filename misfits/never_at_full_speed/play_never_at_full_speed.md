# Play: Never at Full Speed

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Never at Full Speed.
Every school has a set of techniques it does not spar. The eye, the throat, the small joints, the neck taken past where a neck goes.
They are not excluded because anybody doubts them. They are excluded because nobody doubts them: do that to a training partner at full speed and you have no training partner.
So the techniques a school is most confident in are the ones it has never once performed.

## Arc

The bend is this: a technique is excluded from full-resistance practice in proportion to how severe it is, and severity is also the whole of the reason it is believed, so **the confidence and the absence of rehearsal come off one quantity**.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: what makes a technique too dangerous to practise is that it works on the person you would practise it on; and a skill that has not been performed under the conditions of use is not available under the conditions of use.
Take the exclusion first, because the play has to establish that it is **right**, and more than right. Kano's founding move was exactly this: he took the most dangerous techniques out of free practice so that free practice could be done at full effort, and kept them in the forms. That is not a compromise. It is the innovation, and it is why judo could pressure-test anything at all. A school that spars everything has no members left.
Then the preservation, which is honest. The excluded techniques are not hidden or denied. They are named, taught, demonstrated, graded and passed on, with care, by people who believe in them and are not lying about anything. **Everything in this account is done in the open.**
Then the fact that inverts it. The forms are not rehearsal. Rehearsal means doing the thing under the conditions it will be used in, against somebody who does not want it to work, and that is exactly what the exclusion forbids. **What is preserved is the shape, and the shape was never the part that goes missing.**
Then the sentence, which is where the trap closes. _We do not spar that, because it would injure you._ It is true. It is said in good faith, by an instructor with no motive to mislead. And it is one sentence doing two jobs at once: it supplies the reason there is no evidence, **and it supplies the claim of high effect**, so the belief explains its own lack of testing and nothing is left over to want explaining.
Then the night it happens. Under threat, attention goes to the threat, and what a person does is not what they know: it is what they have done most. The measured version is unkind and specific. Officers with an extra martial-arts session a week perform better under anxiety than those without, and **that extra training does not prevent performance falling under anxiety at all**. More practice helps. It does not buy immunity, and it is practice of the things that were practised.
Reach for a settlement and every one of them buys realism with fidelity. Protective gear lets you hit harder and changes what you are hitting. Progressive resistance gets closer and stops short of the thing. Scenario training under induced stress works and is the best of them, and it still cannot deliver the excluded technique at its full effect, because delivering it is the thing that was ruled out. **The one experiment that would settle it is the one nobody may run**, and the reason nobody may run it is the reason it is worth running.

## Stakes

Whether a body of skill can hold anything in reserve.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the instructor is a fraud, because the production refuses to cast one and the account is entirely open, and not whether the technique works, because the play grants that it does and that granting it is the problem, but whether severity can be prevented from buying both the belief and the exemption from testing with the same coin. The production cannot prevent it. It can only show that the exclusion was an advance, that the preservation is honest, that a form is not a rehearsal, that one true sentence supplies a reason and a claim at once, that duress spends whatever has the repetitions, and that the settlement which works buys its realism by giving up precisely the thing that was set aside.
