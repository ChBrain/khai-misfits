# Piece: The Shallows

## Taxonomy

[I Know How It Works](play_i_know_how_it_works.md). The actual thinness of the understanding beneath the felt grasp: a grasp of what the thing does mistaken for a grasp of how it does it, shallow exactly where it feels deep.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

Lies beneath [the Felt Grasp](piece_the_felt_grasp.md) wherever it is carried, met directly at [the Whiteboard](place_the_whiteboard.md), the one place anyone is asked to stand on it and find out how far down it goes.

## Load Bearing

The shallows are what the felt grasp is a feeling about: knowing what a bicycle does, that pedaling turns the wheel, standing in for knowing how, the chain, the gearing, the geometry that keeps it upright, and the substitution goes unnoticed because the gap between knowing-what and knowing-how is specific to explanatory knowledge, wider there than for facts or procedures a person can simply recite (Rozenblit and Keil). It is load bearing because it is what makes the illusion an illusion rather than a variance in confidence: the ground is thin, not merely under-rated. Guard against reading the shallows as a personal failing. They are the ordinary condition of explanatory knowledge, present under the careful and the expert as readily as under anyone, and they are invisible by construction: nothing in daily use of a mechanism, only an actual attempt to produce it, ever reaches down far enough to find them.

## Apparent

Depth, on casual inspection. A person who names every part, uses the thing daily, has opinions about it, reads as someone who has it down.
The reading is honest as far as familiarity goes, and familiarity is not the same measurement as the one the shallows fail: knowing a thing well from use is not knowing how it is built, and nothing about frequent use tells a person which one they have.
