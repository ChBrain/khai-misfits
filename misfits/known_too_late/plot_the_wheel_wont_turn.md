# Plot: The Wheel Won't Turn

## Taxonomy

[Known Too Late](play_known_too_late.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The harm is plain now, and the hand reaches for control.

## Action

At [the Bind](place_the_bind.md), [the Watch](persona_the_watch.md) from [the Watch's seat](position_watch.md) on [Turn the Wheel](plan_turn_the_wheel.md) through [Steering](process_steering.md) meets [the Locked Wheel](piece_the_locked_wheel.md), the control grown slow and costly and resisted by all built around the technology.

## Stage

This is the harm itself, the second horn: when enough is finally known to warrant control, control has become slow, expensive, and resisted by everyone who has since built their own footing on the technology as it stands. The power to steer and the knowledge of where to steer are never in hand at the same time; the wheel turns for no firmness of grip, not because the hand is weak but because the mechanism itself has seized (Collingridge). This is distinct from Path Dependence and Lock-In, which name compounding switching costs around a merely suboptimal standard, where here the harm itself was genuinely unknowable at the moment it could have been cheaply avoided. It is distinct from Chesterton's Fence, which turns on a forgotten purpose behind an existing barrier, where here no purpose was ever forgotten, the harm simply could not yet be seen. And it is distinct from The Horizon Problem, which names a future lying outside the frame of view altogether, where here the knowledge does arrive, in full view, but only after the power to act on it has already gone. No villain: the loss is authored by no one. The lateness is the order of knowing, not a want of nerve, and the watch who reaches for the wheel is not timid, the wheel is genuinely locked.

## Tension

The wheel that will not turn for any firmness of hand.
