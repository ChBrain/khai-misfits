# Piece: The Locked Wheel

## Taxonomy

[Known Too Late](play_known_too_late.md). The harm and the second horn: control grown slow, costly, and resisted just as the knowledge to warrant it arrives.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

At [the Bind](place_the_bind.md), carried by [Steering](process_steering.md), voiced by [the Watch](persona_the_watch.md): a hand on the wheel that will not turn.

## Load Bearing

This is the power problem: when enough is at last known to warrant control, control has become expensive and difficult, resisted by everyone and everything built around the technology, so no firmness of hand turns the wheel cheaply (Collingridge). The power to steer and the knowledge of how to steer are never in hand at the same time. The harm no one could act on early, at the Blind Window, is the very harm no one can cheaply act on late, here, and no villain authored the gap between them: the early hands acted reasonably on no evidence, the late hand is not timid, the wheel is genuinely locked by entrenchment (Collingridge; Genus and Stirling).

This is not Path Dependence and Lock-In. There the engine is compounding switching costs around an arbitrary early standard, and the harm is being stuck with an inferior standard when a better one existed. Here the engine is the inverse timing of knowledge and power, and the harm is a governance failure over consequences that genuinely could not be known when they could have been acted on; lock-in is one mechanism of this late horn, but this misfit is the wider knowledge-power inversion, and its wound is the unsteered harm, not the suboptimal standard.

Nor is it Chesterton's Fence. There a safeguard's purpose was known once and then forgotten, so the remedy is to recover the lost knowledge before acting. Here the harm was never known to begin with: it was genuinely unknowable at the early stage, never available to forget, so no recovery of a lost reason could have helped. There was nothing to remember, only something that had not yet happened.

Nor is it the Horizon Problem. There the future consequence sits simply outside the plan's frame, never entering it at all. Here the knowledge of the harm does arrive, fully in frame, plainly seen and understood, but too late in the technology's life, after the Setting, for that knowledge to buy cheap control.

## Apparent

It looks like a timid overseer who lost the nerve to act, as if resolve alone could turn the wheel.
