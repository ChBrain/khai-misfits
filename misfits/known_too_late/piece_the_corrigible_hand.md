# Piece: The Corrigible Hand

## Load Bearing

The one real answer is corrigibility: building and adopting the technology to stay flexible and reversible, so that when the knowledge finally arrives the power to act on it has not wholly gone (Collingridge). It is costly, deliberately: it forgoes the gains of commitment and scale that a fully settled technology enjoys, trading some of the Setting's efficiency for a wheel that still turns. This is not free caution. It is a standing bet against the version of the Locked Wheel that never had to happen.

And the piece carries the bound as well as the remedy: the dilemma is a tendency, not an iron law. Its horns soften where some harms are foreseeable through real-time and anticipatory technology assessment (Guston and Sarewitz), and where some entrenchments, built for it from the start, prove reversible after all. But foresight is itself imperfect, and it never closes the gap fully; anticipation catches some harms and misses others, and the two horns are eased, not abolished (Genus and Stirling).

## Apparent

It looks like a brake on progress, caution held for its own sake rather than for anything it buys.
