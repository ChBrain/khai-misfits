# The Vote Takes Four Minutes: Reference

The research warrant for The Vote Takes Four Minutes.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the open-meeting requirement, on the empirical work about what
publicity does to a deliberating body, and on the documented practice of the gathering the requirement does
not define, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the open-meeting requirement as **a rule that makes one gathering visible and thereby makes another
one valuable**: the statute defines a meeting, usually by a quorum and an agenda, and requires that one to
be held where anybody may watch it, and it is written and enforced exactly as intended. What it cannot
define is every other occasion on which the members of a body speak to one another, so the settling moves
to the briefing, the one-to-one, the corridor and the call, and the public session becomes the place where
what was settled is put on the record. The play models the documented mechanics, the requirement that
attaches to a defined event, the cost of taking a position in a record that will be read by whoever will
judge it, the venue the requirement does not reach, the session that is held in full and is genuinely not a
sham, the dissent that thins when the transcript is published, and the watcher who cannot tell a short
session from a settled one, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a scheming
board or a cynical clerk.

The governing law is that **a duty of publicity attaches to an occasion and not to a decision**, so it can
move the decision without touching the occasion, and the occasion it leaves behind is complete, minuted,
lawful and no longer where anything is decided.

What makes it structural is that the members are not evading anything. **A position stated in a durable
record is a different object from a position stated among colleagues**, because the record is read later by
constituents, by employers, by the people who will judge a career, and it will be read without the room's
context. So a member who would say "I am not sure about the third option, talk me out of it" in a briefing
will not say it into a transcript, and the reticence is a correct reading of what a record does rather than
a want of courage. The Federal Reserve's decision to publish its transcripts supplies the measured version:
after publication began, members were less willing to voice dissent in the meetings.

What removes any possibility of blame from the rule is that **it works**. The session is open, the agenda is
posted, the minutes are accurate, the vote is recorded and any citizen may attend. Nothing in the statute
failed and no exemption was abused. The gathering the statute defines is exactly the gathering that happens
in public, and the gathering it does not define is not defined because no drafter could enumerate every
occasion on which five people might speak.

And the watcher is left with the hardest part of it, which is that **what they are shown is not false**.
The four-minute session is a real session, and the vote in it is the real vote, legally and practically.
What is missing is not concealed: the reasons were exchanged where the statute does not reach, and the
public record contains a complete account of an occasion that no longer carries the deliberation. **A
citizen watching a ratification cannot tell it from a body that simply agrees**, and neither can the
minutes.

It is distinct from the house's **You Would Have to Tell**, which stages a duty to report deterring the
disclosure the duty was meant to capture: there the thing wanted is **suppressed**, the patient does not
say it and no version of it exists anywhere. Stipulate that here, and the harm changes character entirely,
because nothing is suppressed: the deliberation happens in full, is candid, reaches a decision, and is
simply held where the rule does not reach. The difference is between a thing not said and a thing said
elsewhere, and only the second leaves the watcher a complete and misleading record.

It is distinct from the house's **Not While You Are Watching**, the observer's paradox, where the target is
specified by the absence of an observer and observing is the only way to reach it: there the quantity is
**attention itself**, a speaker monitoring their own speech under a microphone with no audience who will
ever judge them. Stipulate that observation produces no self-monitoring at all here, members perfectly
natural in front of a camera, and this survives intact, because a member who is entirely unselfconscious
still will not commit to a position in a durable record before knowing where the others stand. And
stipulate the reverse, that nothing said carries any consequence for anybody's standing, and this
disappears while the observer's paradox is untouched.

It is distinct from the house's **Anyone Can See It**, where a construal is taken for plain fact and each
side reads the same record as biased against it: there the difficulty is in the reading and the record is
adequate. Here the reading is correct and the record is the problem, since it faithfully reports an
occasion that is no longer the one that matters.

It joins the house's **The Paper Trail** and **Plausible Deniability** on the axis `blame-attachment`, at
the same sign and by a third construction. In The Paper Trail a clinician builds a record so that no fault
can attach; in Plausible Deniability a principal builds an ignorance so that no knowledge can; here a body
builds a **venue** so that no stated position can, and the venue is lawful, ordinary and often the same
briefing the body would have held anyway. **The quantity is the same in all three**, how readily
answerability attaches to whoever can be shown to have known, decided or said, and what differs is what a
party arranges against it: a record, an ignorance, and a room the rule does not name.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the members as
  schemers or the public session as a sham. A member who will not think aloud into a transcript is reading
  correctly what a durable record does, since it is read later, by people who will judge a career, without
  the room's context. The drafter who defined a meeting by a quorum did the only thing that makes a rule
  enforceable. The clerk minutes accurately. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that **a duty of publicity attaches to an occasion and not to a decision**,
  and the decision can move while the occasion stays exactly where the law put it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into better rules or better people. Widening the
  definition to reach serial contact works and reaches the ordinary conversation between two colleagues as
  well, so a body that may not confer informally is a body that arrives less prepared. Requiring
  deliberation to be _conducted_ in public rather than merely held in public asks for something no rule can
  observe, since it would have to police the state of mind in which a member enters the room. Publishing
  more, transcripts as well as minutes, is the move the measured evidence says thins dissent further.
  Exempting preliminary discussion by statute concedes the thing the requirement was for. And accepting the
  ratification as adequate, on the ground that the vote is real and the reasons are published in a report,
  gives up watching anybody decide anything, which is what the requirement existed to provide.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the political economy, the monetary-policy evidence
  and the constitutional history are theirs. The bounds are carried with them. **The evidence is a
  direction and not a verdict on transparency**, since the same literature reports real benefits of
  publicity and the arguments for open meetings do not rest on the quality of deliberation alone. **One
  committee's transcripts are one natural experiment**, and its members are unusually expert and unusually
  watched. **The statutes are one country's**, and other systems define a meeting differently and get
  different edges. **The magnitudes those sources report are deliberately not carried here**, because the
  works themselves could not be read in the pass that cited them: publisher hosts and the article archives
  are blocked to it, so the bibliography, the findings and their directions were verified by search and
  corroborated across independent reports, which is the treatment a citation survives and a reported figure
  is not.
