# Plot: Ask Twice

## Taxonomy

[The Last One to Answer](play_the_last_one_to_answer.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

Spare capacity, and a request that looks like going long.
[The Extra Two Per Cent](piece_the_extra_two_per_cent.md), about to be spent.

## Action

[Hedging](process_hedging.md): [The Hedge](persona_the_hedge.md), in [the duplicate seat](position_duplicate.md), issues a second copy and takes whichever answer arrives first.

## Stage

[The Hedge](persona_the_hedge.md), on [Ask Twice and Take the First](plan_ask_twice_and_take_the_first.md), sends the duplicate after a brief delay so that the duplication is confined to the requests that need it, and discards the loser.
The general result holds outside one company's systems: replicating an operation across diverse resources and taking the first to complete reduces mean and tail latency in name lookups, in database queries and in packet forwarding.

## Tension

The bargain is so good that it exposes what is really going on.
A few per cent of extra total work buys back most of the tail. That is only possible because the underlying pauses are **independent and brief**, and it treats none of them: what has been purchased is the improbability of two machines being interrupted at the same instant, which is usually a good bet and is a bet. Nothing in the fleet pauses any less often than before. So the design goal becomes **tolerating the tail rather than removing it**, which is what the people who named the problem recommend, on the ground that at this scale and complexity eliminating all latency variability is not available. The remedy is to ask the question twice and ignore one of the answers, and it is the best there is.
