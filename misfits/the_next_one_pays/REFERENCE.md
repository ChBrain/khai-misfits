# The Next One Pays: Reference

The research warrant for The Next One Pays.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the repeat player and one-shotter distinction in litigation, the
settling of cases whose judgments would set unwelcome precedent, and the doctrine on vacatur by
settlement, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the composition of case law as **a population one side selected and paid for**: a party who
litigates once needs the tangible outcome, a party who litigates constantly needs the rule, and because
those are different wants they can be traded. The recurring party lets the cases go to judgment where a
judgment would help it and ends the others by paying more than they are worth, which is lawful, open, and
better for the person receiving the money than a verdict would have been. The play models the documented
mechanics, the asymmetry of stakes that nobody arranged, the sorting of a docket by effect on the rule
rather than by merit, the offer that has to exceed the likely verdict in order to work at all, the duty
that requires counsel to recommend it, the court that can decide only what arrives and stays, and the
later party who meets the resulting law without anyone having acted against them, as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, not as a tale of corporate bad faith or a case against settlement.

The governing law is that a court makes a rule only in a case that reaches judgment, and whether a case
reaches judgment is decided by the parties rather than by the court. So the published body of law is not a
sample of the disputes brought: it is what survives a filter operated by whoever has an interest in
operating it, and only one of the two sides ever does.

What makes it structural rather than misconduct is that the filter runs on generosity. An offer below the
likely verdict would be refused and the case would run on, so the only settlements that remove a case from
the law are the ones that leave the claimant better off than winning. The strength of the claim raises the
price, which means the strongest claims are the ones most reliably bought out. And the recommendation to
accept is not a lapse in professional duty but a requirement of it, since counsel who advised refusing a
good offer in order to obtain a ruling for future strangers would be spending this client's case on people
who are not the client.

The cost lands where nobody is looking. The party who bears it arrives years later, has no connection to
the first claimant, cannot identify the transaction, and has nothing to appeal from, because a settled case
leaves no judgment, no reasons and frequently no public record at all. Nobody in the earlier room acted
against them: they were not in it, they were not foreseeable, and every person who was in it came out
ahead.

It is distinct from the house's **Only the Close Ones**, where the clear cases settle and the tried record
fills with toss-ups: there the selection is symmetric, performed by both sides out of shared foresight, it
sorts by how _predictable_ a case is, and what it damages is inference from the record. Here the selection
is one-sided, it sorts by _which direction_ a judgment would move the rule, and what it damages is the rule
itself. There nothing is bought; here the removal is purchased, and purchased by paying the other side more
than they would have won.

It is distinct from the house's **The File Drawer**, where only the studies that worked reach the record:
there the omission is unpaid and the omitter is the same party that produced the result, acting on a shared
norm about what is worth publishing. Here a second party pays money to a first party specifically to keep a
result off the record, and the cost falls on a third who is not in the transaction and does not exist yet.

It is distinct from the house's **The Survivors**, where the evidence is read off what came back because
what did not was destroyed: there nobody selects, the missing cases are missing by attrition, and the error
is the reader's. Here somebody selects, deliberately and at a price, and the reader of the reports is not
in error at all: every authority is correctly stated and correctly decided.

Those three cuts are all on the **engine** and none on the quantity, which is why this play joins the two
of them on the axis `outcome-selection`. In each case the record's membership depends on the outcome, and
what differs is who does the depending: a gatekeeper on a result, a hazard on survival, and here a repeat
player paying to remove the judgments that would have gone the wrong way. **This is the only one of the
three in which the selection is bought**, and the price is what makes the resulting record both incomplete
and impeccable.

It is distinct from the house's **On Good Authority**, where a claim repeated in good faith acquires weight
the evidence never gave it: there the record is complete and its weight is misread. Here the record is
incomplete and reads exactly right.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the recurring
  party as a manipulator or the claimant as a sell-out, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to
  get wrong: a party assessing its own exposure across its own portfolio is doing what every advisor would
  tell it to do, the premium is paid willingly and in full to a person with a strong claim, nothing is
  concealed from that person and no court is misled. The claimant who accepts is right to accept, having
  one case they will never repeat and no use for a rule that would be applied to other people for the rest
  of their life. Counsel is not merely permitted but required to recommend acceptance. And the court is not
  passive by choice: it cannot reach for a dispute that has ended. The failure is authored by nobody and
  emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the filter shaping the law is operated by generosity,
  enforced by professional duty, and invisible to the only institution that could correct it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Refusing to vacate a
  judgment because the parties later settled works, and reaches only the cases that ended after something
  was written, which moves the sorting earlier rather than stopping it. Forbidding the confidentiality
  works, and takes back a term the claimant was paid for, so the bill for the public good falls on the
  person in the worst year of their life. And giving the party who is here once an organised
  representative works by creating a second repeat player, one with its own stake in the rule and its own
  reason to trade a particular client's case for it, so the asymmetry is answered by installing another
  instance of it facing the other way.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the repeat player framework, the empirical studies of
  litigant success and the vacatur doctrine are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the settlement
  side of the mechanism is inherently hard to measure, because settlements are private and usually
  confidential, so the sorting is inferred rather than counted; the advantage of the "haves" in published
  outcomes has competing explanations, among them superior resources, better case screening and simple
  differences in case quality, and scholars disagree about how much of it is rule-play; the selection
  account of Priest and Klein explains the composition of tried cases differently and is staged separately
  in this house; and repeat-player capacity also works for claimants, since organised representation is
  what makes small claims viable at all. **The play makes no claim that settlement should be discouraged**,
  only that a record made of what one side allowed through is not a record of what was brought.
