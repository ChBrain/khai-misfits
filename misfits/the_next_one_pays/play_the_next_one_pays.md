# Play: The Next One Pays

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

The Next One Pays.
Two parties arrive at the same dispute wanting different things from it. One of them is here once. This is the worst year of their life, the sum in question is the largest they will ever argue over, and what they need from the court is a number they can live on. The other will be back next month, and the month after, in a hundred matters shaped like this one, and what it needs is not the number but the rule the number leaves behind, because that rule will govern all hundred. Both wants are legitimate and neither is a trick. They are simply not the same want, and things that are not the same can be traded: the side that needs the rule can pay the side that needs the number to go away, and can pay generously, because one case is cheap and the rule is not.

## Arc

The bend is this: a court makes law only in a case that reaches judgment, and whether a case reaches judgment is decided by the parties rather than by the court.
It runs on two people wanting different things from the same afternoon.
The One Who Came Once is right to want the money, and is not being short-sighted in wanting it. They have one case and will never have another, and the rule that comes out of it would be applied, for the rest of their life, to other people. To ask them to refuse a good settlement so that a stranger four years from now meets a better rule is to ask them to fund a public good out of the worst year of their life.
The One Who Comes Back is right to want the rule, and this is where the misfit refuses the easy story, because none of it is misconduct. It holds a hundred matters like this one, and the exposure it manages is aggregate rather than particular. Paying more than a case is worth in order to end it before judgment is a cost it accepts willingly, the money goes to a person with a strong claim, nothing is concealed from the person receiving it, and no rule anywhere is broken.
The One Who Must Advise is obliged. Counsel acts for this client and for no one else. Set to choose between a sum on the table today and the prospect of a published ruling that would help people the client will never meet, counsel is required to recommend the sum, because advising otherwise spends this client's case on somebody else's benefit. So the mechanism is not evaded by professional duty: it is enforced by it.
And The One at the Bench has nothing to decide. A court cannot reach out and take a case, cannot rule on a dispute that has ended, and keeps no list of the matters resolved before it saw them. The cases that would have produced the awkward ruling do not appear in the reports as gaps. They do not appear at all.
So the reports fill with what one side was content to risk. Every judgment in them is honestly reasoned and correctly decided on the facts in front of it: what has been selected is not the reasoning but the population. The disputes that reached a judge are the disputes one party judged safe to let reach a judge, and the ones it judged unsafe were bought out at a premium and are nowhere.
Then The Next One arrives into that. A stranger, years later, with the same complaint, meets a body of law assembled out of transactions they were never party to, with a person they will never identify, in a case that exists on no docket. They are nobody's victim in particular. Nobody in that earlier room acted against them: they were not in it, they were not foreseeable, and every single person who was in it came out better than they went in.
And every correction charges somebody. Refuse to erase a judgment because the parties settled afterwards, which is what the Supreme Court did, and the answer is to settle sooner, before there is a judgment to erase. Ban the confidentiality, and the premium it was buying comes off the claimant's payment, so the bill for the public good falls on the person least able to fund it. Give the one-shotter a representative who does care about rules, a public interest body or a firm that brings these cases by the dozen, and that representative becomes a repeat player in turn, with its own stake in the law and its own reason to trade a particular client's case for it: the asymmetry answered by installing a second copy of it on the other side of the table.

## Triggers

**[A Thousand Times, or Once](plot_a_thousand_times_or_once.md)**
One party is in court for the only time in its life and needs a number. The other will be back next month in a hundred matters and needs the rule. Closes when both wants are legitimate, neither is a trick, and they are found not to be the same want.

**[Reading the File](plot_reading_the_file.md)**
Cue: a docket of similar matters, assessed together rather than one at a time. Each is scored for what a judgment in it would do to the law, not for what it is worth. Closes when the cases are sorted by their likely effect on the rule, with nothing improper done to any of them.

**[A Very Good Offer](plot_a_very_good_offer.md)**
Cue: a case that would probably be lost, and lost publicly. The number offered exceeds what a verdict would likely have given, counsel is required to recommend it, and the client rightly accepts. Closes when the claimant is better off, the payer is satisfied, and the ruling is not made.

**[Nothing to Decide](plot_nothing_to_decide.md)**
Cue: the matter withdrawn, the hearing vacated, the list clear. A court cannot take up a dispute that has ended and holds no register of the ones it never saw. Closes when the removal has left no trace anywhere that a later reader could count.

**[The Next One Arrives](plot_the_next_one_arrives.md)**
Cue: a stranger, years on, with the same complaint. The law they meet was shaped by a transaction they were not party to, with a person they cannot name, in a case that is on no docket. Closes when the cost has landed on someone who was not in the room and could not have been.

**[Nobody to Put It Back](plot_nobody_to_put_it_back.md)**
Cue: the structure understood, and the answers on the table: refuse to erase judgments, forbid the confidentiality, or give the one-shotter an organised representative. Closes when the settling simply moves earlier, or the claimant pays for the public good out of their own award, or the remedy is a second repeat player with the same divergence from its client.

## Stakes

Whether a body of law can be read as a record of what the courts decided, when what reached them was chosen, and paid for, by one of the two sides.
Every plot moves the same way: toward settlements that are lawful, advised, voluntary and better for everyone who signs them, and away from any instrument that could show what was removed, because a case that ends is a case with no file. The production cannot make a court rule on a dispute that no longer exists, and it cannot ask a person in the worst year of their life to spend it on strangers. What is left to fight over is not whether to settle, everybody should settle, but who pays for a rule that was never made, and whether a system can owe anything at all to a person who has not arrived yet.
