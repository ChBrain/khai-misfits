# Right in Half of Them: Reference

The research warrant for Right in Half of Them.
The term is the house's; the play runs on sexual antagonism at shared loci, and on what happens to a population whose
two sexes are selected in opposite directions through one genome, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging sexual antagonism as **one set of instructions read in two bodies that need opposite answers**: males and
females of a species carry very nearly the same genome and do not need the same things out of it, so a variant that
raises the offspring count of one sex can lower the offspring count of the other, and because the locus is shared it is
expressed in both regardless. The play models the documented mechanics, the locus that carries no label for the sex it
will be read in, the two optima that are different numbers, the juvenile stage at which the sexes agree and the
conflict is measurably absent, the wild pedigree in which a father's fitness runs opposite to his daughter's, the
compromise the population settles at, and the sex-limited expression that resolves it slowly and partly, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of one sex exploiting another.

The governing law is that selection acts on individuals while inheritance acts on a genome the sexes hold in common,
so a variant favoured in one is imposed on the other, and the population settles where neither sex is where it should
be.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The allele does
one thing consistently. The favoured sex competes and wins, taking nothing from the other. The burdened sex lives and
breeds and transmits, correctly. Selection acts properly in both directions.

**And there is no antagonist at all, which the play establishes before anything else.** Nothing here is competing with
anything, nothing is exploiting anything, and nothing is aware of the other side. **This is not an arms race and there
is no escalation in it.** Every quantity in the play would be exactly where it is if neither sex had any capacity to
respond to the other, because the two pressures do not act on each other: they act on the same locus.

**Then the conflict has a start, and the measurement locates it.** In fruit flies, forty haploid genomes were cloned
and their Darwinian fitness read in both sexes. As juveniles, when the two sexes want the same thing, survival
correlated **strongly and positively** across them. As adults, when they do not, reproductive success correlated
**strongly and negatively**. So a shared genome is present in full at both stages and the conflict is present at only
one: sharing alone is not sufficient, and what switches it on is the divergence of what the two bodies are for.

**And it is not a laboratory artefact.** In a red deer population on an island, followed across decades with parentage
and lifetime offspring counts recorded individually, the intersex genetic correlation for lifetime fitness came out at
about minus nought point four eight. **The stags that left the most offspring fathered the daughters that left the
fewest**, so selection ran against males carrying the alleles for high female fitness, in a long-lived wild animal
under no experimental manipulation whatever.

**Then the population settles where neither sex is right.** The trait value rests where the two pulls cancel, weighted
by how hard each pulls, and is at neither optimum under any circumstance. Calling that a compromise invites the wrong
reading: nothing was negotiated and nothing conceded, because no party is present. It is the resting point of two
forces, and it cannot be improved, because moving it toward one sex's optimum moves it away from the other's through
the same locus.

**And the one real remedy separates rather than reconciles.** Where expression becomes sex-limited the conflict at that
locus genuinely ends, one sex can approach its optimum without dragging the other away, and the dimorphism that results
is the visible record of a conflict that used to exist. Nothing is settled between the sexes: the sharing is simply
ended at a point, which is the only shape a resolution can take when the difficulty is that the two parties are the
same. Most loci are not sex-limited, the modifiers must arise and spread like any other variant, and work following the
process has found sex-specific adaptation arrested partway rather than run to completion.

It is distinct from the house's **Not All Hers to Give**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages parent-offspring
conflict, where mother and offspring are selected to disagree over how much is transferred and genomic imprinting
carries the tug-of-war. Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating no conflict of any kind between parent and
offspring over resources, no transfer to disagree about and no imprinting anywhere, and this misfit stands whole:
**the parties here are the two sexes of one generation and nothing is being transferred between them.** That play needs
an asymmetry of relatedness across a provisioning relationship; this one needs only that one locus is read in two
bodies whose best trait values differ, which binds in a species with no parental care at all.

It is distinct from the house's **The Fire That Feeds You**, which stages the rate-of-living and disposable-soma
account of ageing and carries antagonistic pleiotropy, where an allele good early in life is bad later in the same
body. Remove that engine, by stipulating that no allele's effect depends on age in any way, and this stands entire,
because **the trade here is between two bodies at the same age**, not between two ages in one body. The two are the
same shape seen along different axes, and the difference is decisive: an individual bears the whole of an
antagonistically pleiotropic allele's cost itself, and bears none of a sexually antagonistic allele's cost when it is
in the sex the allele suits.

It is distinct from the house's **The Arms Race**, which stages escalation, where each side's move calls forth the
other's and the spending rises without either party gaining. Remove that engine, by stipulating that no party ever
responds to another's move and no escalation of any kind is possible, and this stands unchanged, because **nothing here
responds to anything.** The two selection pressures are not adversaries and are not reacting: they are one quantity
with two correct answers. The escalating version of sexual conflict exists and is a different phenomenon, running
between loci through traits that act on the other sex, and this play stages neither the escalation nor the harm
inflicted.

It is distinct from the house's **The Crowded Middle**, which stages the median outcome of an aggregation rule, where
a position nobody's first choice wins because it is the one that cannot be beaten. Remove that engine, by stipulating
no voters, no preferences and no aggregation rule of any kind, and this stands, because **the compromise here is not
chosen and is not a decision procedure's output.** Selection is not a vote, nothing here is counting anything, and the
balance point is arrived at by two forces cancelling rather than by any rule selecting it.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast either sex as exploiting
  the other or the allele as selfish, because nothing in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the allele is a
  sequence with an effect, no preference and no capacity to know which body it is in; the favoured sex competes with
  rivals of its own sex and takes nothing from the other, and its success is simply leaving more descendants; the
  burdened sex is not less able but built to a specification set by selection elsewhere, and transmits the variant
  onward because it benefits its own sons, so even that is not an error available for correction; the juvenile stage
  wants what the other sex's juveniles want; the compromise is a resting point of two forces rather than an agreement
  between parties; and sex-limited expression, where it arises, resolves the conflict properly. The failure is authored
  by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that one quantity has two correct answers and one setting.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Sex-limited expression genuinely works
  and reaches one locus at a time on evolutionary time, while the load is borne in full every generation. Stronger
  selection on either sex moves the balance point toward that sex and away from the other by the same act, so it
  relieves nothing in aggregate. Sex chromosomes carry only a small part of the genome and leave the autosomal case
  untouched. Dimorphism, where it has appeared, marks conflicts already resolved and says nothing about the ones that
  have not been. And nothing an individual does at any point in its life bears on any of it, since the quantity exists
  only as a property of a population measured across generations.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the experimental demonstration is Rice's, the ontogenetic location of the
  conflict is Chippindale, Gibson and Rice's, the wild pedigree result is Foerster and colleagues' on the Isle of Rum
  population, and the review framing is Bonduriansky and Chenoweth's. The bounds are carried with them: **how far
  intralocus conflict is a transient state on the way to dimorphism rather than a standing load is genuinely
  contested**, and the play carries that argument rather than settling it; the measured cases are one laboratory
  species and one wild population and are offered as the documented shape of the effect rather than as constants, and a
  correlation estimated in one population need not hold in another; genetic correlations are estimates with intervals
  around them and the play treats the sign and the direction as the finding rather than any particular value; the
  account treats a shared trait as one quantity where real traits are many and partly separable, which is its own
  simplification; and interlocus sexual conflict, the antagonistic coevolution between the sexes, is a different
  phenomenon that this play does not stage. **The play makes no claim about any species, any population or any
  organism, and gives no biological, medical or behavioural advice of any kind, and nothing in it concerns or applies
  to people**, only that a shared locus carries no label for the body it is read in, and that a population selected in
  two directions through one genome settles at neither.
