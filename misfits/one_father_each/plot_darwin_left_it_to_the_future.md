# Plot: Darwin Left It to the Future

## Taxonomy

[One Father Each](play_one_father_each.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The question of why the sexes are produced in near-equal numbers across so much of life.
An argument for it, published in 1871.

## Action

[Balancing](process_balancing.md), not yet identified: the fact is in plain view and its cause is not.

## Stage

Darwin argues in the first edition of the Descent of Man that where one sex is in excess more of that sex fail to mate, so parents producing the scarcer sex have the more productive offspring.
In the second edition, in 1874, he takes it out, conceding that the problem is so intricate that it is safer to leave its solution to the future.

## Tension

The person who posed it gave it up, and that is the plot's whole function.
It is worth establishing before anything else that this is not obvious, because everything after it is arithmetic so plain that the finished argument reads as though it needed no discovering. It did. Carl Düsing of Jena set out the mathematics in 1884 and it was lost from view; Fisher restated it in 1930 without citing anybody, and the principle carries his name. **The credit is owed further back than the name suggests**, which is the house's own standard applied to its own source.
