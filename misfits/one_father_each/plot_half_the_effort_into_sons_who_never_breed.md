## Cue

A species in which a few males take nearly all the matings.

## Action

[Allocating](process_allocating.md) meeting its outcome: [The Son Who Never Breeds](persona_the_son_who_never_breeds.md), in [the unreturned seat](position_unreturned.md), is grown at full cost and leaves nothing.

## Stage

[The Son Who Never Breeds](persona_the_son_who_never_breeds.md), on [Wait for a Mating That Does Not Come](plan_wait_for_a_mating_that_does_not_come.md), competes and does not breed, which is the expected outcome here rather than a failure.
[The Better Ratio](persona_the_better_ratio.md), in [the unreachable seat](position_unreachable.md), would remove most of that cost and cannot be held.

## Tension

Extreme inequality among males does not make sons a worse bet, and that is the counterintuitive core of it.
The identity fixes the male **average**, so no amount of variance touches it: a population where four in a hundred males do all the breeding gives those four twenty-five times the average, and the average is unchanged. So the investment in the ninety-six is correct in expectation and returns nothing in fact, simultaneously, and half the population's reproductive effort goes into them. **A female-biased population would produce far more offspring per generation**, and the better that alternative is, the more a son is worth in it, so it is not somewhere anything can stay.
