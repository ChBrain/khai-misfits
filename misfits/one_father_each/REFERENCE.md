# One Father Each: Reference

The research warrant for One Father Each.
The term is the house's; the play runs on sex-ratio theory, and on an accounting identity that fixes a population's
allocation between the sexes at a level that is not the level producing the most offspring, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the sex ratio as **an allocation fixed from outside biology**: every offspring in the next generation has
exactly one mother and exactly one father, so the total reproductive output of all males equals the total of all
females, always, in every species and under every mating system. That is arithmetic and not adaptation. It follows
immediately that the average return per individual is inversely proportional to how many of that sex there are, so
whichever sex is rarer is worth more to produce, so it stops being rare. The play models the documented mechanics, the
identity that cannot be broken, the premium scarcity confers with nobody quoting it, the parent spending a finite
budget where the return is higher, the sons grown at full cost who leave nothing, the better ratio that is unstable in
proportion to how good it is, and the patch where the assumption fails and the prediction holds, as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a wasteful species or a failure to cooperate.

The governing law is that an accounting identity nobody can break fixes the population at equal investment in the two
sexes, and equal investment is not the allocation that produces the most offspring.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The identity
balances. The rarer sex returns more without being better. The parent spends where the return is higher. The son who
never breeds does nothing wrong. There is exactly one decision in the play and it is made correctly every time.

**And the problem is genuinely hard, which the play establishes before anything else.** Darwin set the argument out in
the first edition of the Descent of Man in 1871 and **took it out of the second edition in 1874**, conceding that the
problem was so intricate it was safer to leave its solution to the future. Carl Düsing of Jena gave the mathematics in
1884 and it was lost from view. Fisher restated it in 1930 without citation, and the principle carries his name. The
finished argument reads as though it needed no discovering, and the record says otherwise.

**Then the cause sits outside biology.** A fact of counting cannot be selected on, adapted around or escaped, so what
follows from it follows for every sexual species with no exceptions and nothing anywhere to blame. And the consequence
does not look arithmetical at all: a population at nine females to one male gives each male nine times the average
return of a female. **Nobody set that price and every parent can read it**, from the ratio alone, with no
communication, no information about anybody else and no intent.

**And the cost is large.** In a species where a few males take nearly all the matings, most sons are conceived,
carried, fed and grown to independence, and leave nothing. Half the population's reproductive investment goes into
them regardless, because the identity fixes the male **average** and variance does not move an average: a population
where four males in a hundred do all the breeding gives those four twenty-five times the mean, and the mean is
unchanged. So the investment is correct in expectation and returns nothing in fact, at once.

**Then the better outcome is not a cooperation that failed.** A female-biased population would produce considerably
more offspring per generation. It cannot be held, and the reason is not defection: suppose every parent agreed to
produce daughters only and kept the agreement perfectly, and the arrangement still fails, because one individual doing
otherwise is not cheating, is not gaining at anybody's expense, and is simply making the offspring that returns more.
**The better the ratio is for the population, the more a son is worth in it**, so the quality of the outcome and the
strength of the force leaving it are one quantity.

**And the one real exception arrives as a structure rather than a decision.** Where a population breaks into patches
and the offspring of one or a few mothers mate with each other before the females disperse, a son's rivals are his own
brothers, so an extra son takes matings from his mother's other sons and the return on him collapses. The favoured
clutch is then strongly female-biased, its bias tracking how few mothers share the patch. This was predicted before it
was measured and is what the wasps living that way lay, which makes the exception the argument's own test rather than
an escape from it.

It is distinct from the house's **The Stag Hunt**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages the assurance game, where
a better outcome is available to everybody and is reached only if each party trusts the others to go for it. Remove
that engine entirely, by stipulating perfect trust, perfect communication and a universal agreement kept without
exception, and this misfit stands whole: **the better outcome here is not an equilibrium at all.** There the good
outcome is stable once reached and the difficulty is reaching it; here reaching it is trivial and holding it is
impossible, because a single individual doing otherwise is not defecting, is not gaining at anybody's expense, and
prospers anyway. Nothing that repairs cooperation touches this, since there is no promise in it to keep.

It is distinct from the house's **The Commons**, which stages the tragedy of the commons, where a shared resource is
drawn down because each user takes the whole benefit and bears a share of the cost. Remove that engine, by stipulating
no shared resource, nothing depletable and no externality of use, and this stands entire, because **nothing here is
being consumed**. Each parent spends its own reproductive budget on its own offspring and takes nothing from anybody,
and what the population loses is a composition rather than a stock: the mixture of offspring is wrong, and the mixture
would still be wrong in a world of unlimited resources.

It is distinct from the house's **Right in Half of Them**, which stages intralocus sexual conflict, where one shared
locus is selected in opposite directions in the two sexes and the population sits displaced from both optima. Remove
that engine, by stipulating that no allele has opposite fitness effects in the two sexes and that the sexes agree at
every locus, and this stands, because **the question here is how many of each sex are made**, not what any allele does
once it is in one. That play needs a trait; this one needs only a count, and it binds in a species whose two sexes are
identical in every respect except which they are.

It is distinct from the house's **Hotelling's Law**, which stages minimum differentiation, where competitors converge
on the same position because it is the one that cannot be beaten and variety is what is lost. Remove that engine, by
stipulating no competitors, no positions and no customers to capture, and this stands, since **there is nothing here
to converge on and nobody converging.** The parents are not positioning against each other, and the equilibrium is not
a standoff between rivals but the resting point of a price generated by an accounting identity.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast any parent as short
  sighted or any species as wasteful, because there is exactly one decision in the sequence and it is correct every
  time: the identity balances without being kept by anybody and has no interest of any kind; the rarer sex returns more
  without being better in any respect, and loses the premium by taking it; the parent spends a finite budget where the
  return is higher, which is what every organism does with limited resources, and no single allocation moves the ratio
  perceptibly so none could reasonably be made otherwise; the son who never breeds is healthy, fully grown and getting
  the expected outcome for a male in his mating system rather than failing at anything; and the wasp in the patch is
  not cleverer than anybody, only differently situated. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that an accounting identity prices out the allocation that would produce most.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Agreement does not help, since the
  better ratio is unstable against an individual who is not cheating and gains nothing at anybody's expense.
  Enforcement has nothing to enforce against. Producing the better ratio deliberately is exactly what makes producing
  sons most profitable, so the remedy is its own undoing and is more so the better it works. Local mate competition
  genuinely delivers the female-biased ratio and delivers it only to species that already live in patches, being a
  consequence of structure rather than a strategy anything can adopt. And knowing the whole argument, which has been
  available since 1884, changes nothing for any parent, because the ratio it would have to act against is the one
  making its own allocation correct.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the argument is Darwin's and then Düsing's, the recovery of Düsing's priority is
  Edwards's, the investment refinement and the name are Fisher's, and the local mate competition result is Hamilton's.
  The bounds are carried with them: **how closely observed sex ratios match the quantitative predictions is genuinely
  contested**, and the play carries that argument rather than settling it; sex-determining mechanisms differ across
  taxa and many species have limited control over the ratio at all, so the account is offered as the shape of the force
  rather than as a claim about any organism's ability to respond to it; the polygynous case is used because the cost is
  starkest there and is not representative of every mating system; the account treats reproductive investment as one
  quantity where it is many, which is its own simplification; and the exception is stated for structured populations
  with local mating and is not a general escape. **The play makes no claim about any species, any population or any
  organism, and gives no biological, medical or demographic advice of any kind, and nothing in it concerns or applies
  to people**, only that every offspring has one parent of each sex, and that this alone fixes an allocation nobody
  chose.
