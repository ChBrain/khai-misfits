# Plot: Unless They Mate Where They Hatched

## Taxonomy

[One Father Each](play_one_father_each.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

A population that is not a population: the offspring of one or a few mothers on a single host, mating with each other before the females disperse.
[The Broken Assumption](piece_the_broken_assumption.md), predicted before it was measured.

## Action

[Structuring](process_structuring.md): [The Wasp on the Fig](persona_the_wasp_on_the_fig.md), in [the structured seat](position_structured.md), lays a clutch the rest of the play cannot.

## Stage

[The Wasp on the Fig](persona_the_wasp_on_the_fig.md), on [Mate Where You Hatched](plan_mate_where_you_hatched.md), lays mostly daughters and just enough sons to fertilise them, and sends the daughters out to found patches of their own.
The bias tracks how few mothers share the patch, which is the quantitative form of the prediction.

## Tension

The exception is not an escape from the argument, it is the argument's own test, and it passes.
Nothing here breaks the identity. What breaks is the assumption that a son competes with the population: his rivals are his brothers, so an extra son takes matings from his mother's other sons and the return on him collapses. The theory said this before anybody measured it, and the wasps that live this way lay the clutches it specifies. **The one route to the good ratio is not a decision** and cannot be adopted: it is available to whatever already lives in patches, and to nothing else. Everything that mixes goes back to equal investment, and pays for it every generation.
