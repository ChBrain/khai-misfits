# Plot: Whichever Is Rarer Is Worth More

## Taxonomy

[One Father Each](play_one_father_each.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

A population away from equal investment, and the premium that follows automatically.
[The Rare-Sex Premium](piece_the_rare_sex_premium.md), quoted by nobody.

## Action

[Pricing](process_pricing.md) and [allocating](process_allocating.md): [The Rare Sex](persona_the_rare_sex.md) in [the premium seat](position_premium.md) becomes the better thing for [The Parent](persona_the_parent.md), in [the allocator seat](position_allocator.md), to produce.

## Stage

[The Rare Sex](persona_the_rare_sex.md), on [Be Worth More for Being Fewer](plan_be_worth_more_for_being_fewer.md), averages more offspring per head in exact proportion to its scarcity, without being better in any respect.
[The Parent](persona_the_parent.md), on [Put It Where It Returns Most](plan_put_it_where_it_returns_most.md), spends where the return is higher and leaves more grandchildren for it.

## Tension

The restoring force needs no coordination, no information and no intent.
There is one decision in this whole play and it is made correctly every time: a parent with a finite amount to spend puts it where the return is higher. Nothing else is required. No parent knows the population ratio, communicates with any other, or intends anything about the aggregate, and the aggregate arrives regardless. Fisher's refinement is what makes it exact: the balance is struck in **investment at the age of independence** rather than in numbers, so a sex that is cheaper to rear is produced in larger numbers and the two still cost the same in total.
