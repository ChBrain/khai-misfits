# Play: One Father Each

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

One Father Each.
Every offspring in the next generation has exactly one mother and exactly one father. That is arithmetic and not biology, and it has a consequence nobody would guess from it: the total reproductive output of all the males in a generation is exactly equal to the total reproductive output of all the females, always, in every species, whatever the mating system.
So if one sex is rarer, each individual of that sex averages more offspring, by the same arithmetic. And a parent producing the rarer sex is therefore producing the better-returning offspring, so the rarer sex stops being rare.

## Arc

The bend is this: an accounting identity nobody can break fixes the population's allocation between the sexes at equal investment, and equal investment is not the allocation that produces the most offspring.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: every offspring has one parent of each sex, and a parent has a finite amount to spend.
Take the arithmetic first. Because the two sexes' total output is identical by construction, the **average** return per individual is inversely proportional to how many there are. A population at nine females to one male gives each male nine times the average return of a female, so a parent that makes sons is making offspring worth nine times as much. That parent leaves more grandchildren. The ratio moves back.
Then take what that costs. In a species where a few males do nearly all the breeding, most sons return nothing at all: they are made, fed, grown to independence, and leave no offspring. Half the population's reproductive investment goes into them anyway. **A female-biased population would produce more offspring per generation than a balanced one**, straightforwardly and by a lot, and it cannot get there.
And this is the part that is not a coordination problem. It is not that parents would cooperate if only they could trust one another. Suppose every parent in the population agreed to produce daughters only, and kept the agreement perfectly: the agreement is not stable against one individual that does otherwise, and that individual is not cheating, it is not gaining at anybody's expense, it is simply making the offspring that returns more. **The good ratio is unreachable precisely because it is good**, since the better it is for the population the more a son is worth.
The history is honest about how hard this is to see. Darwin set the argument out in 1871 and **withdrew it in 1874**, saying the problem was so intricate it was safer to leave its solution to the future. Carl Düsing of Jena gave the mathematics in 1884 and it was lost from view. Fisher restated it in 1930 without citation, and added the thing that makes it exact: it is equal **investment** rather than equal numbers, measured at the age of independence, so a sex that is cheaper to make is made in larger numbers.
Reach for a settlement and there is one, and it is not available on request. Where a population breaks into small patches and the offspring of one or a few mothers mate with each other before the females disperse, the sons are competing with their own brothers and the arithmetic changes: the ratio goes strongly female-biased, exactly as predicted, and it does so in the wasps that live that way. The remedy is not a decision. It is a different population structure.

## Stakes

Whether a population can produce the mixture of offspring that would grow it fastest.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether any parent is allocating badly, because each is spending where the return is highest and there is no decision in the play to disapprove of, but whether an outcome that is better for everybody can be held when the arithmetic prices it out. The production cannot find a way. It can only show that the identity is arithmetic and cannot be broken, that it makes the rarer sex worth more per head automatically and with nobody arranging it, that half the reproductive investment therefore goes into sons who mostly return nothing, that the better ratio is not a cooperation anybody could agree to because it is unstable against a single individual who is not cheating, and that the one real exception arrives not as a decision but as a population that has stopped mixing.
