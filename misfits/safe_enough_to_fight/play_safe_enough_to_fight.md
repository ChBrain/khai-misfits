# Play: Safe Enough to Fight

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Safe Enough to Fight.
Two states acquire the means to destroy each other, and the largest war between them becomes unthinkable, and stays unthinkable for decades. That is not a trick. It works.
Then a border district changes hands, and a column crosses a line of control, and a proxy war runs for eleven years in a third country.
Nobody reaches for the ceiling, because nobody would end the world over a district, and both sides know it, and that is exactly why the district was worth taking.

## Arc

The bend is this: the ceiling that makes the largest war impossible is what makes the largest threat unusable, and a threat that cannot be used for a small thing does not deter small things.

It runs on one relation from strategy and one from ordinary judgement. **The ceiling holds**, so a war that could once be won cannot be, and no rational government begins one; and **a threat is deterrent only in so far as it is believed**, and belief tracks proportion, so a response out of all measure with a provocation is discounted however sincerely it is issued.

Take the ceiling first, because the play has to establish that it is an achievement. It was built by people trying to prevent a war and it prevented one. Decades pass with no war between the principals, which is the outcome the whole apparatus was for, and nothing in this play disputes that it was delivered.

Then the fact that inverts it. **The deterrent is present at every rung and effective at only one.** This is not a gap in coverage and cannot be closed by readiness, range or resolve. The weapons exist, and could be used for any provocation whatever, and the reason they will not be used over a border district is that nobody would want to, which is visible to everybody. So the ground beneath the ceiling is not merely uncovered. It has been **cleared**, by the same fact that raised the ceiling.

Then the sharper half, which is why the usual answers do not reach it. **Everybody is calculating correctly.** The claimant pressing a grievance of long standing reads accurately what will not be answered. The government declining to escalate is refusing to trade its own population for a district, which is the only defensible thing it can do. Neither is being reckless, and the recklessness is what the two correct calculations produce together.

And the harm settles somewhere else again. The ceiling protects the two parties who built it. The fighting happens on a line of control, in a border province, or in a country that joined neither calculation, so the people whose safety was bought and the people who pay for it are not the same people.

Reach for a settlement and each one gives back the thing that was wanted. Build credible intermediate options, which is the answer the strategy literature actually gave, and you have restored the usable violence the ceiling removed, because every rung added to the ladder is a rung somebody can climb. Rearm conventionally, and you have bought reach at the low end at permanent cost, and the other side reads it as preparation. Declare that any incursion at all will be met at full strength, which is free, and you have issued a statement nobody believes. Settle the underlying claim, which is the real remedy, and you have proposed the thing the parties have been failing at for as long as the claim has existed.

## Triggers

**[The Big War Becomes Impossible](plot_the_big_war_becomes_impossible.md)**
The ceiling established as a genuine achievement: two states able to destroy each other, a war that could once be won that cannot be, and decades without one. Closes when it is clear that this worked, that it was built by people trying to prevent exactly this, and that nothing in the production disputes it.

**[And So Does the Big Answer](plot_and_so_does_the_big_answer.md)**
Cue: the same fact, read from the other side. Closes when it is shown that the response is present at every rung and effective at one, that this cannot be repaired by readiness or resolve, and that the disproportion is visible to everybody rather than hidden from anybody.

**[Something Small, and Real](plot_something_small_and_real.md)**
Cue: a claim of long standing with no other route. Closes when the action below the line has been taken, when the reading of what will not be answered is seen to be accurate, and when the claimant is understood as calculating rather than gambling.

**[Not Over This](plot_not_over_this.md)**
Cue: the cabinet, weighing it. Closes when the government has declined to escalate, when that is understood as the only defensible choice rather than as weakness, and when both sides are seen to have been right at the same time.

**[And Somebody Has to Live There](plot_and_somebody_has_to_live_there.md)**
Cue: the theatre, and the settlements. Closes when the cost is located on parties to neither calculation, and when each remedy is found to give back what it was for: a ladder whose new rungs can be climbed, a conventional build-up read as preparation, a declaration nobody believes, and a settlement the parties have already failed at.

## Stakes

Whether a protection can be judged by what it prevents.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the ceiling works, because it does and has, and not whether anybody behaved badly, because each party calculated correctly on a real interest, but whether a shield that removes the worst outcome can be counted a success when it is also what clears the ground beneath it. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the ceiling holds, that the same fact makes the answer unusable below it, that the claimant and the government were both right, that the cost fell on neither of them, and that every remedy either restores the violence or is not believed.
