# You Have to Know It to Learn It: Reference

The research warrant for You Have to Know It to Learn It.
The term is the house's; the play runs on organisational learning and on why knowledge that is freely available is
not freely usable, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging acquisition as **a transaction whose precondition is the thing being acquired**: knowledge from outside is
abundant, published, licensable and often cheap, and an organisation that is behind can buy it. What it cannot buy
is the capacity to recognise the value of that knowledge, assimilate it and apply it, because that capacity is
largely a function of related knowledge the organisation already holds, and that knowledge is a by-product of
having done the work itself. Research produces two things: the knowledge it was funded to produce, and the
ability to use knowledge produced by everybody else. Nobody funds it for the second, no supplier sells it, and
the organisation that has it did not buy it. The play models the documented mechanics, the abundance that is real,
the capacity that rests on what the reader brought, the by-product nobody names, the decision to buy that any
adviser would endorse, the delivery that omits nothing, and the bench where the process will not run, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a supplier withholding or a recipient refusing to learn.

The governing law is that the capacity to absorb outside knowledge is a by-product of producing knowledge
yourself, so the organisation that most needs to buy is the one least able to use what it buys.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The supplier
transfers everything transferable. The buyer makes the decision any adviser would endorse. The engineer reads the
document correctly. The funder measures what can be measured. The incumbent receives nothing anybody granted.

What removes any possibility of blame from the buyer is that building instead means funding years of research to
arrive where competitors already are, on a budget that does not stretch to it, while those competitors keep
moving. The decision is not short-termism and not a failure of ambition: it is the decision that would be
defended by anybody assessing it at the time. It is simply the decision that does not produce the by-product, and
the by-product is what would have let the purchase work.

What removes it from the supplier is that a failed transfer costs it the follow-on work, the reference and the
reputation, so it has every commercial reason to withhold nothing and withholds nothing. What it cannot ship is
the accumulated related work that lets its own engineers glance at a procedure and know which line is the one
that bites. That is not a secret being kept and not tacit knowledge somebody declines to write down. It is not the
kind of thing a document carries, because it belongs to the reader rather than the text.

**And the failure is located in neither the package nor the reading, which is where the structure becomes
concrete.** The documentation is complete and accurate. The engineer is competent, follows the procedure, and can
reproduce and explain every step. What is missing is the weighting that would make one of a hundred plausible next
moves obviously the right one. There is no third object in the room to point at, which is why the account that
forms afterwards is about people.

**Then the evaluation records the only thing that has a date.** A transfer is an event with a cost and a
signature and can be verified; absorption is a process with no completion and no observable moment, and asking for
evidence of it at handover asks for something that does not exist yet. So a framework that counts deliveries is
being honest, and a project in which everything was delivered and nothing was absorbed is recorded as a success.

It is distinct from the house's **Not-Invented-Here Syndrome**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages the
rejection of external ideas on grounds of ownership and identity, so its engine is **unwillingness**. Remove that
engine, by stipulating an organisation that wants every external idea it can get and has no attachment to its own,
and this misfit stands entire: the buyer here is actively purchasing outside knowledge and cannot use it. The two
are opposites that look identical from outside, which is the play's closing move: an organisation that **cannot**
use what it was given is indistinguishable, at a distance, from one that **would not**.

It is distinct from the house's **The Success Trap**, which stages the competency trap, where favourable
experience with an inferior routine locks an organisation into it and forecloses the search that would find
better. Remove that engine, by stipulating an organisation with no favourable experience and no routine it is
attached to, and this stands: the buyer has no incumbent practice to be trapped by, is searching rather than
exploiting, and the difficulty is that the search returns things it cannot read.

It is distinct from the house's **The Curse of Knowledge**, where an expert cannot model what a novice does not
know and communicates past them. Nothing here turns on how the supplier explains: the documentation is accurate
and is understood sentence by sentence, and what is missing is not in the explanation and could not be added to it.

It runs against two of the house's plays on this axis, **The Good Move** and **Second Nature**, on the same
quantity and with the opposite sign. There the solver whose first answer works stops searching and never sees
the better one in plain sight, and the expert who cannot picture not knowing explains over the heads of the
people who do not. Both are harms of **having** the prior knowledge this play is short of.

**What sets the sign on this axis is what the knowledge is being asked to do.** Where the task is to take
something in from outside, evaluating a licence, absorbing a partner's research, judging a consultant's
report, prior knowledge is the receiver and its absence is the harm: there is nothing for the new thing to
land on. Where the task is to get **past** what is already held, finding a better solution or picturing not
knowing, the same knowledge is what forecloses, and its presence is the harm. Same dial, opposite sign, and
the discriminator is whether the knowledge is being used to receive or to be exceeded.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the supplier as
  withholding or the recipient as unwilling, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  knowledge really is available and the obstacle is not on the supply side; the supplier's commercial interest
  runs entirely toward a successful transfer and it ships everything shippable; the buyer's decision to purchase
  is the one any adviser would endorse against a budget that will not fund years of research; the engineer
  understands the document and can reproduce every step; the missing weighting is not omitted from the text
  because it is not the kind of thing a text holds; the funder measures the only quantity that has a date and a
  signature; and the incumbent received nothing anybody granted. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges
  from the structure, so the misfit is that the precondition for acquiring the capability is the capability.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Better documentation cannot
  supply weighting, which lives in the reader. More training transfers procedures rather than the surrounding
  knowledge that ranks them. Hiring people who have it imports the capacity into individuals rather than the
  organisation, and it leaves when they do. Partnership requires enough capacity to be a partner rather than a
  customer, which is the quantity in question. Consultants deliver a report the organisation cannot evaluate.
  Doing the research is the actual answer and is exactly what the budget was too small for, which is why buying
  was chosen. And knowing the whole structure, which the field does and has documented since 1990, leaves a
  board with a gap, a competitor moving, and a licence available now.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the absorptive-capacity framework and the organisational-learning
  literature are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether absorptive capacity can be constructed
  deliberately rather than only accumulated is genuinely contested**, and the play carries that argument rather
  than settling it; the account treats firms, public bodies and national technology-transfer programmes as one
  structure, which is its own simplification, since the constraints and timescales differ sharply between them;
  the play does not claim that transfers generally fail, only that when they fail this way the failure is
  located where no party can reach it; and the size of the effect in any particular sector is not established
  here. **The play makes no claim about any organisation, any transfer, any funder or any development
  programme, and gives no advice of any kind**, only that the capacity to use what you buy is a residue of work
  you did not do.
