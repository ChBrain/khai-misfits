# Good Somewhere, Bad Somewhere: Reference

The research warrant for Good Somewhere, Bad Somewhere.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the attractiveness principle from system dynamics, named by
Forrester and set out in his urban work, with its contested reception carried alongside it, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the attractiveness principle as an **equilibrating system**: where people are free to arrive and to
leave, a place or an offering is held at a level of overall attractiveness by movement itself, so improving
one dimension does not raise the total but redistributes the shortfall onto another. The play models the
documented mechanics, the several limits of genuinely different character that cannot be relieved by one
instrument or relieved at all, the improvement made on one of them in good faith, the arrival that follows
because the place is now better, the pressure that arrival puts on every dimension nobody touched, the
settling back to the standing of attractiveness that held before, and the choice nobody wants to make
explicit, which of the dimensions to be poor at, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a
tale of neglect.

The governing law is Forrester's: the only way to control growth is to control attractiveness. Where entry
is open, the sum is held constant by the flow of people, so an improvement is a transfer between axes and
not an addition to the whole. A place cannot be good at everything, and an offering cannot be all things to
all people. Declining to choose which dimension will be poor does not avoid the cost; it distributes it, so
the refusal to be deliberately weak somewhere produces a thing that is weak everywhere.

It is distinct from the house's **Limits to Growth**, of which this is the multi-limit case and where the
distinction sits: there one balancing limit is approached and the system settles against it, and relieving
it lets growth resume. Here the limits are several and unlike, relieving one hands the load to the next,
and the level of attractiveness rather than the level of growth is what the system defends.

It is distinct from the house's **The Brittle Optimum**, where slack is trimmed on one axis and resilience
is lost as an unsought by-product: there a hidden cost is discovered later. Here the cost is not hidden and
not deferred, it is immediate and it is the point, and the question is only where to put it.

It is distinct from the house's **The Commons**, where a shared stock is drawn down by use until it is
gone: here nothing depletes, the arrivals are not consuming a resource to exhaustion, and the system
returns to the same level rather than running to the floor.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the improver
  as naive or the newcomer as a burden, because the source finding is precisely that both are acting well:
  relieving a real shortage is a good act and would be the right one in a closed system, and a person who
  moves toward a better place is doing exactly what a person should do with a free choice. The settling back
  is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, an open boundary and a level that movement defends,
  so the misfit is that a genuine improvement is converted into arrivals rather than into betterment, and
  the ledger closes where it opened.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The correction is to
  choose, in the open, which dimension will be the poor one and to hold it there deliberately, which means
  defending a visible deficiency that any critic can point at and that no one can be thanked for. The
  alternative, refusing to choose, is not neutrality: it spreads the shortfall across every dimension and
  produces a place that is mediocre at all of them, which is the same cost paid in a form nobody has to
  own.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the principle, the naming and the urban model are
  Forrester's. **The contested edge is carried loudly rather than smoothed, because it is not merely
  technical.** Forrester's policy conclusions, in particular the reading that programmes meant to help the
  worst off made the city worse, were disputed on their modelling at the time and have been used since to
  argue against helping people. The play stages the mechanism only, that attractiveness equilibrates where
  entry is open, and takes no part in the policy claim: a mechanism that explains why a relief does not hold
  is not an argument against relieving, and this warrant does not carry it as one. Any staging that reads as
  a case against provision has left the mechanism and is wrong on the house's own terms.
