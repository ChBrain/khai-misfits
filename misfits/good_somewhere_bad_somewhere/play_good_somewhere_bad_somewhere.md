# Play: Good Somewhere, Bad Somewhere

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Good Somewhere, Bad Somewhere.
A diner is loved for four things at once: room enough to sit without waiting, a kitchen fast enough that the plate is still hot, quiet enough to hear the person across the table, and a door easy enough to walk through without booking ahead. It cannot, in the end, be praised for all four at once. Fix the wait and the room fills, fill the room and the kitchen falls behind, slow the kitchen and the quiet goes with it, and the diners who came for the fix are the reason the rest went missing. Nobody ordered the tradeoff. It arrives on its own, every time one gauge gets better, because there are only so many diners in a given season, and they go where the going is good until going there is no better than staying where they were.

## Arc

The bend is this: every gauge a diner values is real, and relieving one for a good reason is exactly what draws the diners who load the rest.
It runs on a total that a free door will not let rise.
Room, Speed, Quiet, and Ease are not the same kind of good: one is space, one is a clock, one is silence, one is a door standing open, and no single fix reaches more than one of them. When the Keeper adds tables to end a real wait, the fix is real too: for one night, the room is roomier and nothing else has moved.
But an easier table is worth mentioning to a friend, and the friend tells another, so the diners who come are drawn by exactly the ease the fix bought. The room fills again, this time with more of everybody, and what the fix gave to Room, the same total draw takes back from Speed and from Quiet: the level held across four gauges instead of raised on one. The dining room is bigger and stands exactly where it stood, worth the same trip it was always worth, trading one shortage for another instead of curing either.
None of it is a mistake. Building the extra tables is a fair answer to a real wait, coming for an easier table is exactly what a free diner should do, and neither the Keeper nor the Newcomer has done anything the other could fault. Attractiveness is a level, not a ledger with room to grow: whatever draws more diners in on one count is drawn down on the counts nobody touched, because the diners who come are the same diners who fill the room, slow the kitchen, and raise the noise.
Try to be roomy and fast and quiet and easy all at once, refusing to choose, and the four gauges settle just short of good everywhere, no single failing large enough to name and none of them fixed. The only way out is to choose, in the open, which one stays short on purpose: chosen and held, rather than left to whichever gauge loses last.

## Triggers

**[The Extra Table](plot_the_extra_table.md)**
The Keeper reckons a real wait can be fixed by building more room, and does: the fix buys real relief, for now. Closes when the room is bigger and the wait, that night, is gone.

**[Word Gets Around](plot_word_gets_around.md)**
Cue: word spreads that the wait is over. The Newcomer, hearing it, comes for exactly the ease the fix bought. Closes when the new tables are as full as the old ones ever were.

**[The Press](plot_the_press.md)**
Cue: the room is fuller than it has ever been, on an ordinary night. The kitchen falls behind the new covers and the room grows loud, though nobody told the cooks to slow down or the diners to talk louder. Closes when Quiet and Speed have given up exactly what Room gained.

**[The Same Wait](plot_the_same_wait.md)**
Cue: a quiet count, after the rush. The rating reads no higher than it read before the fix, and the line is back at the door some nights, though the room behind it is bigger than it was. Closes when it is plain that the total never moved, only what it is made of.

**[Choosing the Poor One](plot_choosing_the_poor_one.md)**
Cue: a second extension sits on paper beside the same numbers that emptied the first one's gain. Choosing instead to hold Room deliberately short, and to say so where every diner can read it, buys back Quiet and Speed at the plain cost of a wait some nights will always show. Closes when the deficiency is named in the open, or left to fall wherever it lands next.

## Stakes

Whether a fix that is real in the room where it is made can ever raise what the whole diner is worth, or only ever moves the shortage to whichever gauge nobody was watching.
Every plot moves the same way: toward the fix, which the Keeper is right to make, and the arrival, which the Newcomer is right to answer, and away from a total that no fix can raise, because a free door holds it level. The production cannot make diners want less of a good table once it is offered; it can only show that the four gauges trade against each other by nature, room against quiet, speed against ease, so a place that will not choose to be short somewhere is a place that will be short everywhere at once, a little, and call it improving. What is left to fight over is not whether to fix the wait, everyone agrees that fixing it is right, but who says out loud which comfort the diner will never quite have, and holds to it once it is said.
