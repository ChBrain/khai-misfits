# Safe to Say: Reference

The research warrant for **Safe to Say** (play and company not yet staged; this warrant is drafted first, to prove the
science and the distinction before authoring). The title is provisional, a reading of the phenomenon named **preference
falsification**: where speaking one's true preference carries a social cost, each person publicly professes not what they
believe but what is safe to say, so the public record diverges from the private truth, every concealer believes their view
more isolated than it is, an order most privately reject is upheld by the very people who reject it, and a small shock can
tip the concealment into a sudden cascade, all cited below, held with the finding that not every public conformity is a
falsification and the tipping is genuinely unpredictable.

## Line of Work

Staging preference falsification as a **private-truth-public-lie structure**: a person holds a genuine preference, a view,
a wish, a judgement, and in public they must decide not only what to do but what to profess. And where professing the true
preference carries a social cost, a frown, a shunning, a lost job, a prison cell, they profess instead what is safe to say,
so their public stance is chosen not to express what they believe but to escape what honesty would cost them. Timur Kuran
called it preference falsification, and set down the calculus: a person's public stance weighs the reputational payoff of
saying the accepted thing against the inner cost of betraying their own conviction, and where the pressure is strong the
reputational payoff wins, so the private truth is kept and a public lie is told. It is locally rational: to say the safe
thing spares you the sanction, and one dissenting voice would change nothing but its owner's fate. But the same rational
concealment does a compounding harm, because each person, looking out, sees only the safe professions of everyone else, and
so cannot tell how many privately agree with them: each believes their own view more isolated than it is, and, believing
themselves nearly alone, conceals the harder, and may even enforce the accepted line on others to prove their own sincerity
(Willer, Kuwabara and Macy). So an order that most privately reject is upheld, sincerely to all appearances, by the very
people who reject it, and no one can see that the appearance is false. And because the true distribution of preference stays
hidden behind the wall of safe professions, the order is brittle in a way no one can measure: a small shock, a first bold
voice, a crack in the pressure, can make it a little safer to speak, which draws out the next voice and the next, until the
concealment tips into revelation and the whole edifice flips at once, surprising everyone, the rulers and the ruled and the
experts alike, as the revolutions of 1989 surprised a world that had read the public record and believed it (Kuran). It is
authored by no villain: each concealer is only sparing themselves a real cost that honesty would carry, and no one intends
to uphold the order they privately reject. The governing law is not that anyone is a coward, but that **where speaking one's
true preference carries a social cost, each person publicly professes not what they believe but what is safe to say, so the
public record diverges from the private truth and every concealer, seeing only the safe professions of others, believes
their own view more isolated than it is and conceals the harder, so an order most privately reject is upheld by the very
people who reject it, and because the true distribution stays hidden a small shock can tip concealment into revelation and
the whole flip at once, surprising everyone, no villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the private
truth held unspoken (Kuran), the pressure that makes honesty costly, the public lie professed to escape it, the false
consensus each reads off the others and the isolation it breeds (the pluralistic ignorance of Katz and Allport), the cascade
that tips the hidden truth into the open, and the free voice that alone lets the private truth surface without a rupture, as
the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a coward among the brave.

It is a private-truth-public-lie structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest neighbours.
It is distinct from Information Cascade, where each person rationally infers from the visible choices of those before them
and swallows their own private hint, so the herd locks onto the first few guesses: there the public actions are honest, each
truly acting on what the herd's choices taught them, and the failure is one of inference, whereas here the public stance is
a deliberate lie, the true preference actively hidden to escape a sanction, so the public signal is not honest information
but concealment, and the engine is social fear and not a mistaken updating. It is distinct from Not Worth Wanting, adaptive
preference formation, where the wanting itself is unconsciously revised to fit what can be had: there the private preference
truly changes, behind the person's own back, so there is no gap between the inner want and the outer report, whereas here
the private preference is unchanged and intact and only its public expression is falsified, a conscious gap between a truth
still held and a lie professed. And it is distinct from Closing Ranks, groupthink, where a close team genuinely converges on
one view and privately comes to hold it: there the inner belief conforms, the agreement real, whereas here the inner belief
dissents and only the outer profession conforms, the consensus a surface over a hidden disagreement.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the concealer as a coward
  among the brave, because the source finding it stages is that each person is only sparing themselves a real cost that
  honesty would carry, a frown or a shunning or a prison cell, and that one dissenting voice would change nothing but its
  owner's fate, so professing the safe thing is the rational move and not a failure of nerve. No one intends to uphold the
  order they privately reject; the upholding is the unmeant sum of every safe profession. The loss is authored by no one: it
  is the order most reject, kept standing because each believes themselves nearly alone, and the misfit is that the very
  concealment each chooses to protect themselves is what hides from them how many would stand with them.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, to lower the cost of
  honesty, the secret ballot that lets a preference be expressed unseen, the free-speech protection that dulls the sanction,
  the anonymity that lets the first truths surface without their owners being marked, so that private truth can reach the
  air without a rupture, but it is neither cheap nor sure: those who hold the order have every reason to raise the cost of
  honesty rather than lower it, protections can be revoked exactly where they are most needed, and where the pressure holds,
  the only other road to the truth is the cascade, which is sudden, uncontrollable, and as able to install a new falsified
  order as to free anyone. There is no gentle dial from the false consensus to the true one.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; preference falsification and its name and model are Kuran's, the account of the 1989
  surprise Kuran's, the pluralistic ignorance it rests on Katz and Allport's and O'Gorman's, and the false enforcement that
  deepens it Willer, Kuwabara and Macy's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: not every public conformity
  is a falsification, since much public agreement is genuine, or genuinely persuaded, so one cannot read every conformist as
  a secret dissident; the cascade's timing is genuinely unpredictable, which is the theory's finding and not a forecast it
  delivers, so it explains the surprise after the fact more than it foresees it; and private preferences are by definition
  hidden, so the account rests partly on inference and is hard to test head-on. The scope is held to expressed preferences
  under real social or political pressure, and not stretched into a claim that every consensus is a fraud or that everyone
  secretly dissents.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the proposed load-bearing
pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Private Truth**: carries the setup (Kuran), the genuine preference the person actually holds, a view or a wish or a
  judgement, kept unspoken because saying it would cost.
- **The Pressure**: carries the force (Kuran; Willer, Kuwabara and Macy), the social or political cost of honesty, the
  sanction that makes the true preference unsafe to say, and which the concealers themselves help to raise by enforcing the
  accepted line to prove their own sincerity.
- **The Public Lie**: carries the engine (Kuran), the falsified public stance professed to escape the pressure, the gap
  between the private truth still held and the safe thing said out loud.
- **The False Consensus**: carries the self-reinforcing harm (the pluralistic ignorance of Katz and Allport), the surface of
  safe professions each reads off the others so that each believes their own view more isolated than it is and conceals the
  harder, upholding the order none can see the others reject.
- **The Cascade**: carries the brittleness (Kuran), the sudden tipping of hidden truth into the open when a small shock
  makes speaking a little safer, drawing out voice after voice until the whole order flips at once, surprising everyone.
- **The Free Voice**: carries the remedy and the bound, the secret ballot and the free-speech protection and the anonymity
  that lower the cost of honesty so private truth can surface without a rupture, costly and revocable and resisted by those
  the order serves, and the marker of the hedge, since not every conformity is a lie and the cascade that is the other road
  to truth is sudden and uncontrollable.
