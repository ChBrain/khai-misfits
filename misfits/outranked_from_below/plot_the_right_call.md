# Plot: The Right Call

## Taxonomy

[Outranked from Below](play_outranked_from_below.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

[The Ready Line](place_the_ready_line.md), after the reset, where the record of every dispatch is still there to be replayed.
The question on the table is not who erred, but whether anyone did.

## Action

[The Dispatcher](persona_the_dispatcher.md), holding [the Dispatcher's seat](position_dispatcher.md), replays [Serve the Highest Ready](plan_serve_the_highest_ready.md) against [The Order](piece_the_order.md), decision by decision, from the Holder's take of the lock to the Middle's preemption to the Foremost's unbroken wait.

## Stage

Each ruling is checked against the one rule the Dispatcher was ever built to keep, run the highest-priority ready task, and each one holds: the Holder's take was uncontested, the Middle's preemption was earned by rank, the Foremost's block was the lock's exclusivity and nothing else. No laggard slowed anything, no task jumped a queue it was not entitled to, no rule was bent or skipped. The inversion is not found inside any single ruling; it is found only in their sum, a scheduler honouring priority at every step and starving its own highest-priority task in the aggregate, because holding a resource confers no rank of its own and nothing in the Order accounts for that (Sha, Rajkumar, and Lehoczky). The diagnosis names no fault to fix in the Dispatcher, the Holder, or the Middle: each was correct, and the correctness is exactly the problem.

## Tension

If nothing done was wrong, then nothing done can be undone to prevent it happening again; the fix, if there is one, has to come from outside the sequence of individually correct calls.
The composition itself is what must change, not any decision inside it, and that is a harder thing to design than punishing a mistake.
