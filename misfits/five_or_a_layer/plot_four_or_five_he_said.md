# Plot: Four or Five, He Said

## Taxonomy

[Five, or a Layer](play_five_or_a_layer.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

An organisation with more people in it than one person can attend to.
[Graicunas's Number](piece_graicunass_number.md), worked out in 1933 and true since.

## Action

[Spanning](process_spanning.md): [The Executive](persona_the_executive.md), holding [the attender's seat](position_attender.md), confronts a count of people to arrange and a week that does not lengthen.

## Stage

[The Executive](persona_the_executive.md), on [Attend to What I Can Attend To](plan_attend_to_what_i_can_attend_to.md), sets a span they can actually hold, which is a small number.
The arithmetic backs them: the relationships a manager must handle go one, six, eighteen, forty-four, one hundred and two hundred and forty-four as the reports go one to six, because the relations among the reports are the manager's problem too, and the recommendation was five at most and probably four.

## Tension

The limit is arithmetic, and that is what makes everything after it structural.
This is not a claim about management style or about how hard anybody works. **A manager with fifteen reports is not failing, they are dividing**, and the load is worse than the count looks because it grows with the relations rather than the headcount. So the organisation cannot be arranged flat and cannot be arranged deep without cost: the number of people is given, the week is fixed, and the only free variable left is which of the two costs to pay.
