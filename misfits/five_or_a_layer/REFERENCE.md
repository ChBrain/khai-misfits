# Five, or a Layer: Reference

The research warrant for Five, or a Layer.
The term is the house's; the play runs on organisational shape and on what a span of control costs at either setting,
all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the organisation chart as **one dial with a cost written at each end**: there is a number of people one person
can attend to and it is small, so an organisation of any size faces a single choice. Widen the span, and each person
receives a slice of a week that was already committed. Narrow it, and a layer is inserted, and a layer exists precisely
so that less reaches the person above. The play models the documented mechanics, the arithmetic that makes the limit a
fact rather than a preference, the layer that makes supervision possible, the honest summary that is useful in exactly
the degree it is lossy, the flattening that buys fidelity back with attention, and the reorganisation cycle that
answers whichever cost is currently visible, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a
bureaucratic middle or a manager who should try harder.

The governing law is that supervision can only be bought with layers and a layer is a device for delivering less, so
every setting of the shape spends the thing the other setting was protecting.

**And this play declares no axis, which its own first sentence requires.** It staged the chart as "one dial
with a cost written at each end", and then carried `hierarchy-depth` at a negative sign, which cannot be
right: widen the span and each person gets a slice of a week already committed, narrow it and a layer is
inserted that exists precisely so that less reaches the person above. **Attention and fidelity are two
goods moving in opposite directions on one dial**, so there is no single quantity to say a sign about, and a
one-dial-two-bad-ends structure is the case the house leaves undeclared. The dial itself is real and The
Frozen Middle holds it, because that play's harm runs one way: more layers, less of the signal survives.
This one is the trade rather than a position on it.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The executive sets
a span they can hold. The manager supervises a small team properly. The summary is accurate. The report does the work.
The reorganisation answers the complaint it was called for.

**And the limit is arithmetic rather than motivational, which is established before anything else.** Graicunas worked
out in 1933 that what a manager handles is not the count of subordinates but the relationships among them, which go 1,
6, 18, 44, 100 and 244 as the reports go one to six, and he recommended a maximum of five and probably four. **A
manager with fifteen reports is not failing, they are dividing**, and no version of working harder produces a
sixteenth hour.

**Then the layer that fixes it is not a defect, it is a definition.** Cut the span and four managers stand where one
did, and the teams are genuinely better supervised, which is not a small thing and is the whole reason the level was
added. In the same act, the person above now reads four accounts instead of holding twenty conversations. That is not
a cost of the layer: a level passing everything through would be a longer meeting, and the week it was created to
protect would be unprotected.

**And the loss needs nobody's interest, which is the sharpest thing in the play.** Give the writer of the summary
nothing to defend, no delivery target, no continuity mandate and no career at stake, and **a faithful summary is still
shorter than its source**. The shortening is what makes it worth reading, so value and loss are one quantity, and the
selection is made by somebody who cannot know what the reader will later need. More care buys accuracy and cannot buy
coverage; only length would, and length is what was unavailable. An omission has no way to announce itself, so nobody
above learns what they were not told.

**Then the remedy is real, was carried out, and was not what it was sold as.** Firms did flatten: across more than
three hundred large American firms the number reporting directly to the chief executive rose from about 4.5 in 1986 to
nearly 7 by 1999, with levels between division heads and the top removed under competitive pressure. That is the layer
cost being paid down, and it is a decision to widen every span beneath it. The follow-up work on the same data found
the flattening read **less like the delegation it was advertised as than like centralisation**, because removing the
levels also removes the places where decisions used to be made below the top.

**And the cycle is not indecision.** Each redraw answers the cost currently being felt and causes the one that will be
felt next, so a sequence that looks like management fashion is a run of right decisions against alternating
complaints. It is not even balanced: **depth is legible on a chart and attention is not**, so the layer cost can be
pointed at in a meeting while the span cost must be borne by somebody long enough to become a complaint.

It is distinct from the house's **The Frozen Middle**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages middle-management
distortion, where a layer measured on continuity while charged with change rationally damps the disruptive signal in
both directions to protect the delivery it answers for. Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating a middle with no
delivery to protect, no continuity target, no career and no reason of any kind to damp anything, and this misfit
stands whole: **honest compression is still compression.** That play needs a middle with an interest; this one needs
only that a week has a fixed length and a summary is shorter than what it summarises.

It is distinct from the house's **The Ninth Woman**, which stages Brooks's Law, where adding people to a late project
makes it later through the training drain and the assimilation delay. Remove that engine, by stipulating no ramp-up
cost at all and new arrivals productive from the first hour, and this stands entire: **the trade has nothing to do
with lateness or with newness.** A steady organisation of long-tenured people faces the identical choice, because the
constraint is how many relationships one person can hold rather than how long anybody takes to become useful.

It is distinct from the house's **Watermelon Reporting**, where a status travels upward green on the skin and red in
the flesh because every rung is paid to move on. Remove that engine, by stipulating rungs with no standing to protect
and no reason to misreport, and this stands: nothing here is misreported. Every account is true, and it is shorter,
and the shortness is what was asked for.

It is distinct from the house's **The Mirror**, which stages Conway's Law, where an organisation's communication
structure is imprinted on the product it builds. There the loss is a matter of shape transferred to an artefact; here
nothing is being built, and the loss is attention divided or an account compressed, which happens in an organisation
that makes nothing at all.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the middle as obstructive
  or the manager as stretched by poor planning, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  executive sets a span they can actually hold, which is the competent thing to do; the manager supervises a small team
  properly and writes up the week for somebody with less time, which is the function the level was created for; the
  summary is accurate in everything it contains and shorter than its source, which is what makes it worth reading; the
  report does the work and has no say in the number that decides how often they are seen; and the reorganisation
  answers a real, evidenced complaint and answers it correctly. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the
  structure, so the misfit is that attention and fidelity are one quantity spent in one direction or the other.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Flattening removes the compressions
  and widens every span beneath, which is what the measured delayering did, and reads as centralisation rather than the
  delegation it was sold as. Deepening restores supervision and reinstates a compression at every new level. Hiring
  more managers at an unchanged span is a layer under another name. Better tooling and better writing improve the
  accuracy of a summary and cannot lengthen it, since its shortness is the whole of its value. And knowing the
  arithmetic, which has been published since 1933, tells anybody exactly which cost they are choosing and has never
  told anybody how to avoid choosing.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the span arithmetic is Graicunas's, the panel evidence on delayering is Rajan
  and Wulf's, and the reading of what the flattening actually was is Wulf's. The bounds are carried with them:
  **whether flatter organisations perform better is genuinely contested**, and the play stages the trade rather than a
  recommended setting; Graicunas's formula counts theoretical relationships rather than measured workload and its
  specific numbers are offered as the shape of the growth rather than as a management prescription, and its
  recommendation of four or five has been argued over ever since; the panel evidence is large US firms across one
  period and is offered as the documented direction of travel rather than as a constant; and the account treats
  organisations of very different kinds as one structure, which is its own simplification. **The play makes no claim
  about any organisation, any manager or any reporting line, and gives no management, organisational or employment
  advice of any kind**, only that the week does not lengthen, that a summary is shorter than what it summarises, and
  that these two facts are the same choice seen from either end.
