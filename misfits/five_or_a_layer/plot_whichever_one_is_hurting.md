# Plot: Whichever One Is Hurting

## Taxonomy

[Five, or a Layer](play_five_or_a_layer.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

Three years on, and the next reorganisation, called because nobody is being managed.
[The Swap](piece_the_swap.md), about to run in the other direction.

## Action

[Reorganising](process_reorganising.md) again: spans cut, managers added, and [layering](process_layering.md) restored, with [summarising](process_summarising.md) reinstated at every new level.

## Stage

[The Reorganisation](persona_the_reorganisation.md) answers the complaint on the table, which is real and evidenced, exactly as it did last time.
[The Report](persona_the_report.md) gets a one-to-one again. [The Executive](persona_the_executive.md) starts reading summaries of summaries.

## Tension

This is not indecision, and that is the hardest part of it.
Each redraw is the correct answer to the cost currently being felt, and the cause of the cost that will be felt next, so a cycle that looks like management fashion is a run of right decisions against alternating complaints. There is nothing to converge on, because no shape holds both. And the cycle is not even balanced: **depth is legible on a chart and attention is not**, so the layer cost can be pointed at in a meeting while the span cost has to be borne by somebody long enough to become a complaint, which biases the whole thing slightly toward flattening. Knowing the arithmetic, which has been published since 1933, tells anybody precisely which cost they are choosing, and has never once told anybody how to avoid choosing.
