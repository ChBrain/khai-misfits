# Play: Five, or a Layer

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Five, or a Layer.
There is a number of people one person can actually attend to, and it is small. Graicunas worked out in 1933 why: it is not the count of subordinates that a manager handles but the relationships among them, and those go one, six, eighteen, forty-four, one hundred, two hundred and forty-four as the count goes one to six. He suggested five at most, probably four. So an organisation of any size faces one choice with two settings. Widen the span, and each person gets a slice of an hour that was already spoken for. Narrow it, and you insert a layer, and a layer exists precisely so that less reaches the person above: that is not a defect of layers, it is the definition of one.

## Arc

The bend is this: supervision can only be bought with layers, and a layer is a device for delivering less, so every setting of the shape spends the thing the other setting was protecting.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's fault: an hour has sixty minutes, and a summary is shorter than what it summarises.
Take the first setting. A manager with fifteen reports is not a bad manager, they are a manager whose attention is divided fifteen ways, and Graicunas's arithmetic says the load is worse than the count suggests because the relationships between the reports are the manager's problem too. Nobody is supervised badly on purpose. There is simply not enough of the person to go round.
Take the second. Cut the span to five and four managers appear where one stood, and above them somebody now reads four summaries instead of twenty conversations. **The compression is the point.** A layer that passed everything through would not be a layer, it would be a longer meeting, and the reason organisations have layers at all is that nobody at the top can hold twenty conversations. So the summary is doing its job when it leaves things out, and what it leaves out is chosen by a person who cannot know what the reader will turn out to need.
And this needs nobody's interest to be served. Give the layer no delivery to protect, no continuity target, no career, no reason of any kind to shade anything, and a faithful summary is still shorter than its source. **Honest compression is still compression.**
So the shape gets changed, repeatedly, and each change is correct. Firms did flatten: across three hundred large American firms the number reporting directly to the chief executive rose from about four and a half in the mid-eighties to nearly seven by the end of the nineties, and the levels between division heads and the top came out, in response to competition. That is the layer problem being solved. Then the follow-up work found the flattening was not what it had been sold as, reading less like delegation than like centralisation, because taking out the levels also takes out the places where a decision used to be made below the top.
Reach for a settlement and there is not one. Flatten and you have widened spans. Deepen and you have added compressions. Hire more managers at the same span and you have added a layer by another name. And knowing the arithmetic, which has been published since 1933, tells you exactly which cost you are choosing and never how to avoid choosing.

Closes when the limit is established as arithmetic rather than as a preference about management style.

**[The Shorter Version Is the Point](plot_the_shorter_version_is_the_point.md)**

Closes when the loss is shown to require no interest, no incentive and no distortion, only the definition of a layer.

**[So You Take the Layer Out](plot_so_you_take_the_layer_out.md)**

Closes when the layers are gone, the spans have widened, and the follow-up finds the flattening read less like delegation than like centralisation.

**[Whichever One Is Hurting](plot_whichever_one_is_hurting.md)**
Cue: the next reorganisation, called because supervision has thinned. Deepen, and the compressions return. Flatten again in three years, and the spans go. Closes when the cycle is shown to be a sequence of correct answers to whichever cost is currently visible, with no setting that holds both.

## Stakes

Whether an organisation can be supervised and informed at the same time.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether anybody manages badly, because both settings are defensible and somebody has to pick one, but whether attention and fidelity can be held together at any shape. The production cannot find the setting that does both, because the two costs are the same choice read from either end: supervision is bought with layers and a layer is a device for delivering less. It can only show that the limit is arithmetic and was published in 1933, that honest compression is still compression and needs nobody's interest to bite, that the flattening which fixes the layers widens the spans and does not decentralise the way it is sold as doing, and that the reorganisation cycle is not indecision but a sequence of correct answers to whichever of the two costs is currently the one being felt.
