# Piece: Graicunas's Number

## Taxonomy

[Five, or a Layer](play_five_or_a_layer.md). The 1933 arithmetic: relationships go 1, 6, 18, 44, 100, 244 as reports go 1 to 6, so the load rises far faster than the count.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

Computed once, in [the org chart](place_the_org_chart.md)'s prehistory, and true ever since.
Answered nowhere: the relationships between reports are the manager's problem whether anybody counts them or not.

## Load Bearing

Without it the span limit is a matter of taste and somebody can always argue for one more report.
It is load bearing because it converts the limit into **published arithmetic**: the manager is not handling a list of people but a web of relations among them, which is why the recommendation was five at most and probably four, and why the limit bites long before anybody feels lazy.

## Apparent

A dated management formula, of historical interest.
It reads as a curiosity from the era of scientific management, and the fact that it is the reason a wide span cannot be fixed by effort is not what it is usually cited for.

## Yearbook

Sets the ceiling that [spanning](process_spanning.md) runs into.
It is why [layering](process_layering.md) is necessary rather than optional, and therefore why [the shorter version](piece_the_shorter_version.md) is unavoidable in any organisation past a certain size.
