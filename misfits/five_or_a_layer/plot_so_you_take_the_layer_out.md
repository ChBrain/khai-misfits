# Plot: So You Take the Layer Out

## Taxonomy

[Five, or a Layer](play_five_or_a_layer.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The complaint that the middle is too thick and the top too far from the floor, with evidence behind it.
[The Org Chart](place_the_org_chart.md), about to be redrawn.

## Action

[Reorganising](process_reorganising.md): [The Reorganisation](persona_the_reorganisation.md), holding [the redesign seat](position_redesign.md), removes levels and widens spans, as large firms did through the nineties.

## Stage

[The Reorganisation](persona_the_reorganisation.md), on [Fix Whichever One Is Hurting](plan_fix_whichever_one_is_hurting.md), answers a real complaint correctly: across three hundred large American firms, reports to the chief executive rose from about four and a half in 1986 to nearly seven by 1999, and the levels between division heads and the top came out, under competitive pressure.
[The Executive](persona_the_executive.md) now hears from the floor with one compression fewer between.

## Tension

The layers went and the spans went with them, and the change was not what it was sold as.
Removing a level is the correct answer to distance and is simultaneously a decision to widen every span beneath it, so the fidelity is bought back with attention. And the follow-up work on the same firms found the flattening read **less like the delegation it was advertised as than like centralisation**, because taking out the levels also takes out the places where decisions used to be made below the top. So the remedy delivered a real improvement, a real counterpart cost, and a third effect nobody was promising.
