# Play: Starting Tomorrow

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Starting Tomorrow.
There is a choice a person faces again and again: a smaller reward now against a larger reward later. The cigarette or the clear lungs, the snooze or the run, the spend or the saved retirement. Seen from a distance, when both rewards are far off, the choice is easy, and the person chooses the larger-later: they resolve, in the calm, to save, to study, to quit, to rise early, and they mean it. But people do not discount the future at a steady rate. They discount the near future far more steeply than the far, so a reward's value does not fall smoothly with its distance but spikes sharply as it comes within reach. George Ainslie set down what follows: when the smaller-sooner reward draws near to now, its steeply-recovered value overtakes the larger-later, and the person's own ranking reverses. The plan made in the calm is broken in the moment, not by a change of mind reasoned out but by the mere approach of the near reward, and the person takes the cigarette, hits the snooze, makes the spend, and resolves, sincerely, to start again tomorrow.
And here is why each move is rational, in its own instant. At every moment the person is only choosing what looks best from where they stand. But where they stand keeps changing, and so does what looks best, because the near reward that looked small from a distance looms large up close. So the self that plans and the self that acts are not the same self, and they want different things, the far-sighted self forever outvoted by the near-sighted one when the near one is on the clock.
And here is why no one in it is a villain. The temptation is to call the plan-breaker weak, a sluggard who simply lacks resolve, but the finding is stranger and kinder than that: the reversal is a feature of how humans discount, not a failure of character. A person of ordinary will, discounting the near future more steeply than the far, will find the near reward's value spike as it approaches and their own ranking flip, so they break the plan not from weakness but from the shape of the curve. The loss is authored by no one. It is the larger-later reward forgone because the sooner one spiked as it neared, and the misfit is that the very plan the person sincerely makes is the plan the moment reliably breaks.
And here is the way out, and why it is so hard. The plan fails because it is left to the moment, and the moment belongs to the present self, so the remedy is to take the choice out of the moment: to foresee the reversal and bind the future self in advance, the mast Ulysses is tied to, the retirement locked in an account that cannot be touched, the app deleted, the cigarettes not bought, so the moment finds no near reward to spike. But it is neither free nor sure. It asks a present self to spend real effort and real freedom binding a future self it does not fully trust, and it works only for the sophisticated who foresee their own bias, not the naive who keep trusting a future self that will reverse just as this one did (Laibson; O'Donoghue and Rabin). The bindings can be undone or worked around, and a self that can never change its mind is not always the wiser self.
And here is the bound that keeps it honest. Present bias is documented and it is contested. The functional form is debated, whether truly hyperbolic or quasi-hyperbolic or something else, and careful elicitation has raised doubts about its size and even its presence in some settings, asking how much of measured impatience is a discounting anomaly and how much is uncertainty about the future or the plain value of a reward in hand (Andreoni and Sprenger). And not all impatience is a bias: to prefer a reward sooner can be perfectly rational, given uncertainty, real interest, or one's own mortality. The behavioural theory is Ainslie's, the economic model and the golden-egg commitment Laibson's, the sophisticated-and-naive distinction O'Donoghue and Rabin's, and the dynamic inconsistency first formalised by Strotz. The house stages the structure and holds the bound in view: that the misfit is not impatience but the reversal, the ranking that flips as time passes though nothing about the rewards has changed, so the plan made in the calm is the plan the moment breaks, and it is always starting tomorrow.

## Arc

The bend is this: a person discounts the near future far more steeply than the far, so when both a smaller-sooner and a larger-later reward are distant they plan for the larger-later, but as the sooner reward draws near its value spikes and overtakes the later, so their own ranking reverses and they take the sooner and break the plan, again and again, a present self forever overriding the plan its earlier self made.
It runs on a plan the moment reliably breaks.
In the calm, both rewards far off, the person chooses the larger-later and resolves to save or study or quit (Laibson).
And the discount is steep up close. People discount the near future far more steeply than the far, so a reward's value spikes as it comes within reach rather than falling smoothly (Ainslie).
And so the ranking reverses. As the smaller-sooner reward nears, its recovered value overtakes the larger-later, and the person's own preference flips (Ainslie).
And the near reward is taken. The plan is broken in the moment, the cigarette taken or the snooze hit, and the person resolves again to start tomorrow.
And the later self pays. The self that inherits the forgone larger-later reward makes the next plan, which the next moment will break in turn.
And it is authored by no one. The reversal is the shape of the curve and not a failure of will, so the loss is the structure's and not a fault.
And the whole is held against its limit. The form is debated, careful elicitation raises doubts about its size, and not all impatience is a bias (Andreoni and Sprenger).

## Stakes

Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward the near reward taken, and away from the larger-later the plan chose, because the discount is steep up close and the sooner reward spikes as it nears until it overtakes the later. The way out, to take the choice out of the moment and bind the future self in advance, spends real effort and real freedom, works only for the sophisticated who foresee their own reversal, and can be undone, since a self that can never change its mind is not always the wiser. It can only show that no one is a villain, that the plan-breaker is not weak but reversed by the shape of the curve, and that the loss is the larger-later forgone and not anyone's fault.
