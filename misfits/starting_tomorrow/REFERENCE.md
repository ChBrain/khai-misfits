# Starting Tomorrow: Reference

The research warrant for **Starting Tomorrow**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon named
**present bias**, or hyperbolic discounting: a person discounts the near future far more steeply
than the far, so their own ranking of a smaller-sooner against a larger-later reward reverses as the
sooner draws near, and they plan in the calm to save or study or quit and then, when the moment
comes, take the immediate reward and break the plan, again and again, all cited below, held with the
finding that the functional form is debated and not all impatience is a bias.

## Line of Work

Staging present bias as a **preferences-reverse-as-it-nears structure**: a person faces, again and again, a choice between
a smaller reward now and a larger reward later, the cigarette or the clear lungs, the snooze or the run, the spend or the
saved retirement. Seen from a distance, when both rewards are far off, the choice is easy and they choose the larger-later:
they resolve, in the calm, to save, to study, to quit, to rise early, and they mean it. But people do not discount the
future at a steady rate. They discount the near future far more steeply than the far, so the value of a reward does not
fall smoothly with its distance but spikes sharply as it comes within reach, and George Ainslie set down what follows: when
the smaller-sooner reward draws near to now, its steeply-recovered value overtakes the larger-later, and the person's own
ranking reverses. So the plan made in the calm is broken in the moment, not by a change of mind reasoned out but by the
mere approach of the near reward, and the person takes the cigarette, hits the snooze, makes the spend, and resolves again
to start tomorrow. It is locally rational at each instant, in the sense that the person is only choosing what looks best
from where they stand; but where they stand keeps changing, and so does what looks best, so the self that plans and the
self that acts are forever at odds, the present self overriding the plan its earlier self made and handing the later self
the bill. It is authored by no weakness of will in the old moralising sense: the reversal is a feature of how humans
discount and not a failure of character, so a person of ordinary resolve procrastinates and over-consumes exactly as the
curve predicts. The governing law is not that anyone is lazy, but that **a person discounts the near future far more
steeply than the far, so when both a smaller-sooner and a larger-later reward are distant they plan for the larger-later,
but as the sooner reward draws near its value spikes and overtakes the later, so their own ranking reverses and they take
the sooner and break the plan, again and again, a present self forever overriding the plan its earlier self made, no
weakness of will required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the plan made in the calm (the larger-later chosen),
the hyperbolic curve that discounts the near far more steeply than the far (Ainslie), the reversal as the sooner reward
nears, the immediate reward taken and the plan broken, the later self who inherits the bill and re-plans, and the mast to
which a foreseeing self binds its future self in advance (Laibson; O'Donoghue and Rabin), as the load-bearing vectors of
the structure, not as a tale of a sluggard who simply lacks resolve.

It is a preferences-reverse-as-it-nears structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Time Inconsistency, where a promise best made today is best broken tomorrow, so no one acts
on it and good outcomes need a hand tied in advance: both descend from the dynamic inconsistency Strotz first formalised,
but there the inconsistency is strategic and faces outward, a pledge to others or a policy whose credibility fails because
it is optimal to renege, whereas here the inconsistency is intrapersonal, a self overriding its own earlier plan under a
present-biased discount curve, no counterparty and no promise, only the person at war with themselves across time. It is
distinct from Seed Corn, where the present is favoured over the future deliberately, the quarter's number hit by consuming
the capacity the future needs: there the short-termism is a chosen response to a metric or an incentive that rewards the
present, whereas here the person means to favour the future and is reversed against their own intent by the mere approach of
the near reward, the plan broken not on purpose but by the curve. And it is distinct from a mere preference for the present,
since not all impatience is a bias: to prefer a reward sooner can be perfectly rational, given uncertainty, real interest, or
one's own mortality, and the misfit is not impatience but the reversal, the dynamic inconsistency by which the ranking flips
as time passes though nothing about the rewards has changed.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the one who breaks the plan as
  a sluggard who simply lacks resolve, because the source finding it stages is that the reversal is a feature of how humans
  discount and not a failure of character: a person of ordinary will, discounting the near future more steeply than the far,
  will find the near reward's value spike as it approaches and their own ranking flip, so they take the sooner and break the
  plan not from weakness but from the shape of the curve. The self that plans and the self that acts are both doing their
  honest best from where they stand; there is no one to blame, only a present self overriding the plan its earlier self made.
  The loss is authored by no one: it is the larger-later reward forgone because the sooner one spiked as it neared, and the
  misfit is that the very plan the person sincerely makes is the plan the moment reliably breaks.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, to foresee the reversal
  and bind the future self in advance, the mast Ulysses is tied to, the retirement locked in an illiquid account, the app
  deleted, the cigarettes not bought, so the moment finds no near reward to spike, but it is neither free nor sure. It asks a
  present self to spend real effort and real flexibility binding a future self it does not fully trust, it works only for the
  sophisticated who foresee their own bias and not the naive who do not (O'Donoghue and Rabin), the bindings can be undone or
  worked around, and over-commitment has costs of its own, since a self that cannot change its mind is not always the wiser
  self. There is no dial that simply makes the far reward feel as near as the near one.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the behavioural theory of hyperbolic discounting and preference reversal is Ainslie's,
  the quasi-hyperbolic economic model and the golden-egg commitment Laibson's, the sophisticated-and-naive distinction
  O'Donoghue and Rabin's, and the dynamic inconsistency and precommitment first formalised by Strotz. The mechanism is
  carried as carefully as the finding: the functional form is debated, whether truly hyperbolic or quasi-hyperbolic or
  something else, and careful elicitation has raised doubts about the size and even the presence of present bias in some
  settings, asking how much of measured impatience is a discounting anomaly and how much is uncertainty about the future or
  the plain value of a reward in hand (Andreoni and Sprenger). The scope is held to the reversal, the dynamic inconsistency
  by which a ranking flips as time passes though the rewards do not, and not stretched into a claim that all impatience is a
  bias or that preferring the sooner is always an error.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Plan**: carries the setup (Laibson), the resolution made in the calm when both rewards are distant, to save or study
  or quit or rise early, the larger-later sincerely chosen.
- **The Curve**: carries the engine (Ainslie), the hyperbolic discount that falls far more steeply over the near future than
  the far, so a reward's value spikes as it comes within reach rather than rising smoothly.
- **The Reversal**: carries the flip (Ainslie), the moment the nearing sooner reward's recovered value overtakes the
  larger-later, so the person's own ranking reverses though nothing about the rewards has changed.
- **The Now**: carries the broken plan, the immediate reward taken when the moment comes, the cigarette or the snooze or the
  spend, and the fresh resolution to start tomorrow.
- **The Later Self**: carries the harm (O'Donoghue and Rabin), the self who inherits the forgone larger-later reward and
  makes the next plan, which the next moment will break in turn, the standing war between the self that plans and the self
  that acts.
- **The Mast**: carries the remedy and the bound (Laibson; O'Donoghue and Rabin), the commitment a foreseeing self makes to
  bind its future self in advance, the illiquid account or the deleted app or the mast Ulysses is tied to, costly and
  circumventable and open only to the sophisticated who foresee their bias, and the marker of the hedge, since the form is
  debated and not all impatience is a bias.
