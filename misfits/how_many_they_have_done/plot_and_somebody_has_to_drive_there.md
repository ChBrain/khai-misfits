## Tension

The remedy works, which the play states before anything else about it.
The centre accumulates the cases, the group becomes what a group with that many cases becomes, and its
results are better than any of the services it replaced. This is not a compromise and it is not a
managerial fashion: it is the thing the evidence points at, and it delivers.
Then the cost, which lands in one seat.
A hundred and forty miles is a morning for some people and an impossibility for others. The family that
visited daily visits weekly. The patient without a car, without a spare week, or without somebody to drive
is the patient for whom attendance is a condition of treatment rather than a preference, and the
distribution of that difficulty is exactly the distribution of everything else.
And **a journey is not a line in a service budget**, so the cost of the reorganisation is real, is borne
entirely by the people who did not choose it, and appears in no account of the decision that produced it.
