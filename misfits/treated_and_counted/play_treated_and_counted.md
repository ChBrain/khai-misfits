# Play: Treated and Counted

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Treated and Counted.
A person sits in a chair in a research unit while a new compound goes into their arm. Everything in the room is an instrument of care: the chair, the blanket, the line taped to the back of the hand, the nurse who checks the rate, the doctor who knows the whole case by heart and asks how the weekend was. And everything the hour produces is an instrument of study: an observation at each interval, a reading logged against a dose, a row added to a table that is slowly answering a question about the compound. Both descriptions of the hour are complete. Neither is a cover for the other. The document said so, plainly, and was read aloud and signed. And still the two accounts meet in exactly one place, which is the person in the chair, because they are the only one in the room who has to be both at once.

## Arc

The bend is this: the hour is honest care and honest measurement at the same time, and the design that made the hour was answering a question, not a person.
It runs on a shelf that has been emptied.
The Enrolled comes because the ordinary answers have all been tried, which makes entering the study the reasonable move and not a leap: with nothing left on the shelf, a place in the unit is the only place there is. The One Who Treats does not oversell it. The Signed Page states the design in full: that this is a study of dose and tolerance, that benefit is not promised, that the compound may do nothing.
And the room answers louder than the page. A drip is a drip. A doctor who knows your whole history is a doctor. The unit's every furnishing is the furnishing of a place where people are made better, so the setting makes a continuous argument that the page spent six sentences denying, and the setting is present for four hours while the page was present for ten minutes.
Then the dose arrives, and the dose is the clearest statement of what the hour is for. It is not set by what this person needs. It is set by where the study has reached: the cohort ahead came through, so this cohort steps up, and had they enrolled a month earlier they would have taken a smaller amount on purpose, because the early steps are deliberately low so that no one is hurt while the ceiling is still unknown. Nobody chose that for them. The Protocol assigns by its own position, which is the only way a dose has ever been learned.
None of it is a deception. The page is true, the doctor is sincere and gains nothing from the second role, the hope is not an error, and the design is not a trick: a compound that skipped the steps would injure everyone who came after. The One After is the party the design serves, and the reason they cannot be consulted is that they are not ill yet, and may be a child now.
So the correction has no clean form. Say it harder and the sentences are understood better and the misconception does not move, because the room is still making its argument. Say it hard enough to actually dissolve it and the hope goes with it, and the hope was the load-bearing thing. Split the roles, so a stranger enrols and the doctor only treats, and the clarity is bought by removing the one person who knows the case. Every fix is paid for by the same person.

## Triggers

**[The Last Door](plot_the_last_door.md)**
The shelf is empty: every ordinary answer has been tried and used up, so a place in the unit is the only place left. Closes when the Enrolled asks to be considered, which is the reasonable move and not a leap.

**[Signed and Understood](plot_signed_and_understood.md)**
Cue: the Signed Page, read aloud in full, stating that this is a study of dose and tolerance and that no benefit is promised. The Enrolled follows every sentence, repeats the design back correctly, and signs. Closes when the page has done everything a page can do.

**[The Rung You Land On](plot_the_rung_you_land_on.md)**
Cue: the cohort ahead came through without reaching the ceiling, so the Protocol steps the amount up. The dose that goes into the arm is the one the study had reached, and a month either way would have made it a different one. Closes when the amount is set by the study's position and by nothing about the person receiving it.

**[The Same Hour](plot_the_same_hour.md)**
Cue: four hours in the chair. The One Who Treats asks after the weekend and means it, watches the rate, adjusts the blanket, and logs an observation at every interval against the dose. Closes when both accounts of the hour are complete and neither has displaced the other.

**[What It Was For](plot_what_it_was_for.md)**
Cue: the table fills in, one row per person, until the ceiling is known and a number can be carried forward. The knowledge is real and it lands, and it lands on the One After, who is not in the room and was never asked. Closes when the study has its answer and the answer belongs to someone else.

**[Saying It Harder](plot_saying_it_harder.md)**
Cue: the gap noticed, and the obvious remedy reached for: more disclosure, plainer words, a check that every sentence was understood. Comprehension rises and the misconception stays, because the room is still arguing the other way. Closes when it is plain that the version blunt enough to work would take the hope with it, or the roles are split and the case knowledge goes instead.

## Stakes

Whether an hour can be honest care and honest measurement at once without the person inside it being asked to carry the contradiction alone.
Every plot moves the same way: toward the page, which is true, and the room, which argues otherwise, and away from any settlement in which one account cancels the other. The production cannot make the design aim at the person, because a dose is learned by steps or not at all, and it cannot make the room stop looking like care, because it _is_ care. What is left to fight over is not whether to run the study, everyone agrees the compound must be learned before it can be given, but who is asked to hold both accounts in the same hour, and whether anyone but the person in the chair ever has to.
