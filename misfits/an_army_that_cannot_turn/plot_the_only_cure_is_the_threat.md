# Plot: The Only Cure Is the Threat

## Taxonomy

[An Army That Cannot Turn](play_an_army_that_cannot_turn.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

[The Palace](place_the_palace.md), and the reasonable question of why the army is not simply made good.

## Action

Through [Weighing](process_weighing.md), [the Ruler](persona_the_ruler.md) on [Hold the Throne](plan_hold_the_throne.md) reads [the Same List](piece_the_same_list.md) in full before answering: promote on merit, exercise at formation scale, push initiative down to the men who can see the ground, let units talk to one another freely. Every item restores a capability the army needs, and every item restores a capability a faction would need, so the reform cannot be granted in one direction only and a ruler wholly convinced of his officers' loyalty still cannot take it. He then holds [the Boundary](piece_the_boundary.md): where the danger from outside grows large enough, the same arithmetic returns the other answer, and rulers under real external pressure do professionalise, do fight better for it, and do become easier to overthrow, one army even carrying the trade struck differently in different units (Talmadge).

## Stage

The question is the right question and deserves the answer it gets. An adviser urging professionalisation is not naive: the reform genuinely works, it is what the army needs, and in the cases where it has been taken it has delivered exactly what was promised, along with the exposure that came with it.

## Tension

The remedy and the danger are one enumeration, so there is no case that can be made, no assurance that can be given and no good faith on either side that separates them, and the play closes on a trade with terms rather than a verdict on a form of government: a sword held so it can never turn on you is a sword that does not turn, and the only ruler who buys the turning is the one who has been frightened into it by something worse.
