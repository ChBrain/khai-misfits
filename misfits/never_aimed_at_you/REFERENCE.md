# Never Aimed at You: Reference

The research warrant for **Never Aimed at You**, a staging of the phenomenon the literature calls
**bystander selection** (Christine Tedijanto, Scott Olesen, Yonatan Grad and Marc Lipsitch): that a drug
given to a person reaches every organism in that person's flora and not only the one being treated, so
most of the selection pressure on any given species is generated by prescriptions never aimed at it. It
is held with the finding that the prescriptions in that mass are overwhelmingly appropriate, that no
decision anywhere in the chain concerns the organism that pays, and that stewardship built on
appropriateness therefore works on the tail rather than on the mass.

## Line of Work

Staging bystander selection as a **harm-outside-the-decision structure**: the whole of it follows from
the fact that a systemic antibiotic does not know what it was prescribed for. A patient arrives with a
urinary infection, or a chest infection, or a wound, and is given a drug that is right for the organism
causing it. The drug then reaches everything else the patient is carrying: the pneumococcus in the
throat, the staphylococcus on the skin, the enterobacteria in the gut, none of which is causing anything
and none of which anybody was thinking about. Those organisms are exposed, selected on, and the
survivors persist, and the perturbation does not fully reverse when the course ends (Dethlefsen and
Relman). Do that across a population and the arithmetic falls out: for most species and most drugs, the
large majority of the exposure a species receives is bystander exposure, generated by prescriptions
written for something else entirely (Tedijanto, Olesen, Grad and Lipsitch). And every one of those
prescriptions may be perfectly indicated. The drug is right, the dose is right, the patient needed it,
and the decision would be identical if the prescriber understood the whole mechanism, because the
alternative on offer at the moment of decision is not a narrower drug but no drug: identification takes
days, the patient is in front of you now, and narrowing empirically before the culture returns kills
people. So the remedy that would work is blocked by a clinical fact rather than by ignorance or haste.
Then the harm surfaces, years later, in a different person, as an isolate that answers to nothing on the
list, and the decision and the damage share no patient, no clinician and no decade (Lipsitch and
Samore). And the correction that has actually been built is appropriateness-based stewardship, which
audits prescriptions against guidelines and removes the ones that should not have been written. That
work is genuine and worth doing and it operates on the inappropriate tail, while the mass generating the
pressure is appropriate, so the intervention is aimed at the fraction and not at the bulk, which is the
finding rather than a criticism of stewardship. The play models the indicated prescription, the blind
hour, the organism nobody is treating, the bystander mass, the stranger's isolate, the trimmed tail and
the bound as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, and not as a tale of a careless prescriber or an
antibiotic-happy public. The ordinary expectation is that resistance is driven by misuse, so cutting
misuse addresses it; the finding is that most of the pressure is generated by correct use, and by
correct use aimed somewhere else. The governing law is that **where a drug reaches every organism in a
treated person's flora and not only the one being treated, most of the selection pressure on any given
species is generated by prescriptions never aimed at it, so the exposure that matters is produced by
decisions that are individually correct and would not change if every decider understood the mechanism
perfectly**.

It is an inference structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from The Commons, where each taker draws from a stock they themselves benefit
from and know they are drawing on: here the prescriber has no interest in the organism that pays, no
view of it, and no share in whatever it is being depleted of, so restrain-your-take does not reach the
taking that matters and an appeal to shared stewardship addresses the wrong decision. It is distinct
from First Do Harm, Illich's iatrogenesis, whose harm lands on the treated patient by way of the
treatment: here the treated patient is helped exactly as intended, and the harm lands on a population
years later by way of that patient's commensals, so no patient in the play is injured by their own
prescription. It is distinct from The Tyranny of Small Decisions, where a large outcome nobody was asked
about is settled by the sum of small choices each individually reasonable: there each chooser is a party
to the outcome and would have chosen otherwise had it been put as one question, whereas here the
prescriber would write the same prescription with the whole mechanism in front of them, because
withholding it harms the patient in the room. And it is distinct from The Horizon Problem, where an
instrument's finite reach leaves consequences past its boundary: nothing here is bounded out and no
redrawing helps, since the organism is invisible not because the frame stops short of it but because it
is the subject of no decision at all and no test at the point of care names it.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast a careless
  prescriber or an antibiotic-happy public, because the finding it stages needs neither and is destroyed
  by both. The prescriptions generating the bulk of the pressure are appropriate ones: the drug is
  right, the dose is right, the patient needed it, and the decision would be identical if the prescriber
  understood the entire mechanism, because at the moment of decision the alternative is not a narrower
  drug but no drug. The patient completing the course is following the instruction they were given and
  would be criticised for doing otherwise. The steward auditing appropriateness is doing genuine and
  necessary work. And the organism that pays is not a party to anything: it is not causing disease, it
  is not being treated, and nobody in the chain has seen it or has any reason to.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction, and here two remedies
  fail for different reasons. Narrow-spectrum prescribing would work and requires an identification that
  does not exist at the point of care, since cultures take days and narrowing empirically before they
  return kills patients, so the obstacle is a clinical fact rather than a habit anybody could reform.
  And appropriateness-based stewardship, which is real, funded and worth doing, operates on the
  inappropriate tail while the mass generating the pressure is appropriate, so it cannot reach the bulk
  by working harder at what it does. Nor is the pressure already applied recoverable: compensatory
  evolution reduces the fitness cost of resistance, so withdrawing a drug does not reliably restore
  susceptibility (Andersson and Hughes).
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The bystander-selection estimate and the finding that most exposure is
  bystander exposure are Christine Tedijanto, Scott Olesen, Yonatan Grad and Marc Lipsitch's; the
  population framing of antimicrobial use and resistance is Marc Lipsitch and Matthew Samore's; the
  demonstration that a course perturbs the whole commensal community and that it does not fully recover
  is Les Dethlefsen and David Relman's; and the account of compensatory evolution and the difficulty of
  reversing resistance is Dan Andersson and Diarmaid Hughes's. The scope is held to systemic
  antibiotics reaching a commensal flora, and is not stretched into a claim that stewardship is
  pointless, that prescribing guidelines do not matter, or that patients should not be treated: the
  source literature says none of those, and the play carries stewardship as correct work aimed at a
  smaller share than is usually assumed rather than as a mistake.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Indicated Prescription**: carries the thing there is nothing wrong with, a drug that is right for
  the organism causing the illness, given to a patient who needs it, by a clinician who would write it
  again knowing everything.
- **The Blind Hour**: carries the clinical fact that blocks the obvious remedy, that identification takes
  days, the patient is present now, and narrowing empirically before the culture returns kills people, so
  the broad drug is not haste but the correct decision under the information available.
- **The Organism Nobody Is Treating**: carries the exposure itself (Dethlefsen and Relman), the whole
  commensal community taking the dose and not returning to its prior state, none of it causing anything
  and none of it in anyone's mind.
- **The Bystander Mass**: carries the finding (Tedijanto, Olesen, Grad and Lipsitch), that for most
  species and most drugs the large majority of exposure is generated by prescriptions aimed at something
  else, so the pressure that matters is produced by correct use.
- **The Stranger's Isolate**: carries the consequence reaching the world (Lipsitch and Samore), a
  resistant infection years later in a person who never took the drug, so the decision and the damage
  share no patient, no clinician and no decade, and no feedback reaches the seat that generated it.
- **The Trimmed Tail**: carries the correction's limit, that appropriateness-based stewardship is genuine
  and necessary work aimed at the prescriptions that should not have been written, and that those are the
  tail while the mass is appropriate, so working harder at it cannot reach the bulk.
- **The Bound**: carries the condition and the honest note (Andersson and Hughes), that the estimates are
  model-based and vary by species and drug, that the trap needs a systemic agent reaching a commensal
  flora, that withdrawing the drug does not reliably restore susceptibility because compensatory
  evolution reduces the cost of resistance, and that none of this is an argument against stewardship or
  against treating the patient.
