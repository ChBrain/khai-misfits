# Play: Never Aimed at You

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Never Aimed at You.
A systemic antibiotic does not know what it was prescribed for.
Somebody arrives with a urinary infection, and is given a drug that is right for the organism causing it. That is the whole of the transaction as anybody in the room understands it: one patient, one infection, one correct drug.
And the drug goes everywhere. The pneumococcus in the throat gets the dose. The staphylococcus on the skin gets the dose. The enterobacteria in the gut get the dose. None of them is causing anything. None of them is why anybody is here. All of them are being selected on, and the ones that survive stay, and the community does not go back to what it was when the course ends (Dethlefsen and Relman).
Now do that across a whole population, and count where the exposure actually falls. For most species and most drugs, the large majority of the pressure on a species is bystander pressure, generated by prescriptions written for something else entirely (Tedijanto, Olesen, Grad and Lipsitch).
And here is the part that will not go away. Those prescriptions are appropriate. Not most of them, not nearly all of them: they are the appropriate ones. The drug was right, the dose was right, the patient needed it, and the prescriber would write exactly the same thing again with the entire mechanism laid out in front of them.
Because at the moment of decision the alternative is not a narrower drug. It is no drug. Identification takes days. The patient is here now. And narrowing empirically before the culture comes back kills people, which is not a rhetorical point but the reason the practice exists. So the remedy that would work is blocked by a clinical fact rather than by ignorance or by haste.
And the harm surfaces somewhere else entirely. Years later, a different person, an isolate that answers to nothing on the list. The decision and the damage share no patient, no clinician and no decade, and nothing travels back to the seat that generated the pressure (Lipsitch and Samore).
And the correction that has actually been built, and funded, and staffed, is appropriateness-based stewardship: audit the prescriptions against the guidelines and remove the ones that should not have been written. That work is real and it is worth doing and it is aimed at the tail. The mass is appropriate. So the intervention operates on the fraction while the bulk goes on generating what it generates, and no amount of doing that job better reaches it.
And nobody in it is doing anything wrong. Not the prescriber, not the patient finishing the course as instructed, not the steward auditing against the guidelines.
And so the misfit is that the ordinary account has it backwards: resistance is supposed to be driven by misuse, so cutting misuse addresses it, and instead most of the pressure is generated by correct use aimed somewhere else. The bystander estimate is Christine Tedijanto, Scott Olesen, Yonatan Grad and Marc Lipsitch's; the population framing is Marc Lipsitch and Matthew Samore's; the demonstration that a course perturbs the whole commensal community and that it does not fully recover is Les Dethlefsen and David Relman's; the account of compensatory evolution is Dan Andersson and Diarmaid Hughes's. The house stages the indicated prescription, the blind hour, the organism nobody is treating, the bystander mass, the stranger's isolate, the trimmed tail and the bound, and holds the bound in view: that the estimates are model-based and vary by species and drug, that withdrawing the drug does not reliably restore susceptibility because compensatory evolution reduces the cost of carrying it, and that none of this argues against stewardship or against treating the patient, so the whole of it is that the organism which pays for the prescription was never the one it was written for.

## Arc

The bend is this: where a drug reaches every organism in a treated person's flora and not only the one being treated, most of the selection pressure on any given species is generated by prescriptions never aimed at it, so the exposure that matters is produced by decisions that are individually correct and would not change if every decider understood the mechanism perfectly.
It runs on a drug that cannot be told what it was for.
A patient with a real infection is given the right drug at the right dose, and the decision is correct on every term available at the time.
And everything else in that person takes the dose. The commensals are exposed, selected on, and do not return to what they were (Dethlefsen and Relman).
And the arithmetic across a population. For most species and most drugs, the large majority of exposure is bystander exposure, written for something else (Tedijanto, Olesen, Grad and Lipsitch).
And a remedy blocked by a fact. Narrowing needs an identification that takes days, and narrowing empirically before it returns kills patients, so the broad drug is the correct decision and not a lazy one.
And a harm that surfaces elsewhere. Years later, a different person, an isolate that answers to nothing, with no feedback reaching the seat that generated the pressure (Lipsitch and Samore).
And a correction aimed at the tail. Appropriateness stewardship removes what should not have been written, which is real work, and the mass generating the pressure was appropriate.
And the whole is held against its own limit. The estimates are model-based and vary by species and drug, withdrawing the drug does not reliably restore susceptibility, and none of it argues against stewardship or against treatment (Andersson and Hughes).

## Stakes
