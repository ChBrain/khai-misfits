# Play: Sold to Find the Price

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Sold to Find the Price.
A company with no traded shares has no price. Not a hidden price, not a disputed price: none. The bank does not have it, the auditors do not have it, and the founders least of all, because a founder's estimate of their own company is the one number in the room with no market behind it. The people who do have it are the ones who will be buying, and each of them holds a fragment: what they would pay, how much they would take, and what they know about the sector that nobody has written down. To set the price you must assemble those fragments. To assemble them you must ask. And the moment you ask, you have created a reason for every one of them to understate.

## Arc

The bend is this: the information needed to price the asset is owned by the counterparty, and the only currency they accept is the asset.
It runs on a question that spoils its own answer.
[The Price No One Holds](persona_the_price_no_one_holds.md) is not concealed by anybody. It is distributed. It exists as the aggregate of what several dozen institutions would each pay, and no single participant, including the ones holding the pieces, can see the whole of it.
[The Ones Who Know](persona_the_ones_who_know.md) hold real information, and stating it truthfully is expensive to them in the plainest possible way: an investor who says the issue is worth more than the range raises the price they are about to pay for it. Enthusiasm is taxed and reticence is free, so a truthful book is not something anybody can simply request.
So there is a payment, and [Bought with the Thing Itself](plot_bought_with_the_thing_itself.md) is the whole mechanism. The bank has no cash to offer the informed. What it has is a price it can set below the level the book implies, and shares it can hand out at its own discretion, and a next issue, and the one after that. The discount is not an error and not a fee: it is the consideration paid for the information, and it is paid in the asset because the asset is the only coin available.
[The Ones Who Cannot Know](persona_the_ones_who_cannot_know.md) close the other side of it. An investor with no information gets a small piece of the issues everybody wanted and the whole of the issues nobody did, so their average fill is worse than the average issue. Price the offer at fair value and they earn less than nothing and stop turning up, and an offering needs them. The discount is what keeps the room populated.
[The One Who Asks](persona_the_one_who_asks.md) is the only party who can make the payment, and also the only party positioned to take a cut of it. The fee is visible, fixed and modest. The discount is invisible, variable and larger. Nothing in the mechanism stops the hand that allocates from being paid by the people it allocates to, and nothing in the mechanism requires it either.
And [The Ones Who Are Selling](persona_the_ones_who_are_selling.md) cannot decline. Setting the offer at the price the asking produced destroys the reason anyone answered the asking. Refusing the process leaves them pricing their own company from the inside, which is the one method guaranteed to be wrong. They pay, and the payment is drawn from the shares they still own.
Then the market opens and prints a number, and the gap between the two is public, arithmetic, and enormous next to the fee. Nobody at the issuer is angry, which is the last strange thing about it: on the day the company was shortchanged, the people who signed for it became rich.

## Triggers

**[No Price to Look Up](plot_no_price_to_look_up.md)**
Cue: a company with no traded shares, a date, and a number that has to be written on a document. Closes when everybody who could set the price has been shown not to have it, and the only people holding pieces of it are the ones on the other side of the trade.

**[The Cost of Saying Yes](plot_the_cost_of_saying_yes.md)**
Cue: an investor asked, without obligation, how much they want and what they would pay. Closes when telling the truth has been shown to raise the price the teller pays, so shading costs nothing and candour costs money, and no instruction can reverse that.

**[Bought with the Thing Itself](plot_bought_with_the_thing_itself.md)**
Cue: a bank that needs an answer and has no cash to buy it with. Closes when the payment has been made in the asset, a price set below what the book implies and shares handed out at discretion, so the price is discovered by selling under it.

**[Only the Ones They Turned Down](plot_only_the_ones_they_turned_down.md)**
Cue: an investor with no information, applying for everything on offer. Closes when their fills have been shown to be small in the good issues and complete in the bad ones, so a discount is required for them to break even and required again for them to stay in the room.

**[Nine Million on the Table](plot_nine_million_on_the_table.md)**
Cue: a first day of trading, and a closing price beside an offer price. Closes when the gap has been shown to run to roughly twice the fee, in public, with nobody at the issuer complaining.

**[Who Collects It](plot_who_collects_it.md)**
Cue: a payment the mechanism requires, passing through the one party with discretion over where it lands. Closes when the discount has been shown to be collectable by the hand that allocates it, in commissions and in personal accounts, so a necessary payment becomes an available rent that nobody designed and nothing prevents.

## Stakes

Whether a price can be found without paying for it out of the thing being priced.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a valuation that lives only in the counterparty, an asking that gives every respondent a reason to understate, a payment made in the asset because no other currency exists, and a seller who cannot refuse the process without pricing blind, and away from any arrangement in which the truth arrives free. The production cannot ask an investor to bid against their own interest, and it cannot ask a company to know what it is worth from the inside. What is left to fight over is not whether the discount was too large, though it may have been, but who owns a cost that is not a fee, not a theft and not a mistake, but the price of the question.
