# Play: Wait Your Turn

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Wait Your Turn.
One line. People are served in the order they joined it, which is the rule everybody would choose and the rule everybody is keeping.
The person at the front is waiting on something that is nobody's fault and will clear in its own time.
Behind them stands somebody whose business would take a moment, and across the room a counter is open, and the distance between them is four steps and one rule.

## Arc

The bend is this: the harm is not congestion, and it does not come from load, and it is at its purest when there is almost nothing to do.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: a single ordered line may not be departed from, and the thing the front of it is waiting for has nothing to do with anybody behind.
Take the discipline first, because the play has to establish that it is **good**. First come, first served is the rule we teach children. It needs no supervision, it cannot be gamed by the confident, it treats the timid and the pushy alike, and any queue that abandons it becomes a queue where the loudest are served first. Nothing in this production improves by relaxing it, and the production never asks for that.
Then take the stall, because it matters that it is not a defect. The one at the front is waiting on a signature, a lookup, a part from the back, a form somebody else has to bring. It is legitimate work, it is proceeding, and there is no one to hurry and nothing to fix.
Then the hinge, which is the whole play. The one behind is **ready now**, and the counter across the room is **open now**, and what stands between them is not a busy server, not a shortage, not a competing customer: it is the order they came in. **The thing they are waiting for is not in use by anybody.** Take everything else away, empty the room, leave one stalled person at the front and one ready person behind and every counter in the building free, and the ready person still waits. That is the defect in its pure state, and it is a defect of **ordering** rather than of capacity.
And the instrument agrees that everything is fine. The counters are idle, so the utilisation figure is low, and it is correct. Nobody chose a misleading measure: a measure of how busy the servers are will report a system with idle servers as underused, and it is right, and the queue is not moving. Watching it more closely reports the same number more often.
Reach for a settlement and there are two, and both work, and both cost real coin. Give each destination its own line and pick a pairing every tick, which recovers the throughput and buys a decision problem that has to be solved again and again for as long as the system runs. Or split the one ordered stream into independent ones, so a stall in one does not hold the others, which is what a transport does when it stops guaranteeing that everything arrives in the order it was sent. Each of those spends something that was worth having. **Neither of them is a way to keep one fair line and also not wait.**

## Triggers

**[First Come, First Served](plot_first_come_first_served.md)**
One line, and the rule that it is served in the order it was joined. Closes when the discipline is established as the fair one and the cheap one: no supervision, no negotiation, no advantage to the confident, and nothing in the production asking for it to be relaxed.

**[Waiting on Something Real](plot_waiting_on_something_real.md)**
Cue: the one at the front, stopped. A signature, a lookup, a part from the back. Closes when the stall is shown to be legitimate work proceeding normally, with nobody to hurry and no defect to remove, and with no connection whatever to anybody standing behind.

**[The Door Was Open](plot_the_door_was_open.md)**
Cue: the one behind, ready this instant, and a counter standing free across the room. Closes when the loss is shown to be pure ordering: the thing they are waiting for is not in use by anybody, so emptying the building of every other customer changes nothing.

**[Nothing Is Busy](plot_nothing_is_busy.md)**
Cue: the meter. Counters idle, utilisation low, and the figure correct. Closes when the instrument is shown to be honest, well chosen and silent, because a measure of how busy the servers are cannot report a harm whose whole nature is that the servers are free.

**[A Line for Every Door](plot_a_line_for_every_door.md)**
Cue: the remedy. One line per destination and a pairing chosen every tick, or the single ordered stream cut into independent ones. Closes when both are shown to work, to recover what was lost, and to spend real coin: a decision problem that never ends, or the guarantee that things arrive in the order they were sent.

## Stakes

Whether a fair rule can be the thing that is wrong.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the system is overloaded, because it is not and the harm is sharpest when it is idlest, and not whether anybody is behaving badly, because the discipline being kept is the one we would all choose, but whether an order that is just between any two people can be unjust across all of them at once. The production cannot resolve it. It can only show that the rule is good and worth keeping, that what the front is waiting on is legitimate and nobody's doing, that the one behind is ready and the door is open and what stands between them is neither scarcity nor competition but sequence, that the instrument pointed at the system reports idleness accurately and says nothing, and that the two settlements available both work and both take something real away: an endless decision, or the promise that things arrive in the order they were sent.
