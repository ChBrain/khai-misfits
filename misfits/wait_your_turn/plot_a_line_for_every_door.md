# Plot: A Line for Every Door

## Taxonomy

[Wait Your Turn](play_wait_your_turn.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

[The Matcher](persona_the_matcher.md), arriving with the only two settlements there are.
[The Fifty-Eight Per Cent](piece_the_fifty_eight_per_cent.md), the measure of what one line was costing.

## Action

[Matching](process_matching.md): [The Matcher](persona_the_matcher.md), in [the matched seat](position_matched.md), gives each destination its own line at [the doors](place_the_doors.md) and chooses a pairing every tick.

## Stage

[The Matcher](persona_the_matcher.md), on [Pair Every Line to a Free Door](plan_pair_every_line_to_a_free_door.md), recovers what the single line was losing and never finishes working, because the pairing has to be chosen again at the next tick and the one after that.

## Tension

Both settlements work, and both take something real away.
Give each destination its own line and the blocked customer walks to the open counter, which is the throughput back. What has been bought is a **decision problem that never ends**: somebody must now pair lines to doors, at every tick, for as long as the system runs, and the quality of that pairing is now the thing the system's performance depends on. The single line needed no such office because it had no choice to make.
Or cut the one ordered stream into independent ones, so a stall in one does not hold the others. This works, and what it spends is the guarantee that things arrive in the order they were sent, which was not a bug: it was the reason a single ordered stream was chosen in the first place, and everything built on top of it assumed it.
**Neither is a way to keep one fair line and also not wait.** The production offers no third option, because the two on the table are what the structure permits: pay an endless decision, or give up the order.
