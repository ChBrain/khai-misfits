# Ninety-Six Times Eight: Reference

The research warrant for Ninety-Six Times Eight.
The term is the house's; the play runs on fish passage at large dams and on what an excellent per-structure figure
does when a run has to cross several of them, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the fishway as **a structure that is excellent and a fraction at the same time**: a dam ends a migration
unless fish can get past it, so passage is required, and the requirement has been taken seriously for decades
with hydraulic modelling, entrance attraction flows, tagged-fish evaluation and revision on evidence. It works.
Across fifteen years, eight large dams on the Columbia and Snake, and nearly twenty-seven thousand radio-tagged
Chinook, sockeye and steelhead, the average tailrace-to-forebay passage efficiency at a single dam is about
ninety-seven per cent, which the authors describe as among the highest recorded for any migratory species and
credit to sustained adaptive management. There is no weak link to find. The run has to cross all eight. The play
models the documented mechanics, the obligation nobody disputes, the figure established rather than claimed, the
absence of any weak structure, the arithmetic of a series that no structure performs, the permit whose scope
follows ownership rather than biology, and the count on a board at the top of the river, as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a badly built ladder or an operator cutting a corner.

The governing law is that excellence is a property of each structure and arrival is a property of all of them, so
a river of individually excellent gates is not an excellent river.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The engineer
builds one of the best passage structures in the world. The operator meets every condition on its licence. The
regulator enforces at the scale its powers reach. The counter publishes what arrived. The fishery manager absorbs
the shortfall.

What removes any possibility of blame from the engineer is that the figure is not merely adequate. It sits among
the highest recorded for any migratory species anywhere, it was reached by decades of deliberate revision rather
than by luck, and raising it further at any one dam is difficult, expensive and worth doing without touching the
quantity in question, because that quantity is a product across structures this seat does not own.

**And the absence of a weak link is what makes the play necessary, because it removes the usual account.** The
familiar story about a chain of structures is that one of them is bad and that finding it restores the whole.
That story is unavailable here: the evidence says the structures are at the top of what has been achieved. An
account that goes looking for the weak dam is looking for something that is not there.

**Then the arithmetic does what no structure does.** A per-dam figure of about ninety-seven per cent applied
across eight dams is roughly three-quarters, which is arithmetic on a published figure and not a finding of the
study that produced it. Observed upstream survival through reaches carrying several dams has been estimated lower
still, in the region of two-thirds, because delay and its consequences accumulate alongside the passage losses
themselves. Nothing new goes wrong at this step and the outcome changes completely.

**And the instrument cannot reach it, for a reason that is not a drafting failure.** A licence attaches to a
structure because a structure is what a party applies for, operates and can be held to. A condition written about
a whole river would fall on parties who are not applicants in that proceeding, could not be enforced against any
one of them, and would arrive at each owner decades apart as their own renewals came up. So the per-structure
scope follows from the fact that rivers are crossed by many owners while instruments run against one owner at a
time, and the quantity that decides the run appears in no document anybody signs.

It is distinct from the house's **Only as Good as the Worst**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages the
O-Ring theory, where output is the product of complementary task qualities so the weakest link dominates and
small differences in reliability produce large differences in value. That misfit's engine is **variation**: it
needs a weak link, and its whole force is that the weak one governs. Remove that engine, by stipulating eight
structures of identical and excellent quality with no weak link anywhere, and this misfit stands entire, because
ninety-seven per cent taken eight times is about three-quarters whether or not anything varies. **The failure here
needs no weak link at all**, which is the sharpest statement of the difference between them.

It is distinct from the house's **Hungry Water**, where a dam starves the coast below it of the sediment that was
building it, so the harm is in what the structure **withholds** from downstream. Remove that engine, by supposing
a dam that passes sediment perfectly, and this stands: the fish still cannot get up, and the direction of travel
here is against the flow rather than with it.

It is distinct from the house's **Heavy Enough to Cross**, where a safety requirement is itself the transport
mechanism for an invasion, and the harm arrives by delivery. Here nothing is delivered and the structures carry
nothing: what accumulates is a **shortfall**, produced by repetition of a success rather than by any transfer.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to suggest that anybody
  built a poor structure or evaded an obligation, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong:
  passage is required and the requirement has been met seriously over decades; the figure achieved is among the
  best recorded for any migratory species anywhere; there is no weak link available to be found and removed; the
  operator meets every condition on the only licence it holds, and answering for the other structures would mean
  answering for somebody else's asset; the regulator enforces at the scale ownership comes in, which is the only
  scale its powers reach; the counter produces the one number describing the run and can attribute none of it,
  because the difference accumulated rather than happening anywhere; and the fishery manager absorbs the whole of
  it with the only lever available. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the
  misfit is that a river of individually excellent gates is not an excellent river.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Improving any single fishway is
  real work, is being done, and moves a figure already near the top of what has been achieved. Setting a
  river-wide passage standard has no party it can be enforced against, since the structures have different
  owners whose renewals arrive decades apart. Trapping and hauling fish past the whole reach substitutes a
  transport operation for a migration and carries its own mortality and its own dependence. Removing a barrier
  genuinely restores connectivity and is a decision about power, water, navigation and flood control rather than
  only about fish. Compensating with hatchery production replaces the run with a different population and is
  contested on its own terms. And knowing the whole structure, which the field does and has documented at
  length, leaves a licence renewal for one dam and a season to set.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the passage evaluations, the multi-dam survival estimates and the
  fishway literature are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether passage structures can be made good
  enough at scale, or whether barrier removal is the only reliable answer, is genuinely contested**, and the play
  carries that argument rather than settling it; the headline figure is for adult Pacific salmonids passing
  upstream at technical fishways on one river system, and passage for other taxa, for downstream migration and
  for juveniles is very different and often much worse, which the play does not model; the eight-fold product is
  arithmetic performed here on a published per-dam figure and is not a result reported by the study; the
  observed multi-dam survival figures come from particular reaches, runs and years and are offered as evidence
  that the series costs more than the multiplication suggests, rather than as a general rate; and the account
  treats a family of rivers, structures and regulatory regimes as one, which is its own simplification.
  **The play makes no claim that any structure was poorly built, that any operator failed a condition, that any
  licence was wrongly granted, or that any dam should or should not be removed**, only that every gate can be a
  good gate and the run still not arrive.
