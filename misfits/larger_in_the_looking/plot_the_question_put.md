# Plot: The Question Put

## Taxonomy

[Larger in the Looking](play_larger_in_the_looking.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

A survey lands in the inbox, a salesperson leans into the pitch, or a person alone weighs whether to take the job across the country: how much would this one thing change your life.

## Action

At [the Asking](place_the_asking.md), [the Asker](persona_the_asker.md), from [the asker's seat](position_asker.md), on [Ask How Much](plan_ask_how_much.md) through [Asking](process_asking.md), takes up [the Question](piece_the_question.md), putting the fair question of how much one factor, the income, the climate, the house, bears on how well a life goes.

## Stage

The question is not idle. A person choosing a job must ask how the income compares, a person choosing a home must ask how the climate will feel, and a survey built to learn what predicts happiness must ask its questions one at a time (Kahneman, Krueger, Schkade, Schwarz and Stone). But a question about a single factor can be answered only by turning the mind toward that factor, since there is no other way to weigh a thing than to first bring it to attention (Kahneman). So the asking, however plainly put, already narrows the frame to the one thing named, before a single answer has been given and before anything has gone wrong in the giving. No villain: the survey, the seller, and the deliberating self all ask in good faith, none of them plants a fixation, and the spotlight the question casts is only what any real question about a factor must cast to be answered at all.

## Tension

The innocent question is already the first turn of the illusion, the frame narrowed before anyone has said a word in answer.
