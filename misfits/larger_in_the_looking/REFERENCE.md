# Larger in the Looking: Reference

The research warrant for **Larger in the Looking** (play and company not yet staged; this warrant is drafted first, to
prove the science and the distinction before authoring). The title is provisional, a reading of the phenomenon named the
**focusing illusion**: when people judge how much a single factor bears on their well-being, the very act of attending to
it magnifies its apparent importance, so they overweight it and mispredict how much it will matter, since in the living
it will be one of many things and rarely in focus, nothing in life as important as it seems while it is being thought
about, all cited below, held with the finding that the factor is not nothing but smaller in the living than in the
judging, a documented bias in the judgments made while it is salient.

## Line of Work

Staging the focusing illusion as an **attention-inflates-the-weighed structure**: a person is asked, or asks itself, how
much some single factor bears on how well its life goes. Would you be happier with a higher income, in a warmer climate,
with this house, after this diagnosis, if you moved. To answer, the mind brings the factor to attention and weighs it,
and this is the only way it can be weighed, by bringing it to mind. But a thing brought to mind looms large: the very act
of attending to a factor magnifies its apparent importance, so the estimate the mind returns is not of how much the
factor will figure in a life but of how much it fills the mind when the mind is fixed on it, and those are not the same
(Kahneman). So the judge overweights the factor, predicting it will matter far more than it will, because in the judging
the factor is the whole of the frame while in the living it is one of many things and mostly out of focus, the income
earned but not attended most hours, the fine climate stepped through on the way to the day's real concerns (Schkade and
Kahneman). The gap is documented and large: Midwesterners and Californians report about the same life satisfaction, yet
both expect Californians to be happier, because asked to compare they focus on the climate, which favors California and
is salient, and neglect the hundred other things that fill a life the same in both places (Schkade and Kahneman); and the
belief that money greatly raises happiness is inflated the same way, income looming large when attention is fixed on it
and moving moment-to-moment mood far less than the fixed attention predicts (Kahneman, Krueger, Schkade, Schwarz and
Stone). It is the same focalism that makes people overpredict how long a good or bad event will color their feelings,
fixing on the event and neglecting the rest of the life that will go on around it (Wilson, Wheatley, Meyers, Gilbert and
Axsom). It is authored by no villain: no one deceives, and the one who asks the question, a survey, a salesperson, a self
weighing a large choice, directs attention to the factor innocently, since to weigh a factor one must attend to it, and
the judge that overweights is doing the only thing a mind can do to estimate importance, bringing the thing to mind,
where it looms. The governing law is not that anyone is foolish or misled, but that **the act of attending to a factor
magnifies its apparent importance, so a factor's contribution to well-being is overweighted whenever it is the thing
being thought about, and the judgment of how much it matters is systematically larger than how much it will matter in a
life where it is one of many things and rarely in focus, no villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics:
the factor whose weight is judged, the attention the judging brings to it, the magnification attention works, the
overweight in the estimate, the rest of the life neglected, and the small real difference the factor makes in the living
(Schkade and Kahneman; Kahneman and colleagues; Wilson and colleagues), as the load-bearing vectors of the structure,
not as a tale of a fool who cannot know its own mind.

It is an attention-inflates-the-weighed structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from The Worst and the Last, where an episode already lived is remembered by its peak and its
end and not its duration, so the self misvalues its own past: there the distortion is retrospective, a memory summarizing
a lived experience by two moments, whereas here the distortion is prospective and judgmental, a factor's importance
inflated by the attention a present judgment or forecast fixes on it, no episode lived and nothing yet remembered, only a
weight misjudged in the looking. And it is distinct from More to Stand Still, where the felt value of a good genuinely
fades as one adapts to it and the level must be raised to feel what one first felt: there the good's effect really
diminishes over time in the living, an adaptation that happens, whereas here the effect was overjudged from the start by
the attention fixed on it, and the adaptation the good will undergo is one of the very things the focused judgment
neglects, so the illusion is a misprediction and not the fading it fails to foresee.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the asker as a manipulator or
  the judge as a fool, because the source finding it stages is that the illusion needs no deceit and no folly: to weigh how
  much a factor matters, a mind must bring the factor to attention, and a factor brought to attention looms large, so the
  one who asks the question directs attention innocently and the judge that overweights is doing the only thing a mind can
  do to estimate importance. No one is misled by another. The error is authored by no one: it is attention magnifying what
  it lands on, and the misfit is that a factor's weight, judged while it is the thing in mind, is larger than the weight it
  will carry in a life where it is one of many things and rarely in focus.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. One can defeat the illusion by widening
  the frame, by attending to how the day is actually spent rather than to the factor in isolation, or by recalling that the
  factor will soon fade from attention and be adapted to, but each asks the judge to hold in mind precisely what the
  focused question crowds out, the whole rest of the life, which is many, diffuse, and hard to bring to attention against a
  single salient factor. The escape works against the grain of how importance is estimated, by attending, and a mind cannot
  attend to everything at once.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the demonstration and the name are Schkade and Kahneman's, the income case is
  Kahneman, Krueger, Schkade, Schwarz and Stone's, the focalism mechanism is Wilson, Wheatley, Meyers, Gilbert and Axsom's,
  and the aphorism is Kahneman's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: the factor is not nothing, only
  smaller in the living than in the judging, and the illusion is a documented bias in the judgments made while the factor
  is salient, not a claim that the factor never matters or that all such judgments are worthless. Income does raise
  reported life satisfaction somewhat and a warm climate is a real good; what the illusion inflates is the weight assigned
  to the single attended factor against everything else, so the house stages the overweighting under attention and holds
  that the factor has a real but smaller effect than the focused judgment gives it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the proposed load-bearing
pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Factor**: carries the object of judgment (Schkade and Kahneman), the single thing whose weight on well-being is
  being estimated, the income or the climate or the house or the diagnosis brought into focus.
- **The Question**: carries the act of attention (Kahneman, Krueger, Schkade, Schwarz and Stone), the asking, by a survey
  or a seller or a self weighing a choice, that directs the mind to the factor, since to weigh a factor is to attend to it.
- **The Magnification**: carries the mechanism (Kahneman; Wilson and colleagues), the inflation attention works on what it
  lands on, a factor brought to mind looming large for as long as the attention lasts.
- **The Overweight**: carries the error (Schkade and Kahneman), the factor rated as mattering far more than it will, the
  estimate of a life's frame taken for the estimate of the life.
- **The Rest of Life**: carries the neglected (Kahneman and colleagues; Wilson and colleagues), the many diffuse things
  that fill a life the same whether the factor is present or not, and the adaptation the factor will undergo, all out of
  focus in the judging.
- **The Small Difference**: carries the reality and the bound (Schkade and Kahneman), the modest effect the factor makes in
  the living, mattering far less than in the judging, drawn distinct from The Worst and the Last and More to Stand Still,
  and the marker of the hedge, since the factor is not nothing, only smaller than the focused judgment gives it.
