# Plot: The Factor in Mind

## Cue

The question answered by turning toward the one thing it named: the raise, the coastline, the extra room, held up alone now to be judged.

## Action

At [the Asking](place_the_asking.md), [the Judge](persona_the_judge.md), from [the judge's seat](position_judge.md), on [Weigh the Factor](plan_weigh_the_factor.md) through [Focusing](process_focusing.md), takes up [the Factor](piece_the_factor.md), bringing the single named thing to mind so its weight can be gauged.

## Stage

To ask how much a factor matters is to ask a mind to do the one thing it can do with the question: attend to the factor and hold it in view (Kahneman). There is no second instrument. Schkade and Kahneman (Psychological Science, 1998) put exactly this to their respondents, asking how much living in California instead of the Midwest bears on life satisfaction, and the only way to answer was to bring the climate to mind and weigh it there. The factor, alone now in attention, has done nothing wrong by being named and held: it is where any honest weighing has to start, since a thing unattended cannot be judged at all. No villain: the judge does not choose to fixate, it does the only thing an answer requires, and the factor is not yet inflated here, only present, the raw material the rest of the weighing will work on.

## Tension

The factor is brought to mind to be weighed fairly, and mind is the one place a thing can only ever loom.
