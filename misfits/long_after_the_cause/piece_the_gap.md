# Piece: The Gap

## Taxonomy

[Long After the Cause](play_long_after_the_cause.md). The hysteresis: the load at which the system could climb back out sits far below the load that tipped it in, so returning to ordinary is nowhere near enough.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

Stretched across [The Queue](place_the_queue.md) between two thresholds: above the upper one the system trips, below the lower one it recovers, and between them both states exist at once, so which one holds depends on where the system has been and not on the load now (Bronson, Aghayev, Charapko and Zhu).

## Load Bearing

Remove it and waiting works: with the trip and recovery thresholds at the same load, the moment arriving traffic fell back to ordinary the system would climb out on its own, and the operator's instinct to let it drain would be correct rather than costly.
It bears the load as the thing that makes the remedy expensive and counterintuitive. Because recovery demands load far under the level the system handled comfortably all last week, no proportionate response reaches it, and the fix must be drastic precisely when the situation looks like it should not need to be.

## Apparent

Impossible, even: that a service failing under ordinary load could have run all week at that same load reads as a contradiction rather than as the plain signature of two thresholds.

## Yearbook

It works together with [The Boundary](piece_the_boundary.md), the gap setting how hard the shedding must be and the boundary naming what that costs.
