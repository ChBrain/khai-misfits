# Pitch: It'll Drain

## Taxonomy

[Long After the Cause](play_long_after_the_cause.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Busy and blameless.
Every machine up, every process running hot, every client doing exactly what the manual says to do with a request that failed, and the spike already a finished bump on the graph.
Nothing false in it: the components really are healthy, the trigger really has passed, and a queue that is being worked really does usually drain.

## Undertow

Under the working, a loop that no longer needs the thing that started it.
Timeouts make retries, retries make load, load makes timeouts, and a cold cache keeps missing into a backend too busy to let it refill, so the system has settled onto a second floor as stable as the first (Bronson, Aghayev, Charapko and Zhu).
No bug and no bad actor: the retry is correct, the fetch is correct, the acceptance is correct, and the loss is the sum of them.

## Nerve

The comfort of a system visibly trying, and the catch that the trying is the load:
that the work the failure generates is enough to cause the failure, so the cause is free to leave and nothing changes when it does, the outage and its engine the one loop seen twice,
and it'll drain is not patience but the one belief that guarantees the waiting continues.

## Echo

After the run: a service flat for an hour with the burst twenty minutes gone, a backend at full CPU serving requests whose callers hung up long ago, an incident with no bug to find and every component passing its own tests (Huang, Magnusson and colleagues).
And the remedy, at a price and never free: shed the traffic hard, drain the dead work, turn the retries off, which means refusing users the machines could have served, and going further than feels warranted, since anything above the lower threshold keeps the loop fed (Beyer, Jones, Petoff and Murphy).
And the bound held in view, because the failure is real and conditional at once: it needs work amplification and a system already in its vulnerable band, so the whole of it is that when the work a failure makes is enough to cause the failure, the cause is free to leave.
