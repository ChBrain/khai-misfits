# Play: The Time It Used to Buy

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

The Time It Used to Buy.
A power station is a building with something very heavy turning inside it, and the turning is how it makes electricity. It is also, incidentally, a flywheel: thousands of tonnes of steel spinning in step with the grid, storing kinetic energy, and physically resisting any sudden change in the frequency the whole system runs on. Nobody bought that. It was not on the invoice, not in the contract, not on any meter, and not in the case for building the plant. It came free with the machine, the way weight comes free with a wall. And every response the system has to a sudden loss, automatic and human alike, was quietly calibrated against the time that free thing was buying.

## Arc

The bend is this: inertia does not stop a disturbance or make it smaller, it slows how fast the frequency moves, so what it provides is an interval.
It runs on a service nobody ever sold.
The Heavy Turning was never anyone's contribution. It is a property of mass in motion, present because generating electricity happened for a century to require a large object spinning at speed, and absent from every invoice because nothing about it was ever a product.
The One Who Closed It is right to close. An old plant reaches the end of its economics, or a rule on emissions arrives, and shutting it is the correct and intended outcome of both. The closure case counts fuel, staff, output and carbon. It has no line for kinetic energy, because kinetic energy has never been a line on anything.
The New Fleet is right too, and this is where the misfit refuses the easy story. Inverter-based generation is not declining to provide inertia the way a person declines a subscription. It is constituted differently: there is no synchronised spinning mass in it to store the energy. Every unit was approved under the connection standards that existed when it was built and complies with them completely, and those standards never asked, because when they were written the question had never needed asking.
So the pool thins, and it is nobody's number. Inertia is a property of which machines happen to be running at a given moment, which is an emergent fact about the whole fleet that no participant chooses, no market prices, and no operator screen displayed. It is ample for decades and then it is not, and there is no threshold anywhere that announces the crossing.
Then a disturbance arrives, and The Scheme is outrun. In South Australia on 28 September 2016, with wind at roughly 40 per cent of supply and few synchronous machines online, the loss of the Heywood interconnector produced a rate of change of frequency above 6 Hz per second: faster than the under-frequency load-shedding scheme could arrest the fall before it dropped below 47 Hz. The scheme was not defective, not neglected and not mis-set. It was correct, and it did not have time. Every setting in it would have worked at the rate of change the system used to have.
And the correction spends the thing that caused the change. Requiring two large synchronous machines to stay online, which the operator did within weeks, means burning fuel and emitting carbon to keep steel turning for its physics rather than its electricity. Synchronous condensers and grid-forming inverters do the job without the emissions and cost real capital, paid by the system rather than by whoever caused the shortfall, because nobody caused it. And every answer starts by naming and measuring a quantity that ran unpriced for a hundred years, which is a reform nobody is thanked for until the event that proves it necessary has already happened.

## Stakes

Whether a system can account for a service that arrives free, when arriving free is exactly what keeps it off every ledger that would have noticed it going.
Every plot moves the same way: toward closures and connections that are each correct and compliant, and away from any instrument that could have priced what they were jointly spending. The production cannot make an inverter store kinetic energy it does not have, and it cannot make a closure case count a quantity that no accounting anywhere recognised. What is left to fight over is not whether to retire the old machines, everyone agrees they should go, but who pays to replace a property that nobody was ever paid to provide, and whether a system can learn to watch a thing before the first time it is missed.
