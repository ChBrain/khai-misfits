# Play: Whoever Comes Instead

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Whoever Comes Instead.
A committee weighs two policies. One spends the resource now and leaves the far future poorer; the other rations it and leaves the far future better off. Everybody agrees the second world is better. Then somebody notices that a policy of that size changes when people meet, when they marry, when they conceive, so that within a few generations the two worlds are not the same people living differently: they are different people. And the people who live in the poorer world are not worse off than they would have been, because in the other world they are not.

## Arc

The bend is this: the choice does not decide what happens to people, it decides which people there are.
It runs on a mechanism nobody built.
[The Timing](piece_the_timing.md) is the whole of it and it is not exotic. Any policy large enough to matter reaches into ordinary life: who moves where, who meets whom, which year a couple decides they can afford a child. Shift a conception by a month and a different person exists. Two centuries on, the population under one policy and the population under the other overlap in nobody.
So [The Ones Who Come](persona_the_ones_who_come.md) arrive into the poorer world, and they are the only people there are to ask. Their lives are harder than the lives in the other world, and their lives are worth living, and they would not trade them for non-existence, because that is not a trade anybody can make.
And [No One to Point At](plot_no_one_to_point_at.md) is where the machinery seizes. The rule almost everybody reasons by is that an act is wrong because it is worse for somebody: you find the injured party, and the injury is the wrong. Run that here and it returns that the policy was not wrong. Not that the harm was small: that there is no bearer.
[The Ones Who Would Have Been](persona_the_ones_who_would_have_been.md) cannot supply one. They are not people who lost something. They are people who do not exist, and a claim needs a claimant.
Then the repair, and its price. You can widen the rule so that an outcome can be worse without being worse for anyone, which does condemn the policy. It also stops being a rule about people, and once outcomes are ranked impersonally the ranking goes on ranking: it will tell you that a vast population with lives barely worth living beats a small one that flourishes, and it will not stop saying so.
So the committee is left holding a clearly worse world, a clearly correct piece of reasoning, and no one it can name.

## Triggers

**[The Choice Changes Who](plot_the_choice_changes_who.md)**
Cue: a policy large enough to matter, and the ordinary business of who moves where and when a couple decides they can afford a child. Closes when the two futures have been shown to contain different people rather than the same people living differently, by a mechanism nobody designed and nobody can switch off.

**[Glad to Be Here](plot_glad_to_be_here.md)**
Cue: the poorer world, arrived at, with people in it. Closes when their lives have been found worth living and they would not trade them, so the only parties with standing to complain do not wish the other policy had been chosen.

**[No One to Point At](plot_no_one_to_point_at.md)**
Cue: the ordinary rule that an act is wrong because it is worse for somebody, applied to find the injured party. Closes when the search returns nobody, not because the harm was small but because it has no bearer.

**[The Rule Is Not Optional](plot_the_rule_is_not_optional.md)**
Cue: the obvious suggestion that the rule should simply be dropped. Closes when dropping it has been shown to cost the thing that makes wrongs answerable to the people they are done to, which is most of what morality was being used for.

**[Widening It Costs More](plot_widening_it_costs_more.md)**
Cue: the better suggestion, that an outcome can be worse without being worse for anyone. Closes when the widened rule has condemned the policy and gone on to rank a vast barely-living population above a small flourishing one, and declined to stop.

**[And the Loss Is Real](plot_and_the_loss_is_real.md)**
Cue: everything checked, and the poorer world still poorer. Closes when the loss has been shown to be real and unassignable at once, so the committee holds a worse world, a correct argument, and nobody to name.

## Stakes

Whether a wrong needs somebody it was done to.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a choice that constitutes its own subjects, a population that is glad to exist and has no complaint to make, a rule that finds no injured party because there is none, and a repair that condemns the policy by giving up the connection between wrongs and the people they are done to, and away from any account that keeps both. The production cannot ask the ones who come to wish they had not, and it cannot produce the ones who would have been, since they are not people who lost something. What is left to fight over is not whether the poorer world is worse, everybody agrees it is, but what to call a harm that is real, foreseen, chosen, and borne by no one.
