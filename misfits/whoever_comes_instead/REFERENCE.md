# Whoever Comes Instead: Reference

The research warrant for Whoever Comes Instead.
The term is Parfit's; the play runs on the non-identity problem and the person-affecting principle it defeats,
all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging non-identity as **a choice that constitutes its own subjects, evaluated by an instrument that assumes it
does not**: the ordinary way to weigh a policy is to ask what happens to people under each option and compare,
and that comparison needs the same people on both sides. A measure large enough to matter does not leave them
the same. It reaches into who moves where, who works, and which year a couple decides they can afford a child,
and a conception shifted by a month is a different person: so two centuries out the two options do not hold one
population living differently, they hold two populations overlapping in nobody. The play models the documented
mechanics, the timing that nobody designed, the population that arrives glad to be here, the alternative
population that is not a party, the rule that finds no injured party because there is none, the repair that
condemns the policy and then will not stop, and the loss that survives all of it, as the load-bearing vectors of
the structure, not as a tale of a committee failing to care about the future.

The governing law is that a person-affecting principle needs a bearer, and a choice that determines identity
removes one without removing the harm. The rule that an act is wrong because it is worse for somebody is not a
technicality: it is the thing that makes wrongs answerable to the people they are done to, and it is doing that
work correctly here. What it cannot do is compare a person against their own non-existence, because
non-existence is not a condition somebody is in. So the search for the injured party returns nothing, and it
returns nothing for the right reason.

What removes any possibility of blame from the committee is that every step it takes is the step it should
take. The projections are honest, the discount rate is argued, the better world is identified and agreed on,
and the decision cannot be declined, since abstaining selects a policy as surely as voting does. The
substitution happens underneath the comparison rather than inside it, so nothing in the method is wrong and
nothing in the method can report what has gone missing: a room that reasoned more carefully would reach the
same verdict sooner.

What removes it from the people who bear the cost is that their standing is destroyed by the same fact that
makes them the ones bearing it. The complaint available to them is that the other policy would have been better
for them, and it would not: under that policy they are absent. Their lives are worth living, which is what
makes the case a paradox rather than an atrocity, since a life not worth living hands the complaint back and
dissolves the puzzle instead of solving it. The harm survives only in the range where nobody suffers enough to
have a claim.

And the repair costs the connection it was called in to defend. Widening the standard so that an outcome can be
worse without being worse for anyone does condemn the policy, immediately and cleanly, because what blocked the
verdict was the requirement that a comparison have a subject. Lifting that requirement is also what lets
outcomes be ranked at all, so the same move that reaches this case reaches every case, and it will go on to
rank a vast population with lives barely worth living above a small one that flourishes. Nobody has found the
amendment that takes the first and declines the second, which is why the standing dispute is still standing.

It is distinct from the house's **Seed Corn**, where this period's number is made by withdrawing from the next,
so the present looks strong and the future pays: there the injured party is real and identifiable, the
successor who inherits the depleted base, and the remedy is to measure the future and put its cost on today's
sheet. Here measuring the future changes nothing, because the cost is already measured and agreed: what is
missing is not the number but anybody to charge it to, and no accounting reform produces a claimant.

It is distinct from the house's **The Horizon Problem**, where every planning instrument has a finite edge and
what lies past it is structurally absent, so the uncosted future lands on whoever arrives there: there the
future is not in the account and the repair is to extend the reach and enter it as a constraint. Here the
future is fully in the account, two centuries out, costed to the decimal by a room that agrees on the figure.
The instrument sees everything and still returns not wrong, so no extension of its reach touches the problem.

It is distinct from the house's **The Made Ground**, where living well on a place raises the floor its heirs
must clear, so a descendant answers for a threshold it laid no grain of: there the inheritance passes to
somebody who would have existed either way, and the unfairness is that the same person is handed a body built
for the old test and a world sitting the new one. Here there is no such person. The descendant under one policy
and the descendant under the other are not one heir with two inheritances, they are two heirs, which is exactly
what removes the unfairness the other play turns on.

It is distinct from the house's **Missing From the Count**, where the undercount is known, quantified, published
and barred from being applied, so a measured error is carried uncorrected: there the people missed exist, are
countable, and the block is a legal rule that could in principle be changed. Here the people who would have
been do not exist, cannot be counted by any method, and the block is not a rule anybody wrote: it is that a
claim needs a claimant, so there is no reform that would let the correction through.

It is distinct from the house's **Sound Both Ways**, where reason's demand for the condition of the condition
carries a valid instrument past what any experience could confirm, and it returns a thesis and an antithesis on
one question: there the surplus is the failure, two answers where there should be one, and the fault lies
upstream in the question. Here the reasoning produces a single answer and everybody accepts that it is valid.
The trouble is not that the method contradicts itself but that it agrees with itself, and the verdict it agrees
on is one nobody can hold.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the committee as
  careless or the policy as reckless, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  projections are honest and the better world is agreed on in the room; the decision cannot be declined, since
  abstaining selects a policy too; the mechanism that shifts who is conceived belongs to no one and is a
  property of acting at scale rather than of acting badly; the rule that finds no injured party is applied
  properly and is doing the work it exists for; and the people who bear the cost are glad to be alive and are
  right to be. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that a choice
  can determine who exists and be evaluated by an instrument that assumes it does not.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Widening the standard so that
  an outcome can be worse without being worse for anyone does condemn the policy, and it goes on to rank a vast
  population with lives barely worth living above a small one that flourishes, and no version has been found
  that takes the first without the second. Dropping the person-affecting rule outright is worse, since it is
  the reason a victim can demand an answer and a bystander cannot be charged. Appealing to the rights of the
  people who come is available and contested, and the play stages it as an attempt rather than a fix. Producing
  the alternative population is not an option, because they are not people who lost something.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the problem, its independent formulations, the proposed repairs
  and forty years of commentary are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: this is a problem in moral
  philosophy and not an empirical finding, so nothing here is a measurement and the play makes no claim about
  what any actual committee did; the strength of the identity-shifting mechanism over long horizons is an
  assumption of the argument rather than a demographic result, and how quickly two populations diverge in
  practice is not settled; several serious attempts to keep the verdict without widening the standard are live
  and are cited above rather than dismissed, so the play stages the case as open; the person-affecting
  restriction has able defenders and the production does not adopt its abandonment; and the Repugnant
  Conclusion is itself disputed, with a substantial literature holding that it should be accepted, so its use
  here as the price of the impersonal repair is a position and not a verdict. **The play makes no claim that
  obligations to future people do not exist**, only that the ordinary rule locating a wrong in the person it
  was done to returns nothing when the choice settles who those people are, and that no repair yet proposed
  costs nothing.
