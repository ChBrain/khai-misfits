## Tension

Here the two limbs separate, and the second is the one with no answer.
Change the size of the areas and the estimate moves, which is the scale effect and is at least
something to reason about: coarser loses variation, finer keeps it, and an analyst can form a view.
Change only the **shape**, holding the number of areas fixed, and it moves again.
**There is no ordering of those answers.** The same territory cut into the same number of pieces a
different way returns a different coefficient, and nothing ranks the tilings, so finer is not truer
and there is no limit the sequence approaches.
And in more than two variables the movement stops being predictable in intensity or even in
direction, so the analyst most familiar with the data is not better placed than anybody else.
