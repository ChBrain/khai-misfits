# Loudest When Hushed: Reference

The research warrant for **Loudest When Hushed** (play and company not yet staged; this warrant is drafted first,
to prove the science and the distinction before authoring). The title is provisional, a reading of the phenomenon
named the **Streisand effect**: an attempt to suppress or remove a piece of information draws far more attention to
it than leaving it alone would, because the act of suppression is itself visible, signals that the information
matters, and provokes defiance and copying on a medium where copies are cheap, all cited below, held with the
finding that the backfire depends on the suppression being visible, seeming unjust or heavy-handed, and meeting a
networked audience, and that quiet, unremarked, or widely-accepted removal need not backfire at all.

## Line of Work

Staging the Streisand effect as a **suppression-advertises structure**: someone wants a piece of information gone,
a photograph, a document, a post, and moves to remove it, by a takedown, a lawsuit, an injunction, a ban. The move
is locally rational: take the thing down and, all else equal, fewer eyes fall on it. But all else is not equal,
because the removal is itself an act, and a visible one. A lawsuit is news, a takedown leaves a hole, a ban is
announced, and each says plainly that here is something someone did not want seen, which is the surest signal that
it is worth seeing. Those told they may not have a thing want it the more for being told, and on a medium where
copying costs nothing, the wanting turns at once into mirrors, reposts, and screenshots that put the thing beyond
any recall. So the suppression draws to the information a flood of attention it would never have drawn on its own,
the obscure photograph seen by a handful becomes the photograph seen by hundreds of thousands, and the attempt to
hide it is exactly what makes it famous. It is authored by no villain: the one who moves to suppress is often
protecting a real and sympathetic interest, a privacy, a reputation, a secret, and acts as any such interest
rationally would; and the crowd that spreads it is not a mob but people reacting to what looks like overreach or
simply drawn to what has suddenly become news. The governing law is not that anyone is malicious, but that **where
an actor moves to suppress a piece of information, the attempt is itself visible and signals that the information
matters, provokes reactance in those told they cannot have it, and invites cheap copying that puts it beyond
recall, so the suppression draws more attention to the information than leaving it alone would, and each rational
move to hide a thing spreads it wider, the opposite of the intent, no villain required**. The play models the
documented mechanics: the suppression that means to hide (the coinage; Jansen & Martin), the signal it sends that
the thing matters, the reactance of those forbidden it (Brehm), the cheap copies that put it beyond recall, the
backfire of amplified reach, and the strategic silence that alone denies the fight its oxygen, as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a censor foiled by a righteous crowd.

It is an information-suppression backfire no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from The Balloon Effect, where pressing on a banned activity in one place merely
displaces it to another, the trade bulging elsewhere: there the activity is relocated and the total roughly
conserved, whereas here nothing is moved, the very same information is amplified in place, seen far more where it
already was because the attempt to remove it advertised it. It is distinct from The Cobra Effect, where a reward
offered to reduce a thing is farmed and breeds more of it: there the engine is an incentive gamed, a bounty that
pays for the thing it meant to end, whereas here there is no reward and no farming, only a suppression that signals
importance and provokes copying, producing not more of the thing but more attention to the one thing suppressed.
And it is distinct from Fixes that Fail, where a fix relieves a symptom and its delayed side effect brings the
problem back through a slow feedback: there the rebound is delayed and runs through symptom relief, whereas here
the backfire is immediate and direct, the act of hiding itself the advertisement, the side effect not delayed but
built into the visibility of the suppression.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the one who suppresses
  as a tyrant or the crowd as heroes, because the source finding it stages is that the backfire follows from the
  visibility of suppression and not from anyone's villainy: the suppressor usually acts to protect a real interest,
  a privacy or a reputation or a secret, and does the locally sensible thing in trying to remove what harms it, and
  the crowd that spreads the thing is reacting to a perceived overreach or drawn by fresh news, not conspiring. The
  loss is authored by no one: it is the attention the information draws because it was suppressed, the obscure thing
  made famous by the attempt to bury it, and the misfit is that the very act meant to hide a thing is the surest
  sign that it is worth seeing.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, to do nothing,
  to let the thing lie unremarked and deny the fight the oxygen that would spread it, but it is not free: the shrug
  means tolerating the very exposure the suppressor wanted gone, accepting that the photograph stays up and the post
  stays live, and it works only before the suppression is attempted, since once the takedown is filed the signal is
  already sent. And where a genuine and urgent harm demands removal, the strategic silence is no option at all. The
  reckoning is that the backfire is inherent in the visibility of suppression, so wherever hiding a thing is itself
  an act that can be seen, the hiding can advertise.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the effect and its name are the Streisand case's and Masnick's, the
  censorship-backfire analysis is Jansen and Martin's, the general backfire model is Brian Martin's, and the
  reactance beneath the defiance is Brehm's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: that the backfire
  is not automatic but conditioned on the suppression being visible, seeming heavy-handed or unjust, and meeting a
  networked audience able and willing to spread the thing, so that quiet, lawful, unremarked, or widely-accepted
  removal can and often does succeed without any backfire, and much material is taken down every day and stays down.
  The scope is held to visible suppression before an audience that can copy and resist, and not stretched into a
  claim that suppression never works or that everything hidden gets out.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the proposed
load-bearing pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Suppression**: carries the trigger (the coinage; Jansen & Martin), the move to remove or hide the
  information, the takedown or lawsuit or ban, locally rational since removing a thing should leave fewer to see it.
- **The Signal**: carries the reason it backfires (Jansen & Martin), that the act of suppression is itself visible
  and says plainly here is something someone did not want seen, the surest sign a thing is worth seeing, so the
  hiding advertises.
- **The Reactance**: carries the psychological pull (Brehm), the wanting of a thing the more for being told one
  cannot have it, the defiance a ban provokes that turns those who would not have looked into those who must.
- **The Copies**: carries the mechanism of irrecoverability (Jansen & Martin), the mirrors, reposts, and
  screenshots that cheap copying spawns on a networked medium, putting the thing beyond any recall the moment the
  suppression draws the eye.
- **The Backfire**: carries the harm (the coinage; Brian Martin), the amplified reach the suppression wins for the
  information, the obscure thing seen far more than it would have been, the outcome the reverse of the intent.
- **The Shrug**: carries the remedy and the bound (Jansen & Martin), the strategic silence that lets the thing lie
  unremarked and denies the fight its oxygen, costly because it means tolerating the exposure the suppressor wanted
  gone and useless once the takedown is filed, and the marker of the hedge, since the backfire needs visible
  suppression before a networked audience and quiet removal need not backfire at all.
