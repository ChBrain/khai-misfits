# The Door Opens Both Ways: Reference

The research warrant for The Door Opens Both Ways.
The term is the house's; the play runs on constitutional self-defence and on what an arrangement must spend to
keep itself, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the amendment clause as **a door that opens both ways**: a constitution has to say how it can be
changed, because one that cannot be changed governs the living by the settled preferences of the dead, cannot
absorb what its authors did not foresee, and holds its authority by enforcement rather than by consent. The same
article, used by whoever holds the required majority, can replace everything else in the document, and it cannot
be drafted to admit improvements and refuse replacements, because nothing inside a procedure distinguishes them.
So a clause is written placing the core beyond amendment, and that clause binds every later generation,
including one that is unanimous and well informed. The play models the documented mechanics, the procedure that
is a condition of lasting, the procedure that is also the route out, the entrenchment written against something
that happened, the entrenchment that commits the objection it answers, the lawful mandate that makes the
question concrete, and the bench with two rulings and no neutral one, as the load-bearing vectors of the
structure, not as a tale of anybody acting badly.

The governing law is that the defence against being ended by procedure is a limit no procedure can lift, so the
protection can only be had by spending the thing protected.

**Nobody in the sequence breaks a rule, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The
framer writes an amendment article, which every serious drafter does. The party registers, publishes and stands,
which is the activity the whole apparatus exists to make possible. The voter votes. The court hears the
application properly and publishes its reasons at length.

What removes any possibility of blame from the framer is that both clauses are right and neither can be
dropped. A text with no amendment procedure is not a stricter constitution but a shorter-lived one. A text whose
procedure reaches everything contains its own repeal, which is not a worry anybody invented: the entrenchment
exists because the door was used. The seat solves the problem twice and hands on two solutions facing each
other.

What removes it from the party is that the objection cannot be to conduct. Forming, publishing a programme,
persuading people and standing for election are the arrangement's own invitation, and there is no moment at
which the seat is doing something a party does not do. Whatever is objected to is a programme that has not been
enacted, and the votes behind it were mostly cast for reasons that have nothing to do with the clause.

And the court closes the case against the last party, because there is no third ruling. Dissolution removes an
organisation that has broken no law and sets aside ballots already counted, which is the thing the institution
exists to prevent. Declining forgoes the protection entirely, since a clause applicable only once the programme
is enacted arrives too late by construction and would not have been worth writing. Declining to rule is a
ruling.

**The bind is stated from inside the literature rather than imputed to it, which is what makes this stageable.**
The scholarship that names constitutional self-defence says plainly that a democracy defending itself must use
means it would otherwise condemn, and works hard on when that is warranted: how imminent the danger must be, how
narrow the measure, what proportionality requires. All of it is real and improves the decision. None of it
produces a version of the decision that costs nothing, because the cost sits in the structure rather than in the
doctrine, so a better test says when to pay rather than how to avoid paying.

It is distinct from the house's **Vetocracy**, which the slate line required it to be tested against before
authoring, and the test was run in the sweep. There each veto point is defensible on its own and the difficulty
is that they **sum**, so the mechanism needs accumulation and time and the repair is to remove points. Remove
that engine, by supposing a constitution with exactly one amendment article and one entrenched clause, and this
misfit stands entire: the bind is present in a single provision read once, nothing accumulates, and there is no
count of anything to reduce.

It is distinct from the house's **Time Inconsistency**, and the two **invert**. There a government cannot bind
its successor, so a promise made today is not credible tomorrow and the difficulty is the absence of a binding
device. Here the framers **can** bind their successors, the device works exactly as intended, and that is the
difficulty. One misfit is about a commitment that will not hold; this one is about a commitment that holds
against everybody who comes later, including those who agree with it.

It is distinct from the house's **The Name Does Not Move**, where an anchor is chosen for being uncontestable
rather than right, so the error is visible and the name stays on it. That anchor is arbitrary by design and asks
nothing of anybody: nobody is excluded by a specimen in a jar. Here the entrenched clause is chosen for being
**right**, is defended on the merits, and the cost is borne by a party and its voters rather than by a
discipline's vocabulary. One misfit trades accuracy for a handle that holds still; this one trades a
generation's authority for an arrangement that survives it.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast any party as an
  enemy or any court as an oppressor, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: an amendment
  procedure is a condition of a constitution lasting and every serious drafter writes one; no procedure can be
  drafted to permit improvements and refuse replacements, since a majority threshold measures agreement and not
  merit; the entrenchment answers a route that was actually used rather than a theory; the objection to it is
  the same objection that produced the amendment article, so both sides are right at once; the party breaks no
  law and its voters voted for ordinary reasons; and the court has two rulings and no neutral one, because
  declining to rule is a ruling. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the
  misfit is that the protection can only be had by spending the thing protected.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Entrenching only a narrow
  core reduces what is placed beyond reach and does not change that something is. Higher amendment thresholds
  slow the route without closing it and obstruct ordinary revision at the same rate. Requiring a referendum
  moves the question to the electorate, which is the body whose composition is at issue. Proportionality tests
  and imminence requirements make the decision better and locate the moment of payment rather than removing
  it. Waiting until a programme is enacted is the option the clause exists to refuse, since by then the
  procedure it protects has been used. And knowing the whole structure, which every party does, leaves a
  hearing listed and an application to be ruled on.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the theory of militant democracy, the drafting of the Basic Law,
  the Strasbourg jurisprudence and the scholarship on unamendability are theirs. The bounds are carried with
  them and they are heavy: **whether entrenchment and party dissolution protect a constitutional order or
  damage it is genuinely contested**, by serious people on both sides, and the play carries the argument
  rather than settling it; the instruments described have been used against ordinary political opposition as
  well as against the case they were written for, which is part of the record and part of the dispute; the
  account treats constitutional arrangements that differ widely as one structure, which is the play's own
  simplification, since most constitutions entrench nothing and some entrench a great deal; and the play names
  no party, no country and no judgment as rightly or wrongly decided. **The play makes no claim that any
  dissolution was correct or incorrect, that any clause should be adopted or repealed, or that any order is
  more or less secure than another**, only that a constitution must be changeable to last, that what can be
  changed can be ended, and that the clause closing that route binds the people it was written to serve.
