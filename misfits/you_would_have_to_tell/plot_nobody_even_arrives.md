## Tension

Here the narrowing moves upstream of the profession entirely, and out of its sight.
The reasoning takes about a minute and is correct: I might say something that would have to be
passed on, and I would rather not be somewhere that is decided by another person's reading of a
threshold. **Nobody here was deterred by a misunderstanding. They were deterred by understanding it.**
And the duty requires that they understand it, since the limit must be disclosed, so the fact of the
rule travels further than the rule: it reaches people through a form, a website or a friend, before
any clinician is involved.
There is no session in which this absence could be noticed, no practitioner who could describe it
and no survey of practitioners that reaches it. **The largest part of the cost is the part that
produces no evidence of itself**, which is why nothing inside the profession could ever have found
this and why the finding had to come from somewhere else.
