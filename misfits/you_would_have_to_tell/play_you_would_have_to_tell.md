# Play: You Would Have to Tell

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

You Would Have to Tell.
A therapist must tell somebody if a patient makes a serious threat against an identifiable person. That is the law in most of the country, it exists because a foreseeable victim was not warned, and it is right.
Because it is the law, the therapist has to say so at the start, before anything is said that would trigger it.
So the patient knows, before they speak, that the one person trained to hear this is the one person who cannot keep it.

## Arc

The bend is this: the duty is a rule about what must be **passed on**, and its whole effect is on what is **said** in the first place.

It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing. The duty removes the listener's discretion at exactly the point where discretion is what a patient is relying on: below the threshold the therapist may use judgement, and above it they may not, which is what makes the rule worth having and what makes the room unsafe to speak in. And the duty must be disclosed, because a patient cannot consent to treatment without knowing its limits, so **the deterrent arrives before the disclosure it deters**, in the first session, in a sentence everybody agrees is owed.

Take the duty first, because the play has to establish that it is **right**. It was written because somebody told a therapist about an intention and nobody told the person it concerned, and she died. A profession that hears such things and passes none of them on is not offering confidentiality, it is offering a place to say them where nothing happens. Every settlement below has to answer that, and none of them does it for free.

Then the fact that inverts it. The patient does not lie and does not conceal. **They simply do not raise it.** The subject that would have been the whole point of the hour becomes the subject that is not brought into the room, and everything else is discussed honestly, so nothing looks wrong from the inside: the notes are full, the sessions are useful, the therapist has no sense of a gap. Surveys after the ruling found therapists reporting exactly this, patients more reluctant to discuss violent thoughts, and patients lost at the moment the limit was explained.

Then the sharper half, which is upstream of all of it. Some do not come at all. A person who suspects what they might say, and who now knows what would happen if they said it, does not book the appointment, and there is no session in which their absence could be noticed. **The channel narrows before anybody is in it**, and the narrowing is invisible by construction, because the missing are not on any list.

Then the measurement, which is why this is a play rather than a worry. Duty-to-warn laws arrived at different times and in different forms across the states, which makes them comparable. Read that way, the mandatory duty is associated with **more** of the outcome it was written to prevent, on the order of five per cent of the homicide rate, and with a movement in suicide too. Nobody can point at a case. **The harm is a rate**, which is the only form it can take, since the whole of it consists of things that did not get said.

And nobody has done anything wrong. The court was protecting somebody who should have been warned. The legislature codified it. The clinician discloses the limit because withholding it would be worse. The patient withholds because the listener is compelled, which is true. Everybody in the chain is behaving well and the number moves.

Reach for a settlement and each one costs the thing the rule was for. A firewall, a promise that nothing said here goes anywhere, is the duty's own removal, and it gives up the warnings the duty does produce. Raising the threshold narrows what must be passed on and leaves the patient with the same problem, since the limit is still disclosed and they still cannot tell where they sit against it. Not disclosing the limit buys disclosure by removing consent, which no profession will write down. And telling patients the duty is rarely used is an invitation to gamble on a stranger's reading of a threshold, offered by the person who would be doing the reading.

## Stakes

Whether a rule can be owed, correctly written, correctly followed, and still leave the thing it protects against more likely.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether therapists are careless, because the clinician here does everything properly and in the right order, and not whether confidentiality should be absolute, because the play refuses that and says why, but whether a duty about passing something on can survive its own effect on whether the thing is said. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the duty is owed and unreplaceable, that disclosing it is required and lands before anything it would deter, that the withholding is neither lying nor concealment and leaves no trace in any record, that some of the loss happens before anybody enters the profession's sight at all, that the outcome the duty was written to prevent is measurably more common where it binds, and that every remedy either removes the duty, removes consent, or invites the patient to gamble.
