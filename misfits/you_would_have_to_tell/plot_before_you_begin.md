## Tension

Here the ordering is put in the open, and it turns out to be forced.
Consent requires the limit to be disclosed before anything it limits, so the sentence that deters
arrives before every disclosure it would deter. That is not tactlessness and not excessive caution.
It is the obligation discharged in the only honest order.
Check the alternatives and each is worse. Disclosed after the disclosure, the limit is a trap. Not
disclosed at all, candour is bought by removing consent, and no profession will write that down.
Softened further, the wording changes how it is felt and not what it says, and what it says is the
operative part.
**So the deterrence is not a side effect of an awkward conversation.** It is a structural consequence
of doing the right thing in the right sequence, and there is no version of the sequence that does
not have it.
