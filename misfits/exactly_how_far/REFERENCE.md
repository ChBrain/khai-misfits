# Exactly How Far: Reference

The research warrant for **Exactly How Far**, a staging of the phenomenon the literature calls the
**alliance security dilemma** (Glenn Snyder): that inside an alliance the strength of a guarantee is a
single dial with a bad end at each stop, so tightening it to answer the ally's fear of abandonment
inherits the ally's quarrels and surrenders the choice of when to fight, while loosening it to keep that
choice reads as unreliability and pushes the ally to arm, to provoke, or to look elsewhere. It is held
with the finding that both fears are well founded, that both parties act on evidence, and that the cure
for each horn is the other horn.

## Line of Work

Staging the alliance security dilemma as a **one-dial-two-bad-ends structure**: a guarantee is not a
switch but a setting, and the setting is read by three audiences at once. The ally reads it as the
measure of whether it will be alone on the day. The adversary reads it as the measure of what it may do
without meeting the guarantor. The other partners read it as the measure of what their own guarantee is
worth, since a patron that hedges on one is a patron that may hedge on them. There is no reading that is
wrong and no reader who is being unreasonable. The ally asking to be told exactly how far is a state
whose survival depends on the answer, and a state that did not ask would be negligent. The guarantor
declining to say is a state that does not want its wars chosen for it by a smaller partner with a border
dispute, and one that signed away that choice would be reckless. The adversary reading the treaty for
what it does not cover is doing the work an adversary exists to do. The trap is that the same act
answers all three, and answers them in opposite directions. Precision is the ally's reassurance and is,
in the same words, a published map of what is not defended (Schelling; Snyder). Ambiguity restores the
guarantor's choice and, in restoring it, removes the certainty the deterrent was made of. And because a
patron's conduct toward one partner is the only evidence the others have about their own guarantee, the
hedge cannot be made quietly: reputation is indivisible across the network, which is what turns a
bilateral doubt into a general one (Christensen and Jack Snyder). The play models the dial, the
published map, the restored choice, the ally's hedge, the watching partners and the bound as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, and not as a tale of a faithless patron or a grasping client. The
ordinary expectation is that a firmer promise is a safer one; the finding is that firmness and safety
come apart, and that every increment of one is spent from the other. The governing law is that **where
one state's protection of another is set by a single dial of commitment, the firmness that reassures the
ally publishes to the adversary the map of what is not covered and binds the guarantor to a quarrel it
did not choose, while the ambiguity that restores the guarantor's choice reads to the ally as
unreliability and to the adversary as opportunity, so each horn's cure is the other horn**.

It is an inference structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Over a Barrel, the hold-up problem, which needs a sunk
relation-specific investment and an appropriable quasi-rent and is cured by integration: nothing here is
sunk, no quasi-rent is appropriated, and tighter integration makes the entrapment horn **worse**, which
is the exact inverse of the hold-up remedy. It is distinct from Someone Will Catch Me, the Samaritan's
dilemma, which carries one risk and one remedy, to precommit to withhold: precommitting to withhold is
here not the remedy but the trigger, since it is the abandonment signal in its purest form, and the
guarantor who makes it has bought its freedom by destroying the thing the freedom was for. It is
distinct from The Calm You Pay For, Bode's sensitivity integral, where suppressing a disturbance in one
band must raise it in another because the integral is conserved: there the trade is a mathematical
invariant in one system with one designer, whereas here nothing is conserved and the trade exists only
because three parties read one signal in three directions, so it would vanish if any audience stopped
reading. And it is distinct from The Double Bind, where contradictory injunctions bind a party who may
neither obey both nor name the contradiction: there the prohibition on speaking about it is what makes
the bind, whereas here everyone may say plainly what the dilemma is, and saying it plainly is precisely
what publishes the map, so candour is the mechanism rather than the escape.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast a
  faithless patron or a grasping client, because the finding it stages needs neither. A small state
  whose survival rests on someone else's promise and does not ask exactly what the promise covers is
  not admirable but negligent. A large state that signs away its choice of when to fight, to a partner
  with a border dispute it did not choose, is not loyal but reckless. An adversary that reads a public
  treaty for what it omits is doing the work adversaries exist to do, and would be incompetent
  otherwise. And the partners who watch how one ally is treated and draw conclusions about their own
  guarantee are reasoning correctly from the only evidence they have. Every reading is sound and every
  reader is acting on evidence.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction, because each
  available correction is the other horn. Precision is the remedy for abandonment and is, in the same
  sentence, the map of what is not covered. Ambiguity is the remedy for entrapment and is, in the same
  silence, the removal of the certainty the deterrent consisted of. Integration is the standard
  remedy for a relationship whose parties fear each other's opportunism, and here it deepens the
  entrapment horn rather than curing it. There is no setting of the dial that is not paid for at the
  other end, and the crisis reads whatever setting is standing on the morning it arrives.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The alliance security dilemma and the inverse relation of abandonment and
  entrapment are Glenn Snyder's; chain-ganging, buck-passing and the network reading are Thomas J.
  Christensen and Jack Snyder's; the credibility mechanics and the threat that leaves something to
  chance are Thomas Schelling's; the parent security dilemma between adversaries is Robert Jervis's and
  John Herz's and is cited to mark the line rather than to be staged; and the finding that entrapment is
  much rarer in practice than the theory predicts is Michael Beckley's and is carried as the play's
  bound. Two of these authors share a surname and the third shares one with a scholar the house already
  cites, so the given names are carried in every affected cell: Glenn Snyder and Jack Snyder are
  different people, and Thomas J. Christensen is not Clayton Christensen of The Success Trap. The
  house's `scholarPolicy.homonyms` declares both surnames for that reason. The scope is held to
  alliances in which one party's guarantee is the other's principal security, and is not stretched into
  a claim that alliances are unwise, that any particular guarantee is hollow, or that the entrapment
  risk is as large as the theory alone would suggest.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Dial**: carries the structure itself (Glenn Snyder), commitment as one continuous setting rather
  than a switch, with abandonment at the loose end and entrapment at the tight one, both fears well
  founded, so there is no position on it that answers both.
- **The Published Map**: carries the cost of the tight end (Schelling; Glenn Snyder), that a guarantee
  precise enough to reassure names the ground it covers and thereby names the ground it does not, handing
  the adversary the one document that says where it may move.
- **The Restored Choice**: carries the cost of the loose end, that ambiguity gives the guarantor back
  the decision of whether to fight, and that the decision it gets back is exactly the certainty the
  deterrent was made of, so the freedom is bought from the thing the freedom exists to protect.
- **The Hedge**: carries the ally's rational response, that a state which doubts its guarantee arms,
  seeks its own deterrent, moves first while it still can, or opens a line to the other side, each of
  which is prudent from where it stands and each of which worsens the guarantor's position.
- **The Watchers**: carries the network (Christensen and Jack Snyder), that a patron's conduct toward one
  partner is the only evidence the others have about their own guarantee, so no hedge is private, and a
  doubt raised in one capital is priced in all of them.
- **The Bound**: carries the condition and the contested magnitude (Beckley), that the trap needs an
  ally whose security genuinely rests on the guarantee and an adversary reading it, that entrapment is
  in practice far rarer than the theory predicts because great powers evade what they signed, and that
  the play stages a real dilemma whose severity is disputed rather than a settled law.
