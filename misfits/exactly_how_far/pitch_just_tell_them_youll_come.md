# Pitch: Just Tell Them You'll Come

## Taxonomy

[Exactly How Far](play_exactly_how_far.md). The default key the whole production is read in.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Tenor

Warm and impatient, the tone of somebody who thinks this has been overcomplicated.
The ally is frightened and asking for very little: say you will come, say it plainly, say it where it can be heard.
Nothing false in it. A promise nobody can quote is not a promise, and a government that would not say the words for a partner whose survival depends on them has already answered the question.

## Undertow

Under the plain words, a public document read by people it was not written for.
To reassure at all you must be specific, and what you name as covered names the ground you did not, so the sentence that steadies the ally is the sentence that tells the adversary where nothing will meet them (Schelling; Glenn Snyder).
And the other setting is no refuge: stay vague and the choice comes home to you, which is exactly the certainty the deterrent consisted of, since a deterrent is a belief in somebody else's head.

## Nerve

The reasonableness of the request, and the catch that the request cannot be granted:
that the ally is entitled to ask exactly how far, that the patron is entitled to decline to be bound, that both are right,
and that just tell them you'll come is the sentence in which the whole dilemma is mistaken for a failure of nerve.

## Echo

After the run: an ally that has done its own arithmetic and begun its own programme, a move planned exactly where the guarantee stopped, and a hedge that could not be made privately because every other partner was reading it (Christensen and Jack Snyder).
And the crisis, which reads whatever setting was standing that morning and permits no adjustment, since a promise improved under fire is not believed by anybody.
And the bound held in view: it needs an ally whose security genuinely rests on the promise, and entrapment is in practice far rarer than the theory predicts, because great powers routinely evade what they signed (Beckley).
