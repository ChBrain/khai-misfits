# Play: On Good Authority

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

On Good Authority.
A claim is made once, on thin or absent evidence. A single small study, a speculation offered as a speculation, a figure with a decimal in the wrong place. It claims no more than it is, and by itself it would sink or be checked. But someone cites it, in good faith, because a working scholar cannot re-derive every upstream claim and must at some point trust a cited source as the ordinary practice of the field allows.
Then a second author cites the first, and a review cites the second as established, and a model builds on the review. Each of these is a reasonable act. To cite a prior source is to stand on the record, and to summarize a body of work in a review is to compress it, and no one of them is positioned to walk the whole chain back to the root. And as the references accrete, the claim takes on a standing the data never gave it, because belief tracks the density of citations and not the weight of evidence, and past a point the sheer count of references reads as many confirmations when it is only many echoes of the one source (Greenberg).
And the network does not merely repeat but distorts. Authors cite the supportive over the critical, a tentative claim is reported in a review as settled, and now and then a paper is cited for a claim it never made, so the record hardens around an assertion that no single study established, its qualifications stripped and its doubts dropped in the retelling (Greenberg). And the name is older than the diagnosis and honest about the shape of it: the Woozle is Milne's, the creature Pooh and Piglet track round and round a spinney by following their own deepening footprints, growing surer a whole pack walks ahead as the ring of tracks widens, a circle of one's own making mistaken for a crowd (Milne). The term was carried from there into research method to mark just this, a statistic or claim that gains its authority by being cited rather than by being shown (Houghton; Gelles and Straus).
And here is why no one in it is a villain. No one fabricates, and each citation is an ordinary and defensible act. The author who cites a source trusts it as the practice of citing allows and as re-verifying every upstream claim would make the work impossible, since most citers reasonably copy a reference rather than read the original, and a field that demanded otherwise would never move (Simkin and Roychowdhury). The reviewer who calls a claim established is compressing honestly, and the reader who takes a much-cited claim as sound is reading the record exactly as it is meant to be read. The loss is authored by no one: it is an authority accreted from the aggregate of individually reasonable citations, and the misfit is that a claim repeated in good faith can carry a weight the evidence never gave it.
And here is the way out, and why it is rarely walked. One can expose a woozled claim by tracing the citation chain back to its root, to find the primary study is small or absent or says something the citations do not, as the citation-network dissection does (Greenberg), and the documented cases show it can be done, a misplaced decimal in an iron measurement propagated for decades until someone tracked it home (Rekdal). But to trace one claim to its source is a research project in itself, slow and thankless, and the practice of citing exists precisely so that no one must, so the escape asks a reader to distrust the very signal, a dense record of references, that ordinarily and rightly marks a claim as sound, and it does not scale to a literature of a thousand claims each resting on a chain no one has walked.
And here is the bound that keeps it honest. This is a mechanism attested in case analyses of particular literatures and in documented instances, not a measured rate at which literatures distort, so the house stages the structure and holds that not every much-cited claim is a woozle and that a dense citation record is more often earned than manufactured. The citation-network dissection and the account of citation bias, amplification, and invention are Greenberg's, the image and the creature are Milne's, the naming in research method is Houghton's and Gelles and Straus's, the academic-urban-legend cases are Rekdal's, and the citation-copying estimate is Simkin and Roychowdhury's. The house stages that a claim can be cited into authority with no villain, and not that a well-cited fact is therefore false, the effect a real and demonstrated hazard and not a licence to distrust every reference.

## Arc

The bend is this: a claim cited and re-cited in good faith accretes an authority the evidence never gave it, because belief tracks the count of citations and not the weight of data, so a weak assertion can be repeated into fact with no villain and no fabrication.
It runs on a record that grows in references while the data stands still.
A claim is made once, on thin evidence, claiming no more than it is.
And it is cited, in good faith, one author standing on the record because it cannot re-derive every upstream claim (Simkin and Roychowdhury).
And the references accrete. A second citing the first, a review the second, the count of citations read as many confirmations when it is only many echoes of the one source (Greenberg).
And the record distorts. The supportive cited over the critical, the tentative reported as settled, a paper now and then cited for a claim it never made (Greenberg).
And the claim reads as fact. A standing the data never gave it, taken as established by a reader reading the record as it is meant to be read.
And the way out is a trace-back. The chain walked to its empty root, slow and thankless and rarely done, and the bound held, a mechanism attested in cases and not a rate (Greenberg; Rekdal).
