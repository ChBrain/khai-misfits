# Place: The Literature

## Taxonomy

[On Good Authority](play_on_good_authority.md). Where a claim is entered once and then cited, and cited again, until the reference list around it stands thicker than the evidence beneath it. Hosts [Said Once](plot_said_once.md), [Stood On](plot_stood_on.md), [The Growing Count](plot_the_growing_count.md), [Reported as Settled](plot_reported_as_settled.md).

## Owner

## Shown

A shelf of published work, one paper standing on the next, bibliographies that lengthen year on year while the study at the root of them stays exactly what it was (Greenberg).

## Holds

[The Claim](piece_the_claim.md).
The single thin assertion first set on the record, made once and hedged, no more than its evidence allows.

[The Citation](piece_the_citation.md).
The ordinary reference, one author standing on a prior source rather than re-deriving it.

[The Accretion](piece_the_accretion.md).
The reference list thickening while the data underneath does not, a count that reads as confirmation and is mostly echo.

[The Amplification](piece_the_amplification.md).
The record distorting as it thickens, the supportive cited over the critical, a tentative claim reported as settled, a source now and then cited for a claim it never made.

## Offers

To the Source, a record to set an honest, modest result down on. To the Citer, a prior source that can be stood on rather than re-derived, the ordinary economy that lets the literature grow at all: a working scholar cannot re-derive every upstream claim, and most citers reasonably copy a reference rather than read it (Simkin and Roychowdhury).

## Withheld

Any requirement that a citer check what is cited against its root. The literature does not ask a new paper to trace its references to origin before adding one more, so nothing here tells a reader whether the density behind a claim is many independent confirmations or one source, echoed forward (Greenberg).
