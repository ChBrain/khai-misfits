# Piece: The Accretion

## Taxonomy

[On Good Authority](play_on_good_authority.md). The mechanism: the network of references growing where the data does not, so the count of citations reads as many confirmations when it is only many echoes of one source.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

At [the Literature](place_the_literature.md), carried by [Citing](process_citing.md), voiced by [the Citer](persona_the_citer.md): a claim now trailing a dozen footnotes, then a hundred, a bibliography thickening year on year while the primary evidence underneath it stays exactly what it was.

## Load Bearing

A second author cites the first, a review cites the second, and the reference list around the claim grows with each pass, but the underlying evidence does not grow with it: it is the same study, the same figure, standing behind an ever-denser wall of citations pointing back to it or to something pointing back to it (Greenberg). This is the pivot the misfit turns on. Belief, in practice, tracks the density of a citation network more readily than it tracks the weight of the data inside it, so a claim ringed by many references reads as many-times confirmed, when a citation-network dissection shows most of those references trace to the same one or two roots rather than to independent support (Greenberg). Nothing in any single citation caused this; the accretion is what the citations sum to once enough of them have accumulated, an aggregate outcome no one authored.

## Apparent

Reads as a mounting weight of evidence, a claim increasingly hard to doubt simply because so many have already, apparently, agreed with it.
