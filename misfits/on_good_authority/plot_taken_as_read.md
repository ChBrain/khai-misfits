# Plot: Taken as Read

## Taxonomy
[On Good Authority](play_on_good_authority.md).

## Owner
- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue
The claim settled in the record, its origins out of sight, standing wherever the field now needs a citation for it.

## Action
At [the Textbook](place_the_textbook.md), [the Reader](persona_the_reader.md) from [the reader's seat](position_reader.md) on [Take It as Read](plan_take_it_as_read.md) through [Crediting](process_crediting.md) takes up [the Authority](piece_the_authority.md), crediting the claim as fact on the weight of how widely it is cited.

## Stage
The Reader meets the claim already dense with references, reported as established in the reviews it has read, and credits it as fact, because a claim this widely cited is, by the record's own ordinary rule, a claim one may take as known (Greenberg). The term for the shape of what it is trusting predates the diagnosis: the effect was named for the Woozle, tracked round and round a spinney by its own deepening footprints, a circle mistaken for a crowd, and carried from there into research method to mark a claim that gains its standing by citation rather than by evidence (Houghton; Gelles and Straus). The Reader has no way, from where it sits, to tell a hundred independent confirmations from a hundred citations echoing one source, since both look, on the page, exactly like a well-supported fact. No villain: to trust the density of a claim's citations is the sound and ordinary way a literature is meant to be read, the very practice that lets any reader build on work it cannot itself re-derive, and the Reader is doing nothing but reading the record as it presents itself.

## Tension
The Reader believes by the count, and the count is the record's own mark of soundness.
Nothing in the reading tells it that the mark was built by repetition and not by evidence.
