# Plot: Reported as Settled

## Taxonomy
[On Good Authority](play_on_good_authority.md).

## Owner
- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue
The references dense enough now that a review has reason to summarize them, rather than restate each one.

## Action
At [the Literature](place_the_literature.md), [the Citer](persona_the_citer.md) from [the citer's seat](position_citer.md), through [Citing](process_citing.md), meets [the Amplification](piece_the_amplification.md), a review compressing the accreted references into a single settled line.

## Stage
The reviewer surveying a crowded citation list does what a review is for, compressing a body of work into a claim readers can use, and in the compressing a tentative result is reported as established, because that is how the literature now reads and the reviewer is summarizing honestly what it finds. Across the citing that built the pile, the supportive study is reached for more readily than the critical one, so the record tilts before the reviewer ever opens it (Greenberg). And now and then a paper is cited for a claim it never made at all, a citation error copied forward rather than checked, the same mechanism documented in a decimal that traveled through decades of iron-content citations before anyone traced it home (Greenberg; Rekdal). None of this is invented on purpose: the reviewer compresses honestly, the citer who reached for the supportive study did not weigh the record and find it wanting, and the misattribution, once made, is simply copied onward by citers who are doing exactly what citing is for. No villain: a review that reports a claim as settled is doing its job, summarizing what a crowded citation record shows, and the record it summarizes is the one the aggregate of good-faith citations has already built.

## Tension
The review states plainly what the record now says.
The record says more than any single study behind it ever showed, and the review has no way to know that from where it stands.
