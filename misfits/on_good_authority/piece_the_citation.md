## Load Bearing

To cite is the practice that makes scholarship possible at all: no author can re-verify every claim upstream of its own, so it trusts the cited source as the field's ordinary practice allows, and the work proceeds. Most citers, reasonably, copy a reference rather than read the source it points to, an estimate documented across citation studies (Simkin and Roychowdhury). This single citation is defensible on its own, exactly the act the citation-network dissection of one biomedical claim traces at the level of the individual reference before it shows what the references sum to in aggregate (Greenberg). Nothing is wrong yet; a claim cited once has gained a witness, not yet an authority. What makes the citation load bearing is what it becomes once it is not alone.

## Apparent

Reads as an independent confirmation, a second voice agreeing with the first, when it is a second voice standing on the first without having walked back to see what the first was standing on.
