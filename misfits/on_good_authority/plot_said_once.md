# Plot: Said Once

## Taxonomy
[On Good Authority](play_on_good_authority.md).

## Owner
- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue
An open question, and a first result thin enough that it could go either way.

## Action
At [the Literature](place_the_literature.md), [the Source](persona_the_source.md) from [the source's seat](position_source.md) on [Say It Once](plan_say_it_once.md) through [Claiming](process_claiming.md) takes up [the Claim](piece_the_claim.md), setting down a single result on a small study or a speculation, stated plainly with its qualifications attached.

## Stage
The Source enters a result into the record once, and states it as no more than it is: a small study reported as small, a tentative reading offered as tentative, sometimes a figure carrying an error not yet caught, a decimal set down wrong (Rekdal). It hedges what it cannot show, because that is what claiming honestly on thin evidence requires, and the claim it leaves behind is exactly the size of its ground. Nothing here overreaches: a citation-network dissection of one such claim finds, at the root, an assertion no larger than the study that made it, its qualifications intact on the page (Greenberg). By itself, a claim this thin would ordinarily sink for want of support, or be checked and corrected at the source before anything is built on it. It does neither here, only because it has not yet been cited. No villain: the Source claims no more than the evidence allows and states its hedges where anyone can read them, and a scholar who reports a small study as small has done exactly what honest claiming requires.

## Tension
The claim sits on the record, modest and hedged and true to its evidence, waiting only to be used.
Its hedges are the only thing standing between it and the fact it will become, and hedges are the first thing a citation leaves behind.
