# Too Sure to Be True: Reference

The research warrant for **Too Sure to Be True** (play and company not yet staged; this warrant is drafted first, to prove
the science and the distinction before authoring). The title is provisional, a reading of the phenomenon named the
**Paradox of Unanimity**: when independent honest judgments should scatter, their perfect agreement is more likely a sign of
a shared systematic error, a biased line-up, a broken instrument, an unseen common cause, than of the truth, so the very
consensus that should reassure should instead alarm, all cited below, held with the finding that the alarm is a Bayesian
weight on hidden correlation and not a rule that unanimity is ever proof of error, since genuine strong evidence can be
near-unanimous.

## Line of Work

Staging the Paradox of Unanimity as an **agreement-too-perfect-to-be-honest structure**: many observers judge the same
question independently, each carrying its own honest error, so if they are truly independent their answers should scatter, a
spread of near misses around the truth. That is what independence looks like. But suppose a shared systematic error is
present, unseen: a line-up built so one face stands out, an instrument miscalibrated the same way for all, an assumption
every observer holds in common. Then the judgments do not scatter; they fall into line, and the agreement becomes near
perfect. And here is the paradox, set down formally by Gunn and colleagues: past a point, each new voice added to a
unanimous chorus should lower confidence in the conclusion, not raise it, because a perfect agreement is better explained by
the systematic error than by the truth, since truth plus honest independent noise would have produced some dissent (Gunn,
Chapeau-Blondeau, McDonnell, Davis, Allison and Abbott). The naive reading runs the other way: each observer, seeing its own
judgment confirmed by the next, takes the agreement as corroboration and grows more certain the more unanimous the verdict
becomes. So the confidence rises exactly as the ground for it falls away, and the collective grows too sure to be true. It
is authored by no villain: each observer judges honestly and independently, in good faith, and no one colludes; the shared
error that aligns them is unseen and unintended; and reading agreement as corroboration is the ordinary, sensible thing, the
right rule when the errors really are independent. No one's dishonesty makes the verdict too clean. The governing law is not
that anyone lies or conspires, but that **when independent honest judgments should scatter, their perfect agreement is more
likely a sign of a shared systematic error than of the truth, so the very consensus that should reassure should instead
alarm, no villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the scatter that true independence should show, the
hidden common cause that aligns the judgments, the perfect agreement that results, the naive corroboration that reads each
new voice as another confirmation, the false certainty that grows as the ground for it falls, and the sought dissent and
tested independence that alone can tell a real consensus from a correlated error (Gunn et al.; the ancient Sanhedrin rule),
as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of liars rigging a verdict.

It is an agreement-too-perfect-to-be-honest structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Information Cascade, where each observer rationally copies the visible choices of those ahead
and discards its own private signal, so the agreement is manufactured by herding and the judgments are not independent at
all: there the correlation is made by copying, whereas here the judgments are genuinely independent and each honestly its
own, and it is precisely their improbable agreement, given that independence, that indicts a shared unseen bias. It is
distinct from Closing Ranks, where a team's unanimity is manufactured by loyalty and the self-silencing of private dissent,
an illusion of agreement over a suppressed disagreement: there the dissent exists and is hidden, whereas here there is no
dissent to hide and no coordination at all, only honest judgments aligned by a common cause they cannot see. And it is
distinct from The File Drawer, where null results are suppressed so the published record over-represents the positive, the
tell being the missing studies: there the distortion is in what is absent, whereas here every judgment is present and honest,
and the tell is the very concordance of what is there, an agreement too clean for independent error to have produced.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the observers as liars or the
  agreement as a rigged verdict, because the source finding it stages is that the trap needs no dishonesty: each observer
  judges honestly and independently and in good faith, no one colludes, and the systematic error that aligns them, the biased
  line-up, the miscalibrated instrument, the shared assumption, is unseen and unintended. Reading agreement as corroboration
  is the ordinary and sensible thing, the correct rule when errors are independent. No one's fault makes the verdict too
  clean. The loss is authored by no one: it is the false certainty placed in a conclusion whose very unanimity indicts it, or
  the true conclusion wrongly doubted, and the misfit is that the perfect agreement which should reassure should instead
  alarm, since honest independent judgment would have left some dissent.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, but at a real cost: to
  seek dissent on purpose, to test whether the judgments were truly independent, to hunt for the common cause, a red team, a
  second instrument, a line-up rebuilt, and to weight the verdict by the prior chance of a systematic error rather than count
  the agreeing voices (Gunn et al.). But this spends effort and time and the comfort of a clean consensus, it can be run on
  only some questions and never removes uncertainty entirely, and it asks the judge to distrust the very agreement that feels
  like proof. The escape works against the grain of the intuition that more agreement is always more evidence, and none of it
  is the reassurance a unanimous verdict seems to offer.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the Bayesian model of the paradox and its identification example are Gunn and
  colleagues', the ancient rule that a unanimous conviction acquits is the Talmud's, the jury theorem whose independence
  assumption is the hinge is Condorcet's, and the principle that systematic errors correlate while independent errors scatter
  is metrology's and the eyewitness-identification literature's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: the
  alarm is a Bayesian weight on the prior probability of hidden correlation, and not a rule that unanimity is ever proof of
  error. Genuine strong evidence can be near-unanimous, and a real signal will rightly command wide agreement, so the paradox
  is not a licence to distrust every consensus but a caution that agreement past what independent error would produce is a
  flag to check for a common cause. The scope is held to independent judgments under a possible systematic error, and not
  stretched into a claim that consensus is generally worthless or that the majority is usually wrong.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the proposed load-bearing
pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Scatter**: carries the setting, the honest spread that truly independent judgments should show, each near miss its
  own error around the truth, the noise that independence looks like (Condorcet's assumption).
- **The Common Cause**: carries the hidden driver, the shared systematic error that aligns the judgments unseen, a line-up
  built so one face stands out, an instrument miscalibrated the same for all, an assumption held in common.
- **The Perfect Agreement**: carries the phenomenon, the unanimity where scatter was expected, the judgments fallen into
  line, the verdict with no dissent (the Sanhedrin rule).
- **The Corroboration**: carries the mechanism of the trap, the naive reading in which each new agreeing voice is taken as
  another confirmation, so confidence rises with the count of those who agree.
- **The False Certainty**: carries the harm (Gunn et al.), the overwhelming confidence placed in the conclusion just as the
  ground for it falls away, since past a point the perfect agreement is better explained by the common cause than by the
  truth, so the more it agrees the less it should be trusted, drawn distinct from Information Cascade, Closing Ranks, and The
  File Drawer.
- **The Dissent Sought**: carries the remedy and the bound (Gunn et al.), the deliberate seeking of dissent and testing of
  independence and hunting for the common cause that alone tells a real consensus from a correlated error, costly and against
  the grain of the feeling that agreement is proof, and the marker of the hedge, since the alarm is a Bayesian weight on
  hidden correlation and not a rule that unanimity is ever proof of error, genuine strong evidence being able to command
  wide agreement.
