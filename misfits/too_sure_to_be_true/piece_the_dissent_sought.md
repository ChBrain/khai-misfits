## Load Bearing

The deliberate seeking of dissent, the testing of independence, the hunting of the common cause: a red team called in, a second instrument brought to bear, a line-up rebuilt from scratch, weighting the verdict by the prior chance of a systematic error rather than simply counting the agreeing voices. This is the one move that tells a real consensus apart from a correlated error, and it is costly: it spends effort, time, the comfort of a clean answer, and it can only be run on some questions, not all of them (Gunn et al.).

The remedy carries its own bound. The alarm raised by unanimity is a Bayesian weight on the prior probability of hidden correlation, not a rule that agreement is ever proof of error. Genuine strong evidence can command wide, even near-unanimous, agreement, and that agreement is not thereby suspect. The paradox is a caution to check for a common cause, not a licence to distrust every consensus on sight.
