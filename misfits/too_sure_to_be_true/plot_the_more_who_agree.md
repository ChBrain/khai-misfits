# Plot: The More Who Agree

## Taxonomy

[Too Sure to Be True](play_too_sure_to_be_true.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The unanimous verdict is read for what it seems to mean, one confirming voice at a time.

## Action

At [the Bench](place_the_bench.md), [the Believer](persona_the_believer.md) from [the Believer's seat](position_believer.md) on [Count the Agreement](plan_count_the_agreement.md) through [Corroborating](process_corroborating.md) builds [the Corroboration](piece_the_corroboration.md), each agreeing voice taken as another confirmation, confidence rising with the count.

## Stage

The reading is sensible, and the rule behind it is the right one, exactly when the judgments feeding it are independent: more honest witnesses agreeing is more reason to believe them, the way Condorcet's theorem promises. What the reader cannot tell from within the count is whether that condition holds. Each further voice is added to the tally as if it were a fresh, separate check on the truth, when it may be nothing more than the same shared tilt speaking again through a different honest mouth (Gunn et al.). So confidence rises exactly as the ground for it falls, and the rise feels like rigour the whole way up. No villain drives the count higher. The Believer is applying good method to a case that has quietly stopped fitting it.

## Tension

The confidence mounting, and the reader unable to see the ground fall away.
