# Priced In: Reference

The research warrant for **Priced In**. The title is a market-native reading of the phenomenon
Grossman and Stiglitz proved, **the impossibility of informationally efficient markets**: a price
that reveals the informed traders' costly research to everyone for free destroys the incentive to do
the research, so the price can never fully reflect the information it was supposed to aggregate, and
a degree of disequilibrium must persist for gathering to pay at all, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the Grossman-Stiglitz result as a **the-signal-that-reveals-effort-for-free-destroys-the-effort
structure**: information about what a thing is worth is costly to gather, and a market price partly
reveals the informed traders' information to the uninformed the moment the informed act on it, at no
cost to the uninformed. So each uninformed trader rationally declines to gather, reading the answer off
the price instead, and if the price reflected all the information there would be no return to gathering
it and no one would gather, and then the price could not reflect it. The governing law is not that
traders are lazy or that markets are broken, but that **a price that aggregates costly private
information, by revealing that information to everyone for free, destroys the incentive to produce it,
so a fully informative price is self-defeating and the market settles instead at an equilibrium degree
of disequilibrium, a price informative enough to be useful and noisy enough that gathering information
still pays**. The play models the documented mechanics: information as a costly private input and the
price as the public conveyor that carries it from the informed to the uninformed for nothing, the
vanishing return to becoming informed as the price grows more revealing, the noise (from random supply
or uninformed trading) that must persist so the informed can profit and stay informed, and the
resulting equilibrium in which the price is never fully efficient (Grossman and Stiglitz), as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a foolish trader or a rigged market.

It is a market-information paradox no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from the house's Information Cascade, where each actor observes the actions of
those ahead and rationally copies them, discarding a private signal they actually hold, so the herd
locks onto early noise: there the private signal exists and is set aside because public actions
outweigh it, here the private signal is never gathered at all because the price already reveals others'
costly research for free, so one is a suppression of a held signal by imitation and the other a
suppression of the production of the signal by free-riding on a public price. It is distinct from Cheaper
Not to Know, the house's rational ignorance of the voter, where a citizen declines costly political
knowledge because a single vote almost never decides: there is no market and no price there, the object
is the electorate's basis for choice, and the ignorance is not self-referential, whereas here the very
efficiency of the price is what removes the incentive that would produce the efficiency, a paradox that
turns on the price aggregating information and Cheaper Not to Know has no aggregator to turn on. It is
distinct from The Winning Bid, the house's winner's curse, where the bidder who most overestimates a
common value wins and so overpays: there the loss falls on the winner through the dispersion of
estimates in an auction, here the result is a standing property of a continuous market's price, that it
cannot be both fully revealing and worth becoming informed for, with no auction and no single winner.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the
  uninformed trader as a free-loader, the informed as a mark, or the market as a fraud, because the
  source finding it stages is precisely that each choice is rational: reading the answer off the price
  rather than paying to gather it is the correct move when the price already reveals it, and gathering
  information only when the return covers its cost is equally correct (Grossman and Stiglitz). The
  outcome is authored by no one and emerges from the composition, a price that conveys information and
  traders who respond to incentives, so the impossibility of a fully efficient price arises with no one
  cheating and nothing rigged, and the misfit is that the market's very success at spreading information
  is what undercuts the production of it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. There is no
  arrangement that delivers both a fully informative price and a standing incentive to become informed:
  the two are in fundamental conflict, so the market can only settle at a price that is deliberately less
  than fully efficient, informative enough to be useful and noisy enough that gathering still pays
  (Grossman and Stiglitz). And that resolution is not free either: it requires that noise persist, which
  in the model means traders whose demand is random or uninformed and who, on average, lose to the
  informed, so the degree of efficiency the market does reach is paid for by a permanent, irreducible
  degree of disequilibrium and by the losses of those who supply the noise. The reckoning is that
  efficiency is self-limiting, bought only by keeping the reward for information alive, and never
  completed.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the impossibility result, the conflict between the
  efficiency of the price at spreading information and the incentive to acquire it, and the equilibrium
  degree of disequilibrium are Grossman and Stiglitz's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the
  finding: that the result holds when information is costly and the price is partially revealing, that
  the noise which lets the informed profit is a modelled feature and not decoration, that the claim is
  the impossibility of a price reflecting all information rather than a denial that markets aggregate
  information usefully, and that the paradox is self-referential, the price's own informativeness
  removing the incentive that produces it. The scope is held to markets with costly information and a
  price that conveys it, and not stretched into a general verdict on market efficiency or a claim that
  prices carry no information at all.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Price**: carries the market price as the public conveyor of information (Grossman and Stiglitz),
  which reveals the informed traders' costly research to the uninformed the moment the informed act, for
  nothing, the aggregator whose very success is the trouble.
- **The Cost**: carries the real expense of gathering information (Grossman and Stiglitz), the research
  and analysis an informed trader must pay for before the price can carry any of it.
- **The Free Ride**: carries the uninformed's rational reading of the answer off the price rather than
  paying to gather it (Grossman and Stiglitz), the move that is correct for each and that removes the
  demand for information.
- **The Return**: carries the compensation the informed need to gather (Grossman and Stiglitz), which
  falls toward zero as the price grows more revealing, so that a fully informative price leaves no reason
  to become informed.
- **The Noise**: carries the randomness, from random supply or uninformed trading, that keeps the price
  from fully revealing (Grossman and Stiglitz), the thing that must persist so the informed can hide
  their trades and profit, and without which the informed could not be paid at all.
- **The Disequilibrium**: carries the resolution (Grossman and Stiglitz), the equilibrium degree of
  disequilibrium, a price informative enough to be useful and noisy enough that gathering still pays,
  the market's efficiency permanently and necessarily incomplete.
