# Play: Priced In

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Priced In.
A market price is a marvel: it takes what is known about what a thing is worth, scattered across everyone who has looked into it, and boils it into a single number anyone can read. If a trader has done the work, learned that a company is worth more than the market thinks, and bought on it, the buying nudges the price up, and the number now carries a little of what that trader learned, out in the open, free to read. This is the market at its best, a machine for spreading what is known, and it is exactly what the phrase means when someone says it is all priced in: the answer is already in the number, so why work it out yourself.
And there is the catch, because working it out is not free. Learning what a thing is really worth costs real effort: research, analysis, time, the price of finding out. A trader pays that cost only if there is a return on it, only if knowing something the market does not lets them trade ahead of the number moving. But the moment they trade, the number moves, and their knowledge leaks into the price for everyone to read at no cost at all. So the better the price gets at revealing what the informed know, the less the informed are paid for knowing it, because the edge is gone the instant it shows.
And here is the turn that makes it a trap and not just a wonder. If the price ever became perfect, reflecting everything anyone had found out, then no one could trade ahead of it, and the return to finding anything out would be zero, and a rational trader would stop paying the cost. But if everyone stops finding out, the price has nothing to reflect. So a perfectly informative price is self-defeating: the more completely it does its job of spreading what is known, the more completely it removes the reason anyone had to know it. The price cannot be both worth reading and worth producing at once.
And none of it is folly or a swindle. The uninformed trader who reads the answer off the price rather than paying to find it out is doing the sensible thing, the answer is right there. The informed trader who only pays the cost when the return covers it is doing the sensible thing too. The market that moves the price to reflect the trades is doing exactly what a market is for. No one free-loads in bad faith and no one is fooled: the failure is that the market's very success at spreading information is what undercuts the making of it (Grossman and Stiglitz).
And because a perfectly efficient price is impossible, the market settles for something short of it. It stays a little noisy, a little wrong, because other traders buy and sell for reasons that have nothing to do with value, and that noise is cover: it lets the informed hide their trades in the crowd and keep some of their edge, so the return to finding out stays above zero and someone still does the work. The price ends up informative enough to be useful and disordered enough to be worth improving, an equilibrium degree of disequilibrium, never quite holding the answer it promises, and never able to (Grossman and Stiglitz).

## Arc

The bend is this: a price that aggregates costly private information, by revealing that information to everyone for free, destroys the incentive to produce it, so a fully informative price is self-defeating and the market settles instead at an equilibrium degree of disequilibrium, a price informative enough to be useful and noisy enough that gathering information still pays.
It runs on a price that spreads what is known.
A market price gathers what the informed have found out about what a thing is worth and shows it, in a single number, to everyone who reads it, so a trader who has done no research can read the answer off the price for nothing (Grossman and Stiglitz).
And it turns on a cost the price does not pay. Finding out what a thing is worth takes real effort, and a trader pays for it only when the return covers it, but the moment they trade their knowledge leaks into the price for free, so the better the price reveals, the less the informed are paid (Grossman and Stiglitz).
And a perfect price would pay nothing at all. If the price reflected everything, the return to finding anything out would be zero and the rational trader would stop, so a fully informative price removes the very reason anyone had to inform it, and cannot hold (Grossman and Stiglitz).
And so the finding-out is what dries up. Each uninformed trader rightly reads the answer off the price, each informed trader rightly stops paying when the return is gone, and the demand for information falls as the price that spreads it succeeds, the making of it undone by the spreading (Grossman and Stiglitz).
And the whole is authored by no one. The reader who free-rides on the price is rational, the researcher who quits when the edge is gone is rational, the market that moves the price is doing its job, so the impossibility arises with no one cheating and nothing rigged.
Keep the market a little wrong, and the finding-out survives. Because other traders buy and sell for reasons unrelated to value, the price stays noisy, and that noise is cover that lets the informed hide their trades and keep enough of their edge that gathering still pays (Grossman and Stiglitz): but there is no arrangement that gives both a fully efficient price and a standing reason to inform it, so the market can only rest at an equilibrium degree of disequilibrium, never fully efficient, and the efficiency it does reach is paid for by the noise traders who, on average, lose to the informed, an incompleteness that is not a flaw to fix but the condition of the price working at all.

## Stakes

Whether a price can be both worth reading and worth producing, when the reading is free and the producing is not.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward the price that spreads what is known, and away from the cost of knowing it, because the answer is already in the number and reading it off is free. The production cannot make the price reward the research it reveals, because revealing the research is exactly what strips the reward: the better the number spreads what the informed found out, the less the informed are paid to find it out, and a perfect price pays nothing. The one thing that keeps the finding-out alive is that the price stays a little wrong, noisy with trades made for reasons unrelated to value, so the informed can hide and keep some edge, and that is not a fix but a permanent incompleteness paid for by the traders who supply the noise and lose. It can only show that no one is a free-loader in bad faith and no one is fooled, that reading the answer off the price and quitting the research when the edge is gone are both correct, and that the move which would make the price perfect, everyone informing it fully, is the move that would leave no one any reason to inform it at all, so the price rests forever a little short of the answer it promises, and could be nothing else.
