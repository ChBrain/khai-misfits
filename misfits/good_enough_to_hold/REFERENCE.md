# Good Enough to Hold: Reference

The research warrant for **Good Enough to Hold**, a staging of the phenomenon the literature calls the
**regulatory licence** view of credit ratings (Frank Partnoy): that once a rule of law is written to
reference a private grade, so that investment grade becomes a legal threshold, what the party buying the
grade is buying stops being its information and becomes the permission it unlocks, so demand for the
product rises as its informational content falls. It is held with the finding that the regulator
delegated in good faith and against the industry's interest, that the buyer relying on the threshold is
complying correctly, and that the reference cannot be withdrawn without either building the assessment
at public expense or writing the standard that becomes the next licence.

## Line of Work

Staging the regulatory licence as a **certificate-becomes-a-permit structure**: a rating begins as an
opinion, sold to whoever wants it, worth exactly what its record of being right is worth. Then a rule
points at it. The capital requirement, the fund mandate, the eligibility schedule each say **investment
grade**, and at that moment the grade stops being a forecast a buyer may weigh and becomes a threshold a
buyer must clear. The change is not in the rating and not in the agency. It is in what the grade now
does. An issuer below the line cannot be held by the institutions that hold most of the money, so it
must buy its way above the line, and what it is paying for is legibility to the rulebook rather than an
assessment of itself. Demand accordingly rises as accuracy falls, because a permission does not have to
be right to be necessary (Partnoy). And the rule does a second thing, which is to decide who may issue
the permission at all, so the designation is itself a licence and the incumbents' pricing power is a
creature of the regulation rather than of their record (Lawrence J. White). Meanwhile the investor stops
investigating, and is right to: it is not lazy, it is compliant, and an investment committee that
overrode a rule-recognised grade with its own analysis would be taking a risk it has no mandate to take.
Then a downgrade crosses the line and every holder keyed to the threshold becomes a forced seller in the
same hour, so a private opinion has been converted into a synchronised, mechanical sale (the Financial
Stability Board's principles on mechanistic reliance). And the exit is not free: strip the reference out
and the regulator must build the assessment in house at large expense, or write a substitute standard
that becomes the next licence, which is why the delegation existed. The play models the licence, the
inverted demand, the second gate, the displaced diligence, the cliff and the bound as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, and not as a tale of a captured regulator or a corrupt agency. The ordinary
expectation is that official recognition of a standard raises its quality; the finding is that
recognition detaches the standard's value from its quality and thereby removes the pressure that
produced it. The governing law is that **where a rule is written to reference a private grade, the
grade's value to the party buying it becomes the permission it unlocks rather than the information it
carries, so demand for it rises as its informational content falls, everyone relying on it is complying
correctly, and it cannot be withdrawn without building the assessment at public expense or writing the
next licence**.

It is an inference structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Regulatory Capture, which requires the regulator to be serving the
industry it regulates: here the regulator acts in good faith, acts **against** the industry's preference
at the time, and the distortion arises from the bare act of referencing a private standard, so a
perfectly incorruptible regulator produces it. It is distinct from Priced In, Grossman and Stiglitz's
impossibility, where the return to gathering information collapses because the price already reveals it:
there the mechanism is informational efficiency and the remedy is noise, whereas here the return
collapses because a rule creates demand independent of accuracy, and no amount of noise restores it. It
is distinct from Metric Fixation and Goodhart's law, where a measure taken as a target ceases to be a
good measure because the measured party games it: nothing here is gamed and no behaviour changes to hit
the number, since the corruption is in what the number is **for** rather than in how it is produced. And
it is distinct from On Good Authority, where a claim is believed because of who said it: there the
deference is a judgement about credibility that a reader could revise, whereas here the deference is a
legal requirement that a reader may not revise, and the investor who did revise it would be exceeding
its mandate.

It runs against the house's **I Can See Why I Think So**, on this axis and at the opposite end. There a
judge defers **too little**, to an adviser's bare number, and does worse than the midpoint of the two
estimates would have done. Here a holder defers **too much**, to a grade a rule has made into a permit.
**What sets the sign is whether deference replaces the work or replaces a comparison the work cannot make.**
A holder could form its own view and the rule is what stops it, so the deference substitutes for work that
exists; a judge cannot see the reasoning behind the number they were sent, so nothing is being substituted
for and the case for moving further is a property of errors rather than anything about the adviser.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast a captured
  regulator or a corrupt agency, because the finding it stages needs neither and is weakened by both.
  The regulator that wrote the reference lacked the capacity to assess every instrument itself, was
  pointing at a party with a genuine record at the time, and was acting against the industry's
  preference rather than for it. The issuer buying its way above the line is buying what it must have to
  be held at all, and a treasurer who declined would be denying its own company the capital it was hired
  to raise. The investor relying on the threshold is not lazy but compliant, and an investment committee
  that overrode a rule-recognised grade with its own analysis would be exceeding its mandate and could be
  held to account for it. And the agency would rather be right than wrong, and cannot convert being
  right into revenue once the revenue follows the permission.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Removing the reference
  is the obvious remedy, it was in fact ordered, and it lands the regulator with two options that are
  both expensive. Build the assessment in house and the public pays for a capability the market already
  had, at a scale no agency has been funded for. Write a substitute standard and the substitute becomes
  the next licence the moment a rule points at it, since nothing in the mechanism is specific to credit
  ratings and everything in it follows from a rule referencing a private grade. The delegation exists
  because the alternative is expensive, which is why the licence keeps being reissued under new names.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The regulatory licence view and the detachment of a rating's value from its
  accuracy are Frank Partnoy's, as is the argument that a party whose product is compelled by rule is not
  disciplined the way an ordinary gatekeeper is; the account of the designation as a barrier that
  regulation itself erected is Lawrence J. White's; and the treatment of threshold-keyed rules as a
  source of mechanistic, synchronised selling is the Financial Stability Board's. The staging is the
  house's. One surname here collides with a scholar the house already cites, Leslie A. White on the
  energetics of complexity in The Last Layer, and they are different people; the house's
  `scholarPolicy.homonyms` declares the surname and both cells carry given names, so the index separates
  them. The scope is held to grades that a rule has made into thresholds, and is not stretched into a
  claim that ratings are worthless, that the agencies acted in bad faith, or that any particular rating
  was wrong.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Licence**: carries the conversion itself (Partnoy), the moment a rule says investment grade and a
  private opinion becomes the permission an issuer needs to be held at all, with nothing in the rating or
  the agency having changed.
- **The Inverted Demand**: carries the perverse result (Partnoy), that a permission need not be right to
  be necessary, so the market for the product grows as its informational content falls and the agency
  cannot convert accuracy into revenue.
- **The Second Gate**: carries the incumbency (Lawrence J. White), the designation deciding who may issue
  a recognised grade, so the pricing power belongs to the rule rather than to any record, and competition
  cannot discipline a product that regulation has made compulsory.
- **The Displaced Diligence**: carries the disappearance of the research (Partnoy), that the investor
  stops investigating and is right to, since relying on the rule-recognised grade is compliance and
  overriding it would exceed the mandate, so nobody is being lazy and the analysis simply stops.
- **The Cliff**: carries the systemic cost (the Financial Stability Board), that a rule keyed to a line
  turns a one-notch downgrade into a simultaneous forced sale by every holder bound to the threshold, so
  the opinion has been converted into a mechanism.
- **The Bound**: carries the condition and the price of the exit, that the trap needs a rule referencing a
  private grade and does not arise where the grade is merely advisory, and that removing the reference
  obliges the regulator to build the assessment at public expense or to write the standard that becomes
  the next licence, which is why the delegation was made in the first place.
