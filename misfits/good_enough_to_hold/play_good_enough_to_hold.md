# Play: Good Enough to Hold

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Good Enough to Hold.
A rating starts life as an opinion. Somebody with a method and a record says what they think a borrower will do, sells the view to whoever wants it, and is worth precisely what being right has been worth to them so far. That is a perfectly ordinary product and it disciplines itself in the perfectly ordinary way.
Then a rule points at it.
The capital requirement says investment grade. The fund mandate says investment grade. The eligibility schedule says investment grade. And at that moment the grade stops being a forecast anybody weighs and becomes a threshold somebody has to clear.
Nothing changed in the rating. Nothing changed in the agency. What changed is what the grade now does (Partnoy).
And here is the consequence that does the work. An issuer below the line cannot be held by the institutions that hold most of the money, so it has to buy its way above the line, and what it is buying is not an assessment of itself. It is legibility to the rulebook. A permission does not have to be right to be necessary, so demand for the product rises as its informational content falls, and the agency cannot turn being right into revenue, because the revenue follows the permission.
And the rule does a second thing that gets less attention. It decides who is allowed to issue the permission. So the designation is itself a licence, and the incumbents' pricing power belongs to the regulation rather than to any record they have, and a more accurate newcomer does not take the business, because the business is not accuracy (Lawrence J. White).
And meanwhile the investor stops investigating. Not out of laziness: out of compliance. The mandate permits what the schedule permits, and an investment committee that overrode a rule-recognised grade with its own analysis would be taking a risk it has no authority to take and would answer for it if the position went wrong. So the research quietly stops being done by the people best placed to do it, and every one of them is behaving correctly.
And then a downgrade crosses the line. Every holder keyed to the threshold becomes a forced seller in the same hour, not because anyone panicked but because the rule says so, and a private opinion has been converted into a mechanism (the Financial Stability Board's principles on mechanistic reliance).
And nobody in it is doing anything wrong. The regulator wrote the reference because it could not staff an in-house assessment of every instrument in the market and was pointing at the best thing that existed, against the industry's preference rather than for it. The issuer is raising the capital it was hired to raise. The committee is complying. The agency would rather be right.
And so the misfit is that official recognition of a standard detaches the standard's value from its quality, and thereby removes the pressure that produced the quality. The regulatory licence and the inversion of demand are Frank Partnoy's; the designation as a regulation-made barrier is Lawrence J. White's; the treatment of threshold-keyed rules as a source of synchronised forced selling is the Financial Stability Board's. The house stages the licence, the inverted demand, the second gate, the displaced diligence, the cliff and the bound, and holds the bound in view: that the trap needs a rule referencing a private grade and does not arise where the grade is merely advisory, and that taking the reference out, which was in fact ordered, leaves the regulator to build the assessment at public expense or to write the substitute that becomes the next licence, so the whole of it is that the moment a rule says a thing is good enough to hold, nobody needs to know whether it is any good.

## Arc

The bend is this: where a rule is written to reference a private grade, the grade's value to the party buying it becomes the permission it unlocks rather than the information it carries, so demand rises as accuracy falls, everyone relying on it is complying correctly, and the reference cannot be withdrawn without building the assessment at public expense or writing the next licence.
It runs on a delegation made for good reasons.
A regulator that cannot staff an in-house assessment of every instrument points its rule at the best assessment that exists, which is a private one with a genuine record, and does so against the industry's preference.
And the grade becomes a threshold. Investment grade in the capital rule, the mandate and the schedule, so the opinion is now a line somebody must clear (Partnoy).
And demand inverts. A permission need not be right to be necessary, so the market for the product grows as its informational content falls, and accuracy stops being convertible into revenue.
And the rule sets a second gate. It decides who may issue a recognised grade, so the incumbency and the pricing power are creatures of the regulation and a more accurate newcomer does not take the business (Lawrence J. White).
And the research stops. The investor relies on the threshold because the mandate permits what the schedule permits, and overriding it would exceed the authority the committee holds.
And the line becomes a mechanism. A one-notch downgrade makes every threshold-bound holder a forced seller in the same hour, with nobody having panicked (the Financial Stability Board).
And the whole is held against its own limit. It needs a rule referencing a private grade, not an advisory one, and removing the reference costs either a public assessment capability or a substitute standard that becomes the next licence.

## Stakes

Whether a standard can be made official without being detached from the thing that made it good.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward a step that is correct on its own terms, which each is, and toward a market in which the product everybody must buy is worth buying whether or not it is right, because a rule pointed at a private grade and thereby changed what the grade was for. The production cannot let a careful reader catch it in the usual way, since nothing is captured and nothing is corrupt: the regulator wrote the reference against the industry's preference and because it could not staff the alternative, the treasurer procuring the rating is raising the capital it was hired to raise, the investment committee relying on a recognised grade is complying and would exceed its mandate by doing otherwise, and the agency would rather be right and simply cannot be paid for it. It can only show that the conversion happens at the moment of reference and not at any moment of misconduct, that the designation deciding who may issue the permission hands the incumbents a pricing power that belongs to the rule, that the diligence stops because compliance replaces it rather than because anyone got lazy, and that a line in a rulebook turns a one-notch opinion into a simultaneous forced sale. And it holds the bound in view, because the trap is conditional and not universal: it needs a rule referencing a private grade and does not arise where the grade is advisory, and the exit that was in fact ordered leaves the regulator to build the assessment at public expense or to write the standard that becomes the next licence, since nothing in the mechanism is peculiar to credit ratings and everything in it follows from a rule pointing at a private one (Partnoy; Lawrence J. White), so the whole of the misfit is that the moment a rule says a thing is good enough to hold, nobody needs to know whether it is any good.
