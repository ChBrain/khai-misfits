# Not Worth Wanting: Reference

The research warrant for **Not Worth Wanting**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon named
**adaptive preference formation**, the sour grapes of the fable: where a good is put out of reach, a
person unconsciously revises their wants to fit what they can attain, coming not to want the
unattainable, so they report themselves content and are, and any measure that trusts what people say
they want reads the constrained life as a chosen and happy one, all cited below, held with the
finding that the adjustment is real but hard to measure and not every lowered want is a hidden
deprivation, since some adaptation is a healthy and autonomous making of peace with the possible.

## Line of Work

Staging adaptive preference formation as a **wanting-shrinks-to-fit structure**: a person wants a thing, a work, a freedom,
a life, and finds it out of reach, walled off by poverty or custom or circumstance. And then the mind does a quiet and
merciful thing: it revises the wanting to fit the reaching, so that the unattainable good comes to seem not worth wanting
after all, the grapes the fox cannot reach reframed as sour. Jon Elster named it, adaptive preference formation, and marked
what makes it a trap and not a simple choice: the adjustment happens behind the person's own back, a causal easing of the
tension between what they want and what they can have, not a considered decision to let the thing go. It is locally kind, in
its own coin: to stop wanting what you cannot have spares you the ache of a longing that could never be met, and lets you
rest content within your walls. But the same adjustment does a quiet harm, because what has changed is not the walls but the
wanting, and the person now reports, in perfect honesty, that they are content, that they did not want the thing anyway. So
any measure of how well a life is going that trusts what people say they want, or how satisfied they say they are, reads the
narrowed life as a chosen one and the deprivation as wellbeing, and the constraint becomes invisible exactly to the
instruments meant to find it (Sen; Nussbaum). It is authored by no villain: no one plants the smaller want, the mind eases
its own tension unbidden, and the person who says they are content is not lying and not a fool, but has genuinely come to
want less. The governing law is not that anyone deceives, but that **where a good is put out of reach, a person
unconsciously revises their wants to fit what they can attain, coming not to want the unattainable, so they report
themselves content and are, and any measure that trusts stated preference or satisfaction reads the constrained life as a
chosen and happy one, so the deprivation is certified as wellbeing and goes unseen, no villain required**. The play models
the documented mechanics: the good put out of reach (Elster), the wall that forecloses it, the unconscious turn by which the
wanting shrinks to fit, the honest contentment that follows, the deprivation masked from every preference-trusting measure
(Sen; Nussbaum), and the capability lens that alone looks past what people say they want to what they are able to do and be,
as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a coward talking themselves down.

It is a wanting-shrinks-to-fit structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest neighbours.
It is distinct from The Open Cage, learned helplessness, where repeated uncontrollable failure teaches an actor that effort
does not pay, so it stops trying even when trying would now work: there the agent stops acting but still wants the outcome,
the desire intact and only the effort abandoned, whereas here the wanting itself is quietly revised away, so the person does
not strain against the wall and fall silent but ceases to wish for what lies beyond it, content rather than defeated. And it
is distinct from any reading as mere sensible acceptance or mature letting-go, because the trap is the adaptation that runs
behind the person's back and settles on a good they had reason to value, not the reflective and free relinquishing of a
whim: the fox that decides, clear-eyed and unforced, that grapes are not worth the climb has lost nothing, whereas the one
whose mouth waters still but whose mind has quietly rewritten the wanting to spare the ache has been narrowed without
knowing it, and only the second is the misfit.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the one who wants less as a
  coward talking themselves down, because the source finding it stages is that the adjustment runs behind the person's own
  back: the mind eases the tension between the wanting and the walls unbidden, and no one plants the smaller want. The
  person who says they are content is not lying and not weak, but has genuinely come to want less, and the wanting less is
  a real mercy, sparing the ache of a longing that could never be met. The loss is authored by no one: it is the good that
  goes unmissed because the wanting for it was quietly revised away, and the misfit is that the kindest adjustment the mind
  can make is the one that hides the constraint from the person inside it and from everyone measuring their contentment.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, to judge a life not
  by what the person says they want or how satisfied they report themselves but by what they are actually able to do and be,
  the capabilities open to them (Sen; Nussbaum), but it is neither simple nor safe: it means looking past a person's own
  sincere testimony about their contentment, which risks a paternalism that overrides real and freely-held preferences, and
  it demands the hard judgement of telling a deprivation-driven adaptation from an autonomous one. There is no reading a
  shrunken want from the outside without the danger of mistaking a made peace for a masked loss, or the reverse.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; adaptive preference formation and its name are Elster's, the sour-grapes image
  Aesop's and in the public domain, the welfare critique and the capability turn Sen's, the capabilities account and the
  deprived-women case Nussbaum's, and the careful line between deprivation-driven and appropriate adaptation Khader's. The
  mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: the adjustment is real but hard to measure and easy to misread, and not
  every lowered want is a hidden deprivation, since much adaptation is a healthy and autonomous making of peace with the
  possible that a good life needs. The scope is held to the unconscious narrowing of wants around a foreclosed good the
  person had reason to value, and not stretched into a claim that contentment is always false, that anyone content with
  little has been cheated, or that people cannot freely and wisely let a thing go.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Grapes**: carries the setup (Elster; the fable), the good the person wants and finds out of reach, the work or
  freedom or life walled off by poverty or custom or circumstance.
- **The Wall**: carries the constraint, the limit that forecloses the good and that the wanting will adapt to, poverty or
  custom or plain circumstance, the thing that does not move when the wanting does.
- **The Turn**: carries the engine (Elster), the unconscious revision by which the wanting shrinks to fit the reaching, the
  tension between want and wall eased behind the person's own back so the unattainable comes to seem not worth wanting.
- **The Contentment**: carries the report (Nussbaum), the honest satisfaction that follows the turn, the person saying, and
  meaning, that they are content and did not want the thing anyway.
- **The Masked Loss**: carries the harm (Sen; Nussbaum), the deprivation made invisible to every measure that trusts stated
  preference or satisfaction, so the narrowed life is read as chosen and the constraint is certified as wellbeing and goes
  unaddressed.
- **The Capability**: carries the remedy and the bound (Sen; Nussbaum; Khader), the lens that looks past what people say
  they want to what they are able to do and be, and the marker of the hedge, since it risks a paternalism that overrides
  real preference and demands the hard line between a deprivation-driven adaptation and an autonomous making of peace, not
  every lowered want a hidden loss.
