# Plot: Real But Not Always

[The Third Option](play_the_third_option.md).

## Owner

## Cue

The shift plain now, and the question left over: when does it hold, and when does it not.

## Action

At [the Choice](place_the_choice.md), [the Chooser](persona_the_chooser.md) from [the chooser's seat](position_chooser.md) through [Choosing](process_choosing.md) meets [the Conditions](piece_the_conditions.md), the elicitation methods and attribute structures under which the shift toward [the Contender](persona_the_contender.md) runs strong, weak, or not at all.

## Stage

The size of the shift, and even whether it appears at all, depends on how the choice is put to the Chooser: whether the Contender, the Alternative, and the Decoy are judged jointly or one at a time, how many attributes separate them, whether the task asks for a choice or a rating (boundary and contested-replication work). Under some of these conditions the shift is as strong as the earlier plots staged it, under others it thins to nearly nothing, and the two fair options are judged close to what they would have been with the Decoy absent. No villain: the Alternative offered its honest trade, the Contender won its unsought comparison, the Decoy sat on the menu for an ordinary reason, and the Chooser applied a cue that is sound almost everywhere, and every one of them acted sensibly across every condition tested, whether the shift showed up or not, only the size of the effect moved, never the reasonableness of the actors producing it. The demonstration and the name belong to Huber, Payne and Puto, the wider reach across choice domains belongs to the attraction-effect literature, and the bound belongs to the boundary and contested-replication work that followed both. This is distinct from Spoiled for Choice, where past a point a larger assortment overwhelms the chooser, so a wider range lowers both the odds of choosing at all and the satisfaction of the choice made, the harm the sheer count of options raising the burden of comparison past what the chooser can bear, whereas here a single added option, dominated and chosen by no one, reshapes the choice between a small, unchanged pair, the harm not paralysis from too many options but a preference reversal caused by one specific, badly chosen addition, present even in a set of just three.

## Tension

An effect real and demonstrated, and not a law that any added option always moves a choice.
