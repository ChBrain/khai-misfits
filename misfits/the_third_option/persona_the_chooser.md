# Persona: The Chooser

## Taxonomy

[Chooser](position_chooser.md), inside [The Third Option](play_the_third_option.md). The archetype of the one who compares the three options and shifts toward the Contender, using the easy local win over the Decoy as a stand-in for the harder judgment the original pair actually demanded.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Projection

A gullible mind fooled by an obvious plant, as though a sharper chooser would see through the decoy at a glance, when the cue it follows, that a clear win says something true, is sound almost everywhere and only misleads here.

## Action

Take the easy win. At [the Choice](place_the_choice.md), the Chooser on [Take the Easy Win](plan_take_the_easy_win.md) through [Choosing](process_choosing.md) takes up [the Shift](piece_the_shift.md), favoring the Contender for the clear comparison it wins against the Decoy, then meets [the Governed Choice](piece_the_governed_choice.md) and [the Conditions](piece_the_conditions.md) (Huber, Payne and Puto).

## Shadow

Its cue is sound, and it is sound in exactly the wrong place. The Chooser is no fool taken in by an obvious plant: a clear win in a direct comparison is ordinarily good evidence of quality, and reading it that way serves a chooser well in almost every setting it meets. What it does not see, and has no ordinary reason to suspect, is that the comparison it is reading so reliably was never the comparison that mattered, the genuine trade-off against the Alternative, still unresolved and just as hard as it always was. It substitutes the easy question for the hard one not from carelessness but because the easy one answers itself so cleanly. It keeps its sound reasoning and loses no cause for blame, the reasoner whose good rule of thumb was aimed, this once, at the wrong target.

## Tell

It read a clear local win as a true sign of quality, as it usually should, and let a comparison that never touched the real choice decide the real choice anyway.
