# Plot: Two Fair Options

## Taxonomy

[The Third Option](play_the_third_option.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

Two options sit on the menu, each with an honest claim, and neither one beats the other outright.

## Action

At [the Menu](place_the_menu.md), [the Alternative](persona_the_alternative.md) from [the alternative's seat](position_alternative.md) on [Trade Fairly](plan_trade_fairly.md) through [Offering](process_offering.md) takes up [the Trade-Off Pair](piece_the_trade_off_pair.md), setting itself and its counterpart side by side so that each is better on one dimension and worse on the other, with no dominance between them.

## Stage

The Alternative is better on one dimension, its counterpart better on a second, cheaper against better made, nearer against bigger, and no line of reasoning inside the pair alone settles which one to prefer (Huber, Payne and Puto, 1982). Neither option is padded or disguised: each is exactly what it claims to be, and the trade between them runs both ways, so a chooser who prefers either one is not making an error. Whatever share falls to each option, absent anything else on the menu, tracks only how a chooser happens to weigh the two dimensions against each other. No villain: nobody built this pairing to confuse anyone, it is simply what two genuinely different, genuinely competitive options look like standing side by side, and the difficulty of choosing between them is the ordinary difficulty of comparing apples to oranges, not a trick. The pair is offered plainly, on its own honest terms, and would remain a fair fight forever if nothing else joined it.

## Tension

A choice that is hard precisely because it is fair.
