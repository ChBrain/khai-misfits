# Plot: A Clear Win Stands In

## Taxonomy

[The Third Option](play_the_third_option.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The local win in hand, and a harder choice, the real one, still to make.

## Action

At [the Choice](place_the_choice.md), [the Chooser](persona_the_chooser.md) from [the chooser's seat](position_chooser.md) on [Take the Easy Win](plan_take_the_easy_win.md) through [Choosing](process_choosing.md) takes up [the Shift](piece_the_shift.md), favoring [the Contender](persona_the_contender.md), whose clear win against the Decoy stands in for the harder trade-off judgment against [the Alternative](persona_the_alternative.md).

## Stage

The share that falls to the Contender rises once the Decoy joins the set, and rises specifically because the Decoy is dominated by the Contender and not by the Alternative (Huber, Payne and Puto, 1982). Regularity says adding an option to a set should never raise the odds of choosing something already there, since a new option can only draw share away and never add to what was present, and independence says the choice between two options ought not turn on the presence of a third that neither chooser nor either option asked for. Both give way at once, and neither the Contender nor the Alternative has changed in any way that would justify it: same price, same features, same honest trade-off between them as before the Decoy ever arrived. No villain: the Chooser's cue, that a clear win signals quality, is sound almost everywhere a chooser meets it, and is simply misapplied here, borrowed from a comparison that was never the real one. What moved is the comparison available, not the comparison that actually decides between the two real options, and the Shift is that easy comparison substituting for the harder one.

## Tension

A preference reversed by a comparison that was never the real choice.
