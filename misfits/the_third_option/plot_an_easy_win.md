# Plot: An Easy Win

## Taxonomy

[The Third Option](play_the_third_option.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The decoy stands beside the option it fails to beat, and the comparison settles itself before anyone asks a question.

## Action

At [Beside It](place_beside_it.md), [the Contender](persona_the_contender.md) from [the contender's seat](position_contender.md) on [Stand Beside It](plan_stand_beside_it.md) through [Comparing](process_comparing.md) takes up [the Local Win](piece_the_local_win.md), beating [The Dominated One](persona_the_dominated_one.md) cleanly and decisively on every count, its own merits unchanged.

## Stage

Because the Decoy is worse than the Contender on every dimension that matters, the comparison between them is unusually easy: the Contender wins outright, a win that costs nothing to see and nothing to doubt (Huber, Payne and Puto, 1982). The Contender does nothing to earn this win beyond standing where it already stood, its price, its features, its own standing exactly what they were before the Decoy arrived. The win is real and trivial at once, real in that the Contender does beat the Decoy, trivial in that beating a dominated option says nothing about beating a fairly trading-off one like the Alternative. No villain: the Contender does not place the Decoy on the menu and does not seek to dominate it, the comparison is simply true, arranged by no one, least of all by the option that wins it. What makes the win worth watching is not the comparison itself, which decides nothing about the real choice, but the credibility it is about to lend a comparison it was never part of (the attraction-effect literature).

## Tension

A real, unsought win about to be spent somewhere it was never earned.
