# Plot: The Decoy Joins

## Taxonomy

[The Third Option](play_the_third_option.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

A third option arrives on the menu, set down beside the pair for no reason connected to the choice it will end up shaping.

## Action

At [Beside It](place_beside_it.md), [the Decoy](persona_the_decoy.md) from [the decoy's seat](position_decoy.md) on [Round Out the Menu](plan_round_out_the_menu.md) through [Joining](process_joining.md) takes up [the Decoy](piece_the_decoy.md), joining the set worse than [the Contender](persona_the_contender.md) on every dimension that matters and not worse than [the Alternative](persona_the_alternative.md).

## Stage

The Decoy is defined by its relation to the other two and not by anything remarkable in itself: worse than the Contender on every dimension that matters, and not worse than the Alternative, so it loses outright to one option and merely trades off against the other, exactly as the original pair traded off between themselves (Huber, Payne and Puto, 1982). No rational chooser would ever take it, since a strictly better option already sits beside it on every count, and choosers rarely do. Its arrival needs no design behind it: a catalog keeps an old model listed, a plan not yet retired stays on the sheet, a weaker candidate files to run because running costs little. No villain: the decoy's presence needs no intent to steer anyone toward anything, a discontinued model or an older plan explains it fully, and nobody who places it is thinking about what it will do to the choice between the other two. What matters is only the asymmetry it introduces the moment it sits down: dominated by one option, merely different from the other, present without needing to be wanted.

## Tension

An option present that will be chosen by no one, and change everything anyway.
