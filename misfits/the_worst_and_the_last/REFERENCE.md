# The Worst and the Last: Reference

The research warrant for **The Worst and the Last**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon named
the **peak-end rule** with its companion **duration neglect**: an extended experience is remembered
not by the sum or the average of its moments but by its most intense moment and its ending, so the
self that recalls an episode and chooses whether to repeat it can value it oppositely to the self
that lived it, preferring a longer suffering that tapers to a shorter one that stopped at its worst,
all cited below, held with the finding that the strict average-of-peak-and-end formula is an
idealization and the neglect of duration is a strong tendency rather than an absolute law, with
documented moderators.

## Line of Work

Staging the peak-end rule as a **peak-and-end-outweigh-the-duration structure**: a person lives through an extended
affective episode, a painful medical procedure, a cold hand held in ice water, a stretch of a film, felt moment by moment
across its whole length, every second of it counting while it lasts. Memory cannot store the episode second by second, so
it does what memory must and compresses the whole to a few salient features, keeping the most intense moment, the peak,
and the final moments, the end, and letting the long middle and the sheer length go. Both anchors are sensible things to
keep: the peak marks how bad or good it got, and the end marks how it resolved, the most recent state and often the one
that bears on what to do next. But the compression drops the duration and the average almost entirely, a duration neglect
so complete that a long episode and a short one with the same peak and end are remembered as near equals (Fredrickson and
Kahneman). And so the remembered value of an episode can invert its lived value: a longer painful trial that ends in
milder pain is remembered as better, and chosen again over a shorter one that ended at its worst, even though it contained
more total pain moment for moment, a preference for more suffering bought by a gentler ending (Kahneman, Fredrickson,
Schreiber and Redelmeier). The trap closes because the self that keeps the score is the self that chooses. The
experiencing self lives the episode and is gone; the remembering self keeps the peak-and-end summary, tells the story, and
decides whether to return, so we choose by remembered utility and not by the utility we actually lived, and the two come
apart in a way neither can simply be called wrong (Kahneman). It is authored by no villain: there is no manipulator in the
loop, only one person whose memory compresses an experience as any memory must and whose chooser trusts that memory as the
only record it has, each doing something adaptive and reasonable, and the divergence falling out of the architecture and
not out of anyone's ill will. The governing law is not that anyone deceives or is foolish, but that **an extended
experience is remembered by its peak and its end and not by its duration or its sum, so the self that recalls it can value
it oppositely to the self that lived it, and the chooser optimizes the remembered episode at the expense of the lived one,
no villain required**. The play models the documented mechanics: the episode lived moment to moment, the peak that memory
keeps, the ending weighted far past its share, the duration and the sum that memory drops, the retrospective summary built
from the two anchors, and the choice that summary drives, benevolent where a kinder ending truly improves the remembered
welfare and perverse where more total suffering is chosen for a better close (Fredrickson and Kahneman; Kahneman,
Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier; Redelmeier and Kahneman; Redelmeier, Katz and Kahneman), as the load-bearing
vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a memory that lies.

It is a peak-and-end-outweigh-the-duration structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from More to Stand Still, where the felt level of a sustained good or bad erodes back toward
neutral as it lasts, the opponent process dimming the experience in real time: there the lived feeling itself changes with
duration, the thing felt less as it goes on, whereas here the lived feeling is whatever it is second by second and it is
the memory of the episode that is skewed, the duration dropped from the recall and not from the living. It is distinct
from Once Bitten, where a bad first draw ends the sampling so a variable option's value runs low across separate trials,
the good outcomes never observed: there the distortion is across many trials in what gets sampled next, whereas here it is
within a single remembered episode in how that one episode is summarized, and the peak-end self may well return, but on a
memory that misvalues the whole. And it is distinct from Starting Tomorrow, where a reward is weighted by how soon it
comes and the near present is overvalued against the future: there the bias discounts the future by delay, weighting
moments by their nearness in time, whereas here the past is mis-summarized regardless of when it happened and the length
is not discounted but ignored outright, the two touching time from opposite sides, one overweighting nearness and the
other blanking duration.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the memory as a liar or any
  party as a manipulator, because the source finding it stages is that the trap needs no ill will: memory compresses an
  extended episode to its peak and its end as any memory must, keeping the two most informative anchors and letting the
  duration go, and the chooser trusts that summary as the only record of the episode it holds. No one deceives; the self
  that lived the episode and the self that recalls it are facets of one person, each doing something adaptive, and they
  simply value the episode differently. The loss is authored by no one: it is the lived experience overruled by its own
  remembered summary, and the misfit is that the self that decides is not the self that felt it.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. There are real ways to use the rule well,
  ending a painful procedure gently to leave a kinder memory and a greater willingness to return (Redelmeier, Katz and
  Kahneman), or attending to how a good thing closes rather than only how it peaks, but each asks a party to shape the
  ending deliberately, sometimes to add duration for a better close or to end a good thing at its height, and none of it
  restores the lived episode to its rightful weight in the choosing. The remembering self still keeps the score, and to
  serve the experiencing self against it is to work against the grain of how the record is kept.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; duration neglect and the peak-end average are Fredrickson and Kahneman's, the
  more-pain-preferred-to-less preference reversal is Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier's, the real-time
  against retrospective clinic demonstration is Redelmeier and Kahneman's, the randomized colonoscopy confirmation and its
  benevolent use are Redelmeier, Katz and Kahneman's, and the experiencing-self against remembering-self framing is
  Kahneman's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: the strict claim that a memory equals the plain average
  of its peak and its end is an idealization, the real weighting is not literally two points, and duration neglect is a
  strong and repeated tendency rather than an absolute law, softening where a duration is made salient or meaningful and
  varying with the trend of the episode toward its close. The house stages the robust core, that retrospective evaluation
  is dominated by the peak and the end and largely neglects duration, enough to invert preferences between episodes, and
  not the strict two-moment formula or a claim that duration never counts.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Living**: carries the experiencing self and the episode as lived, moment by moment across its whole length, every
  second counting while it lasts, the discomfort felt in real time before any memory of it is kept (Redelmeier and
  Kahneman; Kahneman).
- **The Peak**: carries the first anchor (Fredrickson and Kahneman), the most intense moment, best or worst, that memory
  keeps as the mark of how far the episode went.
- **The Last**: carries the second anchor (Fredrickson and Kahneman), the final moments, weighted far past their share of
  the whole because they are the most recent state and the one that reads as how it resolved.
- **The Length**: carries what memory drops (Fredrickson and Kahneman), the duration and the sum, the long middle that
  counted in the living and all but vanishes in the recall, the duration neglect that lets a long episode and a short one
  with the same peak and end be remembered as equals.
- **The Score**: carries the remembering self (Kahneman), the retrospective summary built from the peak and the end, the
  story kept and told, and the fact that it is this self, and not the one that lived the episode, that chooses whether to
  return.
- **The Return**: carries the harm and the remedy-bound (Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier; Redelmeier, Katz
  and Kahneman), the choice the score drives, more total suffering accepted for a gentler ending and a good thing forgone
  that ended at its height, benevolent where a kinder close truly improves the remembered welfare and perverse where the
  lived sum is overruled, and the marker of the hedge, since duration neglect is a strong tendency and not an absolute law.
