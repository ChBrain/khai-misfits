# Play: The Worst and the Last

## Name

The Worst and the Last.
A person lives through an extended experience, and it is felt moment by moment as it happens. A painful procedure, a hand held in cold water, a long stretch of a hard day, every second of it counting while it lasts, the discomfort of the fortieth minute no less real than the discomfort of the first. This is the experiencing self, the one that is there for the whole of it, and its ledger is the plain sum: the longer the hurt, the worse the episode, moment for moment.
But the experiencing self is gone the instant the episode ends, and what remains is a memory, and a memory cannot hold an hour second by second. So it does what memory must and compresses the whole to a few salient features, keeping the most intense moment, the peak, and the final moments, the end, and letting the long middle and the sheer length fall away. In Fredrickson and Kahneman's studies the retrospective rating of an affective episode tracked the average of its peak and its end and was almost blind to how long it lasted, a duration neglect so near complete that a long episode and a short one with the same peak and end were remembered as equals (Fredrickson and Kahneman).
And both anchors are sensible things to keep. The peak marks how far the episode went, its worst or its best, and the end marks how it resolved, the most recent state and often the one that bears on what to do next. Nothing in the keeping is a mistake, and yet the summary it builds can invert the episode it summarizes. A longer painful trial that ends in milder pain is remembered as better than a shorter one that stopped at its worst, even though the longer one held more total pain minute for minute: in the cold-water study, subjects offered the choice preferred to repeat the longer trial, choosing more suffering for a gentler close (Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier).
And here is where it becomes a trap, and not merely a quirk. The self that keeps the score is the self that chooses. The experiencing self lives the episode and vanishes; the remembering self keeps the peak-and-end summary, tells the story of what happened, and decides whether to go back. So the choosing is done by remembered utility and not by the utility that was actually lived, and the two come apart: the episode that was worse to live can be the one chosen to repeat, because it is the one that is remembered better (Kahneman). In the clinic the same divide showed plainly, patients' memories of a colonoscopy or a lithotripsy predicted by the peak and the final minutes and not by the duration or the sum of the pain they had reported minute by minute as it happened (Redelmeier and Kahneman).
And here is why no one in it is a villain. There is no manipulator in the loop, only one person. Memory compresses the episode to its peak and its end because it cannot store the whole, and keeping the most intense and the most recent moment is an adaptive and reasonable thing to keep. The chooser trusts that summary because it is the only record of the episode it holds. The self that lived the hour and the self that recalls it are not opponents and not deceiver and deceived: they are facets of one person, each doing something sensible, and they simply value the same episode differently. The loss is authored by no one: it is the lived experience overruled by its own remembered summary, and the misfit is that the self that decides is not the self that felt it.
And here is the way the ending can be used, and why it cuts both ways. Because the end is weighted so heavily, a gentler close can genuinely improve the memory: in a randomized trial, extending a colonoscopy by leaving the scope briefly in place at low discomfort gave patients a kinder ending, a better remembered experience, and a greater willingness to return, a real service to the remembering self though it added to the total discomfort lived (Redelmeier, Katz and Kahneman). But the same lever that a kind hand pulls to comfort, another could pull to flatter a memory that the whole did not earn, and neither use restores the lived episode to its rightful weight in the choosing. The escape works against the grain of how the record is kept.
And here is the bound that keeps it honest. The strict claim that a memory equals the plain average of its peak and its end is an idealization, the real weighting is not literally two points, and duration neglect is a strong and repeated tendency rather than an absolute law, softening where a duration is made salient or meaningful and varying with the trend of the episode toward its close. The duration neglect and the peak-end average are Fredrickson and Kahneman's, the preference reversal Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier's, the clinic demonstration Redelmeier and Kahneman's, the randomized confirmation and its benevolent use Redelmeier, Katz and Kahneman's, and the two selves Kahneman's. The house stages the robust core, that retrospective evaluation is dominated by the peak and the end and largely neglects duration, enough to invert preferences between episodes, and not the strict two-moment formula or a claim that duration never counts.

## Arc

The bend is this: an extended episode is remembered by its peak and its end and not by its duration or its sum, so the self that recalls it can value it oppositely to the self that lived it, and the chooser optimizes the remembered episode at the expense of the lived one.
It runs on a memory that keeps the wrong things, faithfully.
The episode is lived moment to moment, every second counting while it lasts, the plain sum of it the experiencing self's ledger.
And memory keeps the peak. The most intense moment, the mark of how far the episode went, stamped into the record (Fredrickson and Kahneman).
And memory keeps the end. The final moments, weighted far past their share because they are the most recent and read as how it resolved.
And memory lets the length go. The long middle and the duration dropped from the recall, a long episode and a short one remembered as equals (Fredrickson and Kahneman).
And a gentler close is added. A better end that improves the memory though it lengthens the ordeal, a real service and the same lever a flatterer would pull (Redelmeier, Katz and Kahneman).
And the whole is weighed too late. The lived sum set against the peak-and-end verdict that drives the choice to return, and the bound held, duration neglect a strong tendency and not an absolute law (Kahneman).

## Stakes
