# Piece: The Reckoning

## Taxonomy

[The Worst and the Last](play_the_worst_and_the_last.md). The mechanism: the lived sum set back against the peak-and-end verdict, weighed too late to change the choice it drove.

## Place

At [the Next Time](place_the_next_time.md), carried by [Reckoning](process_reckoning.md), voiced by [the Weigher](persona_the_weigher.md): the tally drawn up after the choice to return has already been made on the summary, asking what the whole actually cost against what the ending said it cost.

## Load Bearing

Two selves size up the same episode and can reach opposite answers: the experiencing self that lived every minute, and the remembering self that kept only the peak and the end and now decides whether to go through it again (Kahneman). The Reckoning is the corrective that tries to serve the experiencing self, setting [The Lived Sum](piece_the_lived_sum.md) back against the verdict that actually drove the choice, and it runs against the grain because the summary already made the decision by the time the full total is drawn up. It is a real correction, not a symbolic one, and it costs something to insist on: attention the remembering self was not going to spend. It also holds its own bound: duration neglect is a strong tendency, not an absolute law, and it softens wherever the length is made to matter, made salient, made to mean something on its own (Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier). The Reckoning is the place that softening would have to happen, if it happens at all.

## Apparent

Reads as pedantry, insisting on a total no one felt as a total, a bill nobody thinks to add up that way. It looks like a technicality laid over a decision already settled.
