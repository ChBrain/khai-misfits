## Place

## Load Bearing

It is a condition of being seaworthy rather than a convenience, and its quantity is set by how empty the ship is.
A hull that has discharged its cargo rides high enough that the propeller loses immersion and the rudder loses authority, the bow slams in a seaway, and the hull girder carries a bending moment outside the condition it was designed for. Water is what is available in the mass required, at the place required, at no cost. So the ballast is not an inefficiency somebody tolerates: it is what keeps the ship answering the helm, and the emptier the leg the more of it there is.
