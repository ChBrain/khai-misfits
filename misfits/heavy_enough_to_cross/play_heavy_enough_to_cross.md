# Play: Heavy Enough to Cross

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Heavy Enough to Cross.
A ship that has discharged its cargo rides too high to cross an ocean: the propeller lifts clear, the rudder loses its bite, the bow slams, and the hull girder is loaded in a way it was not designed for. So it pumps seawater into its tanks until it is heavy enough to be seaworthy. That water is taken from one coast and released at another, and everything living in it is taken and released with it.

## Arc

The bend is this: the weight that makes an empty ship safe to cross is one coast's living water, and the emptier the ship the more of it there is.
It begins with a requirement that is not negotiable.
[An Empty Ship Must Be Made Heavy, and That Is Right](plot_an_empty_ship_must_be_made_heavy_and_that_is_right.md). [The Ballast](piece_the_ballast.md) is what puts the propeller back under the surface and the hull back inside its design loads.
And the only weight to hand is the sea itself.
[And the Weight Is Taken From a Living Ocean](plot_and_the_weight_is_taken_from_a_living_ocean.md). [The Living Freight](piece_the_living_freight.md) comes aboard because it was in the water, not because anybody loaded it.
So it goes out where the cargo comes in.
[So It Is Released Where the Cargo Is](plot_so_it_is_released_where_the_cargo_is.md). The tanks must be emptied to take on freight, which puts the discharge exactly where a working coast is.
And [something stays](plot_and_something_stays.md).
Most of what is released does not survive, and the arithmetic only has to work once. [The Fisher](persona_the_fisher.md) was party to nothing and pays.
Then [the remedy is waived where it is unsafe](plot_the_remedy_is_waived_where_it_is_unsafe.md). [The Safety Exemption](piece_the_safety_exemption.md) is written into the convention itself: exchange is not required where the master reasonably decides it would threaten the ship.
And [the hull is sound and the bay is not](plot_so_the_hull_is_sound_and_the_bay_is_not.md). [The Master](persona_the_master.md) did the seamanlike thing, correctly.

Cue: a berth, a cargo waiting, and tanks that must be emptied to take it. Closes when the discharge is shown to land where a coast is worked rather than anywhere at random.

**[And Something Stays](plot_and_something_stays.md)**
Cue: a bay that receives the same discharge, voyage after voyage. Closes when survival is shown to need only one success against a great many failures.

**[The Remedy Is Waived Where It Is Unsafe](plot_the_remedy_is_waived_where_it_is_unsafe.md)**
Cue: a mid-ocean exchange due, and a sea running. Closes when the instrument written to stop the transfer is found to release the ship from doing so.

**[So the Hull Is Sound and the Bay Is Not](plot_so_the_hull_is_sound_and_the_bay_is_not.md)**
Cue: a voyage completed with nothing gone wrong aboard. Closes when a wholly seamanlike passage is found to have moved a coast's water to another coast.

## Stakes

Whether a working coast can stay the coast it was while the ships that serve it arrive empty.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a ballast requirement that is a condition of being seaworthy, a weight that can only be the living sea, a discharge that must happen where the cargo is, an establishment that needs to succeed once, a convention that releases the ship from its own remedy when the remedy is unsafe, and a passage that is faultless from end to end, and away from any account in which a master was careless or an owner cut a corner. The production cannot fault the master, who may lawfully decline an exchange that would endanger the ship, and it cannot fault the architect, whose ballast system is correct design. What is left to fight over is not whether anybody erred but what to call a coast changed by ships that did everything right.
