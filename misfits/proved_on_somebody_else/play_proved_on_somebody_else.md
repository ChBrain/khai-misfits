# Play: Proved on Somebody Else

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Proved on Somebody Else.
To show that a treatment causes an improvement, you have to remove everything else that could have caused it. That is not a stylistic preference, it is what an experiment is: hold the other causes still, vary the one, and read the difference. So the protocol excludes the patient with three other conditions, and the patient on four other drugs, and the patient at eighty-six whose kidneys will confuse the measurement, and it supports the ones who remain with reminders and monitoring and a specialist centre, because adherence that wanders is one more thing that could have caused the difference. Everything removed is removed for a reason, and the reasons are good. Then the answer comes out clean, and it is written on a label, and the drug is given to a woman of eighty-six with three conditions and four other drugs, and nobody in the sequence did anything wrong.

## Arc

The bend is this: the conditions that make a demonstration decisive are the same conditions that make it unlike the place the answer gets used.
It runs on the fact that everything an experiment must remove is what ordinary practice consists of.
Every exclusion has a reason and most of the reasons are protective. Heterogeneity costs statistical power, and a trial without power exposes its participants to risk and inconvenience and returns no answer at the end of it, which is not a lesser result but an unethical one. So a committee that approves a broad protocol has approved a study that may harm people and settle nothing, and a committee doing its job narrows the entry. **The exclusion is a safeguard, and it is the very thing that makes the finding not about the person excluded.**
The regulator wants the clean signal for the same reason: licensing on a muddy one exposes everybody who will later take the drug. The trialist wants it because a contaminated result is not a result. Nobody in the chain is trading rigour against relevance out of laziness, and a better version of each of them makes the same call.
Then the answer travels. It is written on a label that does not say _in people like the ones we studied_, because a label is an instruction and not a bibliography, and the clinician reading it has a person in front of them who needs treating today. The clinician knows the trial was narrower than their clinic. Knowing it produces no other trial.
And the direction of the gap is the part that will not sit still. The characteristics that get you excluded, several conditions at once, a long list of other medicines, being old, are the same characteristics that make you likely to be prescribed something. **The people held furthest from the evidence are the people the evidence is most often used on**, and that is not an irony anybody arranged, it is what happens when you filter for a clean measurement in a population whose messiness is its clinical reality.
Reach for the remedy and it is already named and already old. Schwartz and Lellouch drew the distinction in 1967 and said plainly that trials had taken the explanatory road without asking, and that the pragmatic one would often have been better: run it in ordinary conditions, with ordinary patients, and measure what actually happens. That answer is right, and it costs the precision that made the first answer worth having, and it needs far more people to reach the same confidence, and it is still not run for most of what is prescribed. Report the limits better, as the field has asked for twenty years, and you have described the gap accurately. It is still there in the morning.

## Triggers

**[Everything Else Is What the Field Is Made Of](plot_everything_else_is_what_the_field_is_made_of.md)**
A treatment to be tested, and a question that can only be answered by holding every other cause still. The Trialist writes the eligibility criteria: no severe comorbidity, no interacting medication, an age band, adequate organ function. Each line has a reason and each reason is sound. Closes when the protocol is written and every excluded group has a defensible entry against its name.

**[You Cannot Consent Somebody to a Question You Cannot Answer](plot_you_cannot_consent_somebody_to_a_question_you_cannot_answer.md)**
Cue: the protocol before an ethics committee. A broader entry would be more representative and would also cost the power to detect anything, and a study that exposes people to risk and returns no answer is not a lesser study, it is an unethical one. The Committee approves the narrow version, for the participants' sake. Closes when the protection and the unrepresentativeness are shown to be the same act.

**[A Decisive Answer About a Population Nobody Treats](plot_a_decisive_answer_about_a_population_nobody_treats.md)**
Cue: the trial run at expert centres, with monitoring, reminders and adherence support, because wandering adherence is one more thing that could have caused the difference. The result comes out clean and large and believable. Closes when the effect is established beyond reasonable doubt, in conditions no clinic reproduces.

**[The Label Does Not Say Who](plot_the_label_does_not_say_who.md)**
Cue: licensing, and a label. It carries the indication and the dose and does not carry the inclusion list, because a label is an instruction rather than a bibliography. The Clinician reads it with a woman of eighty-six in front of them, on four other medicines, who would not have been enrolled. Closes when the drug is prescribed, correctly, to somebody the evidence was not gathered on.

**[The Trial That Would Answer It](plot_the_trial_that_would_answer_it.md)**
Cue: the remedy, which is not new. Schwartz and Lellouch named the pragmatic alternative in 1967 and said the explanatory road had been taken without asking. Run it in ordinary conditions with ordinary patients and you get the answer the clinician needs, at the cost of the precision that made the first answer worth having, and it needs far more people, and for most of what is prescribed it is not run. Closes when the remedy is named, costed, and found still absent.

## Stakes

Whether a question can be answered decisively about the people it is actually about.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether anybody cut a corner, because nobody did and each exclusion has a protective reason behind it, but whether proof and relevance can be bought at the same time. The production cannot make the trial resemble the clinic, because the resemblance is exactly what would reintroduce the causes the trial exists to rule out: the messiness is not an imperfection in the setting, it is the setting. It can only show that the safeguard and the unrepresentativeness are one act, that the people held furthest from the evidence are the ones it is most often used on, that the label carries an instruction and not a population, and that the remedy was named in 1967, is right, costs the precision that made the first answer worth having, and is still not run for most of what a clinic hands out.
