# Proved on Somebody Else: Reference

The research warrant for Proved on Somebody Else.
The term is the house's; the play runs on clinical trial design and on what a demonstration has to remove in order to
be a demonstration, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the trial as **a proof whose conditions are the negation of its subject**: to show that a treatment causes an
improvement, every other cause must be held still, which is not a stylistic preference but what an experiment is. So
the protocol excludes the patient with three other conditions, the patient on four other medicines and the patient at
eighty-six whose organ function would confuse the measurement, and it supports the ones who remain with monitoring and
reminders and a specialist centre, because adherence that wanders is one more thing that could have caused the
difference. The play models the documented mechanics, the criteria that are justified line by line, the statistical
power that makes the narrowing an ethical requirement rather than a preference, the support that separates the site
from any clinic, the distance that opens between the population measured and the population treated, the label that
carries an instruction rather than an inclusion list, and the pragmatic trial named in 1967 and mostly still not run,
as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a fastidious methodologist or a captured regulator.

The governing law is that the conditions which make a demonstration decisive are the conditions which make it unlike
the place the answer will be used, so proof and relevance are bought with the same currency and spent against each
other.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The trialist
removes what would confound. The ethics committee refuses a study that cannot answer. The regulator will not license on
a muddy signal. The clinician has a patient today. The excluded patient was excluded for their own safety.

**What removes any possibility of blame from the design is that the exclusion is a safeguard**, and this is the part
that will not resolve. Heterogeneity costs statistical power, and a study that exposes participants to risk and
inconvenience while returning no answer is not a weaker study but an unethical one. So a committee doing its job
narrows the entry, on the participants' behalf, and **the protective act and the unrepresentative act are the same
act**. There is no version of the approval that both protects the enrolled and represents the treated.

What removes it from the regulator is that the evidentiary bar exists because approving on weak evidence has harmed
people. Requiring an effect shown without confounding is what forces the explanatory design, so the demand for
certainty and the narrowness of the study are one requirement seen twice.

What removes it from the clinician is that declining to extrapolate is not the cautious arm. It is an untested
decision with its own harm: the alternative to prescribing on evidence gathered elsewhere is not a better-evidenced
treatment, it is no treatment, and the condition is present today.

**And half the gap is not about the patients at all.** The monitoring, reminders and specialist delivery exist to stop
adherence explaining the result, and no ordinary service reproduces them, so the estimate is of the drug plus the
scaffolding it was delivered on. That half survives even if every enrolled patient were identical to every treated
one, which is why broadening eligibility alone does not close it.

**Then the direction of the mismatch is systematic rather than random, which is the misfit in a sentence.** Comorbidity,
polypharmacy and age are simultaneously the commonest grounds for exclusion from a trial and the commonest grounds for
being prescribed something. **The people held furthest from the evidence are the people it is most often used on**, and
nobody arranged that: it is what happens when a clean measurement is filtered for in a population whose messiness is
its clinical reality.

It is distinct from the house's **True of No One**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages heterogeneity of
treatment effects, where an average benefit describes no individual patient and the subgroup analysis that would
recover them is underpowered by construction. Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating a population in which every
patient responds identically to the treatment, and this misfit stands whole: **the trial setting still differs from the
clinic in its delivery**, since monitoring, reminders and expert administration are conditions of the estimate and not
properties of the people, so effectiveness still falls short of efficacy among perfectly uniform patients.

It is distinct from the house's **A Hundred Years to Vouch for a Thousand**, which stages the calibration window, where
a relation established over one stretch of record is pushed beyond the range it was calibrated on. Remove that engine,
by stipulating that no estimate is ever used outside its calibrated range, and this stands in a form that is worse
rather than better: the clinician would then be barred from prescribing to anybody the trial excluded, and **refusing
to extrapolate produces no answer, it produces an untreated patient.** There the discipline of staying inside the range
is available and costly; here it is unavailable, because somebody outside the range is in the room.

It is distinct from the house's **Stopped for Benefit**, which stages truncation bias in trials halted early, where an
interim look catches a random high and the published effect is overstated. Remove that engine, by stipulating that no
trial ever stops early and every one runs to its planned end, and this stands entire: **nothing here is overstated.**
The estimate is unbiased for the population measured, and the difficulty is which population that was.

It is distinct from the house's **Not While You Are Watching**, which stages the effect of observation on the observed.
Remove that engine, by stipulating measurement so unobtrusive that no participant behaves differently for being in a
study, and this stands: the gap is in who was enrolled and in what was done around them, not in their awareness of
being watched, and a perfectly blind participant in an expert centre is still receiving care no clinic provides.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the trialist as
  fastidious, the regulator as captured or the clinician as careless, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to
  get wrong: showing a cause requires holding the other causes still, which is what an experiment is rather than a
  preference about one; every exclusion carries a written justification and most are protective; the ethics committee
  refuses a broader entry because a study that exposes people and answers nothing is unethical rather than merely
  weak, so the safeguard and the unrepresentativeness are the same act; the regulator's bar exists because approving
  on weak evidence has harmed people, and lowering it to buy representativeness is the failure it was built to
  prevent; the label carries a clinical indication rather than a screening log because an instruction cannot be a
  bibliography; and the clinician prescribes knowing all of it, because declining to extrapolate is not a cautious arm
  but an untested decision with its own harm. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the
  misfit is that proof and relevance are bought with one currency.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The pragmatic trial is the answer,
  was named in 1967 by the people who drew the distinction, and buys relevance back at the price of the precision that
  made the first answer worth having, needing far more participants to reach the same confidence, and it is not run
  for most of what a clinic hands out. Broadening eligibility spends the power the criteria were protecting, which
  returns the ethics committee to the objection it was right about. Observational real-world evidence has exactly the
  confounding the trial was constructed to remove. Subgroup analysis is underpowered by construction and is the
  neighbouring misfit's territory. Reporting external validity better, pressed for since Rothwell, describes the gap
  accurately and leaves it where it was. And knowing the whole structure, which the field has known for sixty years,
  leaves a woman of eighty-six on four other medicines and a prescription that has to be written today.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the explanatory and pragmatic attitudes are Schwartz and Lellouch's, the
  external-validity critique is Rothwell's, and the representativeness analysis is Tan and colleagues'. The bounds are
  carried with them: **whether pragmatic designs recover applicability at an acceptable price is genuinely contested**,
  and the play carries that argument rather than settling it; the representativeness analysis is one national dataset
  and is offered as the documented scale of the mismatch rather than as a rate that transfers; the account treats
  drugs, devices and behavioural interventions as one structure and treats trial design as though a single explanatory
  and pragmatic pole existed rather than a continuum, both of which are its own simplifications; and the play does not
  claim that trial results fail to hold in practice, only that whether they do is a question the design cannot answer
  about the people it excluded. **The play makes no claim about any drug, trial, regulator, clinician or patient, and
  gives no medical, clinical or regulatory advice of any kind**, only that the conditions of proof and the conditions
  of use are not the same conditions, and that the filter and the prescribing run in opposite directions.
