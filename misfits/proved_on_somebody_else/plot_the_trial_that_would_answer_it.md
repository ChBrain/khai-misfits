# Plot: The Trial That Would Answer It

## Taxonomy

[Proved on Somebody Else](play_proved_on_somebody_else.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The remedy, which is neither new nor obscure.
[The Absent Trial](piece_the_absent_trial.md): ordinary conditions, ordinary patients, measure what actually happens.

## Action

The pragmatic design named and costed against the explanatory one, with [the power](piece_the_power.md) that broadening spends and the participant numbers required to buy it back.

## Stage

The distinction is Schwartz and Lellouch's, drawn in 1967, and their own conclusion was that trials had taken the explanatory road without asking and that the pragmatic one would often have been better.
[The Clinician](persona_the_clinician.md) would read it if it existed. [The Regulator](persona_the_regulator.md) cannot lower its bar to produce it. [The Committee](persona_the_committee.md) cannot approve a study it cannot power.

## Tension

The remedy is right, is sixty years old, and is a price rather than an oversight.
Running in ordinary conditions buys back relevance and spends the precision that made the first answer worth having, and it needs far more participants to reach the same confidence, and for most of what a clinic hands out it is not run. Reporting external validity better, which the field has pressed for since Rothwell, describes the gap accurately and leaves it exactly where it was. **The trade between proof and relevance is not a fault in the system. It is the shape of the problem the system exists to solve**, which is why sixty years of everybody knowing has not closed it.
