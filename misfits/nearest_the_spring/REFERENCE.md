# Nearest the Spring: Reference

The research warrant for **Nearest the Spring** (play and company not yet staged; this warrant is drafted first, to prove
the science and the distinction before authoring). The title is provisional, a reading of the phenomenon named the
**Cantillon effect**, the non-neutral path of new money: money does not enter an economy everywhere at once but through
particular hands first, and those nearest the injection spend it at the old prices before it has bid prices up while
those it reaches last, on wages and fixed incomes that adjust slowest, meet the higher prices before the new money
reaches them, so the sequence in which money diffuses redistributes real purchasing power from the late receivers to the
early with no transfer anyone intends, all cited below, held with the finding that the transmission and its direction are
standard while the magnitude and the net winners in any given episode are contested.

## Line of Work

Staging the Cantillon effect as an **injection-order-redistributes structure**: new money has to enter the economy
somewhere, and it cannot enter everywhere at once. It comes in through a particular hand: the miner who digs the ore, the
borrower who takes the new loan, the seller of bonds to the central bank, the government that spends before it taxes. The
one who receives it first spends it for ordinary reasons, and spends it at the prices that prevail now, before the new
money has circulated and bid those prices up, so it takes real goods at the old prices and is better off in goods and not
merely in money (Cantillon). Then the money moves, hand to hand, and as it spreads it raises the prices it touches, not
all at once but along the path it travels, activity and prices lifting in the interval as the new money works through the
economy (Hume). By the time it reaches the last hands, the wage earner whose pay is set for the year, the pensioner on a
fixed income, the saver holding cash, the prices those hands must pay have already risen, and the money that reaches them
buys less than it would have before, so they meet the higher prices with money that has not grown and lose real
purchasing power (Mises). And the whole of the difference between the first hand and the last is the order in which the
money arrived: the early gain and the late loss are not a tax anyone levied or a transfer anyone designed but the plain
consequence of the money entering at a point and spreading over time, so a redistribution runs from the late receivers to
the early with no one moving a shilling from one to the other on purpose (Cantillon; Mises). It is authored by no villain:
the one who injects the money does so for ordinary and often necessary reasons, to fund a war or a road, to stabilize a
faltering economy, to extend commercial credit, to bring gold out of the ground, and each hand that receives the money
and spends it does so exactly as anyone would, at the prices it finds. No one designs the tilt, and no single spender is
placed to see it, since from inside the flow each transaction is a fair exchange at the going price. The governing law is
not that anyone robs anyone, but that **new money enters through particular hands and spreads over time, so those nearest
the injection spend at the old prices and gain while those it reaches last meet the new prices and lose, a redistribution
by the order of the money's arrival that no one intends and no single hand can see**. The play models the documented
mechanics: the injection at a point, the first spend at the old prices, the spread that bids prices up, the last hand
meeting the risen prices, the order of arrival that determines who gains and who loses, and the net tilt visible only in
the aggregate and hedged in its size (Cantillon; Hume; Mises), as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a
tale of a counterfeiter picking a pocket.

It is an injection-order-redistributes structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from More Than It Can Back, where the issuer of the world's reserve currency must run the
deficits that supply global liquidity and so erodes the very confidence its currency rests on: there the bind is a
systemic obligation at the level of the international monetary order, a provider forced to undermine itself, whereas here
the tilt is a domestic distributional effect of the sequence in which money diffuses, no obligation to supply anyone and
no confidence at stake, only who receives the new money first. And it is distinct from Pushing on a String, where
monetary policy loses its traction at the zero bound and the new money will not move the economy however much is offered:
there the failure is that the money does not circulate or stimulate, a lever gone slack, whereas here the money does
circulate and does its work, and the harm is precisely in the path it takes as it moves, a redistribution by the order of
its spreading and not a failure to spread at all.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the one who injects the money
  as a thief or the early receiver as a swindler, because the source finding it stages is that the tilt needs no ill will:
  the money is injected for ordinary and often necessary reasons, a war to fund, an economy to steady, credit to extend,
  ore to mine, and each hand that receives the new money spends it exactly as anyone would, at the prices it finds, in a
  fair exchange at the going rate. No one moves purchasing power from the late to the early on purpose, and no single
  spender is placed to see the tilt, since from inside the flow every transaction is honest. The loss is authored by no
  one: it is a redistribution by the order in which the money arrives, and the misfit is that being nearest the spring is
  an advantage no one granted and being last is a loss no one levied.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. One might enter the money more evenly, as
  a broad and equal transfer rather than through a particular market, so that fewer hands sit ahead of the price rise, but
  money must still enter somewhere and reach the rest over time, and to spread it perfectly and instantly is to abolish the
  very transmission by which monetary policy does its work. And to withhold the injection altogether, to spare the late
  receivers the tilt, is to forgo the war funded, the economy steadied, the credit extended, so the escape trades the
  distributional cost for the loss of the thing the money was created to do, and no injection reaches everyone at once.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as the
  house's. The staging is the house's; the mechanism and the name are Cantillon's, the account of the non-neutral interval
  and the gradual rise of prices is Hume's, the step-by-step diffusion and the formal non-neutrality are Mises's, and the
  modern distributional argument is the monetary-policy literature's. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding:
  that money is non-neutral along the path of its injection, and that relative prices and the distribution shift in the
  transition, is standard across the classical, Austrian, and modern accounts, but the magnitude of the redistribution and
  even the net winners in any given episode are genuinely contested, since the same expansion that reaches asset holders
  first may also lower unemployment and lift wages, so the house stages the transmission and its direction, that the order
  of the money's arrival redistributes from the late to the early, and holds the size and the incidence as disputed, not a
  claim that monetary expansion always enriches the first and impoverishes the last by a settled amount.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element. The play and its company are not yet authored; these are the proposed load-bearing
pieces the warrant commits the staging to carry, one per vector, no filler.

- **The Injection**: carries the origin (Cantillon), the point where new money enters the economy, the mine or the loan or
  the bond purchase or the government spend, since money must enter somewhere and cannot enter everywhere at once.
- **The First Spend**: carries the early gain (Cantillon), the hand nearest the injection spending the new money at the
  prices that still prevail, taking real goods at the old prices before the money has bid them up.
- **The Spread**: carries the transmission (Hume), the money moving hand to hand and lifting the prices it touches along
  the path it travels, not all at once but in the interval as it works through the economy.
- **The Last to Arrive**: carries the late loss (Mises), the hand the money reaches last, on a wage set for the year or a
  fixed income or cash held, meeting the risen prices before the money arrives and buying less than it would have.
- **The Order**: carries the mechanism proper (Cantillon; Mises), the sequence of arrival that alone determines who gains
  and who loses, a redistribution by position in the flow that no transfer intends and no single hand can see.
- **The Tilt**: carries the harm and the bound (Mises; the distributional-policy debate), the net redistribution from the
  late receivers to the early visible only in the aggregate, drawn distinct from More Than It Can Back and Pushing on a
  String, and the marker of the hedge, since the direction is standard while the magnitude and the net winners in any
  episode are contested.
