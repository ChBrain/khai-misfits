# Plot: What the Order Sums To

## Owner

## Cue

The order named as the whole mechanism, and one question left: what the order sums to, and how far that sum can be trusted.

## Action

At [the Overlook](place_the_overlook.md), [the Reckoner](persona_the_reckoner.md) from [the reckoner's seat](position_reckoner.md) through [Reckoning](process_reckoning.md) meets [the Tilt](piece_the_tilt.md), the net redistribution running from the late receivers to the early.

## Stage

Summed across every hand along the path, the order of arrival yields a net redistribution from the late receivers to the early, a tilt visible only in the aggregate and authored by no one, since every transaction that composed it was a fair exchange at the price then prevailing (Mises). Its direction is not in doubt: money entering at a point and spreading over time must advantage those it reaches first and disadvantage those it reaches last, standard across the classical, Austrian, and modern accounts. But the size of the tilt, and even the net winners in any given episode, are genuinely contested, since the same expansion that lifts asset prices for the early holders may also lower unemployment and lift the wages of the last hands (the distributional-policy debate). No villain: every transaction in the flow, from the first spend to the last, is a fair exchange at the going rate, and the tilt is an emergent net that no single hand can see or intend, real in its direction and held to its bound in its size. This misfit is distinct from More Than It Can Back, a systemic obligation of the reserve-currency issuer to run the deficits that supply world liquidity, a provider forced to undermine its own confidence, and distinct from Pushing on a String, money that will not move or stimulate at the zero bound, a lever gone slack, whereas here the money does move and does its work, the harm lying precisely in the path it takes.

## Tension

The tilt real in its direction, and its size, like the question of who nets out ahead, unsettled.
