# Plot: In What Order

## Owner

## Cue

The spread traced plain across the whole path, and one question left standing: what single thing governs who gained and who lost.

## Action

At [the Overlook](place_the_overlook.md), [the Reckoner](persona_the_reckoner.md) from [the reckoner's seat](position_reckoner.md) through [Reckoning](process_reckoning.md) meets [the Order](piece_the_order.md), the sequence of arrival that alone determines who gains and who loses.

## Stage

The sequence in which each hand received the money, and nothing else, determines who gains and who loses along the path (Cantillon; Mises). The first hand gained because it received early and spent before the rise; the last hand lost because it received late and paid after the rise; between them every hand's fortune is set by nothing but its place in the queue, not by need, not by merit, not by any transfer anyone arranged. This is a redistribution that no transfer intends: no ledger records a payment from the last hand to the first, no contract names either party, and yet the sum the first hand gained and the sum the last hand lost are the same difference, moved by order alone. No single hand can see this, since each transacts only with the hand beside it, at a fair price, ignorant of its place in the whole sequence; only from the Overlook does the order become visible as the entire mechanism. No villain: the order is the geometry of a point injection spreading outward over time, the plain consequence of money having to reach one hand before the next, authored by no one's design.

## Tension

The whole of the difference resting on mere sequence, on when rather than what or who.
