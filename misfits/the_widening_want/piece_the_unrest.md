# Piece: The Unrest

## Taxonomy

[The Widening Want](play_the_widening_want.md). The mechanism: the discontent that can turn to collective action, read as a pressure across cases and bounded by mobilization and opportunity, never a guarantee.

## Owner

## Place

At [the Long View](place_the_long_view.md), carried by [Reading](process_reading.md), voiced by [the Historian](persona_the_historian.md): a shelf of cases laid side by side, each one a long rise followed by a sharp reversal, some of them ending in revolt and most of them not.

## Load Bearing

Across the cases where a long improvement was followed by a short sharp reversal, the gap between expectation and gratification opens furthest right where revolt has historically broken out (Davies, "Toward a Theory of Revolution," 1962). But the gap is a pressure toward discontent, not a trigger for revolt: grievance of this kind is widespread, and revolt is rare, and what decides whether a felt gap becomes collective action is organization, resources, and political opportunity, not the size of the gap alone (Gurr, Why Men Rebel, 1970; the resource-mobilization and political-opportunity accounts). The historian's shelf holds both kinds of case, the gap that stayed a grievance and the gap that became a rising, and the difference between them is not written in the gap itself.

## Apparent

Reads as the inevitable product of any reform, as though relief itself were the cause and every improving regime were merely awaiting its own revolt.
