## Load Bearing

Deprivation is felt relative to a standard, not against zero: people measure their condition against what they have come to expect, not against the absence of it, so the sharpest grievance shows up not among those who have least but among those who gained much and had come to look for more (Stouffer; Runciman, Relative Deprivation and Social Justice, 1966; Gurr, Why Men Rebel, 1970). The comfort of the comparison group has moved, or the comfort of one's own recent past has moved, and either way the yardstick is no longer where it used to sit. The discontent is not a report on the absolute level of well-being. It is a report on the gap, and the gap, as established, widens with the gain.

## Apparent

Reads as the malcontent of the comfortable, a complaint that only someone already well provided for would have the leisure to make.
