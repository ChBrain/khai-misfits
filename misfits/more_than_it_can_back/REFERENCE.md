# More Than It Can Back: Reference

The research warrant for **More Than It Can Back**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon the
literature calls **the Triffin dilemma**: Triffin's account of why a single national currency
serving as the world's reserve asset faces an irreconcilable conflict between supplying the world's
liquidity and preserving confidence in what it supplies, because meeting a growing world's demand
for reserves requires the issuer to send out claims faster than the backing behind them can grow,
all cited below, held with the finding that the conflict is sharpest under a fixed convertibility
promise and softens, without vanishing, into a sustainability-and-confidence tension under a fiat,
floating regime.

## Line of Work

Staging the Triffin dilemma as a **supply-versus-confidence bind**: when one country's currency is also the
world's reserve asset, that currency must do two jobs that pull against each other. A growing world economy needs
an ever larger stock of reserves to settle its trade and hold against shocks, and the only way the rest of the
world can accumulate the reserve currency is for the issuer to send it abroad, by running persistent external
deficits. But every unit sent abroad is a claim on the issuer, and as those claims pile up faster than the backing
behind them, gold under the old promise, the issuer's fiscal capacity under the new, the ratio of backing to
claims falls, and confidence that the currency will hold its value weakens. So the very act that supplies the
world its liquidity is the act that erodes the asset's credibility: to furnish enough, the issuer must issue more
than it can back, and to keep faith, it must furnish too little and starve the world of reserves. Neither the
issuer nor the holders are at fault. The issuer sends out its currency because the world demands it, and is repaid
in a real privilege, borrowing cheaply in its own money; the holders accumulate it because they need reserves and
have no better ones. And yet the same rational supplying and holding drive the backing thin, until a rational
holder, seeing the claims outrun the backing, moves to convert or diversify before the others do, and confidence
fails. The governing law is not that anyone blunders or defects, but that **where a single national currency serves
as the world's reserve asset, the issuer must supply ever more of it to meet a growing world's demand for reserves,
and the very outflow that supplies it accumulates claims against the issuer faster than the backing behind them, so
the more the asset is supplied the less credible its value becomes, and the issuer cannot both furnish the world's
liquidity and preserve confidence in what it furnishes, a structural bind with no defector and no exit but a costly
move to a reserve asset no single nation issues**. The play models the documented mechanics: the reserve role
loaded onto a national currency (Triffin), the world's rising demand for reserves that only the issuer's deficits
can meet (Triffin), the outflow of claims that supplies it, the backing thinning as claims outrun it, the failure
of confidence when they do, and the supranational reserve asset that alone resolves the bind (Keynes; the SDR), as
the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a spendthrift government debasing its money.

It is an international-monetary bind no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest
neighbours. It is distinct from Dutch Disease, where a resource windfall lifts a country's real exchange rate and
prices its traded manufacturing out, hollowing the economy that received the boom: there the mechanism is relative
prices in a resource economy and the harm is deindustrialisation, whereas here nothing is mined and no sector is
hollowed, the bind is the reserve role's own conflict between supplying liquidity and holding confidence, a
conflict internal to a currency doing double duty. It is distinct from Debt-Deflation, where falling prices raise
the real weight of nominal debt and force distressed selling that drives prices lower still: there the spiral runs
through a rising real burden of debt under deflation, whereas here nothing deflates, the currency is wanted more
and more, and what erodes is not a price level but confidence in an asset the world is eager to hold. It is
distinct from The Bank Run, where each depositor withdraws because it fears the others will and a fundamentally
solvent bank is felled by a self-fulfilling panic: the reserve system does end in a run-like scramble to convert,
but the bank run's engine is a pure coordination panic on an institution that was sound, whereas here the backing
genuinely thins as the reserve role forces the issuer to over-supply, so the loss of confidence is a rational
response to a real and structural deterioration, not a groundless panic, and the vulnerability is built by the
supplying itself rather than sprung by fear alone.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the issuer as a
  reckless debtor debasing its currency or the holders as a mob, because the source finding it stages is that the
  bind follows from the reserve role and not from anyone's misconduct: the issuer supplies its currency because the
  world demands it and is repaid in a genuine privilege, and the holders accumulate it because they need reserves
  and have none better, each acting soundly, and the conflict lives in the asset's two jobs, not in a bad actor.
  The loss is authored by no one: it is the confidence that must erode as the reserve role forces the issuer to
  supply more than its backing covers, and the misfit is that the very trust that makes a currency the world's
  reserve is what the world's demand for it steadily undermines.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, a
  supranational reserve asset issued by no single nation, so the world's reserves can grow without draining any one
  country's backing, proposed as Keynes's Bancor and built in part as the SDR, but it is neither free nor easy: it
  asks nations to cede a piece of monetary sovereignty and to govern a shared asset together, a collective
  undertaking the SDR has only marginally achieved, so the reserve role stays lodged on a national currency and the
  bind returns. The reckoning is that the conflict is inherent in one nation's money serving as everyone's reserve,
  so wherever that arrangement holds, the supplying and the doubting return together.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the reserve-currency bind, the deficit-driven supply of reserves, and
  the erosion of convertibility are Triffin's, the supranational reserve asset is Keynes's Bancor and the IMF's
  SDR, and the modern restatement and the "exorbitant privilege" are Farhi, Gourinchas, Rey, and Obstfeld's,
  credited as the later reading the theory carries forward. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding:
  that the dilemma is sharpest under a fixed convertibility promise, where the backing is a finite gold stock, and
  that it softens under a fiat, floating regime into a tension between reserve demand and the sustainability of the
  issuer's position, real and debated but not an iron law of imminent collapse. The scope is held to a single
  national currency bearing the world's reserve role, and not stretched into a claim that any external deficit or
  any strong currency must fail.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Reserve Asset**: carries the double duty (Triffin), a single national currency that is also the world's
  store of reserves, asked at once to be the issuer's money and everyone's safe holding, the object in which the
  two jobs collide.
- **The World's Thirst**: carries the demand (Triffin), a growing world economy's rising need for reserves to
  settle trade and hold against shocks, a thirst that only more of the reserve currency can slake and that no one
  can fault the world for having.
- **The Outflow**: carries the supply mechanism (Triffin), the persistent external deficits by which the issuer
  sends its currency abroad, the only channel through which the world can accumulate reserves and the same channel
  that piles up claims against the issuer.
- **The Thinning Backing**: carries the erosion (Triffin), the claims held abroad outrunning the backing behind
  them, gold under the old promise and fiscal capacity under the new, so the ratio that underwrites the currency's
  value steadily falls the more of it is supplied.
- **The Lost Confidence**: carries the harm and the endgame (Triffin), the credibility that fails when the backing
  is seen to be outrun, the rational holder moving to convert or diversify before the others, a run built by the
  supplying and not sprung by fear alone.
- **The Bancor**: carries the remedy and the bound (Keynes; the SDR), the supranational reserve asset issued by no
  single nation that alone resolves the bind, costly to build and only partly achieved, and the marker of the
  hedge, since the dilemma is sharpest under a convertibility promise and softens under a fiat regime.
