# Play: Seen, and Therefore Settled

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Seen, and Therefore Settled.
An appeal court will correct an error of law all day. Ask it to correct a finding of fact and it will decline, and give a reason that sounds unanswerable: the judge below saw the witness, and we have only the paper.
Then it says something stronger, in the rule itself. Where a finding rests on the credibility of a witness, the deference owed is **greater** still.
So the protection is at its maximum on exactly the channel that carries the least information about whether anybody was telling the truth.

## Arc

The bend is this: the deference is owed in proportion to what the appeal court cannot see, and what it cannot see is the part of the evidence that has been measured and found nearly worthless.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: the trial judge's presence in the room is real, unrepeatable and impossible to transmit; and the information people actually extract from watching a witness is close to nothing.
Take the deference first, because the play has to establish that it is **right**. A trial cannot be re-run on appeal. Somebody had to be in the room, hear the answers arrive, watch the hesitations and form a view, and no appellate bench can do that from a bundle. A system that let every finding of fact be re-decided by a court further from it would not be more accurate, only slower and more expensive, and litigation would never end.
Then the fact that inverts it. Judging truthfulness from a person's manner is a task that has been measured, across two hundred and six studies and twenty-four thousand judges, and the answer is fifty-four per cent. Barely above a coin. **The exclusive advantage the rule protects is exclusive access to a channel that does not carry the signal it is credited with.**
Then the sharper half, which is the same measurement read one line further. People are more accurate judging **audible** lies than **visible** ones. The audible part is exactly what does go up: the words, the answers, the order they came in, all of it on the page. **So the appeal court has the better half of the evidence and defers on account of lacking the worse half**, and a scholar of the rule put it plainly, that a transcript is probably a better basis for judging credibility than the live performance, because it strips out the misleading nonverbal data and keeps the reliable verbal content.
Then the rule's own emphasis, which is where it closes. The deference is not uniform. It is greatest precisely where the finding rests on credibility, so **the doctrine's protection is strongest at the point its premise is weakest**, and that is written into the standard rather than left to practice.
And nobody has done anything wrong. The trial judge formed a view honestly, using a faculty everybody believes in, including the appeal court, including the losing party, and including the judge. The appeal court is correctly conscious of what it lacks. The rule was written by people trying to allocate a decision to whoever was best placed to make it.
Reach for a settlement and each one costs the thing that made the rule worth having. Reviewing credibility afresh means re-running the trial on paper, which the paper cannot support and which never ends. Recording the hearing gives the appeal court the video, which is the channel the measurement says is worse. Telling judges the research exists does not stop them forming an impression, because forming it is not voluntary. **And a rule that allocated the finding to whoever had the transcript would be a rule that the party who was in the room is the one who should not decide**, which is a sentence no system has been willing to write.

## Triggers

**[Somebody Had to Be in the Room](plot_somebody_had_to_be_in_the_room.md)**
The deference established as right rather than as a defect: a trial cannot be re-run on appeal, somebody had to hear the answers arrive and form a view, and a system letting every finding be re-decided further from the evidence would be slower and no more accurate. Closes when it is clear that the play is not asking for the rule to be abolished, because nobody can say what would replace it.

**[The Better Half Goes Up](plot_the_better_half_goes_up.md)**
Cue: the measurement. Judging truthfulness from manner runs at fifty-four per cent across two hundred and six studies, and people do better on audible lies than visible ones. Closes when the inversion is in the open: what travels to the appeal court is the channel that carries more, and what stays behind is the channel that carries less.

**[Hardest Exactly Here](plot_hardest_exactly_here.md)**
Cue: the standard's own wording. Closes when the emphasis is shown to run the wrong way: the deference is not uniform but is greatest where the finding rests on credibility, so the doctrine's protection is strongest at the point its premise is weakest, and that is written into the rule rather than left to practice.

**[Nothing to Appeal On](plot_nothing_to_appeal_on.md)**
Cue: the losing party, reading the judgment. Closes when the harm lands with nobody at fault: the finding against them rests on an impression formed by a faculty everybody credits, the review that exists cannot reach it, and there is no ground of appeal that says the judge's impression of a face was worth about as much as a coin.

**[And You Cannot Run It Again](plot_and_you_cannot_run_it_again.md)**
Cue: the settlements. Closes when each is found to cost what the rule was for: re-deciding facts on paper never ends, sending up the video supplies the worse channel, telling judges about the research does not stop an impression forming, and allocating the finding to whoever holds the transcript is a sentence no system has been willing to write.

## Stakes

Whether a decision can be allocated to whoever is best placed to make it, when the thing that makes them best placed is not what anybody thinks it is.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether judges are careless, because the faculty in question is one everybody uses and believes in, and not whether appeals should be easier, because the production takes no position on that and does not need one, but whether a rule built on exclusive access can survive the discovery that the exclusive thing carries almost no information. The production cannot make it survive. It can only show that the deference is right and unreplaceable, that the channel it protects has been measured at barely above chance, that the channel it discounts is the one that measures better and is the one that travels, that the rule reserves its strongest protection for the weakest ground, that the party who loses has no route because the review that exists is not addressed to this, and that every remedy either re-runs the trial, supplies the worse evidence, or asks a system to declare that being in the room is a disqualification.
