# Seen, and Therefore Settled: Reference

The research warrant for Seen, and Therefore Settled.
The setting is appellate review of findings of fact; the play runs on the fact that deference is owed in
proportion to what the appeal court could not see, and that what it could not see is the channel measured to
carry least, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging appellate deference as **a rule calibrated to the wrong channel**: a trial cannot be re-run on appeal, so
somebody had to be in the room and the finding is allocated to them; the deference is graded, and is greatest
where the finding rests on a witness being believed; judging truthfulness from manner has been measured at
barely above chance; the same measurement finds people more accurate on audible than on visible deception, and
the audible channel is exactly what travels to the appeal; and the review that exists cannot reach an impression,
because an impression leaves nothing to demonstrate against. The play models the presence that cannot be
transmitted, the manner that persuades everybody and carries least, the transcript that holds the better half and
is called the poor relation, the graded standard that protects hardest on the thinnest ground, and the complete
appellate system that contains review without a route, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a
tale of lazy appeal courts or careless judges.

The governing law is that the deference is owed in proportion to what did not travel, and what did not travel is
the part of the evidence that carries least, so the rule's protection is strongest exactly where its premise is
weakest.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The trial
judge forms an impression honestly, using a faculty everybody credits, including the appeal court and the losing
party. The appeal court is accurate about what it did not see and reverses on law without hesitation. The
standard was written by people trying to allocate a decision to whoever was best placed to make it. And the
advice given to the losing party is correct.

**And the deference is right, which the play establishes before anything else.** A trial cannot be re-run on
appeal. Somebody had to hear the answers arrive, and no appellate bench can do that from a bundle. A system
permitting every finding of fact to be re-decided by a court further from the evidence would be slower, no more
accurate, and would leave litigation without an end. **The play does not ask for the rule to be abolished,
because nobody can say what would replace it.**

**Then the measurement, which empties the exclusive advantage.** Judging truth from manner is a task that has
been synthesised across two hundred and six documents and twenty-four thousand judges, and the answer is
fifty-four per cent correct classification. **The thing the appeal court cannot have is a channel that does not
carry the signal it is credited with.**

**Then the sharper half, which is the same synthesis one line further.** People are more accurate at judging
**audible** deception than **visible** deception. The audible part is precisely what goes up: the questions, the
answers, the order they arrived in, complete and verbatim. **So the appeal court holds the better half of the
evidence and defers on account of lacking the worse half**, and the legal scholarship drew that conclusion
directly, arguing that a transcript is probably a better basis for credibility than live testimony because it
eliminates the distracting, misleading and unreliable nonverbal data while keeping the most reliable data, the
verbal content.

**Then the grading, which is where a general principle becomes a specific trap.** The deference is not uniform.
It is weakest where a finding rests on documents the appeal court can read for itself, and **greatest where the
finding rests on the credibility of a witness**, because credibility is exactly what could not be observed from
the record. That is the argument for the grading and it is a good one; it is also why the grading runs inversely
to the measurement, and it is written into the standard rather than left to practice.

**And the review that exists cannot reach it, which is not the same as review being absent.** Findings of fact
are genuinely reviewable, on a standard built to catch what is demonstrably mistaken, and appeals on fact do
succeed. A credibility finding by an experienced judge who heard the evidence is, by construction, **not
demonstrably mistaken**: what left the courtroom was the conclusion and not the material, so nobody downstream
can check the reading against what was read. **The system can be complete, functioning and correctly applied and
still contain review without a route.**

**And every settlement costs what the rule was for.** Re-deciding credibility afresh is re-running the trial on
paper, which is what the deference exists to prevent. Recording the hearing supplies the appeal court with the
video, which is the channel the measurement says is worse. Telling judges the research exists does not stop an
impression forming, because forming it is not voluntary. And a rule allocating the finding to whoever holds the
transcript would be a rule that **the party who was in the room is the one who should not decide**, which is a
sentence no system has been willing to write.

It is distinct from the house's **In Somebody Else's Words**, which is the nearest incumbent and stages the court
interpreter's bind, where the manner reaching the room was produced by somebody else and nothing marks the seam.
Remove that engine entirely, by stipulating that every witness testifies in the court's own language with no
mediation of any kind, and this misfit is unchanged, because **the manner here is the witness's own and is read
directly.** That play needs a seam between the speaker and what the fact-finder receives; this one has no seam
and stages what a fact-finder gets from an unmediated view, which is nearly nothing.

It is distinct from the house's **Which Question First** and **Whoever Asks Last**, which stage the doctrinal
paradox and the Condorcet paradox, both defects in aggregating judgements across propositions or voters. Remove
both engines, by stipulating a single decider at each level and no aggregation anywhere, which is the case here,
and the mechanism is untouched. **Nothing here is aggregated and nobody votes**: this is a division of labour
between levels, with one court finding and another reviewing, and the difficulty is in the allocation rather than
in any summing.

It is distinct from the house's **Marked the Same by Anyone**, which stages the reliability ceiling on validity,
where disagreement between raters bounds how valid a measure can be. Remove that engine, by stipulating perfect
agreement between any two observers of the same witness, and this stands entire, because **agreement is not
accuracy**: two judges who always concurred would still classify at fifty-four per cent, and the rule would still
grade its protection highest on that channel. That play is about a bound imposed by noise; this one is about a
legal allocation of authority to the channel that measures worse.

It is distinct from the house's **Keep Her Course and Speed**, which stages the stand-on obligation, where one
party is required not to respond so that the other's problem becomes determinate. Remove that engine, by
stipulating that nobody's inaction is required to make anybody else's calculation possible, and this is
untouched, because **the appeal court is not holding still for anybody's benefit.** There the suppression buys a
counterpart predictability; here it expresses a claimed epistemic inferiority that turns out to run the other
way.

It is distinct from the house's **Clear from Here**, which stages hindsight bias, where a known outcome makes its
path look inevitable. Remove that engine, by stipulating that nobody overestimates how foreseeable anything was,
and the mechanism runs unchanged, because **the question here is who saw the witness and not how predictable
anything looks afterwards.**

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the trial judge as
  careless, the appeal court as timid or the standard as a piece of self-protection, because nothing in the
  sequence has a decision to get wrong: the impression forms involuntarily in anybody who watches, including the
  appeal judges when they sit at first instance and the losing party at the back of the court; the appeal court's
  account of what it did not see is accurate and it reverses on law without hesitation; the standard was written
  to allocate a decision to whoever was best placed to make it; and the advice given to the losing party is
  correct. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the deference
  is calibrated to the channel that carries least.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Abolishing the deference means
  re-running trials on paper, which is what it exists to prevent and which never ends. Recording hearings gives
  the appeal court the visible channel, which is the one the measurement ranks lower. Judicial education does not
  stop an impression forming, because forming it is not voluntary. And allocating the finding to whoever holds
  the transcript would require a system to hold that being in the room is a disqualification, which no system has
  written.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the synthesis of deception-judgment accuracy is Bond and DePaulo's,
  and the argument that the legal assumption about demeanour is unsupported, including the conclusion about
  transcripts, is Wellborn's and was made in 1991. The bounds are carried with them, and one is owed plainly:
  **neither cited work was read in full**, each being behind publisher access, so each is cited for the question
  it addresses and its findings as corroborated from several independent reports, and **no figure appears here
  that those reports did not state.** The others: the deception synthesis is of **judges in study conditions**,
  overwhelmingly not judicial officers in contested hearings, so applying it to a courtroom is the play's
  extension rather than the authors', and whether trained fact-finders in adversarial proceedings do better is
  **genuinely contested**; the standard is summarised in general terms and its application in any particular
  jurisdiction or case is a matter for a court and not for this house; the structural halves, that manner is
  writable only as a conclusion and that an impression leaves nothing for a standard to grip, are offered as
  descriptions and not as measured results; and no claim is made about the rate at which findings of fact are
  reversed. **The play makes no claim about any court, any judge, any case or any legal system, and gives no
  legal advice of any kind**, only that a rule owing deference in proportion to what did not travel is owing it
  in proportion to the evidence that carries least.
