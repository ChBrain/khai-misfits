## Tension

Here the rule's premise comes apart, and it comes apart in two steps.
The first is that the exclusive advantage is nearly empty: judging truth from manner classifies at barely above a coin, so what the appeal court cannot have is a channel that does not carry the signal it is credited with.
The second is worse and is the same measurement one line on. **People judge audible lies more accurately than visible ones.** The audible part is precisely what does go up: the words, the answers, the order they came in, complete and verbatim.
**So the appeal court holds the better half of the evidence and defers on account of lacking the worse half.** A scholar of the rule stated the conclusion directly, that a transcript is probably a better basis for credibility than the live performance, because it strips out the misleading nonverbal data and keeps the reliable verbal content. Nobody in the system has ever said the appeal court has the better record, and nobody has shown that it does not.
