# You Are the Product: Reference

The research warrant for **You Are the Product**. The title is a reading of the phenomenon the media
political economy calls **the audience commodity**: Smythe's account of why an advertising-funded
medium sells not its content but its audience, so its paying customer is the advertiser and its
product is the reader's attention, and its content is rationally shaped to gather and hold the
audience advertisers will pay for rather than to serve the audience's own interest, all cited below,
held with the finding that the tilt bites where the funding is advertising and the two parties'
interests diverge, and softens where they coincide or where the reader or the public becomes the
payer.

## Line of Work

Staging the audience commodity as a **misaligned-customer, audience-as-product structure**: when a medium is paid
by selling its audience's attention to advertisers, the party who pays and the party who consumes are not the
same, and the thing sold is the audience itself. Smythe named the content the "free lunch," the bait a broadcaster
or a paper gives away to gather an audience, whose attention is the real commodity it produces and sells to
advertisers. So the outlet's revenue comes not from serving the reader but from delivering the reader to the
advertiser, and its rational objective is to produce and shape the attention advertisers will pay for: a large
audience, of a desirable demographic, held in an engaged and receptive mood. Every choice that follows is locally
sound and not malicious. The content is made to attract and hold the target audience, in a frame conducive to
buying, and steered clear of what would drive that audience or its advertisers away. The sum is an information and
culture environment optimised for advertiser value, in which the audience's own informational or civic interest is
served only where it happens to coincide with attention-capture: what citizens need but will not attend to, and
what holds attention but unsettles advertisers, both go undermade, not by anyone's censorship but by the plain
logic of who is paying. The governing law is not that anyone is corrupt or conspires, but that **where a medium is
funded by selling its audience's attention to advertisers, the paying customer is the advertiser and the product
is the audience, so the outlet rationally optimises to produce and shape the attention advertisers will pay for,
and the audience's own interest is served only where it coincides with attention-capture, the content tilting to
what holds a desirable audience in a receptive mood rather than to what that audience needs, the reader being the
product and not the customer**. The play models the documented mechanics: the free lunch of content that gathers
the audience (Smythe), the audience's attention produced and sold as the commodity (Smythe; Jhally & Livant), the
advertiser as the customer whose interest is therefore served, the receptive audience mood the content is shaped
to make, the audience's own interest served only incidentally, and the reader or public funding that would realign
the customer, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a scheming press manufacturing
consent by design.

It is a media-market structure no staged misfit holds, and it is carefully distinct from its nearest neighbours.
It is distinct from Metric Fixation, where a number chosen to stand for a goal is hit exactly and the goal it
stood for dies: there a proxy is gamed and displaces the aim, whereas here nothing is measured and gamed, the
served party is simply the payer from the start, the tilt a misaligned customer and not a corrupted metric. It is
distinct from Regulatory Capture, where a watchdog set to guard the public comes over time to serve the industry
it guards: there an agent turns from its original mandate, whereas here there is no turning and no betrayed
mandate, the outlet serves the advertiser who pays it from the first day, the alignment built in rather than
corrupted. It is distinct from The Filter Bubble, where each click for the agreeable teaches the feed to show more
of it and exposure narrows: there the mechanism is personalised narrowing driven by the reader's own clicks,
whereas here the funding model tilts the whole product, what is made at all, independent of any feed or
personalisation. And it is distinct from After You, the two-sided market that never forms because each side waits
for the other: there the failure is that the market does not come into being, whereas here the market forms and
functions well, and the harm is that its functioning tilts the product against the audience because the advertiser
is the one who pays.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the outlet as a
  scheming manipulator or the audience as dupes, because the source finding it stages is that the tilt follows from
  who pays and not from anyone's design: an advertising-funded outlet rationally serves the advertiser whose money
  it lives on, an advertiser rationally buys the attention it needs, and an audience rationally accepts the free
  lunch on offer, each acting soundly, and the misalignment lives in the market structure and not in a bad actor.
  The loss is authored by no one: it is the audience's own interest served only where it coincides with
  attention-capture, and the misfit is that the very content given free to gather the audience is paid for by
  making the audience the product rather than the customer.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The way out exists, to make the
  audience the payer, through subscription or reader funding, a public or licence-fee model, or membership and
  endowment, so that the one served is the one consuming, but none is free: a paywall shrinks reach and shuts out
  those who will not or cannot pay, public funding asks a collective willingness to fund and courts political
  capture, and donor or member money tilts toward donors and members, so each realignment buys back the audience's
  place at a real cost, and the advertising model persists because the free lunch outcompetes the paid one for
  reach. The reckoning is that the tilt is inherent in funding a medium by selling its audience, so wherever the
  payer and the consumer are different parties, the product is optimised for the payer.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed as
  the house's. The staging is the house's; the audience commodity and the free lunch are Smythe's, the valorisation
  of watching-as-working is Jhally and Livant's, the field history of attention merchants is Wu's, and the
  advertising filter is Herman and Chomsky's, credited only for its structural claim and not for the
  press-conspiracy reading the house declines. The mechanism is carried as carefully as the finding: that the tilt
  bites where the funding is advertising and the audience's interest and the advertiser's diverge, and softens
  where the two coincide, so ad-funding can make excellent content where attention-capture and quality align, and
  where the reader or the public becomes the payer the customer is realigned. The scope is held to
  advertising-funded media and the divergence cases, and not stretched into a claim that all ad-supported media is
  worthless or that audiences are helpless.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per element, one per load-bearing vector, no filler.

- **The Free Lunch**: carries the bait (Smythe), the content given away to gather an audience, worth making only
  because it draws the crowd whose attention is the real product, so the show or the story is a cost of production
  and not the thing sold.
- **The Sold Attention**: carries the commodity (Smythe; Jhally & Livant), the audience's attention produced and
  packaged and sold to advertisers, the thing the outlet is actually in the business of making, the reader the
  product and not the buyer.
- **The Advertiser's Coin**: carries the paying customer (Smythe), the advertiser's money that funds the outlet
  and so decides whose interest it serves, the revenue that comes from delivering the audience and not from
  pleasing it.
- **The Receptive Mood**: carries the shaped state (Jhally & Livant), the engaged, desirable, buying-frame audience
  the content is made to produce, since it is that mood, and not the audience's understanding, that the advertiser
  is paying to reach.
- **The Unserved Interest**: carries the harm (Smythe; Herman & Chomsky, structurally), the audience's own
  informational or civic interest, served only where it coincides with attention-capture, so what citizens need
  but will not attend to and what unsettles advertisers both go undermade, by no one's censorship.
- **The Reader's Coin**: carries the remedy and the bound (Wu; the public-media tradition), the subscription,
  public, or membership funding that would make the audience the customer, costly because a paywall shuts out the
  unpaying, public money courts capture, and donor money tilts to donors, and the marker of the hedge, since the
  tilt softens where the payer and the consumer are the same.
