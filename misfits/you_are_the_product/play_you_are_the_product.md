# Play: You Are the Product

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

You Are the Product.
When a television channel airs a show for free, or a paper prints its news for a few cents that do not cover its cost, or a website gives away its whole content for nothing, a plain question goes unasked: who is paying, and for what? Dallas Smythe gave the answer that changed how the trade is understood. The content, he said, is not the product. The content is the "free lunch," the bait a medium gives away to gather an audience. The real product, the thing the medium produces and sells, is the audience itself, or more exactly the audience's attention, sold to advertisers. So the paying customer is not the reader or the viewer. The paying customer is the advertiser, and the reader is what is being sold to them (Smythe).
And here is what that does to what gets made. If the outlet's revenue comes from delivering an audience to advertisers, then its rational aim is not to serve the audience but to produce and shape the attention advertisers will pay for: a large audience, of a demographic advertisers want, held in an engaged and receptive frame of mind. So the content is made to attract and hold that audience in a mood conducive to buying, and steered clear of what would drive the audience or its advertisers away. Each such choice is sound, and none of it is a plot (Smythe; Jhally and Livant).
And here is the gap it opens. The audience's own interest, in being informed, in being told what it needs to know as a citizen, is served only where that interest happens to coincide with holding attention. Where the two part, the audience loses. What people need to know but will not attend to goes undermade, because attention is what is sold and this does not hold it. What holds attention but unsettles advertisers goes undermade too, because the advertiser is the customer and this displeases them. Neither is censored. Both simply are not what anyone is paying for, so both are made only by accident, where they happen also to gather a crowd.
And here is why no one in it is a villain. The outlet is not a manipulator: it serves the advertiser because that is who pays its bills, exactly as any business serves its customer. The advertiser is not a censor: it buys the attention it needs and gives no orders. And the audience is not a dupe: it takes a free lunch it genuinely enjoys, and the trade is open to anyone who looks. The loss is authored by no one. It is the audience's own interest, served only where it coincides with attention-capture, and the misfit is that the very content given free to gather the audience is paid for by making the audience the product rather than the customer, so the reader who thought themselves served is the thing being sold.
And here is the way out, and why it is so hard. The tilt comes from the audience not being the payer, so the remedy is to make it the payer: to fund the medium by subscription or membership, so the reader is the customer, or by a public or licence-fee model, so the public is. Where the one served is the one paying, the product is aligned to them. But none of this is free. A paywall shrinks the audience and shuts out everyone who will not or cannot pay, so the free reach the advertising model gives is lost. Public funding asks a whole society to agree to pay and courts political capture of what it funds. Donor and member money tilts toward donors and members. Each realignment buys back the audience's place at a real cost, and the advertising model persists because the free lunch outcompetes the paid one for reach.
And here is the bound that keeps it honest. The tilt is felt only where the audience's interest and the advertiser's diverge. Where they coincide, where the audience wants exactly the engaging, well-made thing that also gathers a crowd worth selling, advertising funds it with no harm, and much that is excellent is made this way. The trap is as deep as the gap between what holds the audience and what serves it, and no deeper, and it is not a claim that all advertising-funded media is worthless or that audiences are helpless before it. The audience commodity and the free lunch are Smythe's; the valorisation of watching as working is Jhally and Livant's; the field history of the attention merchants is Wu's; and the advertising filter is Herman and Chomsky's, taken here only for its structural claim and not for the press-conspiracy reading the house refuses. The house stages the market and holds the bound in view: that where a medium sells its audience to its advertisers, what it makes serves who pays and not who reads, no one at fault, and the reader is the product until the reader becomes the payer.

## Arc

The bend is this: where a medium is funded by selling its audience's attention to advertisers, the paying customer is the advertiser and the product is the audience, so the outlet rationally optimises to produce and shape the attention advertisers will pay for, and the audience's own interest is served only where it coincides with attention-capture, the content tilting to what holds a desirable audience in a receptive mood rather than to what that audience needs, the reader being the product and not the customer.
It runs on a medium funded by advertising.
The content is a free lunch, given away to gather an audience whose attention is the real product sold (Smythe).
And the customer is the advertiser. The outlet's revenue comes from delivering the audience, not from pleasing it, so the party served is the party who pays (Smythe).
And the content is shaped to the sale. It is made to hold a desirable audience in a receptive, buying mood, because that mood is what the advertiser pays to reach (Jhally and Livant).
And the audience's interest goes unserved. Where what it needs and what holds its attention part, the outlet makes the second, so the reader's own interest is served only by accident (Smythe).
And it is authored by no one. The outlet serves who pays, the advertiser buys attention, the audience takes the lunch, so the tilt is the market's doing and not a villain's.
And the way out is dear. Make the reader or the public the payer, by subscription or public funding, and lose the free reach, court capture, or tilt to donors, each a real cost (Wu).
And the whole is held against its limit. The tilt is felt only where the audience's interest and the advertiser's diverge, softening where they coincide, so the trap is as deep as that gap and no deeper (Smythe; Wu).

## Stakes

Whether an audience can be served by a medium that is paid to sell it, when the one who consumes and the one who pays are not the same party.
Every plot but the last two moves the same way: toward gathering and holding the attention the advertiser will pay for, and away from serving the audience's own interest, because the outlet's revenue comes from delivering the audience and not from pleasing it. The medium cannot both serve the reader as its customer and sell the reader to its customer, because the same content that gathers a desirable audience in a receptive mood is what there is money to make: the free lunch given to the audience and the audience sold to the advertiser are the one market seen two ways.
