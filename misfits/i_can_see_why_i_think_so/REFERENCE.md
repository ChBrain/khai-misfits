# I Can See Why I Think So: Reference

The research warrant for I Can See Why I Think So.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the advice-taking literature, on the differential-access account of
why advice is discounted, and on the work about combining opinions by averaging, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the revision of a judgement as **two inputs that cannot be compared because only one of them arrives
with its reasons**: a person forms an estimate, and forming it is the same act as assembling the
considerations behind it, so the estimate is held together with everything that produced it. An adviser then
supplies an estimate of their own, and what arrives is a number. The reasoning that produced it stayed where
it was made, because a conclusion is what an opinion is for and nobody sends the working. So the judge is
asked to weigh a claim they can inspect against a claim they cannot, and the weighting that results puts
most of the weight on the side that can be inspected. The play models the documented mechanics, the estimate
that comes with its own reasons attached, the advice that arrives as a conclusion, the asymmetry of access
that makes the two unlike objects, the small revision that follows, the average that would have beaten it,
and the score that is never run against the alternative, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not
as a tale of a proud judge or a poor adviser.

The governing law is that **a judgement carries its reasons for the person who made it and loses them for
everybody else**, so the same quantity is a supported claim on one side of a desk and a bare number on the
other.

What makes it structural is that the discounting is **not a misjudgement of the adviser**. It survives when
the judge rates the adviser highly, and it does not require any belief that the adviser is worse. The two
inputs are simply not the same kind of object: one is an estimate with a visible case behind it, and the
other is an estimate. **Weighting them equally would require the judge to act on information they do not
have**, namely the strength of the reasoning they cannot see, so the asymmetry in the weighting is an
accurate response to an asymmetry in what arrived.

What makes it costly is that the correct move is **arithmetic rather than psychological**. Where two
estimates carry independent errors, their average is closer to the truth than a typical one of them, so a
judge who moved to the midpoint would do better on average than one who moved a short way. That is a
property of errors and not a claim about who is right, and it holds even when the judge really is the better
forecaster of the two. The literature reports that people move only a little of the way, and that they choose
between opinions more often than they average them.

And nothing in the ordinary run of the work corrects it. **The average is a counterfactual nobody scores**:
the judge sees what their revised estimate did and never sees what the midpoint would have done, so the
evidence that would teach the lesson is exactly the evidence the procedure does not generate. A person can
do this for a career and accumulate no experience of it.

It runs against the house's **On Good Authority**, **Seen, and Therefore Settled** and
**Good Enough to Hold**, on the axis `deference-to-judgement` and at the opposite end, and the four are one
dial read from its two harmful sides. In those three the deferring party **stops examining**: a belief comes to rest on a
citation chain nobody has walked, an appeal court adopts a finding it cannot re-examine, a holder takes a
rule-recognised grade in place of a view. Here nobody stops examining; the judge examines hard, and the
trouble is that only one of the two things in front of them can be examined at all. **What sets the sign is
whether deference replaces the work or replaces a comparison the work cannot make.** Where deference is a
substitute for looking, more of it is worse. Where the object being deferred to is a bare conclusion and the
alternative is not looking harder but arithmetic, less of it is worse.

It is distinct from the house's **Not Invented Here**, where an external origin is discounted because it is
external: there the mark is provenance and stipulating an indistinguishable origin removes the harm.
Stipulate that here, advice arriving with no author and no institution attached, and the harm survives
intact, because what is missing is not the adviser's standing but the reasoning behind their number.

It is distinct from the house's **The Skill to See It**, the Dunning-Kruger effect, where the competence
needed to do a thing is the competence needed to judge whether it was done well: there the judge is wrong
about their own ability. Here the judge may be exactly right about their own ability and about the
adviser's, and the discounting persists, because it is produced by what arrived rather than by an estimate
of who is better.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the judge as
  arrogant or the adviser as careless. The judge is weighing a claim they can inspect against a claim they
  cannot, and weighting them equally would mean acting on the strength of reasoning they have not seen. The
  adviser sent a conclusion because a conclusion is what an opinion is and what was asked for. Nobody is
  wrong about anybody's competence, and the discounting survives where the judge rates the adviser highly.
  The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that **a judgement
  carries its reasons for the person who made it and loses them for everybody else**.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into better manners. Asking for the reasoning works
  and turns a second opinion into a second piece of work, which is what an opinion was got instead of.
  Averaging by rule works and gives up the cases where the judge really does know more, which are the cases
  a judge is employed for. Taking more advisers works and runs into the same discounting on each of them.
  Telling the judge about the effect does not supply the missing reasons, so it asks them to weight
  something they still cannot see. And scoring the counterfactual would teach it, and requires somebody to
  compute what the midpoint would have done on decisions nobody is paid to revisit.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the judgement and decision-making research is theirs.
  The bounds are carried with them. **Averaging wins where errors are independent and where the two are
  comparably good**, and the literature is careful that it is not a universal rule, so the play stages the
  common case and refuses the stronger claim. **The differential-access account is one of several
  proposed**, alongside anchoring on one's own estimate and the effort already spent forming it, and it is
  carried here as the account that makes the structure legible rather than as a settled question. **The
  magnitudes those sources report are deliberately not carried here**, because the works themselves could
  not be read in the pass that cited them: publisher hosts and the article archives are blocked to it, so
  the bibliography, the findings and their directions were verified by search and corroborated across
  independent reports, which is the treatment a citation survives and a reported figure is not.
