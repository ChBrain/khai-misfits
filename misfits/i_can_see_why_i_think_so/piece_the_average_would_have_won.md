# Piece: The Average Would Have Won

## Taxonomy

[I Can See Why I Think So](play_i_can_see_why_i_think_so.md). Two estimates carrying independent errors average closer to the truth than a typical one of them.

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Place

At [the revision seat](position_revision.md), where the move is decided.
Nowhere in the room, because the argument for it is about errors and not about either forecaster.

## Load Bearing

Without it there is no loss and no misfit, only two people disagreeing.
It is load bearing because **it holds even where the judge is the better forecaster**: the gain comes from
independent errors cancelling rather than from the adviser being right, so the case for the midpoint
survives every true thing the judge believes about their own advantage.

## Apparent

That averaging splits the difference between a right answer and a wrong one.
It cancels two errors against each other. The intuition that it must dilute a good estimate with a poor one
is the reason the principle is underused.

## Yearbook

The work on strategies for revising judgment: averaging beats choosing across a wide range of cases, and
people choose more often than they average.
