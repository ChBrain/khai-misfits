# Sound Both Ways: Reference

The research warrant for Sound Both Ways.
The term is the house's; the play runs on Kant's Antinomy of Pure Reason and the doctrine of
transcendental illusion that frames it, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the antinomies as **an instrument that fails confidently, in both directions at once**: reason's
business is to refer a conditioned thing to what conditions it, and to go on asking, because an answer that
leaves a condition standing is not yet an answer. That demand is the whole of what makes the faculty
rigorous. It is also what carries it past the last state of affairs any experience could confirm, and the
border it crosses cannot announce itself. The play models the documented mechanics, the rule about
conditions that has no stopping point, the bound that is invisible from inside a valid inference, the thesis
and the antithesis arriving from one faculty on one question, the search for the false premise that fails
because there is none, the question that turns out to be the fault, and the pull that survives being named,
as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a philosopher outreaching himself.

The governing law is that a derivation certifies its own validity and reports nothing about its reach. Form
is what an inference carries, and what changes at the edge of possible experience is not form but whether
anything could confirm the terms, so the step that leaves the checkable world is identical in every
inspectable respect to the step before it. There is no signal to miss, which is a worse situation than a
signal that was missed.

What removes any possibility of blame from the reasoner is that every remedy available to a careful thinker
has already been applied. The usual response to a contradiction is to find the bad step, and both arguments
survive examination, because the fault is upstream of both of them in the question they are jointly
answering. Nothing inside an inspection of the answers can point at the question, so care, rigour and time
reproduce the contradiction sooner rather than resolving it, and the more competent the thinker the faster
they arrive at it.

What removes it from the faculty is that the demand cannot be calibrated. A rule about conditions has no way
to ask whether a given condition lies inside or outside anything checkable, since conditions do not report
that, and a rule that halts at some arbitrary depth is not a rule about conditions at all. So the faculty is
at its most rigorous exactly where it is least entitled, and there is no version of it with this removed
that is still the faculty.

And the resolution costs the whole ambition. The series of conditions is a task rather than a thing, a rule
for going on rather than an object with a size, so a question about its extent admits no answer of the kind
either side was offering: the first two conflicts have both sides false and the second two may have both
true. Which establishes that the questions reason cannot help asking are exactly the ones it is not entitled
to answer, and leaves the asking untouched.

It is distinct from the house's **The Horizon Problem**, where every plan ends at its instrument's edge, so
what lies past carries no cost and casts no shadow until it arrives: there the failure past the edge is
silence, a cost that simply does not register, and the instrument goes quiet. Here the instrument does not
go quiet at all: it goes on answering, promptly and at full rigour, and returns two conclusions that cancel
each other, so what is produced past the edge is not an absence but a surplus.

It is distinct from the house's **No Place in the Theory**, where a theory's earned authority outweighs a
true but mechanism-less anomaly, so sound reasoning rejects a valid discovery: there the reasoning is sound
and the casualty is a fact outside it, correctly weighed and wrongly dismissed. Here nothing outside is
being weighed and no discovery is refused: the casualty is the method's own output, which arrives twice with
opposite signs and refutes itself without any help from the world.

It is distinct from the house's **Reasonable Either Way**, where every analytic choice is defensible and
made after looking, so one analysis owes a debt to the paths never walked: there the latitude is the
mechanism, a garden of forking paths that a different analyst would have walked differently, and the remedy
is to fix the path in advance. Here there is no latitude at all. The derivations are forced, the same two
proofs come to anybody who asks properly, and preregistering the reasoning would change nothing.

It is distinct from the house's **Anyone Can See It**, where two reasonable people read the same scene
oppositely, each sure it sees plainly and the other is biased: there the opposition is between two parties
and is explained by what each brings to the scene. Here one mind, with no doctrine to defend and nothing
riding on the answer, produces both sides itself, so there is no second party to blame and no prior to
adjust.

It is distinct from the house's **The Law of the Instrument**, where the one tool a hand has mastered
reshapes every problem into the shape it can strike, and trims off the rest: there the tool is misapplied to
a problem that wanted another, and the loss is what got trimmed. Here the tool is not misapplied and nothing
is trimmed: it is the right instrument, used correctly, on a question that admits no answer of the sort it
gives, and it produces a full answer anyway.

## Origin

The conflict in the text, and the doctrine that frames it.

| Source                     | Key Work / Event                                                                                              | Scope                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Immanuel Kant**          | Critique of Pure Reason (1781, second edition 1787), the Antinomy of Pure Reason                              | **The spine**: four conflicts of reason with itself, each carrying a proof of the thesis and a proof of the antithesis, on the world's limits in time and space, on simple parts, on freedom against causation, and on a necessary being. |
| **Kant**                   | the Transcendental Dialectic, and the demand of reason for the unconditioned                                  | **The engine**: reason's rule that a conditioned thing be referred to its condition, pursued to a complete series, which is what carries the faculty past the reach of possible experience.                                               |
| **Kant**                   | transcendental illusion, distinguished from logical illusion                                                  | **Why it cannot be taught away**: an illusion natural to reason and inseparable from it, which, in Kant's phrase, even after its deceptiveness has been exposed will not cease to play tricks with reason.                                |
| **Kant**                   | the mathematical and dynamical division of the antinomies, and their resolution under transcendental idealism | **The remedy, and its price**: the first two conflicts resolved with both sides false and the second two with both possibly true, once appearance and what stands behind it are told apart, at the cost of the questions themselves.      |
| **Michelle Grier**         | Kant's Doctrine of Transcendental Illusion (Cambridge University Press, 2001)                                 | **The modern reading**: the account of the illusion as necessary rather than incidental, sourced in the nature of reason, and persisting after detection, which is what makes the structure a trap rather than an error.                  |

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the reasoner
  as overreaching or the faculty as defective, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong:
  the demand for the condition of the condition is the whole of what makes reason rigorous and cannot be
  given a non-arbitrary stopping point; the bound is a limit on confirmation and cannot be encoded in the
  form of an inference, which is what an inference reports; both proofs survive every check available,
  because the fault is upstream in the question they share; and the thinker who applies more care arrives at
  the same contradiction sooner. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the
  misfit is that a method gives no sign of leaving the ground it was proved on.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Drawing the boundary works
  and surrenders the whole ambition with it, since the questions reason cannot help asking become the ones
  it may not answer. Naming the illusion works and does not remove it, by Kant's own account, so the
  critique is a warning rather than a cure and must not be reported as one. Declining to reason past
  experience is not available to a faculty whose rule is to keep asking, and instructing it to stop reaches
  nothing, since the pull issues from the same demand that makes it work.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the antinomies, the doctrine of transcendental
  illusion, the resolution and the two centuries of commentary are theirs. The bounds are carried with them:
  the eight individual demonstrations are contested, several widely judged to rest on assumptions Kant does
  not earn, so the play stages the structure of the conflict rather than endorsing any proof; the resolution
  depends on transcendental idealism, which is itself disputed, and the play does not adopt it in order to
  stage the trap; the antinomies are a claim about pure reason reaching beyond possible experience and are
  not a general theory of contradiction, so nothing here transfers to ordinary disagreement or to formal
  systems; and the reading of the illusion as necessary rather than incidental follows a particular modern
  interpretation among others. **The play makes no claim that reasoning is generally unreliable**, only that
  a derivation certifies its validity and never its reach, and that the edge of what could have checked it
  carries no marker.
