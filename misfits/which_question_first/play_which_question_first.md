# Play: Which Question First

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Which Question First.
A panel of three has a claim before it. The law is agreed by all of them and is not in dispute: the defendant is liable if and only if there was a valid contract **and** it was breached. Two questions, one rule joining them, and a verdict that follows from both.
Each judge votes coherently on every question and applies the agreed rule to their own answers. Nobody is confused and nobody is inconsistent. And whether the defendant loses depends on nothing except which of the two counts the panel decides to take.

## Arc

The bend is this: a majority is decisive on every question separately and the set of answers it produces need not be consistent, so the verdict is settled by the order the questions are put rather than by anything anybody voted for.
It runs on two facts, neither of them anybody's doing: individuals hold coherent views, and majorities are taken question by question.
Take the table, because it can be checked rather than believed. The first judge finds a contract and no breach. The second finds a breach and no contract. The third finds both. Every one of the three is coherent, and each reaches a verdict correctly from their own answers: the first says not liable, the second says not liable, the third says liable.
Now count. On the contract question the majority is two to one **yes**, since the first and third judges both found one. On the breach question the majority is two to one **yes**, since the second and third both found one. Apply the agreed rule to the majority's answers and the defendant is **liable**.
Count the other way. Ask each judge for a verdict and tally those: not liable, not liable, liable. The defendant **wins**, two to one.
**Both counts are real majorities and neither is a trick.** The same three coherent judges, the same agreed law, the same evidence, and two opposite judgments, separated by nothing but whether the panel voted on the issues or on the outcome.
And nobody chose between them by voting. The route is a matter of practice, of who presides, of how the conference happens to be run, and in most courts it is not decided as a question at all. So the losing party is bound by a procedure that was never argued and usually not recorded.
Reach for a settlement and there is not one. Take the issues, and the panel can hand down a verdict that a majority of its own members would have voted against. Take the outcomes, and the panel announces a result that contradicts the reasons a majority of it actually holds, which leaves the law of the next case unsettled. Insist on unanimity and most panels decide nothing. And a rule that always prefers one route is still a rule nobody voted on, applied to every case in advance.

## Triggers

**[Three Judges, Two Questions](plot_three_judges_two_questions.md)**
A claim, a rule all three judges agree on, and two questions that decide it together. Closes when the setup is established as ordinary rather than contrived: an agreed law, an undisputed connective, and three coherent people.

**[Both Majorities Are Real](plot_both_majorities_are_real.md)**
Cue: the vote table. Two to one that there was a contract, two to one that it was breached, and two to one that the defendant is not liable. Closes when the arithmetic is done in the open and both counts are shown to be genuine majorities rather than one of them a mistake.

**[Whichever We Vote On First](plot_whichever_we_vote_on_first.md)**
Cue: the panel deciding how to decide. Take the issues and the defendant loses; take the outcomes and the defendant wins. Closes when the verdict is shown to be settled by the route and by nothing anybody voted for.

**[Nobody Was Inconsistent](plot_nobody_was_inconsistent.md)**
Cue: the search for the error. Each judge is coherent, the rule is agreed, the counting is correct, and the result is still contradictory. Closes when the contradiction is located in the aggregation rather than in any participant, which is what makes it an impossibility and not a failure.

**[It Does Not Say Which Way We Counted](plot_it_does_not_say_which_way_we_counted.md)**
Cue: the judgment as published. An outcome, reasons, and no record of whether the panel tallied issues or verdicts. Closes when the losing party is shown to be bound by a procedure that was never argued, usually not minuted, and not appealable as a ground.

## Stakes

Whether a group of consistent people can reach a consistent decision together.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether any judge reasons badly, because each is coherent and applies an agreed rule correctly to their own answers, but whether majority rule taken question by question can be relied on to produce a set of answers that hang together. The production cannot make it. It can only show that both counts are genuine majorities and neither is a trick, that the two give opposite judgments from the same three coherent views, that the difference between them is the order the questions were put and that nobody voted on the order, that fixing one route in advance settles cases before they are heard rather than dissolving the difficulty, and that the published judgment records the outcome and not the route, so the party bound by it cannot see which of the two it lost to.
