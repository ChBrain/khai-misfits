# Plot: Both Majorities Are Real

## Taxonomy

[Which Question First](play_which_question_first.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The vote table: contract and no breach, breach and no contract, both.
[The Three Views](piece_the_three_views.md), each internally consistent.

## Action

[Tallying by issue](process_tallying_by_issue.md) and [tallying by verdict](process_tallying_by_verdict.md), side by side at [the tally](place_the_tally.md).

## Stage

[The Issue Count](persona_the_issue_count.md), on [Count Each Question on Its Own](plan_count_each_question_on_its_own.md), finds two to one for a contract and two to one for a breach, applies the rule, and returns **liable**.
[The Verdict Count](persona_the_verdict_count.md), on [Count the Verdicts](plan_count_the_verdicts.md), tallies not liable, not liable, liable, and returns a win for the defendant **two to one**.

## Tension

Do the arithmetic in the open, because it can be checked rather than believed.
The first judge finds a contract and no breach. The second finds a breach and no contract. The third finds both. On the contract question the majority is two to one yes; on the breach question, two to one yes; so the rule everybody accepts gives **liable**. Ask the same three for verdicts and it is not liable, not liable, liable: the defendant wins **two to one**. **Both are genuine majorities and neither is a trick.** Same three coherent judges, same agreed law, same evidence, opposite judgments.
