# Only One Person Fits: Reference

The research warrant for Only One Person Fits.
The term is the house's; the play runs on statistical disclosure control and on what a released record has to keep
in order to be worth releasing, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the de-identified release as **a file that is useful and identifying by the same measure**: a body holds
records collected for another purpose and is obliged, or plainly right, to release them, because a finding nobody
outside can check is not a finding and a question about a rare condition or a small place cannot be answered from
a table of totals. It removes the direct identifiers first, competently, to the standard of the day. What remains
is an age, a place, a date, a sequence of visits, none of which any rule calls an identifier and all of which the
question is made of. Detail is what makes a row answer and detail is what makes a row rare, and past a certain
number of attributes rare means one. The play models the documented mechanics, the release that has no safe
alternative, the removal that meets the standard and is not the failure, the quasi-identifier that is the study
and the exposure in one column, the uniqueness produced by counting rather than by naming, the demonstration that
must perform the harm to establish it, the formal remedy that prices the trade instead of ending it, and the
subject who is party to nothing, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of a careless
custodian or a malicious attacker.

The governing law is that the granularity which lets a record answer a question is the granularity which narrows
it to a person, so there is one dial and not two.

**Nobody in the sequence falls short, and that is what puts the failure outside anybody's conduct.** The custodian
releases under the rule. The de-identifier applies the standard exactly. The researcher describes its question.
The demonstrator publishes the method and notifies the holder. The subject is never asked.

What removes any possibility of blame from the custodian is that withholding is not the safe option, it is the
other costly one. A registry that releases nothing leaves published findings unauditable, questions about small
places and rare outcomes unanswered, and a public that funded the collection unable to see what it bought. Those
costs land on people too. The rule the seat follows prescribes release under conditions precisely because refusal
was understood to have victims.

What removes it from the de-identifier is that the standard is met and is aimed at the wrong objects. What a rule
enumerates is what a person recognises as identifying: names, numbers, addresses. What it leaves is age, place,
date and sequence, which nobody calls an identifier, and a rule that did name them would forbid the studies the
release exists for. There is no version of that seat that could have done better under the rule it was given, and
placing the failure there is how the structure gets mistaken for negligence.

**And the uniqueness arrives from arithmetic rather than from any attribute, which is where the structure becomes
concrete.** No single value in the row is dangerous and the combination is, because uniqueness is a property of
how many attributes there are rather than of what any of them says. It is also a property of the file **against
everything else**, so no inspection of a release by its holder can settle it, and the answer keeps moving as the
world outside acquires more tables.

**Then the person who establishes the risk is the person who performs it.** A claim that a file could be
re-identified is answered by the observation that nobody has, and only a re-identification refutes that. So the
evidence that a release is unsafe can be produced only by doing the unsafe thing once, deliberately, in the open,
by somebody whose purpose is to protect the people in the file. That is not an attacker in any ordinary sense and
it is the reason the risk is known at all.

It is distinct from the house's **Missing From the Count**, which the slate line required it to be tested against
before authoring, and the test was run. There a shortfall is measured precisely and a statute bars the measurement
from being applied where it would matter, so the engine is a legal bar on use. Remove that engine, by supposing
the correction may be applied freely, and that misfit is answered. Remove it here and nothing changes, because
nothing in this play is forbidden: the release is lawful, the analysis is lawful, and the difficulty is that the
data works.

It is distinct from the house's **Taken Apart to Be Read**, where the only way to read an object destroys it, so
the reading is unrepeatable and the engine is the destructiveness of observation. Remove that engine, by
stipulating a reading that leaves the object untouched, and this misfit stands entire: nothing here is consumed
and the row was unique before anybody looked. The formal remedy does introduce a budget that each answer spends,
which is the nearest this play comes to that engine, and it is a property of one remedy rather than of the
structure, so it is carried as an Origin row and not as the spine.

It is distinct from the house's **The Abstraction Ladder**, where specificity is lost going up a reporting chain
until the summary no longer carries what the decision needed. That is the same dial turned the other way. Here
nothing is lost: the detail survives, deliberately and correctly, and the difficulty is entirely in what survives.

It runs against the house's **The Precinct Is Not a Person**, on the same axis and with the opposite sign.
There the record is a precinct total, the individual row was never made because the ballot is secret, and a
court must find how people voted from what places did. This warrant already states the opposition in prose,
that a question about a rare condition or a small place cannot be answered from a table of totals: that
sentence is the other play, staged.

**What sets the sign on this axis is whether the row is being used or protected.** Where the output is an
inference about the individual, detail supplies the variation the inference needs and its absence is the harm.
Where the output is the protection of the person in the row, detail supplies the uniqueness that identifies
them and its presence is the harm. Same dial, opposite sign, and the discriminator is which of those two the
record is for. The plays differ again in how the dial got where it is: here a body chooses a release and can
trade the two harms off, and there the setting was fixed once by a constitutional commitment and cannot be
revisited at all.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the custodian as
  cavalier or the re-identifier as an attacker, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong:
  withholding has victims of its own and the rule prescribes release for that reason; the de-identifier removes
  exactly what the standard enumerates and a standard that enumerated more would forbid the studies; the
  researcher's request is a description of its question rather than an overreach; the uniqueness comes from the
  number of attributes rather than from any of them, so no attribute is the one to have removed; the demonstrator
  performs the re-identification to protect the people in the file and has no other way to establish the risk;
  the formal remedy is a genuine advance whose cost is real; and the subject is never asked and cannot know
  whether it is the unique row, because uniqueness is a property of a file it has seen one line of. The failure
  is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that usefulness and identifiability are
  one quantity.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Aggregating to totals removes
  the small-area and subgroup questions that were the reason to release. Suppressing small cells introduces its
  own bias and can be undone by differencing successive releases. Synthetic data inherits the same trade and adds
  a fidelity question of its own. A formal privacy guarantee bounds the worst case, holds against an adversary
  with arbitrary outside information, and still requires a parameter to be chosen and accuracy to be given up.
  Secure enclaves and restricted access convert the problem into an access-control problem and exclude most of
  the people reproducibility was meant to serve. Consent does not scale to records collected decades ago and
  produces participation bias where it is tried. And knowing the whole structure, which the field does and has
  documented at length, leaves a legitimate request on the desk and a question that deserves an answer.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never claimed
  as the house's. The staging is the house's; the disclosure-control literature, the uniqueness results, the
  formal privacy framework and the critical scholarship are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: **whether
  demonstrated uniqueness translates into practical re-identification by realistic adversaries is genuinely
  contested**, and the play carries that argument rather than settling it; the headline uniqueness figure was
  revised substantially downward by a later re-examination and both numbers are given here for that reason;
  uniqueness in a dataset is not the same thing as a successful re-identification, which additionally requires an
  outside table that overlaps and is correct, and the play does not treat the two as equivalent; the account
  treats a family of release regimes, legal standards and data types as one structure, which is its own
  simplification, since jurisdictions differ substantially in what they enumerate and what they permit; and the
  figures cited come from particular populations and particular years. **The play makes no claim that any release
  was improperly made, that any person's record was in fact exposed, that any standard should change, and it
  gives no legal, statistical or data-protection advice of any kind**, only that the detail which makes a record
  worth releasing is the detail which makes it one person.
