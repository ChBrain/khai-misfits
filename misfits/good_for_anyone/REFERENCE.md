# Good for Anyone: Reference

The research warrant for Good for Anyone.
The term is the house's; the play runs on the universal red cell donor group, its use in undifferentiated
emergency transfusion, and the recurring shortages that follow, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the universal donor shortage as **scarcity produced by fungibility**: red cells that carry none of
the common antigens can be given to any recipient, which makes them the correct choice whenever a patient
is bleeding and unidentified, and they are also the only cells a patient of that same group can receive.
The play models the documented mechanics, the compatibility that is a genuine clinical gift, the emergency
reach that is right every time it is made, the stock consumed mostly by patients who turn out to have
alternatives, the small group who compete with the entire population rather than with a rival faction, the
donor ceiling fixed by genetics rather than by persuasion, and the conservation measures that save units
only after the answer arrives, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of waste or poor
stewardship.

The governing law is that compatibility is an invitation rather than a surplus. Because nothing in any
recipient objects to these cells, every patient is a potential claimant on them while only a few are
restricted to them, so the property that makes the group valuable to the whole population is the property
that makes the whole population a draw on its supply.

What removes any possibility of blame is the timing. Determining a patient's group takes minutes that a
major haemorrhage does not allow, so the reach for the universal unit is made before the information that
would refine it can exist. A team that hesitated in order to check compatibility while somebody emptied
would be culpable, and the protocol exists precisely to remove that hesitation. So the consumption of the
scarce stock is the correct decision taken correctly, under the one condition that guarantees nobody can
tell whether this patient needed it.

What makes it structural rather than a management failure is that the supply is capped by biology. Roughly
seven people in a hundred carry the group, with lower proportions in several populations, so a donor appeal
can raise the share of eligible donors who attend and cannot raise the share of the public that is
eligible. Supplies have fallen to critical levels every year since 2019.

And the group with no substitute has no counterparty. They are not competing with a rival faction that
might be asked to take less: everybody alive is compatible with what they alone require, so the pressure on
their supply is a property of the substance rather than an interest that could be negotiated with.

It is distinct from the house's **The Commons**, where a shared stock is drawn down because the cost of one
more taking is split among all: there each taker gains privately and imposes a shared cost, and the
mechanism is an incentive that could in principle be repriced. Here nobody gains anything they are not
entitled to, no cost is externalised by choice, and the draw is created by compatibility rather than by
incentive, so there is no price, quota or rule of access that would leave both claims satisfied.

It is distinct from the house's **The Anticommons**, where too many owners hold vetoes so a resource sits
idle: there the resource is unused because consent cannot be assembled. Here the resource is used
constantly and correctly, and the difficulty is that using it correctly for one claimant is what removes it
from another.

It is distinct from the house's **Not Worth My While**, where a measure that saves many offers each person
almost nothing so each rationally declines: there the failure is under-provision through diffuse benefit.
Here provision is not the decision point at all, since the donors cannot be more numerous however willing
they are, and the misfit lives entirely on the demand side.

It is distinct from the house's **The Only Basket**, where each owner votes locally to protect an
undiversifiable asset and the sum is a shortage: there the shortage is the aggregate of many self-interested
votes. Here there is no aggregation of interests, and a single clinical property produces the whole effect
without anybody choosing anything.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the emergency
  team as wasteful or the recipient as a free rider, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get
  wrong: reaching for blood that cannot harm an unidentified patient is right every single time, and
  hesitating would be culpable; the patient who later turns out to have alternatives received those units
  at the only moment when the distinction was unavailable to anybody; the laboratory works as fast as the
  chemistry allows; the blood service asks properly and cannot enlarge the donor base; the transfusion
  practitioner has perfect information about the shelf and no control over its quantity. The failure is
  authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that fungibility itself produces the
  shortage, and it produces it for the only people who cannot substitute.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Typing faster and
  switching patients off the group works and recovers real units, and the saving lands only on transfusions
  after the answer, never on those given while waiting for it. Giving O positive in major bleeding works
  and moves the decision from who is bleeding worse now to whom the antigen might harm later. Holding a
  reserve for those with no alternative works and leaves somebody unidentified waiting on a crossmatch they
  may not survive. Recruiting harder works on attendance and cannot touch the share of the population that
  is eligible.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the immunohaematology, the transfusion protocols and
  the shortage reporting are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the prevalence of the group varies
  substantially between countries and between populations within them, so the figure used here is a common
  approximation rather than a constant; universal compatibility is a simplification, since other antigen
  systems and pre-existing antibodies matter in repeatedly transfused patients and the term is a practical
  one rather than an absolute; emergency protocols differ between services and have been changing, with the
  O positive substitution adopted unevenly and the rationale for prioritising the negative group turning on
  antigen sensitisation risks that fall differently across patients; whole blood and other products change
  the picture where they are used; and shortage reporting is drawn largely from services that publish it.
  **The play makes no claim that emergency transfusion should be withheld pending compatibility testing**,
  which would kill people, only that a supply everybody may draw on is thinnest for those who may draw on
  nothing else.
