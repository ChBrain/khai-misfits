# Play: Good for Anyone

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Good for Anyone.
Red cells carry markers, and a body that meets a marker it does not recognise attacks the cells carrying it. One group of red cells carries none of the common ones: no A, no B, no RhD. That blood can be given to anybody alive, which is why it is the bag you reach for when somebody is bleeding out and nobody knows yet what they are. It is also the only blood that a person of that same group can safely receive. So the thing that makes it useful to everyone is the same thing that makes it the only option for a few, and there is one shelf.

## Arc

The bend is this: universality is not a surplus, it is a claim by everybody on a supply fixed by how many people happen to be born with it.
It runs on a property that is genuinely a gift.
The Blood That Suits Everybody is not a scarce luxury version of ordinary blood. It is ordinary blood that happens to lack the markers, so nothing in any recipient objects to it, and that is a real and enormous clinical good rather than a quirk.
The One Who Cannot Wait makes the reach correct every single time. Somebody arrives haemorrhaging and unidentified, and typing takes minutes that this person does not have. Reaching for the universal bag is the right decision, made under pressure, by somebody who would be wrong to hesitate. There is no version of this play in which that reach is a mistake.
So The One Who Can Take Anything Else consumes most of the stock, and is not a free rider. They received it because nobody yet knew what they were, which is exactly what it is for. Once their type comes back they can be moved onto their own, and every unit given before that point was correctly given.
And The One Who Can Take Only This is drawing on the same shelf. Around seven people in a hundred can receive nothing but this, and they are not competing with a rival group: they are competing with everybody, because everybody is compatible with it. The property that makes their blood valuable to the whole population is the property that makes the whole population a claimant on their supply.
And The One Who Gives It cannot be recruited past the ceiling. The donor pool for this group is capped by how many people carry the genetics, not by how well the appeal is written, so a campaign can raise the fraction of eligible donors who turn up and cannot raise the fraction of the population that is eligible. Supplies of it have fallen to critical levels every year since 2019.
And the corrections all ration somebody. Type the patient as fast as possible and switch them off it, which is the standard advice and buys back real units, and still costs the minutes it costs. Give ordinary O positive in major trauma even when the type is unknown, which many hospitals now do because the reaction risk is lower during ongoing blood loss, and which quietly makes the choice of who gets the universal bag turn on who might be harmed later by the marker rather than on who is bleeding worse now. Or hold stock back for the people who have no alternative, and let somebody unidentified wait for a crossmatch they may not survive.

## Stakes

Whether a supply can be shared fairly when the thing that makes it shareable is what makes it scarce for the only people who cannot substitute.
Every plot moves the same way: toward correct reaches in the first seconds, correct changeovers when the type comes back, and a donor service asking properly, and away from any arrangement that could serve both claims, because the property that creates the second claim is the one that creates the first. The production cannot make a body accept a marker it does not recognise, and it cannot ask a doctor to wait for a test while somebody empties. What is left to fight over is not whether to give it in an emergency, that reach is always right, but who owns the gap between what seven in a hundred require and what everybody is entitled to ask for.
