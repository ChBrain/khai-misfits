# Play: The Far End of the Wire

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

The Far End of the Wire.
A street is fed by one cable running out from a substation, and power has only ever travelled one way along it: outward, to the houses. Voltage sags a little with every metre of that journey, so the supply end is set deliberately high, which is the ordinary and correct way to make sure the last house on the road still gets enough. Then panels go up, one roof at a time, each installation legal, correctly sized, separately harmless, and approved. On a bright still noon in spring, when nobody is home and everybody is generating, the current on that cable turns round and runs back toward the substation. The same arithmetic that used to lower the voltage along the road now raises it. And the setting that was protecting the last house is the thing that pushes it over the limit.

## Arc

The bend is this: voltage along a line depends on the current and the line's own impedance, and that relationship does not know which way the current is going.
It runs on a sign.
The One Who Strung It built for the only flow there was, and built well. Sagging voltage is what a long conductor does, so the supply end is lifted to compensate, and lifting it is not a bodge: it is how the last house on a long road is kept inside limits under load. Every part of that is correct and would be done the same way today.
The Ones Who Fitted Panels are each correct too, and each separately harmless. One roof exporting does nothing at all. The system has no rule against it and no reason for one, and every application is assessed and approved on its own merits, as applications are.
And The Climbing Volt needs nothing but simultaneity. Nobody coordinates, nobody exceeds anything: the sun is simply up over the whole street at once while the kettles are off, so the flow reverses, and reversal is all it takes. Nothing has broken, degraded or been neglected. The same equation runs, with one sign flipped.
Then the cost is presented, and it is presented to The Last House. Voltage rise is greatest at the electrical end of the feeder, so the household that curtails most is the one furthest from the substation. Its inverter reduces output as the voltage climbs, and disconnects if it stays above the permissible limit, which is the protection doing precisely what it was fitted to do. Legal generation, from correct equipment, shut off by its own safety device. And the thing that decided it was position on a wire: not a choice, not a rule broken, not anything that household can change.
Nobody told them, either. There is a number on that cable, a ceiling belonging to the conductor and its length, and it is not on any application form. The people on the street cannot see it, the installer does not quote it, and it is reached by the arrival of a neighbour rather than by anything the affected household did.
And every correction charges somebody. Reconductor the road and every customer on the tariff pays, panels or none. Fit smart inverters so the whole street shares the curtailment, and the owners pay in output, and sharing it raises the total energy curtailed rather than lowering it, so the fairer answer is the more wasteful one. Cap the export on a full feeder and the entire cost lands on whoever applies last, refused a lawful upgrade for standing in a queue that was never published.

## Stakes

Whether a network can be judged sound when every part of it is correct and the fault is in the direction of travel.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a road built properly for the flow it had, and away from any inspection that could find the problem, because there is no component in it that is wrong. The production cannot make the conductor care which way the current runs, and it cannot stop a street being sunny all at once. What is left to fight over is not whether to connect the panels, everybody wants them connected, but who pays for a ceiling that belongs to a cable, and whether the household at the end of the road should go on paying it alone simply for living there.
