# Stopped for Benefit: Reference

The research warrant for Stopped for Benefit.
The term is the house's; the play runs on interim monitoring of randomised trials, the truncation of trials
that cross a stopping boundary, and the measured inflation of effect estimates that follows, all cited
below.

## Line of Work

Staging early stopping as **a measurement interrupted for the best reason there is**: a randomised trial
cannot run blind to its own accumulating results, because people are in it and half of them are receiving
the other thing, so an independent board looks at intervals against a boundary fixed in advance and stops
when one arm is clearly better. The play models the documented mechanics, the monitoring that is an
obligation rather than a courtesy, the boundary that is rigorous and insufficient, the crossing that is
selected partly on luck, the participant whose presence makes the stop compulsory, the estimate that can
never be revised, and the patient outside the trial who pays for a protection extended to somebody else, as
the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of weak methods or hasty boards.

The governing law is that the stopping time is chosen by the data. Early in a trial the events are few and
the estimate wanders widely, so the looks at which the treatment appears dramatically better are
disproportionately the looks at which chance ran the treatment's way. The trial therefore stops when the
treatment is good and the run has been kind, and at the moment of stopping nothing distinguishes those two
contributions, because both produce the identical table.

What makes the rigour insufficient rather than absent is what boundaries are for. Pre-specified boundaries
control the probability of stopping wrongly under repeated looking, which is a real abuse and one they
genuinely prevent. They do not make the estimate at a crossing unbiased, because any rule that stops on a
large observed effect stops preferentially when the observation was helped: the measured overestimate is
independent of whether formal stopping rules were used.

What removes any possibility of blame is the obligation. The premise of randomisation is genuine
uncertainty, and once the board believes one arm is better that premise has failed for the next person to
be enrolled. A board declining to stop because the effect size would be tidier later would be deliberately
allocating a person by lot against its own conviction. So the body best equipped to understand the
statistical damage is the body least free to avoid it.

And the correction is destroyed by the same act. Stopping is followed by unblinding and by offering the
treatment to the control arm, which follows automatically from the reasoning that justified stopping. From
that moment there is no untreated comparison group anywhere, so the measurement cannot be completed by this
trial, resumed later, or reconstructed by anybody. This is not a provisional figure awaiting refinement.

It is distinct from the house's **The Best on Paper**, where the highest-scoring option is the one whose
estimate ran luckiest so it tends to disappoint, and from **The Winning Bid**, where the bidder who most
overestimates is the one who wins. Those two share the selection-on-noise mechanism with this play and
differ in what surrounds it, which is where the whole of the difference lies. There somebody is choosing,
deliberately, among candidates, in order to get the best one: here nobody chooses a treatment at all, and
what is selected is a _moment_ rather than an option, by a board acting under a protective duty it did not
seek. There the disappointment arrives later and is observable, so the selection can be learned from and
corrected for next time: here the crossover ends the comparison permanently, so the error can never be
measured in the case that produced it. And there the party who overpays is the party who chose: here the
benefit of the stop goes to the enrolled participant and the cost of the estimate goes to a patient treated
years afterwards who was never in the trial and had no representative in the room.

It is distinct from the house's **The File Drawer**, where only the studies that worked reach the record so
science reads surer than it is: there whole studies are missing and their number is unknown. Here nothing
is missing at all. The trial is published in full, the truncation is disclosed, and the inflation sits
inside a paper that has concealed nothing.

It is distinct from the house's **Long Enough to Qualify**, where survival is required in order to be
counted as treated so the surviving is credited to the treatment: there the bias is in how time is
allocated between groups within an analysis. Here the allocation is sound and the analysis is correct, and
what is biased is the instant at which a correct analysis was performed.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the monitoring
  board as hasty or the methods as weak, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the
  monitoring exists so that participants are not left on an inferior arm for the convenience of a clean
  result; the boundary is computed in advance, applied without discretion, and does exactly what boundaries
  are designed to do; the board members generally understand the truncation problem perfectly well and may
  not act on that understanding, since the alternative is allocating the next participant by lot against
  the trial's own belief; the paper reports honestly and discloses the early stop; the guideline committee
  quotes it correctly. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is
  that the obligation to the person in front of you ends the measurement that the person after them needs.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Requiring a substantial
  number of events before a crossing may be acted on works, and means deliberately leaving participants
  longer on the arm the board believes is worse. Shrinking the reported estimate toward a prior works, and
  means publishing a number nobody measured while clinicians go on using the one that was. Continuing
  follow-up after the stop works, and is confounded from the moment the control arm is offered the
  treatment, which follows automatically from the same reasoning that justified stopping. Disclosing the
  truncation prominently informs the careful reader and does not change the figure that travels.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the monitoring practice, the sequential methods and
  the meta-research are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: the thirty per cent figure is an average
  across a heterogeneous set of comparisons and does not describe any individual trial, and comparing
  truncated trials with non-truncated ones on the same question is itself an inference with limitations;
  estimating the true effect of a stopped trial is an active methodological field, with shrinkage and
  adjusted estimators available and argued over, so the position is less hopeless than the play stages it
  at full strength; stopping decisions weigh harm, futility and external evidence as well as benefit, and
  this play addresses only stopping for benefit; and boards do sometimes continue past a crossing where the
  evidence is thin, so the compulsion described here is strong rather than absolute. **The play makes no
  claim that trials should be run past the point where one arm is clearly better**, only that the moment
  chosen to protect the people inside is not a moment chosen to measure anything.
