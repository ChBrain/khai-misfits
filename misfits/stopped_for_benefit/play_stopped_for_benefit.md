# Play: Stopped for Benefit

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Stopped for Benefit.
A trial cannot be run blind to its own results. People are in it, half of them are getting the other thing, and if one arm turns out clearly better it would be indefensible to keep assigning patients to the worse one merely to finish the paperwork. So an independent board looks at the accumulating data at intervals, against a boundary computed in advance, and if the boundary is crossed the trial stops and everybody is offered the winner. Every part of that is correct, and the part that is most correct is the part that does the damage.

## Arc

The bend is this: the trial stops at a moment chosen by the data, and early data is mostly noise.
It runs on an obligation nobody would remove.
The Board That Must Stop It exists precisely so that participants are not left on an inferior arm for the convenience of a clean result. It meets in closed session, it holds the only unblinded view, and its duty is to the people currently enrolled. Nothing about this is ceremonial.
The Interim Look is where the selection happens, and it selects on luck. Early in a trial there are few events, so the estimate wobbles widely around the truth: the looks at which the treatment appears dramatically better are disproportionately the looks at which chance has run the treatment's way. So a trial does not stop when the drug is good, it stops when the drug is good and the run of events has been kind, and those two are not separable at the moment of stopping.
And The Stopping Boundary does not fix it. The boundaries are principled, pre-specified, statistically rigorous, and designed exactly against this problem. Across truncated trials the overestimate of relative benefit averages around thirty per cent, and the effect is independent of whether formal stopping rules were used, and largest where the events are fewest. The rigour is real and it is not the thing that is failing.
Then The One Randomised Next makes the stop compulsory. Whatever the statistics say about noise, the next person through the door would be assigned by lot to a treatment the board now believes is worse. Nobody can defend that, and nobody should have to: the duty to the person in front of you is the strongest obligation in medicine.
And The One Treated After pays for it, without ever appearing in the trial. The published number is the inflated one, it enters guidelines and prescribing decisions, and it describes a benefit larger than the one they will get. They were not in the room, they were not randomised, and no board was watching over them.
And the correction is destroyed by the same act. Once the trial stops it is unblinded and the control arm is offered the treatment, so the comparison that would have settled the number can never be completed by anybody, ever. This is not an estimate that gets refined later. It is a measurement that was interrupted for the best reason there is and cannot be resumed.

Cue: the corrections on the table: wait for more events, shrink the published estimate, or keep following participants afterwards. Closes when waiting means leaving people on the worse arm, shrinking means publishing a number nobody measured, and the follow-up is confounded the moment the control arm is offered the drug.

## Stakes

Whether a number can be trusted when the rule that decided the moment of its measurement was a rule about protecting people.
Every plot moves the same way: toward boards doing their duty, boundaries computed properly, and participants protected from an inferior treatment the moment anybody can tell, and away from any instrument that could complete the measurement, because completing it means continuing to give somebody the thing you believe is worse. The production cannot ask a board to keep randomising against its own conviction, and it cannot recover a comparison that was ended. What is left to fight over is not whether trials should stop, they must, but who owns the difference between the number that was published and the number that was true, and whether an estimate interrupted for the best reason there is can be called an estimate at all.
