# Plot: A Few, Well Chosen

## Taxonomy

[Spoiled for Choice](play_spoiled_for_choice.md).

## Owner

- Project: khai-misfits

## Cue

The paralysis and the regret are plain by now, and a way is sought to ease them.

## Action

At [the Afterward](place_the_afterward.md), [the Curator](persona_the_curator.md) from [the Curator's seat](position_curator.md) on [Cut It to a Few](plan_cut_it_to_a_few.md) through [Curating](process_curating.md) holds [the Curated Few](piece_the_curated_few.md), the cut assortment, the sensible default, or the satisficing that takes the good enough.

## Stage

The remedy is not a trick, it is a trade: cut the assortment to a handful, set a default so no choice need be made at all, or teach the Chooser to take the first good-enough option rather than the provably best one, and each of these buys back the freeze and the regret at the price of the very freedom the abundance offered in the first place (Schwartz). This is the whole cost of the fix, plainly stated, not hidden. And the bound must be held just as hard: the more-is-less effect is genuinely contested, a large meta-analysis finding no reliable effect on average across studies (Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd), with later work locating it only under specific conditions, such as when the options are hard to compare or the chooser lacks a clear sense of their own preferences, rather than as a general law of choosing (Chernev, Bockenholt and Goodman). No villain: the Curator is not rescuing anyone from folly, only offering one way to spend less freedom for more peace, in a domain where even the researchers do not fully agree on when the trade is needed.

## Tension

The way out is real but paid for in freedom, and the effect it answers to is itself contested.
