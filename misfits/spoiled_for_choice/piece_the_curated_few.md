## Load Bearing

The cut assortment, the sensible default, or the satisficing that takes the first good-enough option rather than searching on for the best: each eases the burden of comparison in [The Weighing](piece_the_weighing.md) and the ache of the forgone in [The Regret](piece_the_regret.md). None of it is free. Curation takes the omitted options out of the chooser's hands entirely, and risks cutting out the very one she would have wanted most, so the cure spends the same freedom the abundance offered in the first place (Schwartz).

And the remedy carries its own bound. The effect it answers to is genuinely contested: a large meta-analysis finds no reliable average more-is-less effect across the studies pooled together (Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd), and later work locates it only under specific conditions, choice-set complexity, task difficulty, an uncertain sense of one's own preference (Chernev, Bockenholt and Goodman). So the cut is a relief where the burden is real, and an overcorrection where it is not, and no curator can tell in advance which shelf she is standing at.

## Apparent

Reads as a paternalist narrowing the world, deciding for a shopper what she was capable of deciding herself.
