# Play: Let's Go Round the Room

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Let's Go Round the Room.
Something happened and several people saw it. The sensible thing, the thing any careful team does, is to get everyone who was there into a room and work out what happened between them. More heads, more of the event: each person holds a piece, the pieces are put together, and what comes out is fuller than what any one of them could give. That is the whole reason the meeting exists, and it is not a foolish reason.
And here is what the room does to the remembering. A person's memory of an event is not a list to be read off: it is reached by a path, an order of their own, this bit leading to that one, and the path is idiosyncratic to them. Sitting and listening while somebody else recalls the same event in somebody else's order cuts across that path. What is retrieved is what the room's order reaches, not what your own would have reached, and the pieces your own order would have found are the ones that do not arrive. Weldon and Bellinger measured it: people recalling together produce less than the same number of people recalling separately and pooled afterwards. The room is not lazier. It is disrupted, by each other, continuously and with the best will in the world.
And here is what the room does to the contents. Every account in the room is heard by the others before any of them is fixed, and a detail offered by one person is available to be taken up by the rest. Roediger, Meade and Bergman showed the shape of it: an item another person reports as having been there is later reported by the hearer as their own memory of it, and the reports of two people who have talked converge on each other. The one who speaks first has the most of this effect, not because they are pushy but because everything after them is heard against what they said. Nobody is lying at any point. A memory adopted is a memory, from inside, and there is no felt difference between the thing you saw and the thing you were told and now recall.
And here is what leaves the room. An account shorter than the sum of what walked in, and more uniform than any of the accounts that walked in, in which the shared parts have been reinforced by every person who repeated them and the unshared parts have quietly not been reached. That document is then read, by an investigator, a manager, a court, a family, and by the people who made it. And it is read as several people agreeing, which is the strongest evidence there is, when the agreement was manufactured in the room by the act of meeting rather than observed in the world by several pairs of eyes.
And here is the way out, and why almost nobody takes it. Have every person write their account down alone, in full, before anybody says anything. Then pool the documents. Both mechanisms are gone: there is no other order cutting across yours and no detail to adopt before yours is fixed, and the pooled account is longer and its agreements are real. It costs one step and it is almost never done, because a meeting is what a group does, because the writing feels like an insult to people who came to help, and because the whole intuition of a debrief is that talking it through together is the point.
And here is the bound that keeps it honest. The shortening is measured on word lists and staged scenes rather than on the messy events a real debrief covers, and it weakens or disappears where the people in the room retrieved non-overlapping parts of the material or were made to organise their recall the same way. Collaboration also prunes some errors, since a wrong detail can be challenged in the room by somebody who knows better, so the room is not simply worse: it is shorter and more uniform, and the uniformity is what gets misread. The house stages the structure and holds the bound in view: that a group put together to remember more remembers less, together.

## Arc

The bend is this: a group assembled to recall an event more fully recalls it less fully and more uniformly, and the uniformity is then read as the corroboration that the meeting destroyed.
It runs on a memory reached by a path rather than read off a list.
Each person's recall follows an order of their own, and the order is part of how the memory is reached at all, so there is no version of a person's account that is independent of the route they take to it.
And the room supplies a different order. Listening while somebody else recalls the same event in their order cuts across yours, so what you retrieve is what their route reaches and not what yours would have (Weldon and Bellinger, on collaborative inhibition, with the retrieval-disruption account).
And so the group produces less. The same people at separate desks, pooled afterwards, produce more of the event than the room does, and nobody in the room is doing anything but listening and waiting their turn.
And every account is exposed before it is fixed. A detail offered by one person is available to the others, and is taken up, and is later reported by the hearer as their own memory of the thing (Roediger, Meade and Bergman, on the social contagion of memory).
And the one who speaks first has the most of it, since everything said afterwards is heard against what they said, which is a property of order and not of character.
And what leaves the room is shorter and more uniform than what came in, with the shared parts reinforced by repetition and the unshared parts quietly not reached.
And it is read as several people agreeing. The agreement was made in the room by the act of meeting rather than observed in the world by several pairs of eyes, and nothing in the document says which.
And the whole is held against its limit. The shortening is measured on lists and staged scenes, it weakens where people cover non-overlapping ground or are made to organise the same way, and collaboration also prunes some errors, so the room is not simply worse but shorter and more uniform.

## Stakes

Whether a group can pool what it knows without the pooling being the thing that shortens it.
Every plot but the last moves the same way: toward the account the room can produce together, which is legible, agreed, quick and signed by everybody, and away from the accounts the same people would have produced apart, which nobody ever sees and which were longer. The production cannot let the group hear each other and keep their retrieval separate, because hearing is what a meeting is: the disruption and the pooling are the one act read two ways, and the same listening that lets a wrong detail be challenged is the listening that cuts across the route to a right one. It can only show that nobody lies, that the convener is right to convene and would be negligent not to, that the first speaker is doing nothing but speaking first, that a memory adopted is a memory from inside with no felt seam in it, and that the reader taking agreement for corroboration is doing what agreement is for. And it holds the bound in view, because the shortening is measured on word lists and staged scenes rather than on the events a real debrief covers, it weakens where the people in the room retrieved non-overlapping parts or were made to organise their recall the same way, and collaboration also prunes some errors, so the room is not simply worse. The whole of the misfit is that a group assembled to remember more remembers less, together, and the agreement it leaves with is the meeting's own work being read as the world's.
