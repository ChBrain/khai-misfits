# Play: What Never Arrives

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

What Never Arrives.
A river carries two things to the sea: water, and the ground it has been taking off the land all the way down. A dam is built across it, for the ordinary reasons, and it works: floods damped, water stored through the dry season, power made every hour of every day. Behind the wall the current stops, and a current that stops sets down what it was carrying, so the silt settles quietly on the floor of the reservoir. Nobody removes it. Nobody dumps it. It simply stops moving, because moving is something water does and the water here is still. And eight hundred kilometres downstream, at the mouth, a coastline that had been maintained for ten thousand years by material arriving every spring stops receiving any.

## Arc

The bend is this: a river is a conveyor as well as a supply, and only the supply is ever what a dam is rated on.
It runs on a subtraction that produces no waste.
The One Who Built It answers a real brief and answers it completely. The floods were real, the dry seasons were real, the power is real and is delivered every hour. Everything the work promised, it does, and it goes on doing it for eighty years without a bad report.
The Silt is not a resource anyone asked for. It has no line in the case for construction, no rating, no meter, and no constituency, and it is also the material a coastline is continuously made of. When the current stops it settles, which is not an act by anybody: stilling water is how storage works, and settling is what stilled water does.
Below the wall, The Clear Water comes out. Water released from a reservoir runs clean, and clean water is under-supplied: it has capacity for a load it is not carrying, so it takes one, from the bed and the banks. The channel cuts down, the material left behind coarsens, the gravels that fish spawn in are gone, and the footings of bridges built for a riverbed that used to be higher are standing in air. Kondolf's name for this water is the honest one, and it is hungry.
And The Delta simply stops being maintained. It was never a thing built once: it is a deposit held in balance against its own sinking and the sea by material arriving every year, so removing the arrival damages nothing, breaks nothing, and produces no event at all. It just stops being topped up, and then the sea is further in each decade.
None of this appears anywhere. The dam's accounting is complete and correct for what the dam was built to do, and the coast is another authority, another budget, and often another country. The figures that do exist are startling and are counted at global scale rather than at anybody's wall: soil erosion has raised the sediment the world's rivers set in motion by around 2.3 billion tonnes a year, while retention behind works has cut what reaches the coasts by around 1.4 billion tonnes a year. More moving. Less arriving. For a good many populated deltas, what was done upstream matters more to their survival than the rising sea does.
And the corrections are paid at the wrong end of the river. Pass the sediment through, by sluicing or bypass or drawdown, and you spend storage and head, which is the whole of what the wall was built to hold, and you must do it in flood season when the water is worth most. Add gravel below the dam and you are treating a hundred kilometres of channel a truckload at a time, forever, and none of it ever reaches the mouth. Whoever pays is upstream. Whatever is saved is downstream, decades out, and will never be observed by the payer.

## Stakes

Whether a work that delivers everything it was rated on can be counted honestly, when the thing it stopped was never on the rating.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a dam doing exactly its job, and away from any ledger in which a coastline eight hundred kilometres off appears as one of its outputs. The production cannot make still water carry silt, because carrying is what a current does, and it cannot make the delta hold without arrival, because a delta is arrival rather than a structure. What is left to fight over is not whether to build the dam, everyone agrees the floods and the dry seasons were real, but who spends storage upstream for land downstream, in a season when the water is worth most, for a result that will show up after they are gone and only as something that failed to happen.
