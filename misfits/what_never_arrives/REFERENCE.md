# What Never Arrives: Reference

The research warrant for What Never Arrives.
The term is the house's; the play runs on sediment starvation below dams, set out by Kondolf as hungry
water and measured at global scale by Syvitski, Vörösmarty and colleagues, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging sediment starvation as **a harm made of an absence, delivered somewhere else**: a dam stores water
and stills it, the load the river was carrying settles out in the reservoir, and the flow released below
carries nothing, so the river takes its load from its own bed and the coast that the load was building
stops being built. The play models the documented mechanics, the dam that delivers everything it promised,
the sediment that stops moving without anybody removing it, the clear water below that erodes bed and banks
to recover what it is missing, the delta that was a standing deposit maintained by continuous arrival and
now retreats, the ledger that closes correctly at the dam wall while the cost lands hundreds of kilometres
away and decades later, and the corrections that spend storage and money on something the payer will never
see, as the load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of an arrogant engineer.

The governing law is that a river is a conveyor as well as a supply, and only one of those two functions is
ever the reason a dam is built. Water is the thing people want and the thing the works are rated on. The
sediment is not a resource anyone is asking for, has no line in the case for construction, and is the
material out of which a coastline is continuously remade. Interrupting the conveyor requires no waste, no
negligence and no emission: the load simply stops where the current stops, which is what still water does.

What makes it structural rather than an oversight is where the effects land. Kondolf's hungry water is the
near half: a flow released without its load is under-supplied and will recover some of it from the channel,
so the bed incises, the material coarsens, spawning gravels are lost and bridges and other works are
undermined downstream. The far half is the coast. A delta is not a place that was built once, it is a
deposit held in balance against subsidence and the sea by material arriving every year, so removing the
arrival does not damage it in any visible way and simply stops maintaining it.

The scale is measured. Reservoirs trap on the order of a fifth to two fifths of the sediment the world's
rivers carry, and the paired figures are the whole structure in one line: human soil erosion has increased
the sediment set in motion by rivers by around 2.3 billion tonnes a year, while retention behind dams has
reduced what reaches the coasts by around 1.4 billion tonnes a year. More is moving. Less is arriving. And
for many populated deltas, the engineering upstream matters more to their survival than the rise in sea
level does.

It is distinct from the house's **The Horizon Problem**, where a plan is complete to its instrument's edge
and silent about what lies past it: there the failure is one of knowledge and boundary, the beyond
excluded because it was never in view. Here the beyond is in view, downstream is a real place with people
in it, and the effect is not unknown but unowned: the dam's accounting is correct and complete for the
thing the dam was built to do, and the coast is another authority, another budget and sometimes another
country.

It is distinct from the house's **The Balloon Effect**, where pressure on a banned trade in one place makes
it bulge into the next and the total is unchanged: there an activity moves. Here nothing moves. A flow is
stopped, and the harm at the far end is the absence of the flow, which is a different shape entirely and
cannot be found by looking at where it went.

It is distinct from the house's **Dry Enough to Build On**, where a flood defence works and the ground
behind it fills with what the exceedance will take: there the protection and the exposure are in the same
place, and the harm is a larger version of the hazard the work was built against. Here the harm is of a
different kind from the hazard, and it lands where the work is not.

It is distinct from the house's **Good Somewhere, Bad Somewhere**, where an improvement is equilibrated
away by movement and the shortfall returns on another gauge in the same place: there people move and the
total is defended. Here nobody moves and nothing is defended, a physical stock is intercepted, and the
place that loses is not the place that gained.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the builder as
  arrogant or the beneficiaries as heedless, because the source structure offers nobody a wrong choice: the
  dam is built against real floods and for real power and real water, it delivers all of it, and the
  sediment is not a thing anyone was asked to deliver. Stilling water is how storage works, and settling is
  what stilled water does, so no waste is produced, nothing is emitted and nothing is taken from anyone who
  is present. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, a river being a conveyor as
  well as a supply and only the supply having a rating, so the misfit is that the work is complete and
  correct on every axis it was designed against, and the axis it broke was never one of them.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Passing sediment through
  works, by sluicing, bypass or drawdown, spends the reservoir's storage and its head, which is the whole
  of what it was built to hold, and it must be done in the flood season when the water is worth most.
  Adding gravel below the dam treats a hundred kilometres of channel one truckload at a time, forever, and
  never reaches the coast. And whoever pays is upstream while whatever is saved is downstream, decades out,
  so the payer buys a result they will not observe, for people they will never meet, out of a budget
  answerable to neither.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the naming, the mechanism, the global flux figures
  and the delta work are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: trapping proportions vary widely by
  basin, reservoir and operating regime, delta loss has several causes at once, subsidence from fluid
  extraction and delta channelisation among them, and sediment starvation is one contributor rather than
  the whole account. **The play makes no claim that dams should not be built**, only that a river carries
  two things and the works are rated on one.
