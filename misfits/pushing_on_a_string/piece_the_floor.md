## Load Bearing

The rate cannot fall much below zero because no one holds a bond paying less than the cash they could hold instead, so once the rate reaches the floor it falls no further, drawn in the theory as the flat segment of the money-market curve where a shift in money supply moves output not at all (Keynes; Hicks). The floor is not exactly at zero: the effective bound sits slightly below it, since holding cash carries its own small costs, but the shape is the same, a wall the rate meets and cannot pass.
