# Play: Time Ran While You Were Well

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Time Ran While You Were Well.
Every legal system puts a time limit on bringing a claim, and it is right to. Evidence decays, witnesses die, documents are destroyed on schedules that are themselves lawful and sensible, and a person who did something forty years ago cannot mount a defence to an allegation about it: they have no records, no colleagues and no memory of an ordinary Tuesday. A system in which nothing ever closes is a system nobody can insure, audit, plan under or be finished with. So a clock is set, and it starts running on the day the thing was done. Which was, at the time these rules were written, more or less the day anybody would have known about it.

## Arc

The bend is this: the clock runs from the act, and how long a harm takes to become knowable has nothing to do with when it was caused.
It runs on a period with excellent reasons behind it.
The Clock That Started Then is not a loophole and not a favour to wrongdoers. Repose is a genuine good, and the arguments for it are the strongest arguments in the play: an old allegation cannot be answered, an old record cannot be produced, and a defendant who must remain liable indefinitely is a defendant who can never be told they are finished.
The Evidence That Went is the reason, and it does go. Files are shredded on retention schedules approved by lawyers, the site is demolished, the supervisor dies, and the only two people who could say what happened in that room are one dead and one certain of something that cannot be checked. Nothing improper occurred: the destruction is compliant, scheduled and often mandatory.
The One Who Did Not Know is the turn. This claimant is not sleeping on their rights, because there was no right to sleep on: the fibre lodged in 1974 and the tumour arrived in 2016, and mesothelioma commonly takes twenty to fifty years to appear. For four decades there was nothing to know, nothing to investigate and nothing to bring. The rule has a picture of a claimant who delayed, and this person does not resemble it in any particular.
So the filter sorts by latency rather than by merit. The longer a harm takes to become knowable, the more reliably it is extinguished before it can be brought, which means the mechanism selects precisely against the injuries that hide best. And the injuries that hide best are not a random sample: they include the slow cancers, the structural failures that take decades, and the harms a person cannot speak about for most of a lifetime.
The One Who Must Say No has nowhere to go. A judge can find a claim entirely meritorious and dismiss it without hearing the merits at all, because the point is taken as a preliminary issue and the answer to it is correct. The claim does not fail. It is never tried.
And the correction is real and deliberately incomplete. The discovery rule starts the clock when the injury is or should reasonably have been discovered, and most jurisdictions now apply it to latent disease: which hands the defendant exactly the stale-evidence problem the period existed to prevent, and requires courts to litigate when a person ought to have known something, a question about a state of knowledge that never existed. And underneath it sits the statute of repose, running from the defendant's own act and generally immune to discovery, so in construction and product cases the claim can be dead before the disease is alive, in a jurisdiction that has the discovery rule and applies it faithfully.

## Triggers

**[A Reason to Close the Books](plot_a_reason_to_close_the_books.md)**
A legal system decides that claims cannot be brought forever, because an old allegation cannot be answered and a defendant who is never finished is never at peace. Closes when the limitation period stands on the strongest arguments in the play, and none of them is about protecting anybody who did anything wrong.

**[Running From the Wrong Day](plot_running_from_the_wrong_day.md)**
Cue: a clock that starts on the day the act was done, drafted when the act and the injury were assumed to arrive together. Closes when the start date has been fixed to the defendant's conduct rather than to the claimant's knowledge, and nothing in the rule notices the difference.

**[Forty Years of Nothing](plot_forty_years_of_nothing.md)**
Cue: a fibre inhaled in 1974, and a person who is entirely well. Closes when four decades have passed in which there was nothing to know, nothing to investigate and nothing to bring, and the period has expired against somebody who did not delay by a single day.

**[The Point Is Taken](plot_the_point_is_taken.md)**
Cue: a claim issued, and a defendant with a complete answer that does not touch the facts. Closes when limitation is pleaded properly, by a party entitled to plead it, possibly by one who did nothing wrong and has no way to prove it now.

**[Right, and Out of Time](plot_right_and_out_of_time.md)**
Cue: a preliminary issue, tried before anything else and capable of ending the case on its own. Closes when a judge has found the claim meritorious and dismissed it without hearing the merits, the claim not failing but never being tried.

**[When Should You Have Known](plot_when_should_you_have_known.md)**
Cue: the discovery rule, moving the start to the day the injury was or should have been discovered. Closes when the defendant faces the stale evidence the period existed to prevent, the courts must litigate a state of knowledge that never existed, and the statute of repose runs on underneath regardless.

## Stakes

Whether a system can be called just when the thing that decides who is heard is how long the injury took to appear.
Every plot moves the same way: toward a rule with excellent reasons, applied correctly by people who are not wrong about anything, and away from any instrument that could distinguish a claimant who delayed from one who had nothing to know. The production cannot make a shredded file exist, and it cannot ask a defendant to stay answerable forever. What is left to fight over is not whether claims should close, they must, but what is owed to somebody whose right expired during the years they were well, and whether a filter that sorts by latency was ever choosing on anything a court would recognise as merit.
