# Time Ran While You Were Well: Reference

The research warrant for Time Ran While You Were Well.
The term is the house's; the play runs on limitation periods measured from the defendant's act, the latency
of asbestos-related disease, the discovery rule, and statutes of repose, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging the limitation bar on latent harm as **a filter that sorts by latency rather than by merit**: every
legal system limits the time in which a claim may be brought, and it is right to, because evidence decays,
records are lawfully destroyed, witnesses die, and a defendant who remains answerable indefinitely can
never be told they are finished. The period runs from the day the act was done, which was, when these rules
were framed, more or less the day anybody would have known about it. The play models the documented
mechanics, the repose that is a genuine good, the evidence whose decay takes the defence as surely as the
claim, the start date fixed to conduct rather than knowledge, the claimant who did not delay by a day, the
judge with a rule and no discretion, and the correction that is real and deliberately incomplete, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of wrongdoers escaping on a technicality.

The governing law is that how long a harm takes to become knowable has no relationship to when it was
caused. So a period measured from causation extinguishes claims in proportion to their latency, and latency
is not a property any court would recognise as bearing on merit. The mechanism selects hardest against the
injuries that hide best, and those are not a random sample: they include the slow cancers and the
structural failures that take decades to surface.

What removes the easy reading is that the claimant is not the person the rule describes. Limitation exists
to stop claimants sleeping on rights they hold. A person exposed to asbestos in the 1970s and diagnosed
with mesothelioma four decades later held no right to sleep on, since there was nothing to know, nothing to
investigate and nothing to bring. The conduct the bar discourages did not occur, and the bar applies at
full strength regardless, because it measures elapsed years rather than neglect.

What makes it structural rather than remediable is that the defendant may be innocent and equally
disarmed. Retention schedules are lawful, audited, frequently mandatory and often required by data
protection obligations, and the destruction takes exculpatory records as reliably as incriminating ones. So
the party pleading the bar may be one that never did the thing and can no longer show it, for which the bar
is the only defence left and a correct win establishes nothing.

And the correction hands the problem back. The discovery rule starts the period when the injury was or
reasonably should have been discovered, and most jurisdictions now apply it to latent disease. That is the
right answer, and it delivers to the defendant exactly the forty-year-old allegation with no records that
the period existed to prevent, requires courts to determine when a person ought to have known something
that never crossed their mind, and leaves the claimant who suspected early worse off than the one who
suspected nothing. Beneath it, statutes of repose run from the defendant's own act and are generally not
subject to discovery at all, so in construction and product cases a claim can be extinguished before the
disease exists, in a jurisdiction that has the discovery rule and applies it faithfully.

It is distinct from the house's **Known Too Late**, where a technology can be steered while its harms are
unknown and has set too hard by the time they show: there the loss is steerability, the thing that hardens
is the technology, and nobody's entitlement is extinguished. Here nothing hardens and nothing is
unknown by the end. The harm is fully understood, fully attributable and fully provable in principle, and
what has been removed is the standing to complain of it.

It is distinct from the house's **Early and Uncounted**, where a gene favoured early is never billed for
its late cost because too few of the cohort survive to pay it: there the late cost is genuinely invisible
to the mechanism, since the carriers are dead and selection has no instrument that could see it. Here the
late cost is entirely visible to everybody in the room, and a deliberate human rule declines to hear it.

It is distinct from the house's **The Horizon Problem**, where every plan ends at its instrument's edge so
what lies beyond carries no cost and casts no shadow: there the cost is simply not counted. Here it is
counted, named, quantified in a medical report and then barred, which is a different act entirely: not a
failure to see, but a decision, taken in advance and for good reasons, not to listen.

It is distinct from the house's **The Load It Always Carried**, where every cycle passes every check while
increments sum to a fracture: there the checks are honest measurements that genuinely cannot see the
accumulation. Here the system can see the accumulation perfectly well by the time it matters, and the
inspection it declines to perform is one it ruled out beforehand on principle.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the defendant
  as a wrongdoer escaping on a technicality or the legislature as careless, because nobody in the sequence
  has a decision to get wrong: the period rests on the strongest fairness arguments anyone makes, and none
  of them concerns protecting a person who did something; the records were destroyed in compliance with
  schedules that lawyers approved, auditors checked and privacy obligations often required; the defendant
  asserting the bar is using a defence the legislature provided on purpose, and may itself be innocent and
  unable to prove it for the same reason the claimant cannot prove the claim; the judge applying the rule
  has, in most systems, no discretion to do otherwise. The failure is authored by nobody and emerges from
  the structure, so the misfit is that a period measured from conduct decides cases by latency, and latency
  is not merit.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. The discovery rule works,
  is the right answer, and is now the law in most places for latent disease, and it moves the whole
  evidentiary problem onto the defendant rather than dissolving it. Determining when a claimant ought to
  have known produces a second inquiry into a state of knowledge that never existed, litigated at expense.
  Extending periods for latent injury leaves organisations unable to close any exposure, which is the harm
  repose exists to prevent. And statutes of repose, enacted precisely because the discovery rule made
  liability open-ended, are immune to it by construction, since a period discovery could extend would not
  be a repose.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the doctrine, the latency literature and the
  reforms are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: limitation, discovery and repose vary enormously
  between jurisdictions and by cause of action, and the periods quoted are typical rather than general;
  many systems have equitable extensions, judicial discretion to disapply the period, or special regimes
  for particular harms, so the bar is less absolute in some places than the play stages it; asbestos
  compensation in several countries runs substantially through statutory schemes and trusts rather than
  through ordinary limitation-governed litigation; and latency figures are population ranges rather than
  facts about any individual. **The play makes no claim that claims should never close**, they must, only
  that a clock started by the defendant's conduct decides which injuries can be spoken about.
