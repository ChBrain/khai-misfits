# The Fourth Year: Reference

The research warrant for The Fourth Year.
The term is the house's; the play runs on electoral cycles in criminal sentencing by elected judges, and on
the accountability and independence trade in judicial selection, all cited below.

## Line of Work

Staging judicial electoral cycles as **one wire with a virtue at one end and a defect at the other**: most
American states elect their trial judges, deliberately, because an unremovable judiciary is itself a
democratic problem, and the instrument chosen makes judges answerable to the community they sentence in.
That answerability conducts, it carries the community's standards to the bench, and it conducts most
strongly when the term is shortest. The play models the documented mechanics, the wire strung on purpose,
the electorate exercising a legitimate preference through the only channel it has, the judge whose good
behaviour and bad behaviour produce identical numbers, the scheduling that assigns a defendant to a year,
and the corrections that each move accountability by exactly the amount they move independence, as the
load-bearing vectors of the structure, not as a tale of judges selling sentences for votes.

The governing law is that accountability and independence are not two dials but one, read from opposite
ends. Every increment of the first is exactly a decrement of the second, so there is no setting that
delivers both, and the property under complaint is the property under praise.

What removes the easy reading is that no measurement can allocate the effect to a motive. A judge chasing
votes and a judge sincerely reweighting toward the community's standards as the moment of accountability
approaches produce the same curve, and an elected judiciary exists precisely so that community standards do
reach sentencing. So the same finding is evidence that the system is working and evidence that it is
failing, and there is no instrument even in principle that separates them.

What makes it structural rather than remediable is where the cost falls and how it is recorded. The
difference between two defendants is which year of a four-year term their case came up in, set by arrest
dates, continuances, laboratory backlogs and a listing diary, none of which ever considered them. A
sentence inside the guideline band, or reasonably departing above it, is a sentence the judge was entitled
to impose, so a pattern visible only across hundreds of cases cannot be raised inside one: the harm exists
as a coefficient in a study and never as an error in a file.

And the pressure runs one way for a reason that is not a preference. A release that ended badly has a name,
a face and a date, and fits in thirty seconds. A sentence a year too long has no victim who knows they are
one and no story shape at all. So the asymmetry is a property of what is legible through a channel offering
roughly one bit every few years, rather than of what any electorate wants.

It is distinct from the house's **No Need to Ask**, where a state funded by resource rents need not tax its
citizens and so need not answer to them: there accountability is absent and its absence is the harm. Here
accountability is present, working, and producing the harm by working, which is the opposite arrangement.

It is distinct from the house's **Regulatory Capture**, where a watchdog set to guard the public against an
industry comes in time to guard the industry against the public: there the agency's allegiance is captured
and turned around. Here nobody is captured and nothing turns: the judge is answering to exactly the
constituency the design named, and the design's own beneficiary is the one applying the pressure.

It is distinct from the house's **The Wasted Vote**, where each voter deserts a third candidate who cannot
win and the field locks to two: there the electorate's own reasoning traps the electorate. Here the
electorate's preference is satisfied, not frustrated, and the cost is exported entirely onto a
non-participant who never had a vote in the matter.

It is distinct from the house's **Room for Three in a Hundred**, where the price that clears an
overloaded docket is set by capacity and applied identically to the guilty and the innocent: there the
mechanism is a throughput constraint and the discount is uniform. Here nothing is overloaded, no bargain is
offered, and the variation is temporal, applying to the same person differently depending only on when
their case was reached.

## Restrictions

What the house refuses to model, and how it credits what it stages.

- **No villains**: the elements are reduced to load-bearing vectors. The play refuses to cast the judge as
  venal or the electorate as a mob, because nobody in the sequence has a decision to get wrong: the states
  that chose elective judgeships were answering a real democratic problem and were not wrong to think an
  unremovable judiciary the greater danger; the voters hold a legitimate preference about how firmly
  wrongdoing should be met and express it through the only instrument they have; the judge sentences within
  lawful discretion, gives sound reasons, and applies the standards of the community, which is what an
  elected judiciary is for; the listing clerk who set the date had no view of anybody. The failure is
  authored by nobody and emerges from the structure, so the misfit is that the same finding is proof the
  design is working and proof it is failing, with no measurement anywhere able to say which.
- **No hero**: the play refuses to resolve the misfit into a costless correction. Appointment steadies the
  sentences and hands the choosing to whoever appoints, which is a different unaccountability rather than
  none. Lengthening terms stretches the cycle and thins the answerability in exact proportion, since they
  are one quantity. Retention elections soften the contest and keep the calendar. Tightening the guideline
  band narrows the variance and moves the discretion to the prosecutor, who is also elected and whose
  decisions almost nothing reviews. There is no setting of the dial that gives independence and
  accountability at once.
- **Credit, not claim**: the research above is other people's work, packaged and attributed here, never
  claimed as the house's. The staging is the house's; the studies, their identification strategies and
  their estimates are theirs. The bounds are carried with them: these cycles are not found everywhere, and
  subsequent work examines how common they are and finds them uneven across jurisdictions, selection
  methods and periods, with formal rules and informal norms both mediating; the mechanism behind the timing
  remains argued over, including whether it is anticipation of challenge, media attention, or a sincere
  shift in what the judge believes the community expects; the estimates are averages across large samples
  and say nothing about any individual judge or case; and the systems studied are American, with judicial
  selection elsewhere working differently enough that the finding does not transfer unexamined. **The play
  makes no claim that judges should not be accountable**, an unanswerable judiciary being its own misfit,
  only that the wire carrying the accountability cannot be made to conduct in one direction.
