# Play: Good Years for the Moose

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

Good Years for the Moose.
Nobody hunted the caribou. Nobody logged the caribou's forest for the sake of getting at the caribou. Nobody wanted fewer of them and no policy was aimed at them.
A valley was cut, on a licence, to a plan, and what grew back was young forest full of things a moose likes to eat.
The moose did well. The wolves did well. And the herd that lives in the old forest, eating lichen, competing with nobody for anything, is gone.

## Arc

The bend is this: two animals that never meet, never compete and never encounter one another are joined at the hip, and the thing that joins them is a third animal doing exactly what it should.

It runs on one relation from theory and one from the ground. **Two prey species sharing a predator are coupled through that predator's numbers**: raise one and the predator population rises with it, and the extra predators fall on both, so an increase in one prey is a decrease in the other with no competition between them at any point. And on the ground, cutting mature forest produces open early-seral growth that feeds moose, deer and elk, and does not feed an animal whose winter food is lichen that grows on old trees.

Take the cutting first, because the play has to establish that it is **allowed**. It is licensed, planned, regulated and in most respects sustainable, and early-seral forest is not a wasteland: it is habitat, and a great many species do better for it. Nothing in the harvest plan is a wrong done to anybody. Nobody would sign a plan that said the caribou are the point of this, because they are not the point of it and were never considered as anything but a constraint.

Then the fact that inverts it. **The subsidy goes to somebody else and arrives at the caribou anyway.** More browse means more moose. More moose means more wolves, which is not a problem and is usually counted a success, since a healthy predator population is what a functioning system has. And then a wolf that is out because there are moose to be had crosses a caribou, and takes it, because that is what a wolf does when it meets one. The caribou is not the target of the predation, not competing with the moose for anything, and not present in anybody's calculation.

Then the sharper half, which is why the thing is hard to act on. **What can be seen is not what is causing it.** The proximate driver is predation: the animals are being killed by wolves, and that is what a field season records. The ultimate cause is the habitat, which changed decades ago and is a hundred-year matter to change back. So a manager looking at the evidence sees wolves, and acting on what the evidence shows means acting on the mechanism rather than the cause, forever, because the cause is still there and will keep supplying it.

And nobody has done anything wrong. The forester cut to a plan. The moose ate what grew. The wolves raised what they could feed. The caribou did nothing at all, which is the point: it did not compete, did not encroach, did not change its behaviour, and is the one that is gone.

Reach for a settlement and each one is a killing programme or a century. Reduce the wolves and the decline slows, and you must do it again next year and the year after, because the moose are still there and the forest is still young. Reduce the moose instead and the wolves fall with them, which works, and means shooting a valued animal that did nothing but eat what grew where the light came in. Restore the habitat and you are waiting for lichen on trees that have not been planted yet. And do none of it and the herd goes, on a schedule everybody can already read.

## Triggers

**[Every Step Was Allowed](plot_every_step_was_allowed.md)**
The cutting established as licensed and legitimate rather than as the villain: a plan, a permit, a regulated rotation, and early-seral forest that many species do better for. Closes when it is clear no wrong was done to anybody and no plan ever mentioned the caribou except as a constraint.

**[The Browse Comes Up](plot_the_browse_comes_up.md)**
Cue: light reaching the forest floor. Closes when the subsidy is shown to go entirely to somebody else: moose, deer and elk eat what grows where the canopy opened, and an animal whose winter food is lichen on old trees gains nothing from any of it.

**[The Wolves Did Well](plot_the_wolves_did_well.md)**
Cue: more moose than there were. Closes when the coupling is in the open: a predator's numbers follow what it can feed on, more wolves meet more of everything, and the caribou is taken by an animal that was not looking for it and is competing with nobody.

**[Seen and Not the Cause](plot_seen_and_not_the_cause.md)**
Cue: a field season's records, full of wolf kills. Closes when the split between proximate and ultimate is visible: predation is what the evidence shows and habitat is what produced it, so acting on the evidence means acting on the mechanism, forever, while the cause keeps supplying it.

**[And Something Has to Be Killed](plot_and_something_has_to_be_killed.md)**
Cue: the recovery options. Closes when each is found to be a killing programme or a century: wolves reduced every year while the forest stays young, moose reduced instead and a valued animal shot for eating what grew, habitat restored on a timescale measured in tree lives, and doing none of it on a schedule everybody can read.

## Stakes

Whether a system can be managed when the animal doing the harm, the animal receiving the subsidy and the animal being harmed have no relationship with each other at all.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether the forestry was wrong, because it was licensed and regulated and many species gained by it, and not whether the wolves are villains, because a healthy predator population is what a working system has, but whether anything can be done about a harm whose cause is in the past and whose mechanism is a living animal. The production cannot settle it. It can only show that the cutting was allowed, that the subsidy went to a species with no connection to the one that pays, that the predator's numbers follow its food and the encounter follows its numbers, that what is visible is the mechanism rather than the cause, and that every remedy either kills something every year or waits for a forest.
