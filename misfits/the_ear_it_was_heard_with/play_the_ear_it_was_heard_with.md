# Play: The Ear It Was Heard With

## Estate

[khai-misfits](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-misfits): the house that holds this production and answers for the whole run.

## Name

The Ear It Was Heard With.
A colleague writes seven words about a report: is this the final version?
It is a question about a file. It is also a disclosure that the writer has been looking at it, a statement about who gets to ask, and a request that something happen.
All four were sent. The reader answers one of them. Neither of them has done anything wrong, and by the end of the week they are not speaking easily.

## Arc

The bend is this: the reader selects, the writer cannot restrict what there is to select from, and every attempt to say which one was meant is another message with the same number of sides.

It runs on three findings, none of them anybody's doing. A sign carries several functions **at once**: Bühler's model of language as an instrument has it as a symbol of what it is about, a symptom of the one who sent it and a signal to the one who receives it, and all three are operative in any speech action at any moment, in varying strength. A hearer does not weigh those and pick the intended one: relevance theory says they follow a path of least effort, testing readings in the order they come to mind, and **stop at the first one relevant enough**. And the effect is not the sender's to perform at all: Austin separates what a speaker does in saying something from what they bring about by saying it, and puts the second outside the speaker's control.

Take the sending first, because the play has to establish that the writer is not careless. **Seven words do not carry fewer sides than seventy.** The content side gets shorter; the others do not. A terse message discloses more about the sender's state, not less, and positions the reader more sharply, not less, because there is nothing else in it to attend to. So the writer cannot buy safety by writing less, and the version of the message with only the file in it does not exist and cannot be composed.

Then the fact that inverts it. The reader is not choosing among four labelled options. They are doing the ordinary thing, which is to take the reading that arrives first and is good enough to act on, and stop. **On a Thursday afternoon with a difficult week behind them, the reading that arrives first is the one about who gets to ask.** That is not a misreading. It was sent. Nothing in the message says it was not the operative one, because nothing in any message can say that.

Then the sharper half. **Both of them are right, and each can prove it.** The writer meant the file, and did; the reader was positioned, and was. There is no fact of the matter that a more careful reading would have recovered, because the message did not contain one thing meant surrounded by three accidents. It contained four things sent. So the disagreement is not about what happened. It is about which of the things that happened counts, and neither has any standing to settle that for the other.

And the remedy recurses. The writer, being reasonable, writes again: **I was only asking about the file.** That sentence is a question about a file only on its content side. On the relationship side it says the reader read it wrong, which is a stronger positioning than the first message managed, and on the disclosure side it says the writer noticed and minded. **The correction is more relational than the thing it corrects**, and it is completely accurate, and there is no way to write it that is not.

And nobody has done anything wrong. The writer meant what they say they meant. The reader took a reading that was really there. The correction is true. The advice everybody gives, be clearer, say what you mean, ask if unsure, is good advice and each piece of it is another utterance with four sides.

Reach for a settlement and each one costs the thing it was for. Saying less strengthens the sides that are not content. Saying more is more messages. Meeting in person adds channels and the sides come too, now with a face and a pause to read as well. Asking what was meant is a question with a relationship side of its own, and the answer is an utterance, and so on, for as long as either of them keeps trying.

## Stakes

Whether two people who are both right about the same message have anything to say to each other about it.
Every plot moves the same question forward: not whether either was careless, because the writer meant what they say and the reader took a reading that was there, and not whether people should communicate better, because every piece of that advice is another utterance with the same sides, but whether a message can be made to carry one thing. The production cannot make it. It can only show that the sides are sent together and that no wording removes any of them, that the reader's stopping rule is their own and is what comprehension is, that both accounts of what happened are accurate and neither has standing over the other, that the correction is more relational than the thing it corrects and is true, and that every remedy is an utterance, so the structure is not escaped by trying harder but iterated by it.
